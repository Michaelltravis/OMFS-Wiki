# Pursuit spec sheet — City of Richmond, CA WPCP, Collection and Stormwater O&M
_The single source of numbers, proof, references, stories, quotes, gaps, budgets and decisions for the Richmond proposal. Approve it, and seven writers can work three weeks without asking a question this sheet does not answer._

## How to use this sheet

Proposals are due **2026-09-29 at 14:00 PT** via BidsOnline. Interviews are **2026-10-28**. The contract starts **2027-05-15**.

The RFP scores four things (C-073): Firm Qualifications and Experience **15%**, Project Staffing and Management Plan **30%**, Project Understanding and Technical Approach **30%**, Fee **25%**. Fee opens only after the panel has scored the narrative and the interview (C-077), so 75 of the 100 points are decided by 40 narrative pages, the reference checks the City reserves the right to make (C-075), and the interview. Every row on this sheet exists to move one of those 75 points, or to keep the proposal inside the responsiveness gate that precedes them.

Five constraints shape every decision below.

1. **No clarification is available.** The question window closed 2026-09-04 (C-012). Ambiguities are settled here, conservatively, and recorded in Section 11.
2. **Responsiveness is a gate, not a score.** C-072, C-039 and C-074 let the City eliminate a proposal for a missing mandatory element or a format deviation before scoring begins. The gate items are the notarized surety statement from the surety company itself (C-047), two named key personnel (C-054), five references carrying all six C-052 fields, both Attachment D forms (C-057), and the mandated section order of C-036.
3. **Weight decides pages.** Qualifications carries 15 points; Staffing and Technical Approach carry 30 each. Section 8 allocates pages on that basis, which reverses the pilot.
4. **Never invent a number.** Every figure here is copied from `proof-points/registry.md`, a `verbatim/<slug>/pages/pNNNN.md` page, a wiki block, the requirement exports in `pursuits/richmond-2026/reqs/`, `pursuit.md`, or `trip-report-digest.md`, and carries its source. Where sources conflict, every observed value is shown with its source and a lock is recommended with a reason. Where nothing exists there is a gap decision in Section 7, never a placeholder in body text (voice guide Rule 35: zero editorial residue).
5. **Nothing on this sheet authorizes a bracket, a hedge or a tag in body text.** If a value is not locked by production lock, the writer uses the write-around named here.

**Source keys.** `PP-####` = `proof-points/registry.md`. `PPN-##` = a real fact sourced to a verbatim page but **not yet registered** — register before a writer opens the section (D-24). `TM-####` = `testimonials/inventory.md`. `ST-####` = `stories/catalog.md`. `C-###` / `S-###` = requirement / scope-register item in `pursuits/richmond-2026/reqs/`. `FP §x` = `Desktop\Richmond\Draft Sections\02 Claude Working Drafts\_work\fp_03_qualifications.md`. `TRIP` = `pursuits/richmond-2026/trip-report-digest.md`. `hull` = `verbatim/hull-wwtf-om-2026`. `sm` = `verbatim/santamonica-swip-om-2025`. `ocwut` = `verbatim/ocwut-16-26`. `fulton` = `verbatim/fulton-county-2025`.

**Production dates assumed.** Pink team 2026-09-15 · Red team 2026-09-18 · production lock 2026-09-24 · submission 2026-09-29 · interview 2026-10-28.

## 6. The unmatchable fact

**Mack Mckenzie was the Chief Plant Operator and Assistant General Manager of the Richmond Water Pollution Control Plant.** No competitor can write that sentence, and neither can the incumbent. It is the highest-leverage fact in the pursuit and the easiest one to over-reach on.

### Exact wording allowed

Verbatim from `wiki/resumes/mack-mckenzie.md`, sourced to sm p0086 paras 2–3 (PP-0596, PP-0597):

> **Chief Plant Operator (CPO)/Assistant General Manager | Richmond Water Pollution Control Plant | Richmond, CA.** Mack was responsible for the operation, maintenance, collections system, and laboratory at the Richmond Water Pollution Control Plant, a facility designed for 16-MGD treatment of the City's sewage. He supervised 27 employees within five departments: Operations, Maintenance, Collections, Laboratory, and Administration.

**Also available, verbatim, from the same block:** duties including "fostering a safe work environment, meeting contract deliverables, maintaining client satisfaction, presenting at monthly board meetings, ensuring regulatory and lab compliance, addressing routine and emergency maintenance issues, capital planning, budget compliance, managing large capital projects, implementing efficiency programs, and developing long-term strategic plans"; "Mack also trained operations staff in wastewater theory and practical application"; credentials Wastewater Treatment Plant Operator Grade V, Water Treatment Operator T2, AWT3, Distribution System Operator D2; current standing "Member, California State Water Resources Control Board — Wastewater Needs Assessment Advisory Group (2024–2027)"; and 33 miles of sewer line cleaning and CCTV recording completed at the Fillmore Recycled Water Facility (PP-0605).

### Seven hard rules

1. **Past tense, always.** Mack ran that plant; he does not run it now.
2. **Never print the capacity in the same paragraph.** The resume says "designed for 16-MGD"; the City's plant is 20 MGD today with a 4.6 MG storm storage tank (LN-67, LN-85). Both are true at different dates, and a reader who spots the seam reads it as carelessness about the City's own plant. The box names the role, the four functions, the 27 people and the five departments. Nothing about capacity. If the box and a current-capacity sentence would land on the same page, move the capacity sentence.
3. **Never state when he was there, or for how long.** The bank carries no Richmond tenure dates, and the resume's "Total Water/Wastewater Experience: 11 years" covers a career that also includes Soquel Creek, Novato, SBIWTP San Diego, Limoneira, Santa Paula and Fillmore. Write the role as a completed fact without a period until the account team supplies dates (G-19).
4. **Never write him as proposed until D-01 is decided.** PURSUIT records both key personnel as placeholders by the decision of 2026-09-05 and Mack's role as pending. Until that changes he is a Jacobs operations leader who held that post. The pilot's editor already made this correction once.
5. **Never imply access.** C-014 bars contact with City employees about this solicitation and C-043 requires disclosure of any communication with City staff or Council since the RFP release. A former Richmond WPCP leader on our team invites exactly that question. The wording states his history and nothing about relationships, and the C-043 disclosure is completed on the strength of a positive check, not an assumption.
6. **The prose beside the box says what it changes, once.** One sentence, in the main clause, naming a Richmond-specific consequence — the collections and laboratory scope, or the five-department span, or the monthly board reporting. Not "unique insight". Not "unmatched familiarity" (Rule 34 bars an intensifier without a number in the same sentence).
7. **His Richmond history does not satisfy C-053.2 or C-054.** Those require the named Project Manager and Collection System Manager. Placing the box near the org chart will read as if it does.

### Placement rule

- **Executive Summary:** inside the first 100 words, in a boxed callout beside the opening paragraph. One box, 25 to 40 words, role and functions only.
- **Qualifications (8.4.3):** inside the first 100 words, in the same box with the same wording, at the head of the section, before the corporate-entity table.
- **Nowhere else.** Not the cover letter, not the technical approach, not the fee proposal. The device budget in Section 8 allows these two boxes and no third. This is the only device in the proposal that repeats verbatim, and that repetition is deliberate — it is the single exception to Rule 27's anti-repetition test. A fact this good degrades on the third telling.

### Device rules that apply everywhere (Rule 32)

For device limits, a **major section** is one of 8.4.1 to 8.4.7. A **subsection** is a C-036 sub-heading in 8.4.5, or a numbered subsection elsewhere. Every count in the table above sits inside these limits; where a source draft exceeded one, the count here is the limit, not the ambition.

- **Stat callout:** number, comparator and consequence in 8 to 20 words, and the figure must also appear in the prose on the same page. **One per page maximum.**
- **Fact box:** a verbless triad of outcomes, 15 to 30 words, the client named, no tool names. **One per subsection, closing it.**
- **Pull quote:** 35 words maximum with name, title and organization, never unnamed. **One per major section maximum — and zero today** until Section 5 permissions return.
- **Commitment callout:** a named person or role, a verb, an object, a cadence, and a number or dollar value. **Two per section maximum.**
- **Table share of total words:** 0.35 hard ceiling, 0.25 target. The mandated six-field reference table, the C-001 crosswalk and the fee tables raise this legitimately; nothing else may.
- **One org chart in the whole proposal** — spent in 8.4.4, and C-044's "lines of authority" requirement in 8.4.3 is met with a compact governance list, not a second chart. **One value wheel**, in the Executive Summary. **One Traditional-versus-Jacobs table**, in the Executive Summary. **One map per section.**
- **Every exhibit** carries a numbered caption in brand format, is pointed to in bold from the prose, and has the client's outcome in its rightmost column — "Value to the City", never "Processes".

The voice gate scores per 1,000 words of prose, and pages alone will not hold seven writers to it.
