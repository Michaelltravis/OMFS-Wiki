# Retention-First Workforce Continuity Strategy for O&M Transitions

## Proven Transition and Mobilization Strategy

We recognize that maintaining **uninterrupted compliance and operational stability** during contract transition is critical. Our mobilization plan is structured around a **disciplined, phased approach** that is discussed in detail in our project understanding and technical approach section. Our transition approach is specifically designed to **eliminate compliance risk, maintain operational continuity, and provide immediate stability** for [CLIENT], a coastal New England municipality, its facility staff, and stakeholders.

Jacobs will prioritize **continuity of leadership and institutional knowledge** by retaining the existing WWTF and collection system workforce to the greatest extent possible. We will begin transition planning immediately upon Notice of Award, with the primary objective of maintaining **stable day-to-day management and minimizing disruption** for [CLIENT] staff and the operating team.

## Retention-First Staffing Strategy

- **Workforce retention:** Offer employment to all qualified, interested incumbent staff to retain facility-specific expertise, relationships, and operating history.
- **License and certification continuity:** Maintain required licenses and certifications by confirming credential status early and supporting renewals and training to ensure uninterrupted compliance coverage.
- **Targeted staffing support:** Supplement staffing where needed (e.g., vacancies, coverage gaps, or specialized needs), with qualified resources to ensure management continuity from Day 1.

## Management Handoff and Knowledge Retention

We will implement a **structured leadership transition** that preserves what is working today and strengthens governance over time, which includes:

- **Early leadership alignment:** Project Manager Nathan Callison will meet with [CLIENT] leadership and current facility leadership to confirm near-term priorities, reporting expectations, decision authority, and communication cadence.
- **Role clarity and continuity:** We will maintain clear lines of authority and responsibilities while integrating the management team into Jacobs' safety, reporting, and performance systems.
- **Institutional knowledge capture:** We will formalize knowledge transfer through structured interviews, job shadowing, review of existing SOPs and logs, and documentation of critical operational "tribal knowledge" (e.g., seasonal conditions, equipment quirks, alarms, vendor nuances, and historical maintenance pain points).
- **Employee-focused communications:** We will conduct onboarding discussions and transition check-ins so staff understand expectations, benefits, training pathways, and how Jacobs will support them.

The detailed transition approach for assuming operational control, including milestones and an approximate timeline, is provided in the project understanding and technical approach section.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four-part framework (retention-first hiring, license and certification continuity, structured leadership handoff, formalized institutional-knowledge capture) applies to any O&M transition regardless of facility size or region, and directly counters evaluator concerns about service disruption or loss of local knowledge during a contract changeover. The "begin transition planning immediately upon Notice of Award" commitment and the tribal-knowledge examples are strong, portable language.

Pursuit-specific: name the actual Project Manager or Transition Manager who leads early leadership alignment; tailor the institutional-knowledge-capture examples to the target facility's known operational quirks where pursuit research reveals them; point the cross-reference at the pursuit's own technical-approach section number. # Executive Summary Opening — "Ready for a Change" Readiness Framing (Recipe)

**Headline pattern:** "Positioning [CLIENT]'s [program/system] for its next phase," followed by an italicized one-line thesis such as "[CLIENT] is ready for a change."

**Paragraph 1 — Acknowledge investment, then name the real gap.** Open by crediting the client for what it has already invested in (resiliency work, mechanical/electrical upgrades, collection system rehabilitation) as genuine evidence of its commitment to protecting infrastructure. Then pivot: the client is seeking *more than* regulatory compliance — it wants stronger leadership, clearer communication, and a proactive asset-management approach that gets ahead of problems, plus a trusted partner willing to take justified risks and stand behind its decisions. This reframes the win theme away from "we're technically better" and toward "we're the partner that closes your leadership/communication/proactivity gap."

**Paragraph 2 — Bridge to the value-proposition exhibit.** State plainly that the firm will deliver that change "using the approaches listed in Exhibit [X]" (a value-proposition table — see `value-proposition-table-approach-impact-value.md`), then add one sentence of scope/stakes context: the system serves not just residents but a sensitive environment and a community valued across the wider region, requiring disciplined operations, thoughtful planning, and a long-term perspective.

**Paragraph 3 — Ground the pitch in real facility statistics.** Close the opening with a factual paragraph stating year-round and seasonal population served, treatment capacity (MGD), number of pump/lift stations, miles of collection system, system type (gravity/low-pressure/force main), outfall type, and any unusual operating conditions (tidal influence, wet-weather variability, seasonal demand swings). End with a one-line statement of what those conditions require operationally (e.g., "proactive management and operational discipline").

**Closing beat, later in the section — answer "how" the operational discipline named in Paragraph 3 is actually delivered.** After the leadership/coordinated-operations narrative and the SME/proof-point content, circle back and name the concrete operational response to the conditions named in Paragraph 3: seasonal population changes, tidal infiltration, and wet-weather variability introduce operational complexity that must be actively managed, addressed through proactive monitoring, coordinated operations, and disciplined execution — standard operating procedures, preventive and predictive maintenance, and improved monitoring of pump stations and collection system assets. This closes the loop the opening paragraph opened, so the evaluator sees both the challenge and the specific operational mechanism addressing it. That closing-beat prose lives in `proof-point-examples-coastal-complex-systems.md`, where the source places it.

**Section close — "Your Next Chapter."** End the section by naming what selecting the firm allows the client to do next (strengthen leadership, improve transparency, align capital investment with long-term performance), tied back to the named on-site leader and the technical resources behind them.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the three-beat structure (credit past investment → reframe the real gap as leadership/communication/proactivity, not technology → ground it in real facility stats), the "ready for a change" / "next phase" headline device, and the deliberate loop-closing beat later in the section. Pursuit-specific: the acknowledged investments, the named gap, and every facility statistic must come from actual RFP language, site-visit notes, or published client data — fabricating a "gap" the client did not express undermines credibility with evaluators who wrote the RFP. Use with the paired prose block `exec-summary-readiness-and-system-profile-opening.md`, the exhibit block `value-proposition-table-approach-impact-value.md`, and `leadership-team-and-coordinated-operations-narrative.md` / `proof-point-examples-coastal-complex-systems.md` for the content between the opening and its closing beat.

# Transition Due Diligence and Mobilization Governance Checklist

## Immediate Mobilization and Governance

Upon receiving award, Jacobs will activate our Transition Leadership Team and establish a formal governance structure with [CLIENT], a coastal New England municipality. - Detailed review of O&M manuals, SOPs, CMOM documentation, and historical performance data

- Asset inventory validation and critical equipment condition assessment (including maintenance backlog)
- SCADA/telemetry review, remote access validation, alarm management testing, and cybersecurity verification
- Flood control readiness review (pumps/gates), standby power verification, and storm response procedures
