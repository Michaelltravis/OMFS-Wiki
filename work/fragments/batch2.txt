# CMMS-Integrated Inventory Management Value-Add for a Second Facility

*Presented in the source proposal under "Additional Items for Consideration" — modifications offered without a firm price, pending further scoping information from the client. The umbrella framing sentence is in [swip-scada-redundancy-upgrade-recommendation.md](swip-scada-redundancy-upgrade-recommendation.md).*

## Inventory Management for [SECOND FACILITY]

In recognizing the importance of [SECOND FACILITY], especially considering its role for DPR, [Firm] can extend our CMMS-integrated inventory management services to this facility, providing [CLIENT] a unified asset management system. This proactive system will support accurate part forecasting, timely reorders, prevent obsolescence, and ensure [CLIENT] gets the maximum value from these important assets. Dashboards and reports will provide real-time visibility into inventory status, usage trends, and procurement needs, empowering the team to make informed decisions while minimizing downtime and lifecycle costs. **[Firm]' approach will transform inventory control from a reactive function into a strategic asset management tool aligned with ISO 55001 standards and [CLIENT]'s operational goals.**

## Reuse guidance

This is a short, easy-to-adapt pitch for extending an existing or proposed CMMS-integrated inventory system to a facility that sits just outside the core RFP scope but is operationally connected to it — a second treatment plant, a plant that feeds the same treatment train, or a site the client is counting on for a future program. The structure is worth copying exactly. It opens by naming why the second facility matters to the client's own stated future rather than to the operator's convenience, offers a unified system across both sites as the benefit, lists four concrete capabilities the system delivers (accurate part forecasting, timely reorders, obsolescence prevention, and maximum value from the assets), adds dashboards and reports so the client can see the same data the operator sees, and closes on a bolded standards-anchored claim that reframes inventory from a back-office chore into strategic asset management aligned with ISO 55001. That last sentence is the line evaluators remember; keep it and keep the ISO reference, since it is what separates this from a generic parts-room promise. Replace [SECOND FACILITY] with the actual facility name, and mirror whatever driver the client has stated — resilience, reuse expansion, asset criticality — in the opening clause. The current Chief Plant Operator at the advanced water treatment facility has already demonstrated the power of effective community engagement, providing tours that resonate with public and school groups. Jacobs will build on this strong foundation, supporting the continuation and expansion of existing outreach efforts. We will work closely with [CLIENT] to align outreach with the city's sustainability goals and to ensure consistent, positive messaging about water reuse.

## Strategic Importance of Outreach

Outreach and education are now considered industry standard at advanced reuse facilities, as we've seen demonstrated at West Basin, our most recent addition in California. As utilities move toward **IPR and DPR** (indirect and direct potable reuse), proactive public engagement is critical to building trust and acceptance. Our approach reflects this strategic importance: community education is not an optional add-on, but a core component of long-term project success.

## Jacobs' Leadership in Water Reuse Education

Jacobs has been at the forefront of water outreach for decades. We have helped shape industry best practices and consistently demonstrated that effective education programs build community trust and excitement around water reuse.

# MBR Biofouling (Leech/Physa Snail) Mitigation Case Study

**Sidebar heading:** Leech Mitigation at PWSC Demonstration Plant

The Pure Water Southern California (PWSC) Demonstration Plant, operated by Metropolitan and the Los Angeles County Sanitation Districts, experienced persistent snail (Physa) proliferation during MBR testing. Frequent cleaning managed but did not eliminate the issue, raising concerns about membrane life. Jacobs and Hazen were engaged to identify and test practical mitigation strategies. Current measures being implemented include ultra-fine influent screening, chlorinated reactor feed, anoxic zone incorporation, and free ammonia membrane soaks. These approaches are now under evaluation to inform full-scale MBR design.

## Reuse guidance

This sidebar runs alongside the discounted-engineering offer and does a specific job: it turns an abstract promise of subject-matter-expert support into a named plant, a named problem, and a list of four measures actually being implemented, so the evaluator can see the bench has already solved the exact class of problem being offered. Use it as a supporting proof point wherever a proposal must demonstrate hands-on troubleshooting depth for MBR biofouling or pest issues in a potable reuse context, and place it physically beside the value-add or process-support pitch it substantiates rather than burying it in a technical narrative. The four measures — ultra-fine influent screening, chlorinated reactor feed, anoxic zone incorporation, and free ammonia membrane soaks — are the transferable technical content; keep them together, because the list is what makes the sidebar credible. Confirm the current status of the measures before restating them as settled fact: in the source proposal they were explicitly "under evaluation" and had not yet informed a completed full-scale design. Because this cites a specific, real, named third-party facility and its operators, treat it like a past-performance reference even though it lives in a technical-approach block, and verify permission to name the project in a competitive proposal for an unrelated client. # Plant Process Optimization with Digital Tools and the Replica Digital Twin

## Plant process optimization — leveraging digital tools and data-driven operations

As discussed in the "Strategic O&M Plans Built on Best Practices and Innovation" subsection, Jacobs will apply a comprehensive suite of digital tools and process optimization strategies to ensure the advanced water treatment facility (AWTF) continues to operate reliably and efficiently. At the core of our approach is continuous monitoring and adjustment of all key processes, guided by carefully defined KPIs. To complement real-time process monitoring, Jacobs will build and maintain a calibrated digital process model of the AWTF. Drawing from our extensive modeling toolkit — including BioWin and Jacobs' proprietary Pro2D and Replica™ tools — we will select the most appropriate platform and customize it to [CLIENT]'s influent conditions and treatment processes. This model will be updated regularly with plant data and used to predict issues, evaluate operational changes, and identify optimization opportunities before implementation. For example, the model can assess the impact of lowering the dissolved oxygen (DO) setpoint in the aeration basins, quantifying potential energy savings while maintaining anoxic zone nitrate removal performance.

Additionally, our mobile CMMS applications and cloud-based analytics streamline work orders, inventory, and compliance tracking — improving reliability and transparency while reducing admin time and reactive maintenance.

## Replica™ digital twin: proven ability to avoid unintended consequences in interconnected systems

Jacobs' Replica™ Digital Twin can manage system complexity and optimize operations. The Replica™ model serves as a "single source of truth," with the ability to create scenarios around all components of the system for better decision-making. We've developed Replica™ Digital Twin models for a range of clients similar to [CLIENT], a coastal Southern California potable reuse program. For the Tillman AWPF, Replica™ optimized the flow balance between the original WWTP and the new AWPF, maximizing the capture of available flows to the AWPF. The team also used Replica™ to model system hydraulics and controls to optimize membrane sizing. Once construction and startup are complete, it will be used to troubleshoot equipment issues, optimize operations, and train plant operators.

Exhibit 2-17 shows a sample Replica™ output for an example treatment facility, tracing flow from the pump station through the treatment train and illustrating the tool's scenario and optimization capabilities.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the digital-tools framing (calibrated process model plus mobile CMMS and cloud analytics), the named modeling toolkit (BioWin, Pro2D, Replica™), the "single source of truth" digital-twin argument, and the Tillman AWPF case example are corporate capability content and reuse as-is for any advanced-treatment or potable-reuse pursuit. # O&M Core Management Systems Framework

Jacobs will operate and maintain [CLIENT]'s WWTF and collection system — a coastal New England municipal treatment facility and its collection system — as an **integrated, performance-driven partnership** focused on **safe, compliant, reliable operations and continuous improvement**. We combine disciplined frontline execution with proven systems, data tools, and regional/national SME support, providing [CLIENT] with **consistent day-to-day performance and defensible, transparent decision-making** on maintenance, risk, and investment priorities. Our project team possesses the tools, programs, systems, training, and resources necessary for daily operations. These elements enable the team to accomplish all objectives by organizing, performing, measuring, and improving the multitude of functions that result in efficient O&M. **Exhibit 5-2** summarizes our management philosophy to achieve these objectives.

## Exhibit 5-2. Major elements of Jacobs' approach to project execution

**Health, Safety, and Security.** A framework for compliance with the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) and other safety regulations, vulnerability assessments, job safety analysis, safety training, emergency preparedness and response, and other safety-related functions.

**Compliance and Reporting.** A program of compliance for pollution prevention and waste minimization. Includes analysis and review checklists for all permits, report preparation for regulatory agencies, and environmental compliance training for all employees.

**Operations Management.** Documents treatment strategies and process control functions through standard operating procedures (SOPs), unit process control procedures (UPCPs), and treatment system optimization.

**Maintenance and Asset Management.** Integrates maintenance scheduling, documentation, and tracking; inventory control; and equipment condition assessment into a computerized database that allows us to maximize equipment performance and minimize downtime.

**Laboratory Management.** Standard procedures for sample collection, analysis, and documentation with our quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC) and chemical hygiene plans.

**Human Resources.** A program for the success of all of our employees as individuals and as a team, including recruitment, orientation, and training; standard training needs assessments for all employees; training programs for certification, ethics, compliance, safety, management and succession; and performance appraisals and professional development.

**Financial Management.** Systems and approaches to manage project cost through national purchasing power, documentation of contractual requirements, and continuous monitoring.

**Project Management.** Transparent communication including daily, weekly, monthly, severe weather, and annual client reports, internal measures of project performance, and plans to improve project performance beyond the requirements of our contract.

**Community Involvement.** A comprehensive public education and community involvement program developed in partnership with [CLIENT], including volunteering, charitable donations, and facility tours to benefit the community.

**Customer Service.** A robust program to demonstrate our commitment to be a good neighbor and be responsive to community concerns.
