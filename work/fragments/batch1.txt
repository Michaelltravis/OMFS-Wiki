# Innovation Workshop — Sample Agenda Structure (Proof-of-Execution Exhibit)

To make the annual Innovation Workshop concept credible rather than aspirational, the proposal includes two sanitized sample agendas from workshops already delivered for other O&M clients, framed as: "Sample agendas from previous Innovation Workshops for [other clients] are included below, demonstrating how these sessions have successfully driven alignment on priorities and implementation of actionable improvements."

## Standard format

- **Full-day session**, roughly 8:00/8:30 a.m. to 4:00/4:30 p.m., with an optional post-session networking hour
- **Opening block:** welcome/introductions, coffee/soft start, followed by a keynote or industry-outlook topic (e.g., "becoming a world-class utility," a sector-wide regulatory or funding update)
- **Morning technical blocks (45–60 minutes each):** 2–3 focused topics, often paired as "Part I / Part II" for a single theme (e.g., an optimization topic split across two sessions), each block presented by a named subject-matter expert or the client's own staff
- **Midday break/lunch**
- **Afternoon technical blocks:** additional SME-led topics — commonly energy/decarbonization, biosolids, digester or membrane technology, odor control, regulatory/compliance updates (e.g., PFAS, permit renewals), and grant/funding opportunities
- **Late-afternoon strategic block:** a forward-looking or "de-risking the future" planning discussion
- **Closing:** open discussion and adjournment (or additional discussion/closing remarks)

## Observed topic mix across sample agendas

- Regulatory/uncertainty framing (e.g., PFAS impacts and solutions, becoming resilient to an uncertain future)
- Process intensification / meeting new regulatory requirements
- Energy optimization and sector decarbonization (split across two sessions)
- Digital / "one-water" utility operations
- Adaptive planning and de-risking the future
- Grant and alternative municipal funding sources
- Ecosystem/asset enhancement
- Anaerobic digester technologies
- Biosolids economics ("are biosolids cool?")
- Strategic energy management at treatment plants paired with a circular water economy theme, illustrated with case studies from another country's utility sector
- Membrane replacement and energy-efficiency solutions
- Odor control technology updates
- Value-add process updates: biological phosphorus removal modeling, EV charging stations, biogas reuse, aeration blower upgrades, water plant predictive/preventive maintenance (PDM), LED lighting upgrades

## Proof point

A client quote reinforces the value: the annual workshops are described as providing a wealth of knowledge from industry experts that generates ideas of real value to both the utility and its ratepayers.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the day-long format (keynote → paired technical deep-dives → strategic/adaptive-planning close) and the practice of citing real prior agendas — even briefly — as evidence the workshop model already works elsewhere. Pursuit-specific: only reuse real, shareable prior-agenda content, generalized to remove the other clients' names, exact dates, and venue addresses; replace the topic mix with whichever subset is most relevant to the target facility (pull from `annual-innovation-workshop-program.md`'s topic list rather than reusing this exact mix verbatim). This block pairs directly with `annual-innovation-workshop-program.md` — use that block for the concept and topic menu, and this one for the "we've actually done this" evidence. # Past-Performance Project Description — Structure & Sidebar Pattern

Each project description in this proposal's past-performance appendix follows the same one-page (or one-spread) layout. The pattern works well because it front-loads the relationship story, then backs it up with facility-specific numbers, and closes the loop with independent validation (a client quote and/or third-party awards).

## Layout

**Header block**
- Facility/program name as the title (e.g., "[FACILITY] Wastewater System O&M" or "[FACILITY] Wastewater Treatment Plant DBO")
- Subtitle line: `[CLIENT] | [CITY/REGION]`

**Right-rail sidebar** (short, scannable facts — no narrative)
- Years of Service (start date or duration, e.g., "November 2018 to Present")
- Plant Size (design capacity, MGD)
- Owner Contact — reuse as `[ROLE, e.g., Director of Public Works]` only; drop name/phone/email
- Awards (if any) — list by award name and awarding body, generalized
- A 3–5 sentence pull-quote from the client contact, attributed by role only (e.g., "– [DIRECTOR OF PUBLIC WORKS]")

**Body — "Description of Services Provided"** (1–2 short paragraphs)
- What Jacobs does day to day and the contract structure (term length, assets covered)
- The originating driver: why the client sought a new operator or partner (a compliance event, cost pressure, a competitive procurement, dissatisfaction with an incumbent) and why Jacobs was selected
- The overarching operating philosophy in one sentence (e.g., "a holistic approach combining operations, asset management, technical services, planning, and regional support")

**Body — "Detailed Description of the Facilities Managed"** (run-in bold subheads; use only the subheads that apply)
- **Capital Improvements.** Planning/execution support for upgrades, and how Jacobs' on-site + regional support staff manage CMOM, sewer cleaning, CCTV, callouts, FOG, and CIPP repairs.
- **Treatment Systems and Processes.** A compact process train summary (preliminary treatment → secondary → tertiary/disinfection) plus design and peak/wet-weather flow ratings.
- **Collection System and Supporting Infrastructure.** Miles of pipe, number/type of lift or pump stations, SCADA integration.
- **Operations, Maintenance, and Optimization.** Specific, quantifiable improvements (energy or chemical reduction %, extended equipment life, automation added).
- **Regulatory Compliance and Program Management.** Consent order/permit history, IPP administration, cost-recovery mechanisms.
- **Community Involvement (or Community and Workforce Engagement).** Environmental stewardship, education/outreach, and — where relevant — staff transition and retention outcomes.

**Photo.** One large facility photo (aerial preferred) anchors the bottom of the page.

**Optional callout.** A shaded box for a differentiator that doesn't fit the standard subheads (e.g., a progressive design-build/DBO story, a technology pilot) placed either inline or on a companion page.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four-part shape (header/sidebar → services narrative → detailed facility subheads → photo) and the discipline of only using subheads that have real content behind them — a two-line "Capital Improvements" subhead with nothing meaningful in it reads as filler to evaluators. Pursuit-specific: pick the subhead set that matches what's differentiating for that reference (a brand-new contract should lean on "transition" and "Community/Workforce Engagement"; a decades-long contract should lean on "Operations, Maintenance, and Optimization" and awards). Always replace the owner-contact line with a role placeholder and strip every dollar figure — describe savings/investment qualitatively (e.g., "achieved substantial cost savings compared to the prior operating approach") rather than with numbers pulled from a specific client's contract. Pair with the individual sanitized project narratives in this folder (`project-narrative-*.md`) as worked examples of this structure, and with `similar-facilities-comparison-table-framing.md` for the companion "facilities similar to the Town" exhibit.
