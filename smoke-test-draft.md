# Transition and Odor Management Approach

Jacobs understands the Mid-Atlantic municipality's critical priority for a smooth transition from the incumbent operator while retaining the institutional knowledge that keeps this 6 MGD wastewater treatment facility and its 60-mile collection system running reliably. Equally important is addressing the community's longstanding odor concerns from the nearby neighborhood. Our comprehensive approach combines structured transition leadership, workforce retention, and a science-based odor-management program tailored to this facility's specific characteristics.

## Transition Leadership and Workforce Retention

The incumbent staff operating this facility possess irreplaceable institutional knowledge of the 6 MGD treatment plant, the 15 pump stations, and the gravity-sewer system. Our transition strategy prioritizes their retention and full integration into day-to-day operations. We will offer employment to all qualified and interested existing personnel, preserving system stability and the community's investment in trained, local operators.

A dedicated [TRANSITION MANAGER]—with 25–30+ years of water and wastewater operations experience and a documented track record of successful municipal contract takeovers—will oversee the transition. This specialized leadership is backed by a [PROJECT MANAGER] providing day-to-day on-site accountability and a [REGIONAL OPERATIONS MANAGER] establishing a culture of safety, operational excellence, and employee engagement. Together, this team ensures a seamless handoff: knowledge transfer is structured and deliberate, roles are clarified early, and clear expectations strengthen accountability from the operational start date forward.

Our three-phase transition structure spans pre-start activities, an initial 30-day transition window, and an extended 120-day integration phase:

- **Pre-Start Transition (through contract commencement)**: Due-diligence completion; facility walkthroughs documenting baseline conditions; staffing plan finalization and employment offers to existing staff; SCADA and remote-monitoring system transfer; emergency-response protocol review; and regulatory compliance verification.

- **Initial Transition (Days 1–30 post-commencement)**: Full operational handoff under supervisory presence; skills assessments and targeted training in process control, safety, and emergency response; staff onboarding workshops; activation of process-control monitoring; asset condition baseline assessment; and confirmation of spares, chemical, and solids inventory.

- **Extended Transition (Months 2–6)**: Continued regional SME support; finalization of asset inventory and capital planning data; inflow and infiltration investigation program launch; staff certification and advancement pathway initiation; and sustained focus on operational continuity and safety culture establishment.

This phasing ensures the community receives uninterrupted, reliable service while the team builds institutional depth and workforce confidence. The transition milestone schedule is detailed in [Exhibit: Transition Gantt Chart, Asset ID `111_Hull_0091KO_2`].

## Site-Specific Odor Control Program

Odor complaints from the nearby neighborhood are a stated concern. We address them through a comprehensive, science-based program grounded in a five-step evaluation framework:

1. **Quantify Odor & Corrosion** — continuous hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) monitoring at strategic locations, particularly pump stations and force-main discharge areas where septic conditions pose the greatest risk.
2. **Establish Goals** — define acceptable odor and corrosion thresholds aligned with community stewardship commitments.
3. **Determine Impacts** — sewer process modeling and air dispersion analysis to predict odor release and atmospheric transport.
4. **Evaluate Alternatives** — compare liquid-phase, vapor-phase, and control-through-design options; optimize chemical dosing locations and rates; assess operational improvements.
5. **Select and Implement** — deploy proven, cost-effective controls and monitor effectiveness on an ongoing basis.

## Proactive Early-Warning System

Rather than reacting after an odor complaint is received, Jacobs employs a predictive early-warning system that anticipates conditions likely to generate complaints. We pair real-time hydrogen sulfide sensors deployed at key collection-system locations with process modeling and atmospheric dispersion analysis. When sensor data indicates rising H₂S levels, a sewer-process model calculates predicted odor-causing compound generation and release rates; an atmospheric dispersion model (AERMOD) projects how concentrations will disperse given current and forecast weather conditions; and operations staff receive advance notice to implement proactive mitigation—adjusting chemical dosing, optimizing pump cycles, or modifying ventilation—before off-site impacts occur. This shift from reactive to proactive operations significantly reduces community impacts and demonstrates responsive stewardship during the transition period. ## Collection-System Odor and Corrosion Modeling

For this facility's 60-mile collection system, we will apply the WATS (Wastewater Aerobic/Anaerobic Transformations in Sewers) model, an industry-leading sewer process model developed through collaboration with Aalborg University and continuously refined through application on complex systems worldwide. WATS simulates the physical, chemical, and biological processes driving sulfide generation, odor release, and corrosion throughout the system, identifying odor and corrosion hotspots not readily apparent through field observations alone and evaluating mitigation alternatives in hours rather than the weeks or months required for traditional pilot testing. This science-based approach supports rapid, defensible decision-making and cost-effective solutions that protect collection-system assets and improve community outcomes. ## Community Communication and Rapid Response

All odor complaints are investigated and responded to within 1 hour of notification. The [PROJECT MANAGER] serves as the primary client contact, ensuring timely communication and transparency through monthly summaries of odor monitoring activities, field investigations, and corrective actions taken. This commitment—backed by a regional bench of odor-control specialists and technical support—reflects our understanding that responsive, transparent partnership is essential to community stewardship during the critical transition period and beyond.
