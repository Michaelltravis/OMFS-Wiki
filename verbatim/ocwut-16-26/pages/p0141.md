###### _Emergency Response and On-Call Coverage_

<!-- ¶3 -->
**We’ll update emergency response and on-call coverage as new facilities come online.** For hypochlorite generation, this includes chemical release response protocols, system failure contingencies (with reversion to delivered chemical supply), and hydrogen gas safety monitoring. For heat drying, coverage addresses thermal emergency response, emissions exceedance protocols, and fire/explosion prevention. On-call rotations will expand to cover new process areas, with all emergency response procedures developed during the Schedule 16 Startup Services phase.

###### _Expected Impacts on Operating and Maintenance Costs_

<!-- ¶5 -->
Each CIP project will affect operating and maintenance costs across multiple categories, including labor, utilities, chemicals, maintenance materials, and other recurring expenses. Because these projects are still in design or pre-design stages, specific cost impacts can’t be quantified today. However, we recognize the directional nature of these changes:

<!-- ¶6 -->
- <sup>**OSHG**will shift chemical costs from purchased product to raw materials (salt/</sup> brine) and electrical demand, while eliminating PSM/RMP compliance costs at both Deer Creek and North Canadian, a meaningful reduction in both compliance burden and safety risk.

<!-- ¶7 -->
- <sup>**The heat drying facility**will introduce new utility costs (significant energy</sup> demand for thermal processing), emissions monitoring expenses, and consumable costs, along with the dedicated staffing discussed above.

<!-- ¶8 -->
- <sup>**The landfill transition**will replace land application labor and equipment</sup> costs with hauling and tipping fees. Our analysis suggests these costs may be comparable, with landfill potentially offering cost advantages in the near term, given the investment otherwise needed to bring odor control and biosolids quality to compliant levels for continued land application.

<!-- ¶9 -->
As each project progresses through the Schedule 16 design phases, we’ll work with OCWUT to identify, quantify, and formalize cost adjustments through the Service Approval Form and Amendment process. After we have operated a complete CIP project for 2 years, OCWUT will conduct an audit using GAAP principles to determine fair and equitable cost adjustments. We’ll maintain detailed cost tracking in NexGen from the first day of operations for each new facility, providing OCWUT with transparent, auditable data to support that process.

###### _Hypochlorite Transport Plan_

<!-- ¶11 -->
Schedule 18 requires a plan to transport hypochlorite generated at the Deer Creek facility to the Chisholm Creek facility. We will develop a detailed Hypochlorite Transport Plan in coordination with OCWUT as the Deer Creek generation system design progresses. Because the system isn’t estimated for completion until 2029, a fully specified logistics plan would be premature at this stage. However, the planning framework will address:

<!-- ¶12 -->
- <sup>**Logistics and cost-effectiveness:**transport method, vehicle requirements,</sup> frequency, and route considerations weighed against the alternative of direct chemical delivery to Chisholm Creek. Our initial assessment, informed by our team’s operational analysis, is that hauling costs between facilities may be comparable to direct delivery, making the cost-benefit analysis a key decision point for OCWUT.

<!-- ¶13 -->
- <sup>**Safety and compliance:**chemical handling and transport protocols, DOT</sup> compliance with sodium hypochlorite transport, and spill response procedures.

<!-- ¶14 -->
- <sup>**Responsibility and delivery model:**whether transport is handled by a chemical</sup> transport subcontractor, the hypochlorite system vendor, or through another arrangement. Jacobs does not anticipate self-performing bulk chemical transport between facilities.

<!-- ¶15 -->
- <sup>**Contingency:**temporary reversion to delivered chemical supply if transport is</sup> interrupted or the generation system is offline.

<!-- ¶16 -->
###### _Biosolids Transition—Land Application to Landfill Disposal_

<!-- ¶17 -->
The systemwide transition from land application to landfill disposal is one of the most significant operational shifts in this contract. It affects all facilities, changes subcontractor relationships, alters lab and monitoring requirements, and sets the stage for the eventual integration of the Deer Creek heat drying facility. We’ll develop a detailed Biosolids Transition Plan in coordination with OCWUT as the timeline takes shape.
