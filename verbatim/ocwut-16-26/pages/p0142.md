A key advantage of initiating the landfill transition early in the contract, rather than waiting for the heat drying facility, is that it simplifies the eventual thermal drying integration. Instead of changing multiple elements simultaneously (landfill contracts, hauling logistics, subcontractor scope, regulatory approvals), the early transition establishes those relationships and logistics so that when the thermal dryer comes online, it’s a single internal change rather than a multi-party coordination challenge. The planning framework will address:

<!-- ¶2 -->
- <sup>**Phasing:**how and when each facility transitions, coordinated with OCWUT’s</sup> priorities and the Deer Creek heat drying facility timeline. We may recommend a selective early transition, for example, diverting biosolids with quality concerns (such as paper debris) to landfill first while maintaining land application for compliant material during the transition period.

<!-- ¶3 -->
- <sup>**Hauling logistics:**transport distances, vehicle and equipment requirements,</sup> landfill agreements, and scheduling. Area landfills have indicated capacity for OKC’s biosolids volume, though we will verify capacity commitments and understand restrictions (moisture content limits, percentage of total landfill capacity for wet material) before finalizing agreements.

<!-- ¶4 -->
- <sup>**Cost of transport and final disposal:**framework for estimating per-wet-ton</sup> and per-dry-ton costs, tipping fees, and fuel. The transition will replace current subcontractor costs (currently managed through Synagro) with direct hauling and disposal costs.

<!-- ¶5 -->
- <sup>**Staffing and lab adjustments:**reduced land application crew requirements,</sup> re-deployment of lab personnel currently performing daily biosolids testing, and changes to monitoring protocols once land application ceases.

<!-- ¶6 -->
- <sup>**Regulatory transition:**ODEQ notification requirements, permit modifications,</sup> and closure of land application sites.

<!-- ¶7 -->
- <sup>**Contingency:**continued land application capability if the heat drying facility</sup> timeline shifts or landfill capacity issues arise.

- For our biosolids management strategy, including current operations and long-term solids handling, refer to our Solids Management Plan in this section.

###### **Training and Knowledge Transfer**

<!-- ¶10 -->
**New systems and technologies demand new skills and we’ll build those skills before operational handover, not after.** For each CIP project, our training approach follows a structured sequence:

<!-- ¶11 -->
- <sup>**Manufacturer and vendor training:**Wewill coordinate with equipment</sup> manufacturers for both factory and on-site training during commissioning. For hypochlorite generation, this means hands-on training with PSI/Microclor® electrolytic cell systems, brine management, and control interfaces, leveraging

<!-- ¶12 -->
our direct experience operating PSI units at similar facilities. For heat drying, manufacturer training will cover thermal system operation, emissions monitoring, material handling, and emergency procedures.

<!-- ¶13 -->
- <sup>**Construction contractor and integrator coordination:**Jacobs staff will</sup> participate in startup and proofing activities, gaining hands-on familiarity with each system in its installed configuration before assuming full responsibility.

<!-- ¶14 -->
- <sup>**Process operations training:**Coordination with OCWUT’s engineers to</sup> understand new treatment configurations, control strategies, and performance expectations for each upgraded process.

<!-- ¶15 -->
•<sup>**Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and Standard Maintenance**</sup> **Procedures (SMPs) development:** We’ll develop SOPs and SMPs for each new facility or process using construction contract-provided O&M information and engineer-provided process operations information, as required by Schedule 16 Startup Services.

<!-- ¶16 -->
Training will begin well before operational handover, with staff participating in commissioning, proofing, and startup activities to build hands-on familiarity. For our comprehensive training program structure, refer to our **Staffing and Training Plan earlier in this section.**

<!-- ¶17 -->
###### **Maintenance Program Adjustments**

<!-- ¶18 -->
Every new asset needs to be absorbed into the maintenance program from day one. Our approach to maintenance integration connects directly to our overall Maintenance Plan and leverages NexGen EAM as the single system of record.

<!-- ¶19 -->
•<sup>**CMMS Configuration and Asset Additions.**For each completed CIP project,</sup> we will configure NexGen EAM to incorporate new assets with complete documentation: manufacturer data, model and serial numbers, install dates, warranty terms, and criticality rankings. We’ll establish parent-child asset relationships linked to facility process areas, build Bills of Material from manufacturer documentation for efficient parts identification, and configure automated reporting so new assets are captured in KPI tracking and monthly reports from day one.

<!-- ¶20 -->
•<sup>**Spare Parts and Consumables.**We’ll develop spare parts inventory strategies for</sup> each new facility before operational handover, identifying critical spares based on manufacturer recommendations and our experience, establishing minimum/ maximum inventory levels in NexGen, and coordinating initial stocking with commissioning timelines. For hypochlorite generation, this includes proprietary electrolytic cell cartridges and brine system components. For heat drying, it includes thermal system consumables and emissions monitoring supplies. New consumables such as salt/brine for generation, fuel for drying, and polymer changes will be integrated into procurement schedules.
