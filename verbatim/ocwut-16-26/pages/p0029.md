The over-aeration conditions observed across the system represent one of the most direct opportunities to reduce energy consumption. Aeration typically accounts for the largest share of electricity use at activated sludge facilities, and OCWUT’s baseline data confirms significant energy intensity—averaging approximately 1,964 kWh/MG at North Canadian/Witcher. Our DO monitoring and optimization efforts will tie directly into the required Energy Efficiency Plan. By right-sizing aeration to actual biological demand, we’ll work toward maintaining energy usage within the +5% variance of the established baseline while improving treatment performance.

As compliance stability is achieved, near term enhancements will focus on wet weather resilience and process flexibility. Step feed operation will be evaluated and implemented as appropriate to better distribute organic loading during peak flow events and protect clarifier performance. Existing ferric or ferrous systems will be assessed for chemically enhanced primary treatment during extreme wet weather to reduce solids and organic loading to the activated sludge process and provide additional protection against washout.

<!-- ¶10 -->
Longer term process control improvements will be pursued once consistent compliance is demonstrated. These include evaluating opportunities to add or reintroduce anoxic selector zones to improve settling and reduce aeration demand, assessing underutilized basins for solids reduction or aerobic digestion, and selectively rehabilitating filtration assets to add process stability during upset conditions. All improvements will be prioritized based on measurable benefits to compliance with reliability and operational sustainability.

###### _Deer Creek WWTP— Breaking the Cycle of Reactive Operations_

<!-- ¶12 -->
At Deer Creek, the process control strategy will focus on restoring and maintaining compliance through improved solids handling, filtration reliability, and disinfection stability. Previous permit excursions have largely been tied to inconsistent wasting practices, manual filtration operation, and unstable disinfection control during flow variability. The site visit confirmed these conditions: MLSS was light with excessive DO and early-stage foaming, RAS pumps were running in static mode rather than flow-paced, and the sand filters were being backwashed manually.

<!-- ¶13 -->
Initial actions will include implementing SRT-based wasting to stabilize biological activity and give operators predictable, day-to-day control of solids inventory. This will support stable secondary clarifier performance, more consistent sludge management, and improved coordination with downstream solids handling. Filtration systems will be returned to full automated control with routine turbidity and TSS monitoring to optimize backwash cycles and maintain consistent effluent quality. Disinfection reliability will be improved by tying chlorine and sulfur dioxide pacing directly to process flows and treatment conditions.
