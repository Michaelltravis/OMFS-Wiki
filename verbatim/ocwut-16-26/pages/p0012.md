###### **JACOBS UNDERSTANDS OCWUT'S FACILITIES AND ITS GOALS FOR THIS O&M CONTRACT**

<!-- ¶3 -->
OCWUT’s wastewater system is one of the largest contract-operated programs in the country—four treatment plants and the Witcher Pump Station with a combined design capacity exceeding 110 MGD and peak flows above 180 MGD, a biosolids land application program spanning roughly 170 permitted sites, Hefner Water Treatment Plant (WTP) residuals management, and a septage receiving program. These facilities serve a growing metropolitan area entering a period of significant change, with nearly $200M in planned capital improvements over the next decade, including plant expansions, new disinfection systems, and a transition in biosolids handling.

<!-- ¶4 -->
In preparing this proposal, the Jacobs team thoroughly reviewed the RFP, Exhibit A schedules, and the Sample Service Agreement; attended both mandatory Pre-Proposal Conferences; conducted multi-day site visits across all five facilities; completed background document review; and held focused strategy sessions with process, odor control, solids, maintenance, laboratory, automation, and safety specialists. That preparation gave us a detailed, facility-specific understanding of where things stand today and what it will take to deliver the level of service OCWUT is looking for. Many of the challenges we identify below are also the drivers behind OCWUT’s significant CIP investments, and our approach is built to operate effectively through the transition while contributing O&M insight to the capital program. **Exhibit 1-1** provides a concise summary of our understanding of OCWUT’s goals, the key challenges facing these facilities, and the strategies Jacobs will bring to deliver results. Our strategies reflect the depth of our integrated O&M and engineering expertise, and we present each in detail throughout the corresponding sections of our Technical Approach.
