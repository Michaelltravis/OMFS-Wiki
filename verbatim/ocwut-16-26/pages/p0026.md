###### **OPERATIONS PLAN**

<!-- ¶2 -->
Jacobs will implement a structured Operations Plan that brings consistency and control to day-today operations. The plan **establishes clear, repeatable procedures for operations, maintenance, safety, compliance, and reporting** —areas where variability today drives reactive performance and inconsistent outcomes.

<!-- ¶3 -->
It defines roles, routines, and escalation paths, so issues are identified and addressed early, not after they impact compliance or reliability. A formal SOP library will govern all critical activities, including startup and shutdown, routine monitoring, abnormal conditions, and work practices, so work is performed consistently across shifts, regardless of operator. SOPs will remain living documents, updated as equipment, permits, and operating conditions evolve, and reinforced through shift turnover, training, and routine supervisory oversight.

###### **Jacobs Understands the Process Control Challenges and Goals for OCWUT’s Four Facilities: Process Stability Starts with the Fundamentals**

<!-- ¶6 -->
OCWUT’s four wastewater treatment facilities share a common challenge, process control fundamentals have eroded, and the resulting instability shows up in permit excursions, reactive operations, and equipment running well outside its intended operating envelope. Our observations during site visits confirmed what the compliance record shows: these facilities aren’t limited by their design. They’re operationally constrained by inconsistent solids management, underutilized instrumentation, and aeration systems running without meaningful feedback or control.

<!-- ¶7 -->
•<sup>**At North Canadian**, the plant had recently experienced an upset requiring re-seeding of the biological</sup> process. Air distribution across the aeration basins was poor—low dissolved oxygen (DO) at the front of the train and excessive DO at the tail end—suggesting the facility may not always need all four basins in service. Blowers have no variable frequency drives (VFDs) and rely on manual valves for air distribution, with DO probes positioned only at the unaerated influent and end of the aeration basins. Return activated sludge/ waste activated sludge (RAS/WAS) control appeared to follow a set-and-forget approach rather than active process management. The Phase 3 plant, effectively just a flow equalization basin (FEB), discharges directly to the combined effluent—a compliance risk any time untreated or septic flow reaches it. The Phase II chlorine contact basin had floating sludge with a permanently placed sludge judge, a sign of chronic solids carryover. These conditions, combined with the sulfur dioxide system’s inability to match chlorine demand during high flows, have driven the facility’s history of fecal coliform and chlorine residual violations. •<sup>**At Deer Creek**, mixed liquor suspended solids (MLSS) appeared very light with excessive DO and early-</sup> stage foaming—indicators of a young sludge age and over-aeration. Secondary clarifiers showed excessive algae growth with no covered weirs, creating conditions where sloughing algae can blind the sand filters downstream.
