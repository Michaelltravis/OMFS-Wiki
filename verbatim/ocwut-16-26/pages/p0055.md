###### **PUMP STATION OPERATIONS AND SEPTAGE RECEIVING**

<!-- ¶2 -->
###### JACOBS UNDERSTANDS WHY WITCHER PUMP STATION O&M AND SEPTAGE RECEIVING MATTER

<!-- ¶3 -->
The Witcher Pump Station is the critical hydraulic link between Oklahoma City’s collection system and the North Canadian WWTP. Built in 1981 and upgraded in 2012, it operates as a staffed site 12 hours per day, six days per week and is OCWUT’s sole designated septage receiving point, serving licensed haulers across the Oklahoma City service area. Our approach will deliver critical benefits for OCWUT and the community with fewer odor complaints in high-visibility areas, a more defensible biosolids program, and long-term value preserved at the station that ties your collection system to North Canadian.

<!-- ¶4 -->
It directly affects odor at North Canadian, biosolids quality in the land application program, and community trust in the neighborhoods nearest the station.

<!-- ¶5 -->
###### **Pump Station Operations**

<!-- ¶6 -->
###### **Challenges We’ll Address**

<!-- ¶7 -->
We’ll operate the Witcher Pump Station as an integrated extension of the North Canadian treatment process—not simply as a conveyance asset. Our approach centers on three priorities:

<!-- ¶8 -->
**Odor and H₂S.** Our site visits confirmed H₂S-driven corrosion at the influent flow path and odor at the station itself, particularly where flow discharges to the junction box and drops into the gravity line feeding North Canadian. The station’s proximity to a neighboring school, soccer fields, and residential areas makes odor management a high-visibility priority. Current odor masking with Super D provides a short-term measure only

<!-- ¶9 -->
•<sup>**Hydraulic reliability and downstream protection.**We’ll manage wet well levels</sup> and lead/lag pump sequencing to maintain steady flow to North Canadian while minimizing surge. Controlled pump cycling—particularly management of the second pump’s on/off frequency—will reduce force main pressure transients that release H₂S at the downstream junction box. We’ll also test and exercise standby power transfer sequences on a regular schedule to prevent overflow during outages.

<!-- ¶10 -->
**Wet Weather Basin Maintenance.** The three earthen-bottom wet weather basins accumulate grit and settled solids that contribute to odor when basins are activated. The flow equalization basin (FEBs) were cleaned in 2023, and use is currently discouraged due to odor.

<!-- ¶11 -->
•<sup>**Targeted odor reduction at the source.**We’ll optimize the existing ferrous</sup> chloride feed system for H₂S control, focusing on dosing rate and injection point to maximize reaction time within the force main before flow reaches the gravity transition. This is a practical, chemistry-based approach that replaces ineffective masking agents with sulfide reduction you can measure, document, and defend to neighbors. (See our overall odor control approach in the **Odor Control Strategy** section discussed earlier in this Technical Approach.)

<!-- ¶12 -->
**Septage Receiving Rigor.** Site observations noted limited testing of received septage loads beyond pH measurement and visual inspection. Several porta-potty loads were received during our visit. The septage program is regulated under Schedule 6 and tied directly to the biosolids program, so intake discipline has direct downstream consequences.

<!-- ¶13 -->
**Generator Reliability.** We observed that the two generators were leaking antifreeze and likely would not run in that condition. While it did not appear the radiator itself was leaking and this could simply be caused by a loose hose connection, for reliability it requires deeper investigation and inclusion in a preventive maintenance (PM) program.

<!-- ¶14 -->
•<sup>**Condition-based asset stewardship.**The four main station pumps and three</sup> retention pumps are on quarterly and annual vibration analysis schedules, respectively (per Schedule 2). We’ll integrate vibration trending with CMMSdriven PM, generator exercise logs, screen condition tracking, and cathodic protection inspections to maintain high availability and meet Schedule 5 predictive maintenance and PM requirements, protecting OCWUT's capital investment in the hydraulic backbone downstream assets depend on. **See the Maintenance Plan (Assets) section that follows.**
