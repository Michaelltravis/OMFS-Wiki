###### **Process Control Doesn’t Operate in a Silo**

<!-- ¶8 -->
**Our approach to process control recognizes the interdependencies with odor control, wet weather management, solids and residuals management, energy efficiency, and maintenance/reliability.** Those topics are addressed in detail in their specific subsections of this Technical Approach.

<!-- ¶9 -->
For example, process optimization and energy efficiency are linked at every facility. The aeration improvements described in our facility-by-facility strategies, including expanded DO monitoring, right-sized basin service, and future DO-based control, directly support the Energy Efficiency Plan and OCWUT’s expectation that electricity usage stay within +5% of the established baseline per facility per contract year.

<!-- ¶10 -->
Process control decisions also inform how we manage standby and redundant equipment. Our Equipment Rotation Plan (Schedule 3) will provide equal annual service time for all parallel equipment, including pumps, blowers, clarifier drives, and filtration systems, so standby units are ready when needed. For equipment temporarily out of service, we’ll follow Schedule 3’s specific requirements: cleaning and lubricating mixing equipment, rotating all rotating equipment weekly, keeping electrical motor heaters energized to prevent condensation, and purging chlorine systems with nitrogen gas. This is particularly relevant at North Canadian, where the multi-plant configuration means some treatment trains may be idle during low-flow periods, and at Deer Creek, where blower reliability challenges make it critical that standby units stay in ready condition. Preventive maintenance will continue on all equipment whether in active service or not—a discipline that current operations have not consistently maintained.
