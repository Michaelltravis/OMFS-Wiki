###### **SLUDGE MANAGEMENT PLAN**

<!-- ¶2 -->
###### **Why Sludge Management Matters to OCWUT**

<!-- ¶3 -->
Sludge management is one of the daily responsibilities in this contract with the highest stakes. When it runs well, it’s invisible: storage stays controlled, hauling stays on schedule, land application stays compliant, and the community doesn’t think twice about it. When it doesn’t, problems compound quickly. Backlog builds on the pad, odor complaints follow, and compliance exposure grows.

<!-- ¶4 -->
**OCWUT's program does not need reactive management. It needs a partner who treats solids as a planned, coordinated operational function from day one.**

<!-- ¶5 -->
Jacobs will manage sludge from generation through final use or disposal as one fully integrated program, connecting process control, dewatering, storage, odor management, hauling, land application, and compliance reporting into a coordinated system that stays ahead of problems rather than chasing them. This Sludge Management Plan addresses Schedule 14 requirements directly and aligns with our Solids Management Plan, Odor Control Plan, and Process Control Plan. Solids, odor, and process performance do not operate independently. We manage them together.

<!-- ¶6 -->
###### **Understanding OCWUT’s Existing Program**

<!-- ¶7 -->
OCWUT operates an established and regulated sludge and biosolids program that requires daily coordination and disciplined execution across four facilities, approximately 170 permitted land application sites, and roughly 13,500 acres.

<!-- ¶8 -->
ODEQ-approved Sludge Management Plans are in place for North Canadian, Deer Creek, Chisholm Creek, and South Canadian. Jacobs will manage operations in full compliance with each plan and support renewals, updates, and new site permitting as needed with OCWUT approval.

###### _Each facility presents its own operational profile._

<!-- ¶15 -->
**North Canadian** serves as the primary solids processing hub, handling thickening, dewatering, lime stabilization, and stacking pad storage for the system. Site observations confirmed elevated hydrogen sulfide conditions in solids handling areas and a significant presence of plastics in the lime-stabilized biosolids, a nuisance for landowners and a compliance risk for the land application program. Jacobs will begin by restoring debris removal equipment to eliminate foreign material from biosolids before land application. If existing equipment can't achieve the required performance, we'll evaluate additional solutions and present recommendations to OCWUT before implementation. We’ll evaluate covered storage options at the underutilized 2.5-acre compost pad that would further reduce biosolids volume, expand drying capacity, and control odors. This would produce a better-suited product for land application.

<!-- ¶16 -->
**Deer Creek** sits adjacent to a residential development, making odor during sludge handling a high-visibility community concern. The RFP requires hydrogen peroxide dosed at 300 mg/L in the sludge transfer line prior to the belt press, a protocol that should deliver an 86% reduction in hydrogen sulfide and a 93% reduction in mercaptans. Site observations indicate this dosing hasn't been consistently implemented. Jacobs will have this protocol in place from day one, coordinated with ferrous chloride feed adjustments to stabilize belt press room conditions and reduce community impact. We'll also fully support the solids processing upgrades coming online at Deer Creek, proactively optimizing the system as improvements are commissioned.
