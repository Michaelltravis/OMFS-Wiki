###### **Community Engagement**

<!-- ¶2 -->
**We’re already an active part of the Oklahoma City community** , as illustrated in **Exhibit 1-10** . With the O&M project, we intend to grow that engagement. Our approach will benefit both OCWUT and the communities it serves by making facilities a source of pride, growing the next generation of water professionals, and demonstrating that reliable wastewater operations and good neighbor practices go hand in hand by:

<!-- ¶3 -->
- <sup>**Helping the community understand how wastewater operations protect quality of life.**We’ll host periodic</sup> open house events for residents, schools, and community groups at OCWUT facilities to show how treatment operations protect public health, nearby waterways, and quality of life. We’ll participate in community events with practical public education materials that explain how proactive operations reduce odors, minimize service disruptions, and support reliable service.

<!-- ¶4 -->
- <sup>**Supporting education and growing the next generation.**We’ll support classroom visits, STEM outreach, and</sup> educational partnerships with local schools and colleges to help students understand treatment processes, water stewardship, and water sector careers. By introducing young people to the science and technology behind clean water, we’ll help build interest in the skilled workforce that utilities like OCWUT need.

<!-- ¶5 -->
- <sup>**Growing economic opportunities through workforce development.**We’ll create internships, mentoring</sup> programs, and career opportunities in partnership with local and regional schools and colleges to build a pipeline for water sector careers. These programs create economic opportunities while strengthening the long-term workforce available to OCWUT and the broader Oklahoma water community.

<!-- ¶6 -->
- <sup>**Championing environmental stewardship.**We’ll join local environmental cleanup and stewardship efforts</sup> that reinforce the connection between reliable utility operations and protection of Oklahoma City’s waterways, watersheds, and green spaces.

<!-- ¶7 -->
- <sup>**Investing in the community fabric.**We’ll support appropriate City and OCWUT initiatives and community</sup> organizations through charitable contributions, food drives, and active participation in events like youth sports leagues, recreation programs, and neighborhood activities—showing up in ways that are visible, practical, and reflect the values of the communities we serve.

###### WE ARE COMMITTED TO THE COMMUNITIES WE SERVE

<!-- ¶16 -->

<!-- ¶17 -->
Across our O&M projects in the U.S., our teams volunteered more than 3,918 hours on community stewardship activities last year alone, and nearly 26,000 hours since 2009. From environmental cleanups to food banks, STEM education, and so much more—our people give back because it matters to them personally.

<!-- ¶18 -->
That commitment runs deep here in Oklahoma. Our OKC office ran a school supply drive across the state in partnership with the nonprofit **Open for Business Oklahoma** , and our staff developed a **STEAM curriculum** that’s now in the hands of school districts statewide. Our OKC and Tulsa teams show up for environmental cleanups and community events like **Up with Trees** and **Habitat for Humanity** . In Tulsa, our staff mentor students in robotics competitions, **Math Counts** , and the **Tulsa Regional STEM Alliance** —and we financially sponsor the **Tulsa Engineering Foundation’s E-Week** and the **Metropolitan Environmental Trust (M.e.t.)** .

<!-- ¶19 -->
Closer to home, our OKC office is an active partner with **Keep Oklahoma Beautiful** , participating in quarterly metro-area clean-ups. Jacobs’ own Bronte Bagwell serves as chairperson of the Oklahoma City Committee of Keep Oklahoma Beautiful—a role that reflects the kind of local leadership we bring not just to our projects, but to our community.
