###### **EMERGENCY OPERATING PLAN**

<!-- ¶2 -->
###### **Protecting Service, Public Health, and Community Confidence**

<!-- ¶3 -->
Wastewater service can’t pause during an emergency. When something goes wrong, OCWUT needs a partner that will act quickly, communicate clearly, and keep operations under control. We will develop and implement an emergency operating plan that protects public health, supports regulatory compliance, and helps sustain community confidence when conditions are at their most challenging. We will coordinate this plan with OCWUT’s Emergency Response Plan, Flood Emergency Response Plan, and Risk Management Plan; the City’s Emergency Operations Plan; and each facility’s Spill Prevention, Control, and Countermeasure (SPCC) Plan so response actions are aligned before an event occurs. This approach will help OCWUT reduce disruption, protect critical treatment functions, and recover more quickly when emergencies happen.

<!-- ¶4 -->
###### **Prepared, Trained, and Ready To Act**

<!-- ¶5 -->
Strong emergency response starts long before an event occurs. Our facility assessments identified specific vulnerabilities that will inform emergency planning priorities at each site, including flood risk at North Canadian's influent pump station, H2S conditions that restrict access to critical work areas, and redundancy gaps in key process equipment that require defined backup protocols. We will build on OCWUT’s existing procedures and strengthen them with a practical, facilityspecific emergency operating plan tailored to your risks, assets, and operating conditions. Our team will review current emergency procedures and unit process control procedures, confirm emergency equipment availability, and identify critical assets and likely failure points so response efforts stay focused where they matter most.

<!-- ¶6 -->
We will prepare staff through routine drills, tabletop exercises, and scenariobased training covering severe weather, process upsets, power loss, staffing shortages, and other high-consequence events. Ahead of forecasted events, we will stage personnel, equipment, and materials to shorten response time and maintain continuity of operations. We will also maintain agreements for emergency equipment and specialty services so additional resources are available when needed without delay. This preparation will help OCWUT protect permit compliance, maintain treatment continuity, and respond with confidence under pressure.

<!-- ¶7 -->
###### **Rapid, Structured Response When It Matters Most**

<!-- ¶8 -->
When an emergency occurs, we will activate a clear, practiced response structure focused on safety, operational stability, and communication. Our team will implement the emergency operating plan using defined roles, responsibilities, escalation procedures, and communication protocols from the outset. Project Manager David Pitocchelli and Emergency Response SME Megan Ellis will stay actively engaged during major events, providing leadership across operations, coordination, and executive communication so response actions remain aligned, timely, and focused on OCWUT’s priorities.

###### **Fast Recovery and Return to Full Operations**

<!-- ¶13 -->
Response is only one part of effective emergency management. Restoring full operations quickly and learning from each event matter just as much. Following an emergency, we will assess system conditions, prioritize repairs based on operational impact, and restore treatment capacity and system performance through a structured recovery process coordinated with OCWUT and regulatory agencies as needed. We will document actions taken, evaluate system performance, and conduct after-action reviews to identify improvements. We’ll fold those lessons into updated procedures, training, and planning so OCWUT’s emergency readiness grows stronger over time. This approach will help your facilities recover faster, reduce repeat issues, and improve resilience across the full contract term.
