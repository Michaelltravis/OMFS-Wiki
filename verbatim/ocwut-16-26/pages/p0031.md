<!-- ¶2 -->
###### _Operating Under Low-Flow and Minimum-Load Conditions_

<!-- ¶3 -->
Our process control strategy also addresses low-flow and minimum-load conditions—periods that carry their own operational risks. During sustained dry weather, reduced hydraulic loading can lead to excessive solids retention times, over-aeration relative to actual oxygen demand, and elevated detention times that promote septic conditions in underutilized basins.

<!-- ¶4 -->
Jacobs’ process control strategy serves as the focal point for daily operations by maintaining steady-state treatment, preventing process upsets, and supporting permit compliance through effective monitoring, verification, and reporting. **We align process control with sampling, laboratory testing, field observations, and routine performance review, so operators identify changing conditions early and respond in a timely, consistent way.** UPCPs define targets, control limits, monitoring points, and response actions by unit process, grounded in plant design, influent characteristics, performance goals, and applicable permit requirements.

<!-- ¶5 -->
- <sup>**At North Canadian**, where the three-plant configuration provides significant</sup> operational flexibility, we'll take basins out of service during low-flow periods to maintain appropriate velocities and loading rates through active treatment trains, reducing energy consumption while preventing stagnation.

<!-- ¶6 -->
Our process control strategy operates in tandem with the Sampling and Testing QA/QC Plan (Schedule 3), which provides the data foundation for every process decision we make. Operators can’t control what they can’t measure, and compliance starts with data you can defend.

<!-- ¶7 -->
- <sup>**At Deer Creek**, where organic loading already approaches design capacity during</sup> normal conditions, low-flow periods present an opportunity to recover biological stability, rebuild solids inventory, and perform targeted maintenance on blowers, filters, and clarifiers.

<!-- ¶8 -->
Process control is carried out through a disciplined weekly rhythm supported by the proven tools summarized in **Exhibit 1-14** . We document weekly process control decisions, formally review and refresh the process control plan at least annually, and **equip both on-site staff and OCWUT with tools that streamline reporting and provide immediate access to system information** , as illustrated in **Exhibit 1-15** .

<!-- ¶9 -->
- <sup>**At Chisholm Creek and South Canadian**, low-flow strategies will focus on</sup> adjusting SRT targets, reducing basin service, and throttling back aeration to match actual demand—preventing the over-aerated conditions we observed at both facilities during our site visits.
