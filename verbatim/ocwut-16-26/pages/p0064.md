###### _Full PdM Technology Suite_

<!-- ¶3 -->
Beyond vibration and electrical testing, our PdM program includes motor signature analysis to detect rotor and stator faults, particularly valuable for the aging motor fleet at North Canadian, along with ultrasound testing for leak detection, bearing lubrication monitoring, and electrical discharge detection; infrared thermography to identify abnormal heat patterns in electrical and mechanical systems; and oil analysis to evaluate lubricant condition and contamination for early wear detection.

<!-- ¶4 -->
All PdM findings feed directly into NexGen. Deficiencies identified through any predictive testing method will be addressed within 60 days (PD-5 KPI). Over time, trending PdM data will refine PM frequencies and justify targeted investments in monitoring technology where the return avoided downtime is clear.

###### **Corrective Maintenance Program**

<!-- ¶6 -->
**Disciplined corrective maintenance isn’t just about fixing what’s broken; it’s about preventing cascading consequences and building the data that makes the entire program smarter over time.**

<!-- ¶7 -->
At North Canadian, we’ve documented a pattern that illustrates why this matters: grit system downtime leads to primary clarifier bypass, which sends grit into aeration basins, accelerates wear on clarifier drive mechanisms and downstream pumping equipment, and creates conditions for compliance events. Reporting data shows this cycle repeating nearly every month. That pattern is the direct result of reactive maintenance, and it’s exactly the kind of failure chain a structured corrective program breaks by addressing root causes, not just symptoms.

<!-- ¶8 -->
**Every corrective maintenance event will be tracked in NexGen with full documentation: failure mode, root cause, and operational impact recorded on every work order.** We’ll apply Failure Mode and Effects Analysis to systematically eliminate repeat failures at the root cause. Each corrective event becomes an input to improve the overall maintenance program by adjusting PM frequencies, targeting PdM investments, or escalating chronic issues to capital planning. The result: corrective maintenance runs as a controlled process, not a reactive response. Our response and closure discipline is aligned with Schedule 5 KPIs: corrective work

###### **Maintenance Staffing Approach**

<!-- ¶17 -->
We will staff each facility with dedicated maintenance teams sized for that facility’s complexity and workload. Daily preventive and routine corrective maintenance will be performed by personnel assigned to each facility, not shared across distant sites. South Canadian’s geographic distance from the northern facilities (45–50 minutes) and its new equipment profile demand a dedicated on-site team focused on warranty compliance, commissioning support, and a PM-heavy maintenance ratio from day one. The northern facilities—North Canadian, Deer Creek, and Chisholm Creek—will share specialized resources for electrical, I&C, and heavy corrective maintenance through a coordinated model that takes advantage of their relative proximity.

<!-- ¶18 -->
We're **also restructuring how drain and clean work gets done** . Operators—not mechanics—will execute the Schedule 15 drain and clean program, with mechanics following on for post-clean inspections and any preventive maintenance the drain-down reveals. We've sized the operations workforce specifically for this new approach, adding hourly operator capacity at every facility (detailed in our Staffing and Training Plan in Section 5 – Required Plans). The result for OCWUT: more mechanic wrench time on PM, PdM, and corrective work that protects long-term asset value and operational reliability.
