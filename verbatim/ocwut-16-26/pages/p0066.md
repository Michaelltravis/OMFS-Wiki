###### **Inventory Management and Spare Parts**

<!-- ¶3 -->
We will manage OCWUT’s spare parts inventory through NexGen, building on the existing well-organized system already loaded into the platform with part numbers, descriptions, locations, and minimum/maximum quantities assigned to individual assets. A dedicated Stores/Parts Manager will maintain inventory integrity by receiving, cataloging, issuing, cycle counting, and reordering.

<!-- ¶4 -->
Our critical spare parts strategy starts with the Schedule 20 definition of Critical Assets and maps their failure-prone components to a stocking plan that supports the 2-day return-to-service requirement (CM-3). Inventory levels will be aligned with Minimum Inventory Levels compliance requirements and verified through regular cycle counts. Automated reorder triggers within NexGen based on minimum/maximum levels will prevent stockouts while controlling carrying costs. Bills of material linked to each asset will enable efficient parts identification during corrective events.

<!-- ¶5 -->
Parts and supplies to be paid for from the M&R Fund will be inventoried with detailed justifications and General Manager approval per Schedule 20. All inventory is the property of OCWUT.

###### **Maintenance and Repair (M&R) Fund and Rehabilitation and Replacement (R&R) Fund Management**

<!-- ¶7 -->
We’ll manage the tiered M&R Fund structure with the precision of documentation that OCWUT’s contract demands:

<!-- ¶8 -->
- <sup>**Level A (≤$10,000):**Parts and supplies reimbursed without prior Service</sup> Approval Form (SAF); labor at Jacobs’ expense, including subcontractors and overtime. Documentation submitted weekly and included in the Monthly Payment Request.

<!-- ¶9 -->
- <sup>**Level B ($10,000–$50,000):**Requires an executed SAF prior to ordering</sup> materials. OCWUT pays labor costs including subcontractors.

<!-- ¶10 -->
- <sup>**Level C (>$50,000):**OCWUT competitively procures; Jacobs cooperates fully with</sup> procurement and installation coordination.

<!-- ¶11 -->
Each year, we will develop and submit the annual M&R budget, aligning with the condition assessment cycle and capital planning process. We’ll manage expenditures within 5% of the approved annual budget to avoid unrecoverable costs, a discipline that requires real-time fund tracking in NexGen with automated alerts as spending approaches thresholds. We understand that the R&R Fund addresses single asset rehabilitations or replacements exceeding $10,000 that substantially extend service life by 10 or more years. An executed SAF is required before procurement.

<!-- ¶12 -->
**We build documentation discipline into every maintenance activity — not because the contract requires it, but because it's how we protect the assets and the program's financial integrity.** Every PM completion, every inspection result, every corrective action is recorded in NexGen with timestamps, photographic evidence, and clear accountability. When a piece of equipment reaches end of life, the maintenance record speaks for itself by demonstrating the care that went into extending its service and supporting a straightforward path to M&R Fund reimbursement. That same data trail feeds our condition assessments, informs capital planning recommendations, and gives OCWUT full transparency into how maintenance dollars are being spent. This level of documentation ensures every maintenance dollar is traceable, justified, and aligned to asset condition, risk, and lifecycle decision-making.

<!-- ¶13 -->
###### **On-Call and Emergency Maintenance Response**

<!-- ¶14 -->
We’ll maintain an on-call maintenance rotation to address after-hours equipment issues and emergency situations: one mechanic and one electrician on call per week for the northern facilities group (North Canadian, Deer Creek, Chisholm Creek), with a separate mechanic rotation for South Canadian. On-call staff will be facility-familiar personnel drawn from the existing dedicated teams, rotating responsibilities to distribute workload equally.

<!-- ¶15 -->
The Critical Asset 2-day return-to-service requirement (CM-3) is backed by Jacobs’ three layers of response: on-site critical spare parts inventory stocked specifically for high-risk equipment, on-call staff availability for immediate mobilization, and Regional Maintenance Team support for specialized or heavy lift work that exceeds the on-site team’s capability. Emergency events—severe weather, flooding, and equipment failure cascades—will trigger enhanced staffing protocols coordinated between operations and maintenance leadership, with clear escalation paths and communication procedures documented in our Emergency Response Plan.
