We’ll train staff to work safely in the conditions they’ll face across OCWUT’s facilities, including chemical systems, confined spaces, H₂S-prone areas, electrical equipment, laboratory settings, biosolids handling areas, and maintenance work zones. We will provide role-based training during transition, refresh it annually, and update it whenever duties, equipment, or site conditions change. Training will cover confined space entry and rescue, lockout/tagout, fall protection, electrical safety, arc flash, hazard communication, chemical handling, emergency response, and first aid/CPR/AED. We’ll pair that training with field verification, supervisor observations, and documented follow-up, so each employee understands what applies to their job, what PPE to use, when to use it, and how to use it correctly.

<!-- ¶2 -->
OCWUT’s facilities present real chemical and process hazards that require disciplined daily control, not stand-alone paperwork. During our site visit, H₂S levels at North Canadian and Deer Creek created access restrictions and safety concerns, including areas where personnel couldn’t enter because of elevated gas readings. We will respond to those conditions with practical field controls. **As part of our startup investment, we’ll equip staff with Blackline personal gas monitors for continuous H₂S monitoring, real-time alerts, and man-down detection.** This will give employees, supervisors, and project leadership better visibility into exposure conditions and a faster way to respond when conditions change.

<!-- ¶3 -->
We will also integrate safety, process control, and emergency response into routine operations, so field work matches the written plan. That approach will be especially important in areas with known H₂S exposure and odor-related safety concerns, including North Canadian and Deer Creek. We’ll align our safety practices with our Risk Management Plan, Process Safety Management requirements where applicable, each facility’s Spill Prevention Control and Countermeasure (SPCC) Plan, and related emergency response procedures so OCWUT gains a **stronger safety culture, stronger regulatory readiness, and better protection for staff, assets, and the environment.**

###### **Physical and Electronic Security**

<!-- ¶5 -->
**OCWUT’s wastewater facilities are critical infrastructure, and we will treat site security that way.** Our Security Plan will protect people, facilities, and operational assets through controlled access, routine inspection, disciplined documentation, and rapid response when conditions change. Site Security SME

<!-- ¶6 -->
**Keith Waddell, CPP, CFE** , will support our team in aligning practical field security practices with OCWUT’s facility access, restricted areas, and documentation requirements. We’ll conduct regular inspections of perimeter fencing, gates, lock functionality, and fence integrity. If we identify a disturbance, breach, or other security concern, we will secure the area, document the condition, report it promptly to the General Manager or designee, and complete or coordinate corrective action without delay. We’ll keep gates closed and locked except when admitting authorized personnel or vehicles, and access will be limited to individuals presenting proper identification in accordance with OCWUT requirements. We will maintain secure access to AMAG-protected areas, server rooms, control rooms, and other restricted spaces except for approved entry.

<!-- ¶7 -->
Physical security and digital security must work together. We’ll align site security practices with OCWUT’s electronic security requirements and Information Technology Systems implementation approach, so credentials, access controls, OT, and facility data are managed as one coordinated system. Keith will support this coordination by helping our team evaluate access control practices, restrictedspace protocols, incident documentation, and security-sensitive communications through an operations lens. We’ll support controlled access to SCADA and OT environments, disciplined account management, and secure handling of operational information in coordination with OCWUT’s IT and SCADA teams. We will follow OCWUT’s security requirements, support additional security measures developed during the contract term, and protect security-sensitive information through authorized channels only.

<!-- ¶8 -->
###### **Drug Detection and Deterrence**

<!-- ¶9 -->
Because these facilities are safety-critical, workforce reliability matters just as much as technical capability. We’ll implement a Drug Detection and Deterrence Plan aligned with the City of Oklahoma City’s Human Resources Workplace Standards. The plan will support a safe, dependable work environment through pre-employment screening, required background checks, employee and supervisor awareness training, monitoring procedures, and a process for documenting and reporting criminal convictions as required by the City. This approach will help protect employees, reduce workplace risk, and strengthen public confidence in the team operating OCWUT’s facilities.
