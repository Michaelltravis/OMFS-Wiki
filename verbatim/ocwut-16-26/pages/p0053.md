###### **ENERGY MANAGEMENT – A CORE OPERATING DISCIPLINE**

<!-- ¶2 -->
###### **Understanding OCWUT’s Energy Landscape**

<!-- ¶3 -->
OCWUT's four wastewater treatment facilities and the Witcher Pump Station represent a combined treatment capacity of more than 110 MGD. (Facilityspecific energy profiles, baseline energy intensities, and the operational drivers we observed during site visits are documented in the **Process Control Strategy** section earlier in this Technical Approach.)

<!-- ¶4 -->
OCWUT has made clear that energy management is not optional; it's a contractual obligation. This includes developing and implementing an Energy Efficiency Plan, maintaining electricity usage within +5% of an annually established baseline, meeting with the General Manager annually to audit usage variances and discuss consumption practices, and reporting monthly and annually on energy performance.

<!-- ¶5 -->
OCWUT has confirmed that the Energy Efficiency Plan baseline will be established using the annual average kWh/MG of effluent flow at each facility, a flow-adjusted metric that measures genuine operational efficiency rather than penalizing operators for flow variability outside their control. CIP projects will be considered in baseline development, and OCWUT will remain responsible for providing electricity.

###### **Our Approach: Baseline-Driven Energy Management**

<!-- ¶13 -->
Jacobs will **manage energy as a core operating discipline—embedded in daily decision-making, continuously monitored, and fully transparent to OCWUT** . Using historical data, SCADA, and operational trends, we’ll establish a clear, defensible baseline for each facility that reflects actual conditions, seasonal variability, and process loading. From that foundation, we’ll track performance in real time, convert data into actionable insights, and identify opportunities to reduce energy intensity while maintaining process stability, compliance, and system resiliency.

<!-- ¶14 -->
###### _Tracking Performance Where It Counts_

<!-- ¶15 -->
**Our approach focuses on the largest controllable energy drivers, aeration and pumping, where operational discipline has the greatest day-to-day impact.** The facility-by-facility aeration optimization strategy is described in **Process Control Strategy** , and the flow- and demand-responsive aeration control we'll deploy through SCADA is described in the **Phase 3 Automation Integration** subsection of the **SCADA/OT/Cybersecurity** section earlier in this Technical Approach. Proactive maintenance that keeps motors and blowers operating at peak efficiency is detailed in the Maintenance Plan.

**framework that ties it together.** Energy performance will be tracked using kWh per million gallons treated, or kWh per thousand pounds of pollutant removed.
