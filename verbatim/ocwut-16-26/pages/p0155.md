###### **SOLIDS MANAGEMENT PLAN**

<!-- ¶3 -->
Effective solids management is one of the responsibilities in this contract with the highest stakes and one of the most visible. Backlog on the stacking pad creates direct fee exposure. Odor during dewatering drives community complaints and can limit access to operations and maintenance. Belt press downtime creates cascading risk across storage, hauling, land application, and compliance. OCWUT does not need a solids program that reacts to problems. You need one that prevents them.

<!-- ¶4 -->
**Jacobs will deliver a fully integrated Solids Management Plan with clear operational triggers, daily monitoring, and structured escalation protocols designed to keep OCWUT ahead of Schedule 21 thresholds and avoid deductions under both COM-3 and COM-5.**

<!-- ¶5 -->
We’ll deploy an experienced team to support, plan, monitor, and evaluate the current solids program, bringing together expertise from compliance, engineering, delivery services, and on-site management. We’ll coordinate with a subcontractor to manage biosolids across all four facilities as a single, connected system. This plan aligns directly with our Odor Control Plan and Process Control Plan so that solids, process performance, and odor are managed together rather than in isolation. For land application, we'll prioritize agricultural sites outside of densely populated areas and plan truck routes that keep biosolids transport off residential and commercial corridors. This approach directly reduces odor exposure and truck traffic in the neighborhoods that matter most to Oklahoma City residents turning a common community concern into a non-issue.
