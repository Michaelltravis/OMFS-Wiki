- **Future innovations that lower costs and improve performance.** Beyond the services defined in the base scope, we bring proven tools and capabilities to reduce costs and improve performance, including Intelligent O&M analytics for chemical and energy optimization, structured odor evaluation programs, and accelerated implementation of OCWUT’s NexGen EAM platform. These are practical, implemented solutions already delivering results for utilities of similar size and complexity. Full details are provided in Section 8 Innovative and/or Alternative Recommendations.

<!-- ¶2 -->
- **More than an operator: integrated support from a proven partner to large utilities.** We deliver O&M services for some of the most complex utility systems in the country. In Jackson, Mississippi, we stabilized and now operate three WWTPs and 99 pump stations following emergency federal oversight—demonstrating our ability to restore performance in highly challenging environments. Jacobs has provided full-service water and wastewater O&M since 1980, supporting some of the largest and most complex public systems in the U.S.—including Baltimore (180 MGD), Wilmington (134 MGD), Baton Rouge (300 MGD), and Jackson (145 MGD).. For OCWUT, this means seamless integration between operations and capital improvements—from startup and commissioning to long-term asset integration— without requiring the City to bridge that gap.

<!-- ¶3 -->
- **A culture of doing things right.** Ethics and accountability are foundational to how we operate. Our expectation of “perfect compliance and perfect reporting of non-compliance” drives behaviors that align with our long-term partnership mindset and commitment to transparency. As a result, we are the go-to O&M firm for **c** ommunities looking to make a change.

<!-- ¶4 -->
- **A team built for OCWUT’s success.** Our highly qualified team combines local leaders who are familiar with OCWUT’s facilities, an experienced regional management team, and a deep bench of technical experts. Leading the charge is Project Manager **David Pitocchelli** , who knows these facilities firsthand from his previous work as project manager for this program. Assistant Project Manager **Alex Rodriguez** combines Oklahoma roots with 13 years of multi-facility operations experience, including leadership of a 180-MGD facility for Seattle Public Utilities. Project Director **Steve Carpenter** provides independent oversight, drawing on his leadership of Oklahoma City’s Hefner WTP and his role in stabilizing complex systems such as Jackson, Mississippi. Strategic Advisor **Rick Warner, PE, WEF Fellow** brings 25 years of water and wastewater leadership and a trusted relationship with OCWUT leadership. Together, this team brings the experience, continuity, and accountability needed to deliver results.

<!-- ¶5 -->
We commend OCWUT for modernizing the contract to reinforce your operational priorities and hold the operator accountable for results. However, the contract by itself cannot fully achieve the performance transformation you envision. Two additional prerequisites are critical: pricing that responsibly supports the level of service expected, and a cultural evolution within the staff to embrace consistent, disciplined execution. Jacobs’ proposal is designed not only to comply with the updated agreement but to support these broader conditions that ultimately determine success.
