Each facility’s Unit Process Control Plan (UPCP) will define specific target operating parameters and metrics aligned with Schedule 3 requirements— **solids retention time** (SRT) targets by season and loading condition, target MLSS concentrations for each treatment train, DO setpoints by basin zone, sludge blanket depth limits for secondary clarifiers, effluent quality targets for total suspended solids (TSS), biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), ammonia, and chlorine residual that provide operating margin below NPDES permit limits. During transition, we’ll establish initial targets based on design data, historical performance, and conditions observed during our site assessments, then refine them as we collect operating data and confirm biological process response. These parameters and metrics will be documented in the UPCPs, reviewed weekly during process control meetings, and formally updated at least annually or sooner when conditions, equipment, or permit requirements change.

At North Canadian, our process control strategy will focus first on re-establishing and sustaining permit compliance through stabilization of biological treatment, secondary clarification, and hydraulic performance. Past compliance challenges have been driven by hydraulic instability, equipment reliability issues, and limited operational flexibility, which together reduced the plant’s ability to manage solids inventory and respond effectively to wet weather events. The immediate objective is to stabilize the process, protect clarifiers, and restore predictable performance under both dry- and wet- weather conditions.

<!-- ¶32 -->
Secondary clarifier performance will be further strengthened through routine sludge blanket monitoring and trending, combined with ongoing solids flux and state point analysis. These tools will help operators understand how MLSS concentration and settling characteristics affect available clarifier capacity, particularly during wet weather. This information will guide decisions on wasting, flow routing, and step feed operation to prevent hydraulic overload and solids washout.

<!-- ¶33 -->
**A core element of our compliance recovery strategy will be implementing SRT-based wasting.** This approach links daily wasting volumes directly to a defined biological target rather than relying on fixed schedules or reactive adjustments.
