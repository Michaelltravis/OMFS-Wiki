###### **MAINTENANCE PLAN (ASSETS)**

<!-- ¶2 -->
Jacobs approaches maintenance through an asset management lens— understanding what you have, knowing its condition, and making every dollar count toward long-term reliability, compliance, and safety. Our closed-loop operating model links asset risk, disciplined planning and scheduling, execution quality, and continuous performance feedback, so every maintenance activity is deliberate, traceable, and tied to cost and risk. **The goal isn't just to fix things; it's to prevent failures, extend asset life, and build a program that gets smarter over time.**

<!-- ¶3 -->
We’ve implemented thousands of asset management programs across North America and around the world, and we didn’t just adopt industry best practices; we helped write them. That depth of knowledge is what we’ll bring to OCWUT every day.

###### **What We Found Across the System**

<!-- ¶5 -->
You've set the expectation clearly: every maintenance dollar should protect asset value and extend useful life. Meeting that standard starts with truly understanding what you have. During our March 2026 site visits, technical due diligence, and review of available operating data, we developed a detailed picture of where these facilities stand today, and what it will take to move them from reactive maintenance to sustained reliability. That understanding shapes every element of our plan that follows. Four themes emerged consistently:

<!-- ¶6 -->
•<sup>**H₂S-driven equipment degradation is the most pervasive maintenance**</sup> **challenge across the system.** At North Canadian, hydrogen sulfide concentrations in the headworks, grit building, and solids handling areas are severe enough to prevent personnel access for routine preventive and corrective maintenance. Personal monitors triggered high-level alarms repeatedly during our walkthrough. Electrical systems, HVAC components, structural concrete, and instrumentation throughout the headworks show visible deterioration from prolonged exposure. At Deer Creek, domed primary clarifiers trap H₂S, and the dewatering building cannot be safely accessed during belt filter press operations. At both facilities, odor control systems are largely non-operational—many are obsolete, with original manufacturers no longer providing support. The Witcher Pump Station’s sealed junction box, which receives the force main discharge with no odor control or exhaust, is a suspected major contributor to the concentrated H₂S conditions at North Canadian’s headworks. This isn’t just an odor problem; it’s an access, safety, and asset protection problem that directly limits the maintenance team’s ability to do their job.
