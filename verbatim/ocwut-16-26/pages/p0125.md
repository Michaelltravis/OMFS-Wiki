###### **OKLAHOMA LAW: REGULATORY KNOWLEDGE AND ENVIRONMENTAL COMPLIANCE**

<!-- ¶5 -->
Operating wastewater facilities in Oklahoma requires more than knowing permit limits. It requires understanding how federal, state, and local regulations translate into daily operational decisions and applying that knowledge consistently across every facility. We’ll bring that hands-on understanding and apply it from day one at OCWUT.

<!-- ¶6 -->
OCWUT's facilities operate within a layered regulatory framework that our team knows well. At the federal level, this includes the Clean Water Act, Clean Air Act, National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System permitting requirements, and biosolids management standards under 40 CFR Part 503, along with applicable stormwater and waste management standards.

<!-- ¶7 -->
In Oklahoma, these requirements are implemented through ODEQ under the OPDES program. The specific administrative rules governing OCWUT's operations include:

<!-- ¶8 -->
- <sup>**OAC 252:606**: OPDES permitting, reporting, and compliance requirements</sup>

<!-- ¶9 -->
- <sup>**OAC 252:616**: Biosolids management standards applicable to Class B treatment,</sup> land application, and documentation

<!-- ¶10 -->
- <sup>**OAC 252:656**: Laboratory accreditation and certification requirements</sup>

<!-- ¶11 -->
- <sup>**OAC 252:515**: Operator certification requirements governing</sup> staffing qualifications

<!-- ¶12 -->
- <sup>**OAC 252:100**: Air pollution control regulations covering emergency generators</sup>

<!-- ¶13 -->
- •<sup>**OAC 252:656 & 627**: Water reuse</sup>

###### **RISK MANAGEMENT, PROCESS SAFETY, AND ENVIRONMENTAL EMERGENCY PROGRAMS**

<!-- ¶15 -->
The North Canadian facility operates under both a **Risk Management Plan (RMP)** and **Process Safety Management (PSM)** requirements, given the presence of chemicals that trigger these thresholds. We'll maintain current RMP/ PSM documentation, conduct required program updates, and coordinate PSM compliance as an integrated component of the facility safety program.

<!-- ¶16 -->
At each facility, we’ll maintain a current **Spill Prevention, Control, and Countermeasure (SPCC) Plan** , updated as required and integrated with the overall Emergency Operating Plan and the facility's day-to-day operational protocols.

<!-- ¶17 -->
**We’ll complete and submit annually by March 1 Tier II Emergency and Hazardous Chemical Inventory** forms for North Canadian, South Canadian, Deer Creek, and Witcher Pump Station—one of those firm, non-negotiable deadlines our team tracks and executes without prompting.

<!-- ¶18 -->
We’ll align emergency response planning with OCWUT's Emergency Response Plan and the City of Oklahoma City's Emergency Operations Plan, with coordination across the Flood Emergency Response Plan, RMP, and SPCC Plan.

<!-- ¶19 -->
###### **OKLAHOMA CORPORATION COMMISSION (OCC)— PETROLEUM STORAGE TANKS**

<!-- ¶20 -->
OCWUT's facilities include underground and aboveground petroleum storage tanks at North Canadian, South Canadian, and Chisholm Creek, all subject to OCC's Petroleum Storage Tank Program. These tanks used for regular unleaded and diesel fuel require active permit certification, routine inspection compliance, and meticulous recordkeeping under OCC regulations.

<!-- ¶21 -->
**We'll maintain current Petroleum Storage Tank Program Permit Certifications for all three facilities and integrate underground storage tank inspection and compliance activities** into our facility management protocols. We’ll incorporate spill prevention measures for these tanks into each facility's **SPCC Plan** , creating a unified approach to petroleum release prevention across the portfolio.

<!-- ¶22 -->
We understand how these regulations translate into sampling frequencies, process control targets, record-keeping standards, and reporting timelines, and we incorporate those requirements directly into plant operations, not as an afterthought.
