The current state of odor control equipment is a safety concern as much as an odor concern. At North Canadian, 30 odor scrubbers are installed, but only three are operational. At Deer Creek, seven are installed with only three operational. Site visit observations confirmed that H₂S levels in multiple buildings were so high that areas could not be entered, and some gas monitoring systems were unprogrammed or non-functional. Operators are working in conditions that exceed safe exposure thresholds because the systems designed to protect them aren’t running. **Jacobs will prioritize restoration of odor control and ventilation equipment at both facilities as a safety and odor control obligation, coordinated with OCWUT's CIP program to address the systemic equipment failures identified in the odor control evaluation.** Headworks and solids handling areas carry the highest combined safety and odor risk, so we’ll address these first.

###### **Wastewater Treatment Facility Odor Control**

<!-- ¶7 -->
At the treatment facilities, odor control will focus on process stability, equipment reliability, and targeted engineering assessment.

<!-- ¶8 -->
We will maintain aerobic conditions in critical process areas to prevent hydrogen sulfide formation. We’ll actively manage solids inventory and sludge age to avoid conditions that contribute to odor generation. We’ll optimize sludge handling processes to reduce retention time and minimize exposure.
