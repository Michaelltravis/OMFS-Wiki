**Exhibit 1-19.** _Odor Control Evaluation_

<!-- ¶16 -->
**Exhibit 1-18.** _Jacobs’ step-by-step process will identify sources of odor, including the expected dispersion. Using this information, improvements can be prioritized to create the most significant positive impact on the neighboring community._

<!-- ¶17 -->
We’ll **detect** odor through routine field presence and targeted monitoring. Operators will patrol both facilities and the collection system using portable hydrogen sulfide meters, with off-site patrols conducted during known highodor periods. All findings will be logged with time, weather conditions, and wind direction, creating a traceable record of field conditions.

<!-- ¶18 -->
We’ll **respond** quickly and consistently, with written guidelines in place so operator response is immediate and consistent rather than improvised ( **Exhibit 1-18** ). We’ll **improve** continuously by tracking odor data, complaint history, and operational metrics to refine chemical dosing, adjust process control, and target improvements where they're needed most. This approach replaces reactive responses with consistent, repeatable performance.

<!-- ¶19 -->
###### **Collection System Odor Control**

<!-- ¶20 -->
Odor control begins upstream. At OCWUT, our site observations confirmed that a significant portion of odor originates in the collection system before flow ever reaches the treatment facilities. Long force mains and pump stations, including major conveyance from the Witcher Pump Station, create extended detention times that allow septicity and hydrogen sulfide formation to develop well upstream of the plants. Controlling odor at its source is more effective and more efficient than managing it at the fence line ( **Exhibit 1-19** ).

<!-- ¶21 -->
Day-to-day operations structured around our “ **prevent, detect, respond, and improve” framework** will address these upstream drivers through optimized wet-well management, refined chemical dosing, and integrated field data. But operational control alone isn't enough to solve a system with OCWUT's complexity and history.
