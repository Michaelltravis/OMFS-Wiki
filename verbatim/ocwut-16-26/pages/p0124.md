###### **JACOBS IN OKLAHOMA: A 30-YEAR TRACK RECORD WITH THE OKLAHOMA DEPARTMENT OF ENVIRONMENTAL QUALITY**

<!-- ¶3 -->
Our team has 30 years of experience working with the Oklahoma Department of Environmental Quality (ODEQ), supporting land application programs, and maintaining compliance across Oklahoma as biosolids regulations have changed. The City of Duncan provides the clearest demonstration of our relationship with ODEQ and our Oklahoma regulatory experience. **Since 1995, Jacobs has provided full-service operations and maintenance for the City of Duncan's wastewater infrastructure under ODEQ oversight.** Our scope at Duncan mirrors OCWUT's in similar ways: full-service WWTP operations, compliance sampling, laboratory services, biosolids handling, and a land application reuse program managing effluent across 38 irrigation pivot sites. That sustained record of compliance and operational performance earned the Duncan WWTP the **Oklahoma Water Environment Association's Municipal Plant of the Year award in 2025** .

<!-- ¶4 -->
During the 2018 catastrophic flooding event, **we kept the Duncan WWTP operational by repurposing equipment, securing mobile systems, and coordinating closely with ODEQ** . That's the kind of regulatory relationship that matters most when conditions are most challenging.

<!-- ¶5 -->
Since the start of the contract, we’ve also supported the City of Duncan with their Industrial Pretreatment Program (IPP). In 2025, when a permitted industry raised questions about revised limits stemming from our updated Technically Based Local Limits and Sewer Use Ordinance work, we engaged ODEQ directly on the City's behalf. **ODEQ confirmed our reasoning was sound, expressed confidence in us as the Control Authority representative, and commended the quality of our permit documentation** .

<!-- ¶6 -->
Beyond compliance, we’ve also delivered nearly **$5M in cost and energy savings for the City through targeted process improvements and operational optimization** , demonstrating that regulatory compliance and operational performance are not competing priorities.

<!-- ¶7 -->
###### <mark>WHAT THIS MEANS FOR OCWUT</mark>

<!-- ¶8 -->
Oklahoma’s regulatory environment has evolved significantly over the past 3 decades, and we have been actively engaged throughout that time. **Our Oklahoma-based engineering staff work directly with ODEQ on construction permitting and operational compliance** . ODEQ Coordination and Community Engagement Lead **Arya Simon** brings 8 years of experience as a Construction Permitting Engineer in ODEQ's Water Quality Division followed by a year with EPA's Clean Water State Revolving Fund, giving her an insider's understanding of Oklahoma's regulatory process to keep projects moving through agency review. This firsthand knowledge of agency processes, regulatory expectations, and staff coordination enables us to anticipate reviews, streamline permitting, and resolve agency comments efficiently.

<!-- ¶9 -->
We routinely prepare permit applications, support inspections, and maintain compliance documentation, guiding projects from planning through approval and operation. **Our long-standing relationships with ODEQ and deep understanding of Oklahoma’s regulatory framework help reduce project risk, avoid delays, and ensure consistent compliance** .

<!-- ¶10 -->
At OCWUT, we’ll bring that same disciplined approach: permit requirements incorporated into daily operations, Oklahoma Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (OPDES) Discharge Monitoring Reports delivered to OCWUT at least 3 business days before regulatory due dates, complete and defensible records maintained for inspections and audits, and proactive communication with ODEQ when conditions change. When ODEQ conducts inspections, we’re prepared. When reporting is due, it is accurate and on time. And as the regulatory landscape shifts, particularly on biosolids, we’ll position OCWUT to respond.

<!-- ¶11 -->
That is not the framework we’re proposing to build. _It is how we already operate in Oklahoma every day._
