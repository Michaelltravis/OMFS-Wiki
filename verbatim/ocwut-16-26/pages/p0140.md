###### **Staffing, Resource, and Cost Impacts**

<!-- ¶3 -->
Each planned CIP project will change how OCWUT’s facilities are staffed, maintained, and operated. We’ll identify and plan for these changes well before each project reaches commissioning, working through the Schedule 16 design phases to build a complete picture of staffing, resource, and cost impacts. The following analysis addresses each project’s anticipated impacts. For detailed staffing levels and organizational structure, refer to our **Staffing and Training Plan earlier in this section.**

<!-- ¶4 -->
###### _Staffing Levels and Classifications_

<!-- ¶5 -->
•<sup>**OSHG (Deer Creek and North Canadian):**The transition from gaseous chlorine</sup> to OSHG will fundamentally change chemical handling at both facilities. Current operations involve 150-lb cylinder handling at Deer Creek and one-ton cylinders at North Canadian—each with its own safety protocols, PSM/RMP compliance requirements, and operator skill sets. On-site generation shifts the operation to bulk liquid chemical management with electrolytic cell systems, brine handling, and hydrogen gas ventilation. We anticipate that existing operations staff can absorb hypochlorite generation responsibilities with targeted training, rather than requiring additional staff. However, the maintenance skill set will need to expand to include electrolytic cell systems, brine quality management, and chemical feed calibration. We’ll finalize staffing projections as system designs are completed.

<!-- ¶6 -->
•<sup>**Biosolids Heat Drying Facility (Deer Creek):**This is an entirely new process unit</sup> requiring dedicated operator coverage. Heat drying demands 24/7 operation with specialized skills in thermal system management, emissions monitoring, material handling, and high-temperature safety. We’ll work with OCWUT’s design engineers through the Schedule 16 phases to determine the specific staffing model, including shift structure, operator classifications, and whether dedicated maintenance technicians are needed on-site or can be supported by our Regional Maintenance Team during the initial operating period. Our operations and design subject matter experts (SMEs) will support OCWUT to ensure safety, sizing, and equipment reliability is captured during the design phases of the Deer Creek expansion project.

<!-- ¶7 -->
•<sup>**Land Application to Landfill Transition (Systemwide):**The shift from land</sup> application to landfill disposal will reduce certain staffing requirements while creating new ones. Current land application operations require significant lab resources such as daily pH and fecal coliform testing on each day’s biosolids production and the field staff coordinating application logistics. Moving to landfill disposal will reduce lab testing requirements and associated staffing, potentially freeing approximately one FTE on the solids side currently dedicated to lime stabilization activities. In exchange, hauling coordination will increase, and we’ll work with subcontractors to manage transport logistics to area landfills. We will work with OCWUT to confirm this approach remains the best once landfill disposal is finalized.

<!-- ¶8 -->
###### _Maintenance Staffing and Specialized Skill Sets_

<!-- ¶9 -->
Each CIP project introduces equipment and systems that require new maintenance competencies. **We’ll identify these skill gaps early and address them through a combination of on-site training, manufacturer certification, and support from our national technical resources.**

<!-- ¶10 -->
•<sup>**For OSHG systems**, maintenance staff will need proficiency in electrolytic cell</sup> maintenance and replacement cycles, brine system management and softening system upkeep, sodium hypochlorite storage tank inspection, and chemical feed system calibration. At South Canadian, where PSI/Microclor® onsite generation equipment is already being installed as part of the current construction, we will gain direct operating experience with this equipment beginning at contract start, building institutional knowledge that transfers directly to the Deer Creek and North Canadian installations.

<!-- ¶11 -->
•<sup>**For the heat drying facility**, maintenance requirements will include thermal</sup> equipment and dryer system maintenance, emissions control systems, conveyance and material handling equipment, and high-temperature safety system inspection. Our Regional Maintenance Team and national technical resources, including biosolids SMEs with direct thermal drying experience, will supplement on-site staff for specialized maintenance needs during the initial integration period.
