<!-- ¶4 -->
When Jacobs assumed operations at Waterbury’s 27-MGD WWTP, the City had been receiving significant odor complaints driven by four chemical wet scrubbers in disrepair and a secondary source from the on-site merchant incinerator. Jacobs conducted a formal odor study to identify and quantify each source, then executed a two-track response: an immediate operational control plan that reduced public complaints by more than 55% within the first year, followed by a $2M capital upgrade that included a carbon absorber polishing stage, new chemical dosing pumps, and replacement of aging air piping across both scrubber systems. The result was a permanent, measurable fix to a problem that had eroded community confidence in the facility. 126_009385

###### **ODOR CONTROL**

<!-- ¶6 -->
Odor control is one of the most visible performance measures in wastewater operations. Jacobs brings experience across the full odor control life cycle, including formal odor studies, dispersion modeling, operational improvements, capital upgrades, and ongoing fence line monitoring.

<!-- ¶7 -->
We follow a consistent framework of field sampling and source identification first, dispersion modeling to quantify off-site impact, operational improvement where possible, and capital recommendations sized to deliver the most community benefit per dollar invested. Operators conduct routine H2S monitoring using portable meters, make scheduled fence line patrols during high-odor seasons, and follow documented corrective action protocols when readings exceed thresholds.

<!-- ¶8 -->
