###### **Asset Management System (AMS) — From Framework to Field Results**

<!-- ¶2 -->
A maintenance program without an asset management framework is just a task list. We’ll work closely with OCWUT to develop a structured AMS aligned to ISO 55001 and documented in a Strategic Asset Management Plan (SAMP) that defines policy, roles, decision rights, resources, and implementation timeframes. **We’ll tailor our proven AMS framework, built on the globally recognized model developed by the Institute of Asset Management, to OCWUT’s scope and priorities.** Every element will map concrete deliverables, owners, and governance touchpoints, so the program is auditable, durable, and drives measurable results ( **Exhibit 1-29** ).

<!-- ¶3 -->
Maintenance Manager **Brian Daniels** will lead day-to-day maintenance delivery across all facilities, and Asset Manager **Amy Dembinski, CRL** , will lead development of the AMS and its strategic implementation. **Amy** brings 18 years of CMMS expertise, having **administered more than 100 CMMS databases across Jacobs’ portfolio** , and will be supported by our O&M Asset Management Team.

<!-- ¶4 -->
###### _Governance, Strategy, and Decision Making_

<!-- ¶5 -->
We'll establish a maintenance governance charter that guides OCWUT's asset management strategy and drives development of the Asset Management Policy—defining objectives, decision rights, and change control. The SAMP will set measurable targets for reliability, risk, safety, and cost. During mobilization, we'll complete a baseline condition and criticality assessment across all OCWUT’s assets and produce a risk-ranked register that drives our Maintenance Plan, informs capital improvement planning, and ties staffing and materials strategy to asset risk and workload. Maintenance decisions are driven by a standardized risk model—likelihood of failure and consequence of failure—so resources are consistently prioritized to the assets that matter most.
