###### **Biosolids and Disinfection Systems as Odor Drivers**

<!-- ¶3 -->
OCWUT operates a large and complex biosolids land application program, with approximately 170 permitted sites and more than 13,000 acres. Site observations identified several challenges that directly affect odor, including high moisture content in cake, debris in biosolids, storage limitations, and hydrogen sulfide generation in dewatering areas. OCWUT's requirements explicitly link biosolids management to odor control performance, making consistent solids quality and handling practices critical to systemwide odor control.

<!-- ¶4 -->
We will manage biosolids with a focus on consistency, stability, and odor reduction. This includes improving dewatering performance, reducing storage time, coordinating hauling and land application activities, and evaluating alternatives such as landfill use where conditions or storage constraints require it. We’ll manage sensitive sites and coordinate application practices to minimize community impact, especially in areas with higher visibility.

<!-- ¶5 -->
OCWUT is transitioning from chlorine gas to OSHG at key facilities. Our team will support this transition by integrating hypochlorite generation systems into daily operations, optimizing dosing strategies, and supporting startup and operator training. By aligning disinfection practices with process control, we’ll reduce variability that can contribute to downstream odor conditions and improve the reliability and acceptance of land application sites.

<!-- ¶6 -->
###### **Fence-Line Performance and Compliance**

<!-- ¶7 -->
We’ll align our approach with OCWUT's performance expectations and Schedule 3 requirements. This includes maintaining dissolved sulfide below 0.5 mg/L at the influent flow at North Canadian, hydrogen sulfide below 10 ppm at the base of all bar screens and belt press units across all facilities, and hydrogen sulfide below 4 ppb at the fence line. We’ll record all analyses and document them at 12-hour intervals in accordance with Schedule 3.

<!-- ¶8 -->
Monitoring locations will represent actual community exposure, including both upwind and downwind conditions. When measurements approach defined thresholds, we will take immediate action through operational adjustments, chemical optimization, or temporary mitigation measures. **This keeps odor control tied to measurable, documented performance.**

<!-- ¶9 -->
When Jacobs took over operations at the City of Waterbury Water Pollution Control Facility, existing odor control systems were in disrepair, and the community was experiencing significant complaints. We conducted an odor study, identified major sources, and implemented a short-term control plan that decreased odor complaints by more than 55% between Year 1 and Year 2. Long-term improvements included a carbon absorber polishing stage, new chemical dosing pumps, replacement of aging air piping, and a $2M odor control systems upgrade completed in partnership with Walsh Engineers.

<!-- ¶10 -->
###### **Rapid Response and Community Engagement**

<!-- ¶11 -->
Odor complaints are a direct signal from the community. **We will respond to all complaints within 1 hour.** Field teams will verify conditions, identify the root cause, and implement corrective actions. **Each complaint will be documented, tracked, and resolved.** Monthly reporting will summarize complaint trends and actions taken, providing transparency and accountability. We do not just log complaints. We resolve them.

<!-- ¶12 -->
###### **Integration with Operations, Maintenance, and Capital Planning**

<!-- ¶13 -->
Odor control is fully integrated into operations, maintenance, and capital planning. It is embedded in process control strategies, maintenance planning, and emergency response procedures. Odor data will also inform capital planning decisions to target improvements where they provide the greatest benefit, including ongoing facility upgrades and new infrastructure coming online through the contract term.

<!-- ¶14 -->
###### **Proven Results**

<!-- ¶15 -->
Across our operations, this approach has consistently reduced odor complaints, improved process stability, and minimized reliance on capital-intensive solutions. **By focusing on process control, maintenance, and targeted optimization, we deliver measurable improvements that communities can see and feel.** These results reflect a repeatable approach that we'll bring to OCWUT from day one.
