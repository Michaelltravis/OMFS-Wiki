###### _Cathodic Protection and Corrosion Control_

<!-- ¶2 -->
We’ll inspect cathodic protection systems per RFP §2.2.3 to verify integrity and operability and apply protective coatings during drain and clean activities to minimize corrosion and extend structural life. Proactive corrosion monitoring is particularly important given the elevated H₂S environment at North Canadian and Deer Creek, where we observed significant concrete deterioration along primary clarifier weirs and structural corrosion in headworks buildings during our site visits.

<!-- ¶3 -->
###### _Building, Grounds, and Housekeeping_

<!-- ¶4 -->
Schedule 3 defines detailed housekeeping standards, and we’ll integrate every one of them into NexGen as scheduled work orders so they’re tracked, measurable, and auditable alongside our mechanical PM program. Key elements include daily interior cleaning and litter removal, bi-weekly floor maintenance, semi-annual window washing and wall cleaning, HVAC system maintenance, pest control, and exterior painting and corrosion touch-up.

<!-- ¶5 -->
For grounds maintenance across the extensive acreage at North Canadian, Deer Creek, and Chisholm Creek, we’ll subcontract lawn and landscaping services based on our assessment that dedicated grounds subcontractors deliver more consistent results than in-house mowing programs at facilities of this scale. South Canadian’s smaller footprint will be maintained in-house. Any housekeeping issues identified during OCWUT’s monthly inspections that remain uncorrected for 30 days trigger fees at risk— **our integrated CMMS tracking prevents items from falling through the cracks by assigning owners, due dates, and escalation triggers to every task.**

<!-- ¶6 -->
###### **Predictive Maintenance (PdM) Program**

<!-- ¶7 -->
PdM catches what PM schedules can’t: the early signs of degradation that happen between scheduled intervals. **We’ll deploy a comprehensive PdM technology suite across OCWUT’s facilities, selectively targeted at equipment based on its criticality and failure modes, ensuring the highest-value equipment receives the most advanced monitoring.**

<!-- ¶8 -->
###### _Vibration Analysis_

<!-- ¶9 -->
We’ll perform quarterly vibration analysis on all large rotating equipment: the four 1,250 HP pumps at Witcher Pump Station, ten blowers (350–800 HP) at North Canadian, South Canadian SBR blowers (and the new oxidation ditch equipment as it comes online), and lift station pumps and blowers at Chisholm Creek and Deer Creek. Annual vibration analysis will cover additional rotating equipment per Schedule 2, including retention station pumps, lift station gear boxes, sand filter pumps, and Phase 3B blowers at North Canadian.

<!-- ¶10 -->
When results identify deficiencies, we’ll open corrective work orders within 5 days (PD-1 KPI). The value of this discipline is tangible: vibration trending on North Canadian’s blowers, for example, will help us detect the mechanical issues that led to the recent blower failure incident, potentially preventing a recurrence.
