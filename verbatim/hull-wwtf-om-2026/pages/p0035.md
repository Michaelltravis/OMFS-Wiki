Our approach **aligns directly with the required collection system work** by integrating I/I investigations into the annual cleaning and CCTV program and the required I/I tracking investigations deliverable. CCTV findings and field observations will be documented, defects will be ranked by severity and I/I potential, and results will be managed through the CMMS/ data tools, so progress is **measurable and transparent** . Where inflow sources are identified, Jacobs will also support practical control measures consistent with collection system permit expectations (e.g., identifying and addressing illegal storm connections where applicable).

<!-- ¶7 -->
We will identify areas potentially influenced by saltwater intrusion and distinguish those impacts from groundwater inflow and direct inflow. We’ll use tools such as SmartCover®, WATS modeling, CCTV findings, field observations, maintenance history, and available wet‑weather and tide‑ influenced flow patterns to **prioritize targeted inspections and corrective actions** including manhole sealing, joint repair, lining, or other rehabilitation where warranted.

<!-- ¶8 -->
###### **_Sewer Pump Station Operations_**

<!-- ¶9 -->
Our operating approach uses alarm history, pump run‑time and level trends, field observations, and cleaning records to identify **early warning signs** such as uneven runtimes, excessive cycling, grit or grease buildup, abnormal wet well conditions, odor generation, and the development of force main or septicity issues. Because the Town’s pump stations and force mains are central to system performance, we will, where conditions warrant, increase wet well cleaning frequency, adjust maintenance intervals, and **target corrective actions before they become service disruptions** . Available remote monitoring and SCADA information will be used to strengthen response, documentation, and operational decision‑making across the pump station network. This same data evaluates current wet well cleaning practices and applies Wet Well Wizard‑style optimization, confirming that cleaning frequency, odor and solids control, bypass planning, and maintenance costs align with actual field conditions rather than fixed intervals alone. Below highlights activities associated with our approach.

<!-- ¶10 -->
###### **Daily**

<!-- ¶11 -->
- µ<sup>Respond to all pump station alarms</sup>

<!-- ¶12 -->
###### **Weekly (Monday and Friday, or more often as conditions dictate)**

<!-- ¶13 -->
- µ<sup>Inspect each station and wet well for grease, grit, floatables,</sup> corrosion, odor, high‑water conditions, and other abnormal operating conditions

<!-- ¶14 -->
- µ<sup>Confirm security, inspect lights and alarms, record pump</sup> hours, review run‑time balance/cycling, and create work orders for deficiencies

<!-- ¶15 -->
- µ<sup>Keep station areas clean, rotate pumps, and</sup> exercise generators

<!-- ¶16 -->
###### **Monthly Inspections**

<!-- ¶17 -->
- µ<sup>Review alarm history, run times, and station trends to</sup> identify emerging maintenance or reliability concerns

<!-- ¶18 -->
- µ<sup>Operate pumps manually to verify performance, inspect</sup> floats and valves, test backup generators, record electrical data where available, and document deficiencies.

<!-- ¶19 -->
###### **Quarterly/Annual Inspections**

<!-- ¶20 -->
- µ<sup>Remove excess grit, grease, and trash from wet wells and</sup> related structures; inspect pumps, floats, level sensors, check valves, and electrical components; load‑test generators; and review station condition and maintenance history to identify repairs, rehabilitation needs, or operating changes that will improve reliability and reduce recurring maintenance demand

<!-- ¶21 -->
Logbooks document conditions at each station, and predictive, preventive, and emergency maintenance activities will be scheduled accordingly. Wet wells will be periodically pumped down and cleaned with a vacuum truck to remove bottom debris and floatables, and walls will be washed down.

<!-- ¶22 -->
###### **_CCTV and Sewer Line Cleaning_**

<!-- ¶23 -->
Cleaning and CCTV of 20% of the collection system will occur annually during the first 5 years. An annual summary will also be provided, as well as a 5‑year work plan. After 5 years, we’ll continue cleaning/CCTV on a 10‑year cycle, or more frequently, as conditions warrant. These requirements will be met through a **planned work-order-driven program** that uses blockage history, customer complaints, wet‑weather observations, alarm trends, pump station findings, and CCTV results to continuously refine priorities. This program will focus on lines and structures associated with recurring maintenance demand, force main discharge impacts, odor and FOG concerns, and suspected I/I contribution areas. We will use NASSCO‑based inspection results, cleaning records, and field observations to document recurring defects, **identify developing problems early, and support rehabilitation and CIP planning** .
