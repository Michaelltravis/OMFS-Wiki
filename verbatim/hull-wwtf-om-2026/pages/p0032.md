###### **BIOSOLIDS AND RESIDUALS MANAGEMENT**

<!-- ¶3 -->
Jacobs will manage all biosolids processing, thickening, storage, hauling, and disposal in full compliance with 40 CFR Part 503 and applicable Massachusetts regulations, while maintaining the Town’s current thickened sludge management approach and hauling program to the Cranston WWTF. The program is designed to provide **resilient, predictable, operations** that protect public health, minimize odor impacts in this sensitive coastal setting, stabilize cost exposure in a volatile regional disposal market, and support **long-term operational sustainability** at the WWTF. Consideration will be given to the Town’s coastal location, seasonal population fluctuations, and limited regional biosolids outlets that influence hauling logistics and disposal reliability.

<!-- ¶4 -->
Drawing on our extensive biosolids experience across New England, our approach emphasizes **disciplined solids inventory management** , optimized thickening and hauling practices, and proactive monitoring of regional disposal capacity trends. Jacobs will collaborate with the Town during the **Annual Innovation Workshop** to identify opportunities for a structured **Biosolids Strategic Review** . This evaluation may include assessment of thickened versus dewatered solids scenarios, hauling distances and market outlets, odor risk considerations, lifecycle cost impacts, and potential funding opportunities. Findings will be documented in a formal memorandum with recommendations supporting short‑ and long‑term biosolids management strategies. The evaluation will address the long‑term limitations and options for biosolids disposal, factoring in potential long‑term avenues and, if necessary, CAPEX requirements for dewatering or other improvements. If biosolids dewatering emerges as the

<!-- ¶5 -->
most viable long‑term option, implementation strategies will prioritize **minimizing CAPEX costs while maximizing OPEX savings** , with a design ready for execution at the appropriate time.

<!-- ¶6 -->
###### **_Stabilization, Thickening, and Biosolids Inventory Management_**

<!-- ¶7 -->
Sludge thickening and residuals handling are optimized to increase solids concentration, reduce chemical and energy use, lower hauling frequency, and prevent solids accumulation in process tanks. The gravity thickener serves as the primary solids concentration step, producing thickened sludge suitable for transport to Cranston while maintaining **stable plant process performance and minimizing odor potential** .

<!-- ¶8 -->
Seasonal variability is addressed through adaptive operating strategies. During colder months, when sludge freezing risks may limit thickening or storage flexibility, operations will be adjusted to maintain **stable solids inventory and process performance** . Possible enhancements to improve winter reliability, such as adjusting the thickening schedule and evaluating potential insulation improvements to the sludge storage tank, will be reviewed with the Town and incorporated into asset management and capital planning recommendations as appropriate.

<!-- ¶9 -->
A formal biosolids inventory management protocol establishes target sludge age, storage durations, and action thresholds. This framework limits excessive solids retention that can lead to odor generation, septicity, or downstream process instability while maintaining operational flexibility during wet weather events or market disruptions.
