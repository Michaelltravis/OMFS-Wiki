###### **_The Town’s Collection System and Its Challenges_**

<!-- ¶3 -->
The Town’s collection system presents distinct coastal operating challenges, including saltwater intrusion, remaining I/I, long force mains, hydrogen sulfide generation, odor concerns, FOG and solids accumulation, and recurring wet well maintenance. These conditions place added stress on pump stations, force mains, and gravity sewers. The system also includes a low‑pressure network with Town‑maintained grinder pumps that requires responsive service and structured asset tracking.

<!-- ¶4 -->
Our **proactive, data-driven collections program** addresses these conditions preventive maintenance, targeted cleaning and CCTV, disciplined pump station and wet well management, and structured use of alarm, complaint, inspection, and work‑ order data. This approach supports earlier identification of issues, improved hotspot definition, strengthened I/I tracking and investigation, optimized odor and septicity control, and clearer prioritization for rehabilitation and capital improvement planning (CIP). Our approach also improves the differentiation between saltwater intrusion and rainfall‑driven I/I, allowing corrective actions to **target root causes** . Collection system odor‑control considerations are addressed in the Odor Control subsection later in this approach.

<!-- ¶5 -->
###### **_Collection System Operations_**

<!-- ¶6 -->
Our collection system approach incorporates data, field execution, and clear accountability to stay ahead of recurring issues while improving **system visibility and decision-making** . We will continuously adjust priorities to gain better visibility into system condition, clearer identification of problem areas, and stronger support for rehabilitation and CIP decision‑ making. Key elements include:

<!-- ¶7 -->
- µ<sup>Responding to sewer overflows within 1 hour or sooner.</sup>

<!-- ¶8 -->
- µ<sup>Annual cleaning and CCTV of 20% of the system annually</sup> for the first 5 years, then 10% annually thereafter, supplemented by the sewer line rapid assessment tool (SL‑RAT) for hotspot identification. Additional attention will be given to recurring problem areas, wet‑weather hotspots, and locations associated with odor, grease, or hydraulic restriction concerns.

<!-- ¶9 -->
- µ<sup>Monitoring, controlling, and evaluating the Town's use of</sup> bioxide, In‑Pipe Technology, and Wet Well Wizard‑style optimization tools as part of our collections routing and pump station operating strategy, using wet well conditions, odor observations, run‑time trends, levels, alarms, and cleaning history to adjust treatment, cleaning frequency, and field priorities based on actual conditions.

###### **_Support for the Town’s I/I Reduction Efforts_**

<!-- ¶18 -->
We will support I/I reduction through a **focused, data-driven program** that protects wet weather performance, reduces peak flows, and supports **long-term collection system reliability** . An I/I baseline will be established from existing system information (including prior studies and historical operating trends), using wet weather indicators, pump station performance, and maintenance history to target the highest risk sewer sheds first.

<!-- ¶19 -->
Using SmartCover® manhole mounted units in the collection system will help identify and reduce I/I in the system. Traditional I/I isolation methodologies require 18 to 24 months of field assessment and analysis, whereas SmartCover® can produce results **up to 12 months sooner** . Selected manholes using SmartCover® units during dry and wet weather periods will be installed, and will be relocated to different parts of the collection system when needed. Additional information on SmartCover® technology is provided in **Appendix E** for the Town’s review.
