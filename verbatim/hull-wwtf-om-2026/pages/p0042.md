###### **ENERGY MANAGEMENT: A CORE OPERATING DISCIPLINE**

<!-- ¶2 -->
Jacobs manages energy as a **core operating discipline** embedded in daily decision‑making, continuously optimized, and fully transparent. The Town expects its partner to actively manage power utilization, provide clear monthly reporting, and continuously identify opportunities to **improve efficiency and reduce costs** .

<!-- ¶3 -->
We will establish a **baseline-driven energy management program** aligned with the Town’s performance standard of 2,500 kWh/MG. Using historical data, SCADA, and operational trends, we will develop a clear, defensible baseline that reflects actual facility conditions. From that foundation, performance will be tracked in real time, data will be converted into actionable insights, and opportunities will be identified to **reduce energy intensity while maintaining process stability, compliance, and resiliency** .

<!-- ¶4 -->
###### **_Optimizing High-Impact Energy Uses_**

<!-- ¶5 -->
Our approach focuses on the largest controllable energy drivers, including aeration, pumping, and solids handling, where operational discipline has the greatest impact. Proven practices include optimized setpoints, equipment run‑time strategies, proactive maintenance, and operator‑friendly performance tracking. Our approach provides the Town with **transparent reporting, measurable results, and confidence in shared savings** under the 50/50 gainshare structure for both energy savings and excess use.

<!-- ¶6 -->
###### **_Collaboration on Opportunities for Phased Energy Optimization_**

<!-- ¶7 -->
Beyond daily optimization, we will collaborate with the Town to evaluate additional opportunities for improved efficiency. Based on our experience at similar facilities, focus areas will include:

<!-- ¶8 -->
- µ<sup>**Pumping system optimization,**ensuring pumps operate</sup> within preferred efficiency ranges and reducing unnecessary energy use across influent, effluent, and return flows

<!-- ¶9 -->
- µ<sup>**Solids retention time (SRT) and process optimization,**</sup> minimizing excess biomass and associated aeration demand

<!-- ¶10 -->
- µ<sup>**Aeration system optimization,**including air delivery,</sup> distribution, and control strategies to match process needs without over‑aeration

<!-- ¶11 -->
- µ<sup>**Primary treatment optimization,**maximizing removal of</sup> BOD and TSS in the most energy‑efficient process step to reduce downstream energy demand

<!-- ¶12 -->
- µ<sup>**Plant water use optimization,**reducing unnecessary</sup> internal recycling that increases pumping and treatment loads

<!-- ¶13 -->
These strategies represent proven best practices Jacobs has successfully implemented at comparable facilities. Following baseline development, we will prioritize **no-cost and low-cost operational improvements first** , followed by evaluation of utility incentives, grants, and cost‑effective capital investments where justified.

<!-- ¶14 -->
###### **_Proven Success with Significant Energy (and Chemical) Optimization_**

<!-- ¶15 -->
We will explore the value of leveraging our Intelligent O&M tools and data‑driven optimization capabilities to identify and sustain savings. These tools have proven highly successful at a range of facilities. For example, at a wastewater facility in Arizona, Jacobs optimized aeration blower performance using real‑time data and DO control strategies, achieving a **20% reduction in energy use and approximately $300,000 in annual savings** while maintaining full compliance. Similarly, at a large WWTP in the Mid‑Atlantic region, our predictive modeling approach reduced chemical usage by more than 20%, demonstrating how data‑driven optimization can deliver measurable cost savings without compromising performance.

<!-- ¶16 -->
By combining disciplined operations, advanced analytics, and a collaborative improvement process, we provide the Town with a **transparent, accountable, and continuously improving energy management program** that reduces lifecycle costs, supports sustainability goals, and delivers shared financial benefits.

###### **_Jacobs Grant/Loan Support Services_**

<!-- ¶18 -->
As energy efficiency and consumption improvements are identified, we can also help identify potential funding sources. Water and wastewater assets require a continuous cycle of capital planning, repair, and replacement. At some point in the life cycle of the Town’s facilities, significant investment will be required. When that time comes, preparation and a clear understanding of available financing options is critical.

<!-- ¶19 -->
We routinely help clients develop funding strategies for capital projects. Our financial services team can work with the Town to explore options, avoid pitfalls, and **maximize return on investment** . Common funding solutions include grants and low‑interest loans, traditional municipal funding, and user fees and other charges. The grant and loan application process can be complicated and time‑consuming. Applications often require financial plans, environmental review documentation, and descriptions of planned projects, estimated costs, and schedules. Whatever the requirements, we can help the Town stay ahead of the process. This structured approach to evaluating and securing funding is illustrated in **Exhibit 5-12** on the following page.
