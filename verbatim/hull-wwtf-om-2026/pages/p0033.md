Hauling schedules and solids production trends will be reviewed routinely to ensure sufficient operational flexibility is maintained during high flows, maintenance events, or disposal constraints. Process performance will be tracked through solids balance monitoring, key thickening and biosolids production KPIs, and routine equipment inspections, with operational adjustments guided by data and reviewed during weekly process meetings.

<!-- ¶2 -->
Residuals handling practices will be coordinated with the Town’s odor control program. Existing odor control plans will be reviewed and refined, or a site‑specific odor control plan will be developed if needed, consistent with RFP requirements. Biosolids handling procedures, staging practices, and removal frequencies will be aligned to minimize odor impacts. If temporary biosolids staging becomes necessary, mitigation measures will be implemented and material will be removed promptly to ensure stable operations are maintained and nearby neighborhoods are protected.

<!-- ¶3 -->
###### **_Hauling and Disposal_**

<!-- ¶4 -->
Biosolids hauling and disposal will be managed in alignment with the Town’s current operational approach, with thickened transportation to the Cranston WWTF as the primary disposal outlet. This established disposal pathway will be maintained while confirming that hauling logistics, scheduling, and documentation are handed efficiently and in full regulatory compliance.

<!-- ¶5 -->
Given that the volatility of the regional biosolids disposal market, production volumes, hauling costs, and disposal capacity are actively tracked to **anticipate changes and reduce cost risk** , we will:

<!-- ¶6 -->
- µ<sup>Track dry tons produced and unit hauling/disposal costs</sup>

<!-- ¶7 -->
- µ<sup>Monitor market trends and regulatory developments</sup>

<!-- ¶8 -->
- µ<sup>Provide early notification to the Town of significant cost or</sup> regulatory changes

<!-- ¶9 -->
- µ<sup>Support evaluation of alternative disposal or beneficial</sup> reuse strategies, if needed

<!-- ¶10 -->
Quarterly cost trend tracking against the established $/dry ton baseline provides **early visibility of market shifts** . If market pressures increase, operational adjustments or alternative outlets will be evaluated in coordination with the Town while maintaining reliable biosolids removal. All chain‑of‑custody documentation, biosolids production records, and disposal reports will be maintained in **audit-ready electronic format** and integrated into the Town’s compliance reporting systems.

<!-- ¶11 -->
###### **_Regulatory Compliance and Reporting_**

<!-- ¶12 -->
All required biosolids reports will be prepared accurately and on time. We will maintain documentation of production volumes, quality parameters, and final disposition in secure electronic systems and integrate them into the Town’s compliance dashboard. We will ensure that all interstate biosolids transport and disposal documentation meets applicable Massachusetts and Rhode Island regulatory requirements.

<!-- ¶13 -->
We will actively manage biosolids production rates, inventory levels, and hauling efficiency to reduce the likelihood of reaching the 10% dry ton cost adjustment threshold. We remain focused on **stabilizing costs through operational optimization** rather than reacting to market changes. All biosolids‑related assets, including thickening equipment, pumping systems, and storage infrastructure, will be incorporated into the Town’s asset management and CMMS system, including preventive maintenance schedules, condition monitoring, and lifecycle planning supporting **reliable performance** .

<!-- ¶14 -->
###### **_Strategic Planning and Future Market Conditions_**

<!-- ¶15 -->
The New England biosolids market continues to face tightening regional disposal capacity, longer hauling distances, and increasing disposal costs. Reduced availability of regional outlets for liquid thickened sludge disposal has heightened market volatility.

<!-- ¶16 -->
To support long‑term resiliency, Jacobs will work with the Town to explore operational and infrastructure strategies that may improve flexibility and reduce risk. These may include on‑ or off‑site dewatering options, partnerships with regional facilities that provide processing capacity, or other approaches that expand disposal alternatives. Such strategies have the potential to reduce hauling volumes, lower transportation‑ related greenhouse gas emissions, and strengthen overall system resiliency while helping the Town proactively respond to evolving market conditions.

<!-- ¶17 -->
Through structured evaluation, proactive leadership, and disciplined asset management, Jacobs will support the Town with a **forward-looking biosolids program** that is stable, resilient, and aligned with sustainability and fiscal objectives.
