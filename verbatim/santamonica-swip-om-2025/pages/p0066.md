EXHIBIT 2-28. _Key Elements in the ERP_

<!-- ¶2 -->
|**EVENT**|**JACOBS’ APPROACH**|
|---|---|
|**Chemical Spill Response**|Individual safetydata sheets(SDS)willguideproper clean-upand control measures.|
|**Asset/Equipment**<br>**Failure Response**|Identification of asset conditions, critical assets, and acquiring equipment spares that may be<br>placed in inventoryso emergencyreplacements can be made.|
|**Fire Response**|Facility staff will be trained on fire prevention and fire suppression. Local and regional<br>maintenance staff are available for equipment/facilityrecoveryoperations.|
|**Flood Response**|Proper maintenance of facilities so flood control equipment and measures at the facilities are<br>in top condition. Regional staff, properly certified and qualified, are available to fill in for staff<br>shortages that may result from regional flooding. Jacobs’ regional technical support staff are<br>available immediately to optimize Plant chemical dosing and operations should flooding affect<br>incomingraw waterquality.|
|**Severe Weather Response**|Maintenance ofgenerators,mock drills,debrisprotection,and extendedpower contingency.|
|**Pandemic/Health**<br>**Emergency Response**|A comprehensive employee health program, including mental/emotional health. Regional<br>staff are available for assisting with operations and maintenance during extreme events, and an<br>emergencystaffing plan will helpmaintain safe operation under reduced staffingif required.|
||Continuous monitoring and evaluation of trends to proactively identify potential upsets.<br>Response will include immediate additional testing to identify atypical contaminants, jar testing,|
|**Process Failure**<br>**Response**|<br>and process optimization, which may be required based on changed water quality conditions.<br>Conduct comprehensive source water quality testing to detect possible contamination.<br>Procurement resources are available to remedy chemical shortages. Leverage Jacobs technical<br>resources to identifythe issue and assist in the development of the recovery plan.|
|**Power Failure Response**|Generator startup procedures;sustainedplant operations until utility power is restored.|
|**Emergency**<br>**Telephone**<br>**Numbers**|An emergency contact list with site numbers will be posted at each worksite and include project<br>contacts, including the City, plant manager, safety manager, the Public Safety Department,<br>police, fire, and ambulance for surrounding communities—emergency (911) and non-emergency<br>numbers,area hospitals,and serious incident reportingand crisis management.|
|**Emergency**<br>**Equipment**<br>**Inventory**|If emergency equipment is a rental item or provided as a specialty service by a vendor,<br>agreements for 24/7 service will be put in place in advance.|
|**Continuity of**<br>**Operations (COO) Plan**|An effective COO makes sure of uninterrupted operations during immediate needs, such as<br>a pandemic, and plans for longer range needs such as retirement, promotions, etc. The COO<br>focuses on roles and responsibilities, notification requirements, subcontractor guidelines,<br>quarantine guidelines, exposure reporting, and travel requirements, and identifies resources,<br>both internal and external,to respond to staffingaugmentation needs.|
|**Pipeline Failure/**<br>**Blockages**|Train field crews on a comprehensive response plan for the various types of pipeline failure<br>to guide them in appropriate response, whether it’s bypassing, isolating, or venting/<br>flushing and/or rodding sections. CPO will communicate condition to the City and provide<br>updates as appropriate.|
|**Advance Contracting**|Establish in advance agreements for 24/7 service for any emergency rental equipment or<br>servicesprovided byservice vendors.|
|**Earthquakes**|Public and employee safety is the top priority; staff follow posted instructions to seek shelter and<br>avoid hazards, then calmly evacuate to designated areas, with the Safety Supervisor coordinating<br>communication and re-entry only after structural assessments.|
