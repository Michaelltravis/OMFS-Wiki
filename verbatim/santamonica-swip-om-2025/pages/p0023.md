<!-- ¶2 -->
###### Implementing Improvements

<!-- ¶3 -->
Our O&M history at the City of Clovis has resulted in various lessons learned and improvements:

<!-- ¶4 -->
- µ<sup>The MBR, membrane-specific cleaning, and back-pulse</sup> process have been improved. The system now utilizes MemPulse technology to extend fiber life through cleaning and reduce the need for maintenance cleans.

<!-- ¶5 -->
- µ<sup>We have improved the SCADA format and system to</sup> provide more operability and control of equipment on- and off-site, improved monitoring from process equipment, and improved screen configurations based on operator input.

<!-- ¶6 -->
- µ<sup>We have improved the solids handling process through</sup> intensive testing of sludge quality and polymer choices that work best with an MBR/aerobically digested sludge. Through this testing we have learned the best process parameters to maintain the sludge quality and minimize solids production.

<!-- ¶7 -->
The Clovis Team

<!-- ¶8 -->
Our MBR process produces great quality water for Title 22 reuse. Good screening at the head of the plant is important to keep material from fouling the membranes. Properly maintaining the blowers and the aeration system is the other big piece of it, because good air scour is critical. Nobody wants to replace membranes early–they are expensive! In general, there are more instruments that need to be calibrated and maintained, and operators will need to learn some new things. But once you learn the process, the system is easy to run.

<!-- ¶9 -->
– Tony Duffy, Jacobs Plant Manager at the Clovis WWTP/WRF
