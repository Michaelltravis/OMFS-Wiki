##### **Section 4**

<!-- ¶3 -->
#### Suggested Modifications

<!-- ¶4 -->
_Jacobs provides comprehensive, integrated solutions that go beyond routine operations, saving time and reducing costs while aligning fully with the City’s goals for resilience, reliability, and sustainability. Drawing from our deep bench of subject matter experts, Jacobs proposes the following modifications to the Scope of Work. These suggestions reflect our understanding of Santa Monica’s vision for SWIP as both a water supply asset today and a platform for future potable reuse innovation._

<!-- ¶5 -->
###### **DISCOUNTED ENGINEERING SERVICES**

<!-- ¶6 -->
We are proud of our “more than an operator” DNA, and are eager to engage our entire team to help the City of Santa Monica with various tasks related to the SWIP. We are offering the City 1000 hours of discounted engineering at a 2.3 multiplier each year of our partnership. We’ve got some great ideas of how we can work together, and we look forward to discussing this more with the City. Some of these potential tasks could include the following.

<!-- ¶7 -->
###### **_Process and Engineering Support_**

<!-- ¶8 -->
Jacobs has a deep bench of process experts in wastewater, drinking water, and potable reuse, and familiar with the unit processes and chemical systems at SWIP. Our O&M team will reach out to them on an as-needed basis to troubleshoot challenges with operations.

<!-- ¶9 -->
If challenges go beyond simple troubleshooting, additional evaluation can support process optimization. The same SMEs can develop plans to further evaluate processes, such as the issue of cartridge filter replacement and the potential correlation with STT. Our team can dive into the issue, request additional data, summarize findings, and provide recommendations for operational adjustments. Some potential additional studies include the following:

<!-- ¶11 -->
µ<sup>Chlorate and other quality control issues related to</sup> chemical supply, storage and handling

<!-- ¶12 -->
Depending on the study topic, the scope will be adjusted to accommodate the budget within discounted engineering services.

<!-- ¶13 -->
###### **_Preparing for Direct Potable Reuse_**

<!-- ¶14 -->
We understand the City plans to incorporate DPR into its water supply portfolio. DPR will likely require additional monitoring beyond the requirements for IPR, especially when integrating two existing facilities. We propose a value-added task to allow for Jacobs to evaluate potential additional monitoring and possibly install additional analyzers at SWIP and at Arcadia. The additional data collection taken today could build a data repository to give regulators confidence in a future DPR scenario.

Leech Mitigation at PWSC Demonstration Plant

<!-- ¶16 -->
The Pure Water Southern California
(PWSC) Demonstration Plant, operated by
Metropolitan and the Los Angeles County
Sanitation Districts, experienced persistent
snail (Physa) proliferation during MBR
testing. Frequent cleaning managed but did
not eliminate the issue, raising concerns
about membrane life. Jacobs and Hazen
were engaged to identify and test practical
mitigation strategies. Current measures being
implemented include ultra-fine influent
screening, chlorinated reactor feed, anoxic
zone incorporation, and free ammonia
membrane soaks. These approaches are
now under evaluation to inform full-scale
MBR design.
