**Howard Brewen |** Director of Operations (continued)

<!-- ¶2 -->
Director | Woodland Davis Regional Water Treatment Facility (RWTF), Woodland Davis Clean Water Agency | **_Woodland, CA._** Howard provides leadership for the operational staff of Woodland’s three RWTF, which includes a river intake and pump station, 36-inch 5.1-mile raw water pipeline, a 30-MGD WTP, finished water pump station, and a 36-inch, 7.8 mile distribution pipeline. In his role as Director, Howard is involved in all facets of facility operations from liaising with clients and budgeting, to permitting and certifications.

<!-- ¶3 -->
Director | Twin Oaks Valley WTP, San Diego County Water Authority | **_San Marcos, CA._** Howard provides senior leadership to the operational staff of this 100-MGD submerged membrane filtration WTP. The plant’s features include fine screening, membrane filtration, ozone disinfection, biologically active carbon contactors, chlorine addition, and two 7.5-mg clearwells. As Director, Howard is involved in all facets of facility operations from liaising with clients and budgeting, to permitting and certifications.

<!-- ¶4 -->
Superintendant | Water Resource Recovery Facility (WRRF), City of San Luis Obispo | **_San Luis Obispo, CA._** Responsible for the daily O&M of the WRRF, including but not limited to the following: administering all wastewater treatment, reclamation, and effluent disposal activities; work oversight; review and evaluation of facility personnel; compliance with federal, state, and local water biosolids and air quality regulations; management of an annual $3.5M line item budget and an annual $600K to $3M capital improvement project (CIP) budget.

<!-- ¶5 -->
Wastewater Treatment Operator | WRRF, City of San Luis Obispo | **_San Luis Obispo, CA._** Howard was responsible for all WRRF processes, including the O&M of bio-filters, activated sludge systems, and disinfection systems using chlorination and dechlorination methods. He conducted basic laboratory practices and principles for compliance with regulatory standards. Additionally, Howard led capital improvement projects (CIP) and special projects, which included planning, coordination, and implementation of upgrades and enhancements to the facility’s infrastructure and processes. These projects aimed at improving efficiency, reducing operational costs, and establishing long-term sustainability of the wastewater treatment operations.

<!-- ¶6 -->
Wastewater Collections Operator | Wastewater Collection Systems and Storm Drains, City of San Luis Obispo | **_San Luis Obispo, CA._** Howard managed operations, maintenance, and emergency repairs of wastewater collection systems and storm drains for the City of San Luis Obispo. This included routine inspection and cleaning of sewer lines to prevent blockages and overflows, as well as performing preventive maintenance to ensure system reliability. Howard conducted emergency repairs to address urgent issues such as pipe breaks or severe blockages, coordinating with other City departments and contractors as needed. He oversaw the maintenance of storm drain systems to ensure proper drainage and mitigate flooding risks during heavy rainfall. Howard also implemented best practices for safety and regulatory compliance, and maintained detailed records of all maintenance and repair activities to support operational efficiency and regulatory reporting.

<!-- ¶7 -->
Manager | WRRF Intern Program, City of San Luis Obispo | **_San Luis Obispo, CA._** Howard established a robust Intern Program for individuals interested in obtaining state certification as a wastewater treatment operator, where they volunteer a minimum of 1,840 hours as Operators in Training (OIT). The program has successfully placed over 90% of its interns in permanent jobs within the industry.

<!-- ¶8 -->
Principal Lead | WRRF Energy Efficiency Project, City of San Luis Obispo | **_San Luis Obispo, CA._** Acting as the principal lead, Howard collaborated with Pacific Gas and Electric (PG&E) in a $9.75M Energy Efficiency Project, the first of its kind as a public/private partnership in the state of California. The project included the design and replacement of equipment that was non-compliant or non-efficient from an energy or regulation perspective. The overall goal of the program was the integration of process technology, energy usage, and instrumentation system optimization to reduce overall energy usage at the facility.

<!-- ¶9 -->
Core Leadership Team | WRRF Upgrade Project, City of San Luis Obispo | **_San Luis Obispo, CA._** Member of the core management team overseeing a $140M facility upgrade project to deliver the latest in water resource recovery technology, processes, and sustainability as well as incorporation of research sites and an educational learning center. This included not only design decisions but also the integration of new standard operations procedures, operations optimization, new treatment equipment, and the collection of data used in predicting trends allowing operations to be proactive, not reactive. The core management team was responsible for the decision-making process for all aspects of the project.
