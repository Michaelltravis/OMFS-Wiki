###### **APPENDIX E: ADDITIONAL DIGITAL TOOLS FOR NEXT LEVEL OPTIMIZATION**

<!-- ¶2 -->
While our Base Fee includes numerous value-added services at no additional cost, we've identified additional opportunities to generate substantial savings and operational improvements for the County. They include energy and chemical optimization initiatives from advanced data analytics and AI tools through our Palantir partnership and Dragonfly's AI-powered collection system defect coding. We're presenting these options separately to provide transparency and flexibility in your selection process. Each item offers significant potential for cost savings—with power optimization potentially saving over $1.6 million over five year and chemical optimization around $1.1 million over five years—while also improving efficiency, enhancing compliance safeguards, and increasing operational reliability. These technologies allow for risk-free testing of operational strategies, provide actionable insights through machine learning, and enable more effective management of your wastewater facilities and collection systems. Dragonfly typically provides a 30-40% savings over manual CCTV defect coding while delivering more consistent results. Though not part of our Base Fee proposal, they represent strategic investments that could deliver exceptional long-term value to the North Fulton wastewater facilities, and we look forward to the opportunity to explore them further with the County if they are of interest.

<!-- ¶3 -->
###### **INTELLIGENT O&M DRIVEN BY ADVANCED DATA ANALYTICS AND AI**

<!-- ¶4 -->
Jacobs’ partnership with Palantir brings cutting-edge artificial intelligence and data analytics capabilities to the County's operations. This technology transforms operational data into actionable insights by identifying patterns and correlations that human operators might miss. We implement machine learning algorithms that analyze operational data from multiple sources—SCADA, laboratory information management systems, maintenance records, and energy consumption—to identify optimization opportunities and predict potential issues. These advanced analytics capabilities provide operators with decision support tools that enhance both day-to-day operations and long-term planning, improving process stability during challenging operating conditions and optimizing resource utilization across all facilities.

<!-- ¶5 -->
###### **_Power Use Optimization_**

<!-- ¶6 -->
Power optimization is always a focus at wastewater treatment plants, which tend to be among the largest electricity users in a given community. Traditional optimization techniques used by contract operators and consultants have been applied for decades with good results, including better monitoring, lighting retrofits, VFD, and high efficiency motors. Jacobs has a mantra about “challenging the accepted,” so we wanted to do something more, and applied data science and machine learning techniques to the problem at sites that had already been optimized using industry standard methods.

<!-- ¶7 -->
The results have been nothing short of astounding. Using only what has worked the best for a particular site under the same operating conditions in the past, Jacobs has been able to further optimize power consumption by 10-25%. We have successfully applied this to a range of plants and different high power usage —aeration, UV power, high service pumping, real time rate structure management, etc., and typically without the need for additional instrumentation. None of this has ever impacted permit compliance. We use a “push notification” to the operations team ( **Exhibit E-1** ), so they are the final decider of implementing any recommendations for setpoint changes. Even so, staff adoption of push notifications has been very strong from the beginning, typically over 75%.
