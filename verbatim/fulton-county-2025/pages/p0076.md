###### **SLUDGE/BIOSOLIDS MANAGEMENT**

<!-- ¶2 -->
###### **_Industry-Leading Expertise in Comprehensive Biosolids Management Solutions_**

<!-- ¶3 -->
Jacobs' biosolids O&M programs successfully manage the disposal and beneficial use of over 40,000 dry tons of solids every year. Additionally, we have designed hundreds of biosolids projects worldwide and completed more than 4,000 water and wastewater facilities. Nearly all of our treatment projects under contract operations involve some aspect of biosolids management and processing, including thickening; Class B and Class A digestion; fats, oils, and grease (FOG) and food waste; stabilization; dewatering, drying, energy resource recovery and cogeneration; nutrient analysis for nuisance struvite/vivianite; side stream treatment; and biosolids disposal and beneficial reuse.

<!-- ¶4 -->
Because Jacobs employs the world's top talent in the operation and design of thickening, digestion, dewatering, and energy recovery technologies, our O&M team on the ground will have the support of a diverse array of experts dedicated to addressing biosolids issues and delivering technology solutions. They include specialists in biosolids regulatory compliance to ensure that onsite staff who are not familiar with digester regulations, sampling, and reporting requirements have the tools needed to deliver compliant, efficient biosolids operations.

###### **_Biosolids Facilities Assessment: Key Findings_**

<!-- ¶6 -->
Our biosolids engineering team has conducted a comprehensive assessment of solids management requirements and operational challenges at the North Fulton facilities to develop a customized strategy for optimizing solids handling processes.

<!-- ¶7 -->
###### **_Big Creek WRF_**

<!-- ¶8 -->
Big Creek is under construction, with the aerobic digesters currently being built. Half of the dewatering capacity is inoperative due to a problem with one of the Schwing screw presses. Temporary dewatering is onsite. With one screw press inoperative, it appears that there is a need for temporary dewatering due to the presence of the mobile dewatering belt filter press. Many factors are probably responsible for this situation, and to anticipate every situation that could occur with the Schwing presses would not be possible; however, if screw press throughput is not adequately met with only one unit, then new plans should be considered for greater redundancy.

<!-- ¶9 -->
###### **_Johns Creek Enivronmental Campus (JCEC)_**

<!-- ¶10 -->
JCEC’s biosolids program appears to be effective in dewatering, delivery of cake to the transport trucks, and service programs for transport and disposal. One centrifuge was inoperative during the site visit, but it appeared to be in regular maintenance, and the ability to manage daily inventory of solids also appeared to be managed in a productive manner. We are planning to continue with similar biosolids operations.
