###### **_Planned Improvements to Meet Future Needs_**

<!-- ¶2 -->
The County places high priority on permit compliance with substantial safety margins, particularly for phosphorus removal, even when this approach requires additional chemical usage and expense. There are plans to expand JCEC from 15 to 20 MGD, with a design-build team currently evaluating options. For Big Creek, the future plans to expand from 32 to 38 MGD capacity would require adding a primary clarifier, a bioreactor, and more membranes. The County is also considering a biosolids disposal project at Big Creek, potentially involving drying or other processing technologies.

<!-- ¶3 -->
###### **_Key Current and Future Challenges_**

<!-- ¶4 -->
Our understanding includes insights into critical challenges faced by the facilities, which briefly note below. We provided more detailed discussion of these challenges and our approach to providing solutions under each of these topics covered later in this section.

<!-- ¶5 -->
**Staffing/Workforce:** Throughout the wastewater O&M industry, there are significant challenges in hiring and retaining qualified and certified staff, and the North Fulton facilities are not immune to these issues. Additionally, training and motivating a high performing workforce requires a culture that values these important components of a comprehensive O&M delivery approach.

<!-- ¶6 -->
**Odor Control:** The JCEC facility has experienced odor complaints from nearby residents. The plant was designed with special attention to aesthetics and odor control due to proximity to expensive homes. All plants have odor control systems, with Johns Creek and Big Creek utilizing chemical scrubbers and GAC, while Little River uses GAC alone for its headworks. Operations must meet odor control requirements.

<!-- ¶7 -->
**Biosolids Management:** Little River's biosolids are hauled to Big Creek for processing. A key challenge at Big Creek is the lack of dedicated thickening processes for primary sludge and waste activated sludge, which limits dewatering efficiency and increases disposal costs. Future plans may include a drying facility at Big Creek, where there is available real estate from demolished sand filters.

<!-- ¶8 -->
**Membrane Operations:** The North Fulton facilities face several optimization challenges including membrane performance management.

Big Creek has new Kubota flat plates, Little River's hollow fiber membranes are six years old, and JCEC's are 16 years old, which is well past expected life, requiring imminent replacement. All three plants use enhanced biological phosphorus removal and present opportunities for improvement in operations. Chemical optimization opportunities also exist across the facilities, particularly in correctly dosing sodium hypochlorite and sodium hydroxide for the chemical scrubbers at Big Creek and JCEC (saving approximately $500/day), proper Phos-Zorb application, and addressing the significant under-provision of sodium hydroxide for alkalinity at Little River.
