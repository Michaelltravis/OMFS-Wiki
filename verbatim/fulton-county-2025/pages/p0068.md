###### **_Reducing Real-Time/Peak Power Consumption_**

<!-- ¶3 -->
The cost of electrical power varies with season and time of day. The cost is highest on summer days, when regional air conditioning places the greatest demands on power supply ( **Exhibit 2-31** ). In addition, most institutional customers’ power bills reflect a peak instantaneous power draw for the year.

<!-- ¶4 -->
###### **_Maximizing Equipment Availability and Treatment Capacity_**

<!-- ¶5 -->
Most equipment systems include a standby process unit. Our approach to preventative maintenance is to strive to achieve 99.98% uptime, including standby units. As a result, each unit process usually will have more than rated capacity, allowing higher flows to be treated, and decreasing the likelihood of collection system overflows. Of course, higher than design peak flows would not be processed if effluent compliance were jeopardized.

<!-- ¶6 -->
The fine screens at JCEC and Big Creek warrant special attention, as they are the final barrier protecting the membranes from damage by debris. Center-fed band screens like those at JCEC experience gradually wear of the bottom seals until the opening at the seals exceeds the opening of the media (two millimeters). For this screen type, it is necessary to remove equipment from the channel to inspect bottom seals. Horizontal drum screens like those at Big Creek also have seals, but they are not located at the bottom and can be easily inspected at deck level. JC Solutions will carefully follow the manufacturers’ recommendations for seal inspection and maintenance.

JCEC uses inline flow equalization, which means water levels in the membrane tanks, bioreactors, and primary clarifiers rise to store peak flows. Big Creek WRF has inline flow equalization, as well as offline equalization for peak wet weather flows. Little River WRF lacks flow equalization at the plant but has some storage in the collection system.

<!-- ¶12 -->
We will use available equalization facilities to store a portion of the flow during the part of the day with the highest power rate. This approach would not be used when significant rain is forecasted because available storage must be preserved for the rain event.

<!-- ¶13 -->
Each plant has diesel-fueled engine generators as backup to utility power. Assuming air permitting and paralleling of the two power supplies (grid and generators) are feasible, JC Solutions would operate generators during periods of the highest power pricing to reduce peak power consumption as well as peak demand for the year.

###### **_Ammonia-Based Aeration Control_**

<!-- ¶15 -->
Big Creek WRF is equipped for ammonia-based aeration control (ABAC). In ABAC, aeration is controlled based on an ammonia setpoint rather than a dissolved oxygen

<!-- ¶16 -->
(DO) setpoint. This reduces aeration energy cost. In an MBR process, it is possible to take this too far. Operating at low DO concentration can allow organic foulants to reach the membranes, impacting performance.
