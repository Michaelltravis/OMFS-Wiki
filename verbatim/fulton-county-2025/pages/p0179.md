<!-- ¶1 -->
Since commencing operations in 2009, the Clovis Reuse Facility has performed well and has been able to reliably and consistently meet all State of California NPDES and water reclamation regulatory requirements. All contract performance guarantees; regulatory, environmental, residuals and odor have also been met consistently and reliability. There have been some shortfalls with the electrical guarantee which primarily is related to how the Guaranteed Maximum Electrical Utilization (GMEU) is calculated, but in reality, the facility has been run efficiently in terms of KwH/MG treated. Jacobs has also worked very closely with our client on several improvements to ensure treatment plant sustainability well into the future.

<!-- ¶2 -->
Our O&M history at the City of Clovis has resulted in various lessons learned and improvements:

<!-- ¶3 -->
- µ<sup>The MBR, membrane specific cleaning and back-pulse process has</sup> been improved. The system now utilizes MemPulse technology to clean the fibers to extend fiber life and reduce the amount of maintenance cleans that need to be completed.

<!-- ¶4 -->
- µ<sup>We have improved the SCADA format and system which as compared</sup> to the original, has more operability and control of equipment on-site as well as off-site, improved monitoring from process equipment and improve screen configurations based on operator input and feedback.

<!-- ¶5 -->
- µ<sup>Improvements have been made to the solids handling process through</sup> intensive testing of sludge quality and polymer choices that work best with an MBR/aerobically digested sludge. Through the testing we have learned the best process parameters to maintain the best sludge quality and minimize solids production.

###### SAFETY FOCUS CONTRIBUTES TO GREAT PERFORMANCE AT THE CITY OF CLOVIS

<!-- ¶12 -->
Since 2009, Jacobs and the City have been partners and have had no recordable safety injuries for over 15 years. The team has been focused on pollutant removal, laboratory QA/ QC, maintenance and asset management, serving the community, sustainability, and continuing to enhance our safety culture. Current work involves generating additional savings through implementation of Palantir for the ultraviolet system, and completing more maintenance work inhouse without contracting outside vendors.

<!-- ¶13 -->
"Our MBR process produces great quality water for Title 22 reuse. Good screening at the head of the plant is important to keep material from fouling the membranes. Properly maintaining the blowers and the aeration system is the other big piece of it, because good air scour is critical. Nobody wants to replace membranes early–they are expensive! In general, there are more instruments that need to be calibrated and maintained, and operators will need to learn some new things. But once you learn the process, the system is easy to run."

<!-- ¶14 -->
– Tony Duffy, Jacobs Plant Manager at the Clovis ST/WRF
