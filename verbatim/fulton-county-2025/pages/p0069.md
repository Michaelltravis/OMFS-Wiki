###### **_Ultraviolet Disinfection_**

<!-- ¶6 -->
The plants have permit limits for bacterial indicators (fecal coliform and E. coli). These bacteria are larger than the pore size of the MBR membranes and are completely blocked if the membranes are intact. Usually, effluent limits for bacterial indicators can be met by an MBR process without downstream disinfection. However, many virus pass through the membrane pores, and this creates a human health risk not addressed by effluent limits for bacterial indicators. It is good practice to operate the downstream disinfection process to inactivate virus and to kill any bacteria present due to membrane damage. JC Solutions will always operate the UV disinfection process at its design dose to reduce the risk of pathogens on human health.

###### **_Solids Dewatering at JCEC and Big Creek_**

<!-- ¶8 -->
JCEC and Big Creek are similar in that they produce primary sludge (from primary clarifiers) and secondary sludge (from the MBR process). Both plants lack a thickening process other than what can be achieved in the primary clarifiers or by the membranes. Primary and secondary sludges are directed to aerated holding tanks/digesters, where the solids concentration decreases somewhat as digestion occurs.

<!-- ¶9 -->
Different dewatering technologies are used at these plants—centrifuges at JCEC ( **Exhibit 2-32** ) and screw presses at Big Creek. As a minimum, these dewatering processes must be operated so cake passes a paint filter test, which is a requirement for landfill disposal. Individual landfills may have additional requirements.

###### **_Protection of Potable Water Supply_**

<!-- ¶19 -->
Drawings of Big Creek WRF show a double backflow preventer to isolate the potable water supply (1W) from plant reuse water (3W). This is not appropriate for a high hazard connection such as plant effluent. There is pressurized reuse water on the downstream side of the backflow preventers, and failure would result in contamination of the potable water supply. We will help the County determine whether a reverse pressure zone (RPZ) backflow preventer is required.
