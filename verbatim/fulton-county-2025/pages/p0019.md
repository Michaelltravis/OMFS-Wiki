###### 2.5 **PROJECT UNDERSTANDING, APPROACH TO ACHIEVING THE COUNTY’S GOALS, AND SUCCESSFUL EXPERIENCE AT OTHER LOCATIONS**

<!-- ¶2 -->
###### **PROJECT UNDERSTANDING**

<!-- ¶3 -->
Our history working with Fulton County facilities provides us with unique insights into these three sophisticated MBR plants. Our team includes individuals with direct operational experience at these facilities, giving us firsthand knowledge of both their design strengths and operational challenges. We have also drawn on our site visits and thorough review of the documentation provided during the procurement process, as well as visits in the past year to other similar Kubota MBR facilities and the Kubota research center to validate our understanding and insights. Based on those efforts, we’ve developed a comprehensive understanding of the current state of these assets, the County’s operational philosophy, and future needs. We recognize the County’s commitment to exceptional compliance performance and aesthetic considerations, particularly at the Johns Creek Environmental Campus (JCEC). Our O&M approach will balance these priorities while identifying opportunities for process optimization, particularly in the areas of chemical usage, membrane management, and biosolids handling.

###### **LITTLE RIVER PLANT**

<!-- ¶7 -->
The County is completing an upgrade to 32 MGD (max month average) for this MBR plant, designed with future expansion capability to 38 MGD. The facility utilizes a comprehensive treatment process including screening, grit removal, primary clarification, biological nutrient removal, membrane filtration, UV disinfection, and post-aeration before discharge to the Chattahoochee River.

<!-- ¶8 -->
This 15 MGD (max month average) MBR plant features aesthetically-pleasing design elements to blend with surrounding high-value residential areas. The facility utilizes Kubota membranes and includes innovative design elements such as a self-cleaning trench-style pump station. The plant discharges to the Chattahoochee River and is located in close proximity to a water intake facility.

<!-- ¶9 -->
This smaller facility rated at 2.6 MGD (annual average capacity) currently operates at approximately 0.8 MGD. The plant was converted from a conventional oxidation ditch system to an MBR process, repurposing existing structures including oxidation ditches for bioreactors and former clarifiers for post-anoxic zones. Unlike the other facilities, this plant is physically located in Cherokee County but serves Fulton County.

Major assets include:
•	 28 wastewater pump stations in the North
Fulton service area, plus associated forcemain
air release valves
•	 5 potable water pump stations (Freemanville,
Bethany, Providence, Mansell, and Henderson)
•	 Land Application System (LAS)/reuse pump
stations at both Little River and JCEC

<!-- ¶47 -->
The wastewater pump stations pump influent to the three treatment facilities: Big Creek WRF, JCEC,
and Little River WRF. They are distributed across various locations including Roswell, Alpharetta,
Milton, Atlanta, Duluth, Dunwoody, Sandy Springs, and Woodstock. The scope of services requires
operating and maintaining these pump stations on a 24-hour basis, including wet well cleaning
(minimum quarterly), inspections, and ensuring continuous operation to prevent sanitary sewer
overflows. There are both wastewater pump stations and potable water pump stations, though
work on the latter is limited to maintenance and inspection services only, not operations.
