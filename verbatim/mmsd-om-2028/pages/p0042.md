## PRICING

<!-- ¶3 -->
This narrative outlines the basis for our pricing, including key assumptions and areas where proposers’ alignment may be needed to ensure an equitable, applesto-apples comparison. MMSD’s contract structure is intentionally prescriptive: the Base Fee funds all minimum staffing, preventive maintenance (PM), safety, security, documentation, and performance standards. Under this model, accurate and responsible pricing is essential to sustaining operational reliability.

<!-- ¶4 -->
Jacobs has priced this contract as an operating commitment, with the funding necessary to sustain reliable operations and maintain a stable, high-performing workforce. Experience across large municipal O&M contracts shows that underfunded pricing could lead to deferred PM, increased corrective maintenance (CM), staffing instability, and reactive management. Our pricing is intentionally structured to prevent these risks.

<!-- ¶5 -->
###### A. Staffing and Labor Cost Basis

<!-- ¶6 -->
###### **Above and Beyond Minimum Staffing**

<!-- ¶7 -->
Jacobs’ Base Fee fully incorporates all positions required under RFP Exhibit E – Minimum Staffing Plan and RFP Exhibit B section DD.c – Internship and Co-op Program. In addition, we’ve included several “Above and Beyond” roles within the Base Fee to improve service quality, mitigate operational risks, and ensure compliance. Excluding regional and national support, this represents 9.5 additional FTEs beyond the minimum requirements. **_Section II. Staffing, Exhibit II-5_** on page II-5 provides further details on our Staffing Plan.

<!-- ¶8 -->
Jacobs’ proposal offers stabilized staffing at or above the Minimum Staffing Plan to address the chronic vacancies facing MMSD. Our approach does not rely on deferred work, informal role stacking, or unplanned overtime. Each position is resourced to deliver the defined scope, ensuring stable operations, predictable performance, and a sustainable workforce model from Day 1.

<!-- ¶9 -->
###### **Preventive, Corrective, and Task Order Labor**

<!-- ¶10 -->
- PM labor is fully included in the Base Fee and was developed bottom-up using asset counts, PM schedules, and job planning expectations described in **_Section IV.A.3 Maintenance Approach_** .

<!-- ¶11 -->
- CM labor was estimated at approximately 57,000 hours annually (~32 FTEs), consistent with 2023–2024 CMMS history. These hours are not included in the

<!-- ¶12 -->
Base Fee and are priced in accordance with the CM fee structure. Given deferred maintenance concerns, we believe some “catchup work” may initially be required.

<!-- ¶13 -->
- Approximately 4.5 FTEs are allocated to task order completion (sewer cleaning, CCTV work, etc.). Sewer cleaning and televising on pipes with a diameter greater than 48” will be subcontracted, consistent with current practice. Accordingly, these are not included in the Base Fee and are separate from the 32 FTEs shown for CM.

<!-- ¶14 -->
###### **Wage Rates and Overtime**

<!-- ¶15 -->
- Represented labor wage rates are adjusted for cost-of-living increases based on current collective bargaining agreement rates. Non-represented and supervisory wage rates were informed by market benchmarking, comparable contracts, and Jacobs’ actual cost experience to ensure we can attract and retain quality staff.

<!-- ¶16 -->
- Overtime required to meet Base Fee and PM obligations is included in the Base Fee. Overtime associated with CM activities is assumed to be reimbursed at applicable rates, when approved by MMSD.

<!-- ¶17 -->
###### **Electricity and Onsite Generation**

<!-- ¶18 -->
Jacobs’ pricing reflects a clear expectation of improved power generation system reliability relative to 2024 actuals, enabled by disciplined asset management and rigorous maintenance execution.

<!-- ¶19 -->
- At JIWRF, our estimate assumes an average annual turbine output of approximately 9 MW, a level achieved as recently as 2022 but materially higher than 2024 performance.

<!-- ¶20 -->
- At SSWRF, pricing reflects improved cogeneration availability informed by historical trends and targeted reliability practices.

<!-- ¶21 -->
These assumptions are supported by PM and reliability management practices described elsewhere in our proposal. MMSD retains full visibility into system performance through existing governance, reporting, review/approval of applicable CM work, and audit mechanisms.

<!-- ¶22 -->
###### B. Natural Gas

<!-- ¶23 -->
Natural gas quantities were modeled by end use, including turbine fuel, dryer fuel demand, and balance-of-plant consumption. Dryer gas assumptions reflect heat recovery credits associated with turbine operation.
