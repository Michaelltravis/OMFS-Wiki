###### **3.8. Staffing, Coverage, and Special Maintenance Functions**

<!-- ¶3 -->
###### **3.9. Capital Project and Asset Life-Cycle Integration**

<!-- ¶4 -->
We recognize that the Minimum Staffing Plan in RFP Exhibit E is structured to ensure the vast majority of PM and CM is self-performed. Our staffing approach, detailed in **_Section II – Statement on Staffing, Training, and Corporate Culture_** , is to not just fill positions—we’ll staff to outcomes with a Planner/Scheduler model, CMMS champions, and cross-trained crews for 24/7 reliability.

<!-- ¶5 -->
We’ll structure shifts/hours for complete coverage, including capital project support, routine and special requests, wet-weather events, and emergencies. Because the CBA currently directs maintenance shifts in an 8-hour day, Monday through Friday structure, we have followed that structure in our proposed shift schedule.

<!-- ¶6 -->
In addition to this typical shift schedule, we’ll designate staff who will be on standby/on-call to address emergency, wet weather, and related situations to ensure complete coverage as needed. Additionally, we recognize that implementing a shift schedule with 7-days-per-week scheduling may be of interest to MMSD, and we’ll explore this option with MMSD and the unions if appropriate.

<!-- ¶7 -->
Our approach is a core of self-performing maintenance crews, supported by a dedicated Planner/Scheduler team. Industry baseline is roughly one planner per 20 craft; with NEXGEN and disciplined routines we intend to trend toward a 1:30–1:40 ratio as the system stabilizes. Each crew will designate a CMMS Champion responsible for field data quality and photo evidence. Separate WRF and Conveyance teams will coordinate through weekly planning meetings to balance resources and backlog.

<!-- ¶8 -->
We’ll track housekeeping, grounds, coatings, tunnel cleaning, and fleet as preventive tasks in the CMMS.  Jacobs’ good-housekeeping standards will be embedded in job plans. Well use subcontractors (e.g., specialty deep-cleaning/ coatings) when it is clearly more efficient, but plan, inspect, and close out subcontractor work through the same workflow and governance.

<!-- ¶9 -->
Jacobs’ data driven CIP approach leverages asset data collected during maintenance activities to optimize performance, reduce risk and ensure asset reliability. MMSD’s manages hundreds of asset types across its diverse facilities with various operating environments. By developing asset-type specific likelihood of failure factors for the various operating conditions, Jacobs can quantify risk reduction. We’ll apply proven methods, including cost-benefit analysis, multicriteria evaluation and risk-based planning, to balance performance, risk and cost effectively within a CIP.

<!-- ¶10 -->
A deep understanding of asset level data and its system-wide impacts lead to development of projects that deliver measurable value. With MMSD’s CIP exceeding $2B, as shown in **Exhibit IV-47** , there are many competing priorities and ongoing projects. It’s critical to maintain visibility into current investments and future planned projects to ensure strategic alignment and efficient resource allocation.

###### **3.9.1. Integrated Approach to CIP Planning**

<!-- ¶14 -->
Jacobs’ CIP process is an integral part of our asset management program, focusing on maximizing asset life through strategic maintenance, rehabilitation, and renewal. The goal of our CIP process is to drive effective, defensible decisionmaking. We use methodologies such as benefit–cost analysis, multi-criteria analysis, and risk-based decision-making to balance performance, risk, and cost, delivering optimal outcomes in terms of community satisfaction, asset reliability, and financial sustainability.
