###### 7. SUSTAINABLE APPROACH TO OPERATIONS

<!-- ¶2 -->
Jacobs is committed to delivering solutions that make sustainability a core operating discipline—not an aspiration. Under our leadership, sustainable performance becomes measurable, repeatable, and embedded in daily decisionmaking. We’ll establish clear sustainability KPIs such as energy and chemical use per million gallons treated, energy and chemical use per ton of solids processed, MMBtu per ton of Milorganite®, and natural gas MMBtu per ton of Milorganite®, along with greenhouse gas (GHG) intensity and renewable energy contribution. Combining innovation, collaboration, and disciplined execution, we will help MMSD advance its 2035 Vision and position MMSD as a national model for climate-smart, high-performance wastewater operations.

<!-- ¶3 -->
###### **7.1. Energy Optimization and Decarbonization**

<!-- ¶4 -->
As discussed earlier, Jacobs has identified significant opportunities for chemical and power optimization at MMSD’s facilities that will **reduce operating costs and greenhouse gas emissions** . Using advanced process modeling, SCADA analytics, and predictive algorithms, Jacobs will optimize aeration, pumping, and solids handling systems. Based on results at comparable large utilities, these measures have achieved **power reductions of 15–25 percent** , with Scope 2 and Scope 3 greenhouse gas reductions occurring proportionally as power and chemical use declines.

<!-- ¶5 -->
Jacobs will update MMSD’s greenhouse gas inventory and identify priority **emissions-reduction strategies** . Additional Scope 1 and Scope 3 reductions will be evaluated through targeted assessment of resource recovery, fugitive methane, flaring reduction opportunities, process-related N₂O, and other optimization

<!-- ¶6 -->
measures. Industry-leading tools will be used to quantify emissions and track progress in support of MMSD’s long-term decarbonization objectives.

<!-- ¶7 -->
Jacobs also brings extensive experience optimizing UV disinfection systems to balance treatment performance, energy demand, and lifecycle cost. As MMSD expands UV use, Jacobs will collaborate with MMSD to manage increased electrical demand through process optimization and alignment with MMSD’s goals for expanding on-site solar generation at SSWRF, supporting progress toward energy self-sufficiency and climate-smart operations.

<!-- ¶8 -->
###### **7.2. Resource Recovery and Biosolids Optimization**

<!-- ¶9 -->
Jacobs will enhance MMSD’s longstanding leadership in sustainable resource recovery by **improving the efficiency of biosolids, energy, and reuse operations** . We’ll optimize digester performance and gas production, increase the efficiency of dewatering and drying systems, and reduce fossil fuel demand in Milorganite® production. Working collaboratively with MMSD, we’ll evaluate opportunities to divert high-energy side streams, such as primary scum and high-strength organic waste, into the digesters to boost renewable gas generation and advance **resource recovery** .

<!-- ¶10 -->
###### **7.3. Green Infrastructure Integration and Climate Resilience.**

<!-- ¶11 -->
Jacobs will collaborate to support MMSD in meeting its green infrastructure detention capacity goals and adapt to increasing climate variability We will ensure that treatment and conveyance assets continue to operate sustainably and reliably under changing climate conditions protecting public health, safeguarding water resources, and strengthening the region’s long-term resilience.
