###### _Systemwide Integration for Optimized Operations_

<!-- ¶6 -->
Our operating approach for the WRFs’ liquid treatment trains focuses on disciplined and data-driven process control across MMSD’s entire conveyance and treatment system. We’ll coordinate flow management in the ISS and tunnel with real-time plant hydraulics to maximize throughput, prevent overloads, and achieve zero CSOs by 2035. Pump station operations will align dynamically with conveyance conditions. We’ll optimize clarifier and aeration processes in tandem to balance biological loading and solids management between JIWRF and SSWRF.

<!-- ¶7 -->
A **centralized digital operations dashboard** integrating SCADA, CMMS, and process analytics will make hydraulics, energy use, aeration performance, and effluent quality visible to operators. This data-informed platform will support stable operations, early risk detection, and proactive decision-making:

<!-- ¶8 -->
- **Preliminary and Primary Treatment.** Monitoring screens, grit removal, and clarifier performance will prevent headloss and ensure efficient solids capture. We’ll optimize primary sludge withdrawal rates to balance downstream biological and solids loading.

<!-- ¶9 -->
- **Secondary (Biological) Treatment.** Jacobs will evaluate, in collaboration with MMSD, implementation of advanced DO control with real-time ammonia/nitrate monitoring and model-predictive control of aeration systems to reduce energy use while ensuring compliance. Aeration and RAS flows will be dynamically adjusted based on real-time loadings to maintain stable mixed liquor suspended solids (MLSS) concentrations, prevent secondary clarifier stress, and support responsive wet weather operations.

<!-- ¶10 -->
- **Tertiary and Disinfection.** To ensure reliable pathogen control and readiness for future _E. coli_ permit requirements, we’ll maintain automated chlorination and dechlorination systems with feedback loops tied to flow and chlorine residuals using machine learning, in combination with MMSD’s new UV system. These automated systems will still require human operator review and approval.

###### _Specific Optimization Opportunities at SSWRF and JIWRF_

<!-- ¶15 -->
We’ll address two high-priority operational challenges at SSWRF and JIWRF that highlight the complexity and importance of optimizing liquid treatment processes in collaboration with MMSD to **ensure reliable performance, regulatory compliance, and cost efficiency** . At SSWRF, persistent settleability issues that have resulted in effluent violations call for solutions such as primary clarifier stabilization, selector zone optimization, and enhanced process control. At JIWRF, there’s opportunity for significant gains by improving aeration efficiency, refining channel mixing, and applying intelligent process control. Together, these approaches underscore our commitment to leveraging advanced technologies and operational expertise to deliver robust, sustainable solutions for MMSD.

<!-- ¶16 -->
**Addressing SSWRF Settleability Issues.** Settleability at SSWRF has been an ongoing challenge for MMSD. Jacobs commends MMSD on upgrading the diffuser system and including an optional unaerated zone to select out filamentous organisms. Jacobs will partner with MMSD to maximize the benefit of these upgrades while pursuing additional targeted improvements, including those presented in **Exhibit IV-17** .

<!-- ¶17 -->
As stated in the diffuser upgrade design, the new selector zone alone cannot guarantee a sufficient reduction in sludge volume index (SVI) to maintain treatment and prevent clarifier overloading. Beyond optimizing existing and planned components, there are opportunities at SSWRF for low-cost improvements and process intensification with short payback periods that we’ll discuss with MMSD. **Jacobs brings proven technologies and experience to help unlock these gains, including the following examples:**

<!-- ¶18 -->
- **Hydrocyclones** . Hydrocyclones improve sludge settleability and SVI by selectively wasting lighter flocculant sludge while retaining denser granules and functional organisms (phosphorus accumulating organisms, amonia-oxidizing bacteria, nitrite-oxidizing bacteria) that stabilize biological processes. This technology is widely adopted, compatible with ongoing aeration upgrades, and requires minimal infrastructure (e.g., upgrade waste activated sludge [WAS] pumping facilities). Jacobs has implemented hydrocyclones globally with strong results.
