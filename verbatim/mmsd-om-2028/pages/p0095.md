**3.2.4. Outcomes of an Integrated Reliability-focused Maintenance Program** Our integrated, reliability-focused maintenance program will align planning, execution, and reliability analysis into a single, transparent process that drives better decisions, reduces risk, and supports the SAMP, capital planning, and improved long-term reliability. Key items for success include:

<!-- ¶3 -->
- **Connecting Data Across Systems:** Integrating linear asset GIS and inspection history so tunnel and collection system risks are visible alongside plant and vertical process assets.

<!-- ¶4 -->
- **Standardizing Asset Information:** Enforcing naming conventions, hierarchy standards, and required fields in NEXGEN to ensure consistency and accuracy.

<!-- ¶5 -->
- **Leveraging Operational Insights:** Mapping SCADA tags to assets to capture runtime, starts, and available condition indicators such as vibration and temperature.

<!-- ¶6 -->
- **Defining Evidence Standards:** Establishing clear photo documentation and meter point requirements for each asset class to validate work quality and compliance.

<!-- ¶7 -->
- **Partnering for Success:** Working closely with MMSD as a key partner to ensure alignment, transparency, and shared accountability across all reliability initiatives.

<!-- ¶8 -->
- **Backlog Discipline and Transparency:** Managing CM/PM backlog as a leading risk indicator—using NEXGEN and dashboards to track and report aging, priority, and resourcing—so critical work is executed before it becomes deferred maintenance that’s not completed until the equipment fails.

###### **3.3. Reliability Enhancement Plans: Improving Equipment Uptime at JIWRF and SSWRF**

<!-- ¶10 -->
Based on our review of MMSD’s O&M manuals and the RFP and scope requirements, Jacobs’ maintenance team has already developed initial WRF-specific reliability enhancement plans focused on the equipment and systems that most directly drive uptime at JIWRF and SSWRF. These plans apply the same integrated maintenance backbone described above (standardized asset data, evidence-based work execution, and reliability analytics) to deliver measurable increases in availability, fewer repeat failures, and stronger operational stability. The result is enhanced reliability from more predictable performance and fewer unplanned disruptions caused by nuisance alarms and unexpected equipment shutdowns. At the same time, it will lead to better prioritization of maintenance labor and spares, and clearer performance visibility for MMSD.

**3.3.1. JIWRF Cogeneration Reliability and O&M Enhancement Plan** We can increase equipment availability by reducing nuisance alarms, preventing unplanned equipment shutdowns, and converting priority PM work to condition-driven actions across the fuel gas conditioning and compression system, cooling water, fuel quality, turbine operating envelope, and waste heat systems. As illustrated in **Exhibit IV-46** , our plan focuses on three practical levers: (1) make data actionable, (2) make alarms meaningful, and (3) prevent repeat shutdowns through disciplined root cause analysis and closure.

<!-- ¶14 -->
**Actionable reliability data flow:** We’ll connect process signals and equipment condition indicators into a consistent flow from the control system and historian into reliability analytics, then into the CMMS and dashboards, so abnormal conditions trigger the right response work order, and follow-through (not just alarms).

<!-- ¶15 -->
**Unified alarm strategy and alarm hygiene:** We’ll tier alarms (information, warning, critical, and safety shutdown) with clear expectations for response and escalation. To do that, we’ll evaluate and optimize the alarm system so operators only see alarms that matter, without weakening the safety and equipment-protection shutdowns that are there to prevent damage or unsafe conditions.
