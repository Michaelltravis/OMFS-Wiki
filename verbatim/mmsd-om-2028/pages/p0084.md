###### **2.6. Monitoring, Tracking, and Reporting Operational Parameters**

<!-- ¶5 -->
**Monitoring,** Jacobs will deliver a comprehensive, data-driven **tracking and** framework for monitoring, tracking, and reporting **reporting of** operational parameters across MMSD’s wastewater **operational** system. Our approach focuses on transparency, **parameters** actionable insights, and continuous improvement through validated, real-time data and analytics.

<!-- ¶6 -->
The system will continuously refine dosing strategies for hypochlorite, ferric chloride, polymer, bisulfite, and other key chemicals to maintain process stability and minimize waste. Monthly reports will document usage, cost, and correlation to process conditions, with analytics identifying opportunities for chemical-use reductions of 10 to 30% without affecting permit compliance.

<!-- ¶7 -->
###### **2.6.3. Tunnel System Operations**

<!-- ¶8 -->
In partnership with MMSD, Jacobs will align monitoring with the **Standards of Performance** in Exhibit C and jointly establish KPIs for shared-cost items such as energy, chemicals, and biosolids. Using data from **SCADA, CMMS, Hach WIMS, NEXGEN, and financial systems, Jacobs will generate unified dashboards accessible to both MMSD and Jacobs** . These dashboards provide a single, defensible view of our performance, linking process control, cost management, and compliance.

<!-- ¶9 -->
MMSD’s ISS plays a vital role in controlling CSOs and maintaining system reliability. **Jacobs will integrate ISS performance parameters into the broader operational monitoring framework.** The system will continuously monitor tunnel water levels, pump station flows, and gate positions through **SCADA integration** . Predictive modeling will assess **hydraulic performance and storage utilization** , using weather forecast data to optimize system operations during wet-weather events. Real-time tracking of energy use and runtime for tunnel dewatering pumps will provide visibility into one of the largest contributors to shared power costs. After each event, Jacobs will prepare post-event reports summarizing captured volume, pump-out rate, overflow prevention, and energy utilization. Tunnel operations data will also feed into the **digital twin model** to evaluate alternative control strategies and support long-term optimization. This integrated approach will enable coordinated decision-making across treatment plants, conveyance, and storage, maximizing CSO capture efficiency while minimizing cost and energy use.

<!-- ¶10 -->
Jacobs will configure its Intelligent O&M platform to monitor all Exhibit C parameters, including effluent quality, solids handling, and conveyance reliability. Real-time dashboards will display validated data from all source systems, while alarms and KPI exceptions will automatically generate corrective-action logs. Monthly joint reviews will evaluate efficiency, reliability, and compliance trends, and all sampling and laboratory data will maintain full traceability for permit documentation.

<!-- ¶11 -->
###### **2.6.1. Integrated Performance Management Framework**

<!-- ¶12 -->
Jacobs’ performance monitoring program unifies **process, maintenance, and cost data within a single analytics framework** . The **Intelligent O&M** platform, successfully deployed at systems such as in Jackson, MS, and Wilmington, DE, provides continuous data validation, analytics, and performance visualization through automated workflows and machine learning. This framework supports compliance tracking, performance measurement, and fulfillment of the Annual Settlement requirements in Article 61 of the Service Agreement.

<!-- ¶13 -->
###### **2.6.2. Chemical Usage Monitoring and Control**

<!-- ¶14 -->
Jacobs will track **chemical feed and consumption** to support efficient operations and accurate cost allocation. Automated metering, CMMS integration, and the Intelligent O&M chemical optimization module will record real-time dosing rates, flow-proportional usage, and cumulative consumption by process and day. Deliveries and inventory will be reconciled against meter data and purchase records.

<!-- ¶15 -->
###### **2.6.4. Milorganite Production and Biosolids Operations**

<!-- ¶16 -->
Jacobs will apply the same level of precision and transparency to biosolids and Milorganite production as to liquid treatment operations. Monitoring will include **solids feed rates, dewatering efficiency, polymer use, dryer energy demand, and production output** by shift and day. We’ll use dryer and furnace data already integrated into SCADA for continuous monitoring of temperature, airflow, emissions controls, and product quality.

<!-- ¶17 -->
We’ll routinely review product temperature trends monitored and archived for **EPA 503 compliance** to confirm compliance while identifying opportunities to **improve energy efficiency and operational consistency** . Monthly reports will summarize production, energy use, chemical inputs, and product output, supporting reconciliation, planning, and sustainability objectives. We provide similar reporting for JXN Water, as described in **Exhibit IV-36** .
