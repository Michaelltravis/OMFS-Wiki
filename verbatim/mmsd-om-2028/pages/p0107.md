###### IV. APPROACH

<!-- ¶6 -->
Jacobs will manage MMSD’s access control and surveillance infrastructure, including periodic, client-approved access audits to verify compliance. Surveillance systems at JIWRF, SSWRF, and the Thirteenth Street Garage will be centrally monitored using the Genetec platform at a location mutually determined with MMSD leadership. Jacobs will perform PM, routine functionality checks, and coordinate closely with MMSD IT to ensure timely resolution of outages. Key and lock control will be centrally managed using controlled issuance and inventory tracking to maintain accountability and alignment with MMSD procedures.

<!-- ¶7 -->
###### **4.2.4. Incident Reporting and Response**

<!-- ¶8 -->
Jacobs will maintain written, MMSD-approved Site Security Procedures consistent with Exhibit M, defining responsibilities, escalation paths, and required staff training. These procedures will align with MMSD’s timelines for reporting security events including, but not limited to unknown intruder or trespasser, theft, vandalism, drone incursion and facility breaches to ensure a coordinated rapid response and action through MMSD’s Control Room or Process Monitoring Room (PMR); when necessary, relevant law enforcement agency will be contacted along with operations management.

<!-- ¶9 -->
Jacobs recognizes that MMSD’s security responsibilities extend beyond treatment facilities to include entry gate access control, pump stations, conveyance assets, and remote monitoring locations. To support these requirements, Jacobs can integrate alarms from MMSD’s SCADA systems into the security platform to provide redundant notification of critical events. This approach enables resilient, continuous monitoring of security conditions through the security control center in parallel with MMSD’s Operations Center, providing a dual-layered approach to protection.

<!-- ¶10 -->
All security and emergency events will follow the same notification hierarchy through MMSD’s PMR to maintain unified coordination across intruder, chemical, fire, or weather-related incidents through a documented security plan.

<!-- ¶11 -->
###### **4.2.5. Active-Shooter and Workplace-Violence Preparedness**

<!-- ¶12 -->
In accordance with Exhibit M and MMSD’s Security Procedures, Jacobs will incorporate workplace-violence and active-shooter preparedness into its Safety and Security Plan. All employees will receive awareness training during onboarding and annual refreshers covering recognition of warning signs, communication protocols, and protective actions. Jacobs’ Security and Safety Manager will coordinate onsite response, assist law enforcement, and ensure incident reporting and investigation meet required timelines. Jacobs has a robust active shooter and workplace violence prevention program that is based on recognition of human behaviors that are focused on grievance interruption. The information is based on the FBI publication _"Making Prevention a Reality, Identifying, Assessing and Managing the Threat of Target Attacks.”_

<!-- ¶13 -->
###### **4.2.3. Integrated Prevention and Awareness**

<!-- ¶14 -->
Security awareness is built into every employee’s onboarding and annual training. Jacobs’ BeyondZero® culture reinforces the principle that everyone shares responsibility for maintaining a safe and secure workplace. Staff will be trained to recognize suspicious activity, protect MMSD property and data, and follow established notification protocols. In addition, Jacobs has completed more than 100 Risk and Resilience Assessments using the ANSI J-100 methodology. This expertise enables Jacobs to anticipate, evaluate, and respond effectively to potential threats and vulnerabilities.
