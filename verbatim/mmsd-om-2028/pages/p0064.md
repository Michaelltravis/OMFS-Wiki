**Maximize the share of digested biosolids sent to the Milorganite process— beyond the current ~30–35% limit—while maintaining nitrogen content and product quality.** To increase digested biosolids feed, we first need to manage the factors that currently limit how much can be blended into the total Milorganite feed. The key risks are changes in pellet quality, higher chaff and dust production, and a drop in Milorganite nitrogen content. To address these issues, we propose a pilot study to evaluate replacing the existing dryer screw mixers with a more effective pug mill mixer equipped with VFD drives. We understand MMSD will soon study adding new dryers at SSWRF, and those new dryers may include improved cake mixing. However, it may be more cost-effective to upgrade mixing in the existing D&D facility now, rather than waiting for construction of a new SSWRF dryer facility.

<!-- ¶3 -->
**Maximize the amount of landfill gas used in the JIWRF turbines and dryers while maximizing the power generation in the turbines.** Historical data shows that the generator auxiliary equipment has contributed to most of the down time for the Solar Turbines. Jacobs will work with MMSD during the transition and CMMS programming to ensure the proper PM/PdM is assigned to all the generator system assets. This will include identifying spare parts and ensuring they are onsite to minimize any power generation down time. Maximizing turbine availability will reduce natural gas use and purchased power while increasing the amount of waste heat available for drying and building heat. In the future, when systems such as auxiliary equipment fail, Jacobs will deploy our SMEs to analyze failures, implement corrective actions, and prevent repeat issues.

<!-- ¶4 -->
**Increasing the concentration of dryer cake feed solids to reduce energy needed for drying.** The energy required to produce Milorganite could be more than half of the energy used by MMSD. We estimate that for every 1% increase in cake solids, $350K/year in purchased natural gas can be saved. In addition, increasing the cake solids would potentially increase dryer capacity, resulting in more available dryers and more reliable Milorganite production.

<!-- ¶5 -->
MMSD, along with Jacobs team member Marty Dierker, is currently running tests on the feasibility of increasing cake dryness. Thickening and dewatering chemicals; thickened and dewatered sludge concentrations; increased chaff and dust production; pellet shape and hardness; and dryer throughput could each adversely affect cake solids and Milorganite quality. Adding a more efficient pugmill mixer, as discussed above, could help address some of these issues, but must be carefully evaluated and tested to ensure Milorganite quality is not compromised.

Our team members **David Porter** and **Dave Oerke** specialize in optimizing belt filter presses and dewatering performance for facilities across the U.S. Jacobs’ experience optimizing belt filter press performance and reducing polymer/ferric dose at several similar facilities can also be applied to MMSD operations. We suggest MMSD explore using an online analyzer on the feed sludge line of the common belt filter press, which would provide **more accurate pacing of polymer to mass, rather than flow and polymer dose, and reduce required operator attention. In addition, we recommend continuing MMSD’s work with vendors to determine the optimum type and dose of polymer.**

**Increasing digester gas production via increased high strength waste.** MMSD currently digests high strength waste from Miller-Coors Brewery, airport de-icing fluid, and food waste from a partnership with Insinkerator. One way to increase digester gas production is to increase the amount of high strength waste used. We understand that MMSD plans to increase the amount of storage for high strength waste and seeks additional high strength waste sources. **As an added value, Jacobs could partner with MMSD and Marquette University (Marquette) to identify and test potential sources of high strength waste.** We performed a similar evaluation with Marquette and the Green Bay Metropolitan Sewerage District and identified several high strength waste sources that boosted digester gas production. The survey for Green Bay identified industries closer to Milwaukee than Green Bay that Green Bay did not use and could be available to MMSD.
