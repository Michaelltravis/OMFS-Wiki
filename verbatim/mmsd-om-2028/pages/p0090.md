###### 3. APPROACH TO PM AND CM

<!-- ¶7 -->
###### **3.1. Jacobs Understands and Supports MMSD’s Maintenance Objectives and Challenges**

<!-- ¶8 -->
Although facilities appear to be in good physical condition, with some noticeable recent housekeeping, our review suggests that maintenance activities aren’t well-documented or visible to MMSD, which limits data and analysis to inform asset management decisions. Given the age and complexity of legacy and newer infrastructure, MMSD needs more than confirmation that assets run. Rather, MMSD needs an integrated and accessible asset registry complete with key attributes, maintenance records, condition data, criticality scores, and risk scores to support long-term, risk-based investment decisions.

**MMSD has asked for a different model—one that delivers proof of work and verifiable outcomes through clear, auditable documentation.** We recognize the Draft Service Agreement’s intent: execute needed work and support authorization and payment with CMMS-based records and supporting documentation. This aligns with how Jacobs runs maintenance programs every day: closed-loop planning, execution, and reporting so MMSD has decision-grade visibility into what was done, why it was needed, and what risk it mitigated. This model advances the One Water/One Vision and avoids deferred maintenance practices that can introduce operational risk.

<!-- ¶18 -->
Our approach starts with safety and regulatory compliance—they set the maintenance standard, priorities, and urgency. We won’t “wait for thresholds” or visible deterioration to justify preventive work; we’ll plan and execute PM/PdM as a disciplined, front-end investment to prevent failures, avoid unplanned outages, and reduce the likelihood of compliance-impacting events. We’ll document every work order with field evidence (photos, parts usage, meter readings), supervisor verification, and timely closeout. Real-time dashboards—shared with MMSD—to show PM compliance, CM backlog, labor, cost, and asset health, so no work goes unverified and no trend goes unseen. In addition, our team is experienced in tracking and reporting data in NEXGEN and supporting CMMS configuration improvements, both of which will be core to the maintenance program. Finally, we’ll proactively remove a known execution barrier raised by labor—parts availability—by strengthening material readiness (critical spares, min/max levels, kitting/staging, and tighter integration between planning and storeroom/ procurement) so planned maintenance does not slip into reactive work.
