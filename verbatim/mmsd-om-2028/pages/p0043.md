III. PRICING

<!-- ¶2 -->
###### C. Chemicals and Process Consumables

<!-- ¶3 -->
- Chemical quantities were developed from historical actual consumption.

<!-- ¶4 -->
- SSWRF pricing assumes continued ferric chloride addition and RAS chlorination consistent with 2024 operations until aeration upgrades materially change treatment dynamics. JIWRF pricing assumes continued dual conditioning with ferric and polymer to maintain Milorganite product specifications.

<!-- ¶5 -->
- Historically, SSWRF has added both _ferric_ chloride and _ferrous_ chloride to the primary clarifiers; ferrous being used as a lower cost ferric alternative.  We’ve normalized historic chemical flows around total Iron (Fe) added and assumed ferric as the sole coagulant for CEPT - this is a $300,000 per year premium over historic practices (after the 60/40 split).

<!-- ¶6 -->
- Our proposal also includes provision for a chemically enhanced primary treatment (CEPT) polymer companion, guided by our SMEs. This provision adds ~$400,000 to our chemicals price after the 60/40 cost sharing split.

<!-- ¶7 -->
- We expect to re-evaluate both iron salt and CEPT polymer addition and the return activated sludge chlorination practices after completion of the ongoing aeration system upgrade.

<!-- ¶8 -->
- Unit pricing reflects the favorable pricing we secure through our strategic sourcing group, with quantities reflecting anticipated reductions achieved through intelligent O&M optimization and our investment in dual-feed coagulant functionality at SSWRF. These efficiencies are incorporated while fully maintaining regulatory and product compliance.

<!-- ¶9 -->
###### D. Maintenance Economics and Cost Stability

<!-- ¶10 -->
In asset-intensive O&M contracts, underfunding PM and supporting functions quickly leads to increased CM volume, added administrative burden, and increased governance friction. Jacobs’ Base Fee is intentionally structured to fund PM and its enabling functions at a level required to sustain performance and avoid cost migration into CM and administrative rework. Our pricing reflects the reality that PM, planning, documentation, and material readiness are not overhead. Rather, they are the mechanisms that allow MMSD’s performance standards to be met consistently over the life of the contract.

<!-- ¶11 -->
###### E. Other Base Fee and Contractual Cost Elements

<!-- ¶12 -->
Non-WRF utilities (potable water and fuel oil) were estimated based on historical actuals. All remaining Base Fee cost categories are fully included, including solid waste, insurance, fleet maintenance, PPE, security subcontractor support, janitorial services, permit fees, laboratory costs, IT support, and general operating supplies. No material Base Fee scope elements have been excluded or deferred.

<!-- ¶13 -->
Markup percentages for allowable reimbursable costs, inclusive of overhead and profit components are provided in the Price Proposal Worksheet in accordance with the RFP.

<!-- ¶14 -->
Our financial proposition to MMSD anticipates payment for the pre-term costs, as reflected in the associated price form.

<!-- ¶15 -->
Pricing assumptions do not embed contingency for Differing Conditions, which are addressed through the contractual adjustment mechanism.

<!-- ¶16 -->
###### F. Potential Saving Opportunities

<!-- ¶17 -->
From a pricing perspective, there are several areas that could offer meaningful cost-saving opportunities for MMSD, should MMSD wish to pursue them. Examples include:

<!-- ¶18 -->
- **Insurance requirements –** Aligning the insurance language with Jacobs’ corporate policy **would reduce the annual cost by approximately $1,143,500**

<!-- ¶19 -->
- **Inflationary adjustment index –** Amending the index to reflect our proposed modification **would generate an additional $682,000 in annual savings**

<!-- ¶20 -->
- **Staffing levels –** Our price includes **9.5 Above‑and‑Beyond FTEs** . These positions can be scaled **if MMSD prefers a lower‑cost option (up to $1,300,000/year)**

<!-- ¶21 -->
Jacobs remains fully flexible and open to discussing these items—or any other mechanisms MMSD may wish to consider—to arrive at a pricing structure that best supports MMSD’s priorities.
