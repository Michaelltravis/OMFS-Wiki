# Reporting and Communication

Transparency is the foundation of our partnership with [CLIENT], a regional water utility trust operating a multi-plant wastewater system. From day one, we'll establish a communication framework designed around a simple principle: no surprises. You'll know what's happening at your facilities, why it's happening, and what we're doing about it before you have to ask.

Our communication approach connects Jacobs leadership to the appropriate [CLIENT] counterparts at every level — what we call a "zippering" model (**Exhibit 1-2**). Project Director **Steve** and Executive Sponsor **Kevin Dahl** will engage directly with [CLIENT]'s General Manager and [CLIENT] leadership. Project Manager **David** will work closely with the General Manager's designee. Plant managers and functional leads will coordinate with [CLIENT]'s operational and engineering staff. **Rick Warner**, as Strategic Advisor, will provide additional senior-level support as needed.

## Structured Communication and Reporting Framework

**Exhibit 1-7** summarizes our communication and reporting commitments, aligned to [CLIENT]'s requirements in the contract schedules and the Service Agreement. **Items marked with ★ are items above and beyond what is required in the RFP and provide additional touchpoints that reflect how Jacobs values communication and our commitment to full transparency.** This table represents our proposed framework, which we will refine with [CLIENT] during the chartering process.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the "no surprises" principle statement, the four-level zippering pairing sentence pattern (executive ↔ executive, PM ↔ designee, plant managers ↔ operational staff, strategic advisor as reach-back), and the two devices that follow it — marking above-and-beyond commitments with a symbol, and framing the reporting table as a proposal to be refined jointly rather than a fixed schedule.

Pursuit-specific: the names, the client's title conventions (General Manager, designee), and the schedule citations.
