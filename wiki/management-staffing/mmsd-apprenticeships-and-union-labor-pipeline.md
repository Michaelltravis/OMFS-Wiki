# Building the Labor Pipeline with Apprenticeships and Union Partnerships

We see an immense opportunity to build apprenticeship programs customized to [CLIENT]'s needs and focused on recruiting to attract and build an industry labor pipeline. Our plans include:

- Continue to foster union partnerships we've formed to engage people from all workforce demographics.
- Help connect unions to students at the high school and trade school levels, help articulate what membership in a union could do for careers, and encourage students to consider a career path in the trades needed at [CLIENT]'s facilities.
- Partner with local area chambers of commerce and participate in trade-focused high school technical programs and job fairs.
- Expand on our internal training programs to include the [CLIENT]-custom apprenticeship model.

**Jacobs has engaged with the State of Wisconsin to build a state-approved apprenticeship program, administered through MATC, that's new in the region. With Jacobs as the primary sponsor, we envision forming a strong and lasting relationship with a community of educators and creating an apprenticeship program for long-term development of a local workforce.**

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four-part pipeline play — keep the union partnerships you have, connect unions to high school and trade school students, work the chambers and job fairs, and extend internal training into a client-custom apprenticeship model. The callout pattern (a named state authority + a named college + Jacobs as primary sponsor) is the proof device worth reproducing wherever a comparable program exists. Pursuit-specific: the state, the college, and the sponsorship claim. Pairs with [union and labor relations](mmsd-union-and-labor-relations-and-employee-transition.md) and [long-term retention and succession planning](mmsd-long-term-retention-and-succession-planning.md); the full workforce development program lives in Subsection H of the source proposal.
