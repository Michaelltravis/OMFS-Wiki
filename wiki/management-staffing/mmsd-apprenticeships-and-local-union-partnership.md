# O&M Apprenticeships and Local Union Partnership

#### 1.2. O&M Apprenticeships and Partnering with Local Unions Build the Pipeline of Talent

The region's unemployment rate is among the lowest in the country, and while this is an indicator of strong economic growth, it also limits the pool of applicants to fill vacancies. We see an immense opportunity to use apprenticeships as a solution to this challenge. We'll develop the framework for the apprenticeship program in collaboration with [CLIENT] and the unions. Toward this end, we've met frequently with leadership from the region's local unions—AFSCME, IUOE, IAM, Steamfitters, Electricians, and Painters—and through these meetings, we've gained an understanding of union capacity and membership needs to inform a relevant apprenticeship program. Modeled after apprenticeships that we've managed for other utilities, the program will integrate training, licensing, and promotion criteria, with the objective of affecting operational excellence, regulatory compliance, and long-term workforce sustainability.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the problem-to-solution move (constrained labor market → apprenticeship pipeline), the joint development commitment with client and unions, and the three-part program content — training, licensing, and promotion criteria — tied to operational excellence, regulatory compliance, and workforce sustainability.

Pursuit-specific: the labor-market statement, the named locals, and the claim of frequent pre-proposal meetings. In a non-union environment, replace the union partnership with community-college or state-registered apprenticeship sponsorship and keep the rest of the structure intact.
