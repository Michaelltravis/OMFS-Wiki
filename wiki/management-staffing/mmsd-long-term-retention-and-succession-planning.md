# Long-Term Retention and Succession Planning — Keep People, Grow People, Honor Knowledge

We'll preserve [CLIENT]'s institutional knowledge and turn today's team into tomorrow's leaders. We provide fair pay and benefits, visible career pathways, recognition for certifications and performance, and a culture of respect and inclusion for smoother labor relations and higher retention. As a result, our staff will envision a future at [CLIENT] with Jacobs. Three elements of our retention and succession plan follow.

## Integration: one team, one standard

We begin by integrating qualified incumbent staff you wish to retain alongside new hires, pairing senior staff with junior operators for skills transfer, and aligning everyone to common performance and safety expectations. Orientation reinforces our code of conduct and a "One Water, One Team, One Vision" culture. Supervisors will conduct regular check-ins to assure people feel respected, informed, and supported. We'll use the same respectful and transparent approach with labor partners that we've used with other unionized workforces, some of which are listed in **Exhibit II-8**.

## Dedicated team focused on attracting, developing, and retaining employees

Our [CLIENT]-dedicated HR Team, described earlier, focuses on attracting, developing, and retaining high performance staff. Our culture is built around our people. That means providing support at all levels so we can keep and grow our staff today and in the future. This includes:

- Attracting interns, veterans, and others to leadership.
- Building safety and well-being focus into daily work (see ***Sections II.F Safe Work Environment*** and ***II.G. Positive Culture*** for details).
- Developing staff through technical and leadership training via our O&M University, and focused career succession and mentoring.
- Providing competitive training programs and wages. We cover training and exam costs, provide promotion opportunities for operators-in-training (OIT)'s first certification, and offer certification bonuses (e.g., up to $1K per level) with higher awards for advanced licenses (e.g., up to $3K for PE/CSP). Maintenance staff are rewarded for attaining CMRT and CRL credentials that elevate their skills.

## Succession planning — a structured, visible path to advancement

Our team is structured to provide pathways to advanced positions and fill vacancies as they occur. With a national shortage of certified wastewater operators, proactive succession planning and job marketing is essential. Each department will identify critical roles and designate successors with readiness plans tied to skills, licenses, and leadership milestones. We align these plans with ***Section II.E Technical Training*** and the annual Workforce Development Plan discussed in ***H. Workforce Development Program***, so training investment is aimed at the roles [CLIENT] needs most. Tools from our Resource Planning Group — career succession planning, leadership development guides, one-on-one career counseling, project manager internship, and leadership-engaged mentor programs — give supervisors and employees a common language and playbook for growth. Elements of the plan include:

- Defined ladders from OIT to licensed Operators and into shift lead/supervisor roles.
- Analogous tracks for maintenance (mechanical, electrical, instrumentation and controls [I&C], lab, and support functions).
- Cross-training blocks that deliberately build redundancy and resilience.
- Apprenticeship integration (machinists, steamfitters, electricians, and fleet/auto mechanics).
- OIT and instrument technician-in-training roles, and internships/co-ops to sustain the pipeline.
- Avoiding disruptions from retirement by hiring and cross-training replacement staff in advance of scheduled retirements, prepping candidates for licensing, and using staff from our regional bench to backfill positions while successors onboard. Our Resource Planning Group will support mentoring, rotations, and leadership workshops to speed readiness for first-time leads and supervisors.
