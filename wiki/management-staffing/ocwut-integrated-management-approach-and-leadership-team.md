# Integrated Management Approach and Leadership Team

## An Integrated Management Approach Built for [CLIENT]'s Complexity

[CLIENT]'s wastewater system — a regional water utility trust operating four treatment plants, a major regional pump station, and a complex solids management program — requires more than routine contract operations. **It demands an integrated management approach built on transparency, accountability, and a partnership structure that keeps [CLIENT] leadership always informed and in control.**

Jacobs will bring that approach. **Drawing on extensive expertise in delivering program management refined across large-scale contracts nationwide, we'll establish a governance structure, communication cadence, and performance monitoring framework that gives [CLIENT] clear visibility into every aspect of facility operations, maintenance, compliance, and cost management** from day one and every day thereafter. This isn't a bolt-on management layer; it's how we operate. Structured accountability, proactive reporting, and collaborative decision-making are built into our management DNA.

## Leadership That Knows [CLIENT]

**Leading this effort will be Project Director Steve Carpenter — a trusted name in [CLIENT]'s water community.** Steve ran [CLIENT]'s principal water treatment plant and brings deep relationships with [CLIENT] staff built over years of local service. He'll provide management oversight, drive compliance and communication, and serve as the independent quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC) voice on the project — reporting above the day-to-day project management structure to give [CLIENT] confidence that oversight is genuine and unfiltered.

Daily operations will be led by **Project Manager David Pitocchelli, who will lead daily operations and serve as [CLIENT]'s primary point of contact, and Assistant Project Manager Alex Rodriguez, who brings firsthand knowledge of [CLIENT]'s operations from his previous role in Oklahoma.** Together with Steve and our corporate support team, we'll maintain a "zippering" communication approach, illustrated in **Exhibit 1-2**, that connects the right Jacobs leaders to the right [CLIENT] counterparts at every level of the organization.

## Proven Leaders to Build a High-Performance Culture and Elevate O&M

Steve's career centers on turning around large, complex O&M contracts — rebuilding teams, re-empowering staff, and lifting performance. He led the Jackson, Mississippi, water system transformation and the cultural reset at West Basin, California. Before that, he ran [CLIENT]'s principal water treatment plant, so he already knows your culture, your community, and how [CLIENT] operates. Steve will anchor transition, drive compliance oversight, and keep communications with [CLIENT] transparent — holding our team accountable to the outcomes that matter to you.

**David Pitocchelli, Project Manager.** David previously worked at [CLIENT]'s wastewater operations and built strong relationships with the team that are still in place today. That familiarity means a smooth transition and accelerated performance improvements. He'll lead day-to-day operations with the site-specific knowledge to move quickly and the trust already in place to make collaboration easy.

**Alex Rodriguez, Assistant Project Manager.** Returning to Oklahoma, Alex brings operational expertise from large, complex facilities across Jacobs' portfolio alongside his prior Oklahoma experience. He'll back David up day-to-day, coordinate execution across the four WWTPs, and step in as on-site deputy whenever needed.

This high-caliber leadership team brings the technical depth, cultural playbook, and local understanding to drive change and elevate [CLIENT] O&M from day one. (Exhibit graphic asset ID 153_009385.)

## Transparency You Don't Have to Chase

Our management approach is anchored in the belief that [CLIENT] shouldn't have to chase its operator for information. You'll have real-time access to performance data, structured reports delivered on schedule, and proactive communication about issues before they become problems. That's the standard we hold ourselves to and it's the standard this Management Plan describes.
