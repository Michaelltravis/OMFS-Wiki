# Asset Manager, Laboratory Manager, and CIP Construction Manager Profiles

## Amy Dembinski, CRL, Asset Manager

**Amy brings 18 years of experience in enterprise asset management, CMMS implementation, and maintenance reliability for water and wastewater operations**. Based in Tulsa, she's currently **supporting [CLIENT]'s NexGen EAM implementation**, applying best practices to strengthen maintenance efficiency, asset visibility, and decision-making for sustainable, long-term reliability. Amy develops preventive, predictive, and reliability-centered maintenance tasks from technical documents and refines them with field staff to match real operating conditions. She has startup and transition experience across multi-facility wastewater systems, lift stations, and solids-related programs. Amy supported project implementations and data migrations in Wilmington, Goodyear, Red Bluff, Baltimore, and Soquel Creek, **adding 10,000 assets to Jacobs' portfolio and training 85 new users while keeping maintenance schedules on track,** and wastewater programs in Bixby, Jackson, Vancouver, Waterbury, and San Marcos. She'll help build a more disciplined, data-driven asset management program that connects daily maintenance with long-term reliability.

*"I transform fragmented data, undocumented processes, and siloed expertise into accessible, actionable intelligence — building systems that strengthen institutional memory, improve decision making, and support long-term asset stewardship. For [CLIENT], this means creating sustainable frameworks that connect people, processes, and data while ensuring asset management practices are efficient and reliable."*

## Scott Golden, Laboratory Manager

**Scott** will lead the laboratory program that supports permit compliance, process control, and data integrity across [CLIENT] facilities. Scott brings **21 years of water and wastewater experience** in laboratory analysis, regulatory reporting, process control, and industrial pretreatment support. As **Project Manager and IPP Coordinator for the Duncan WWTP**, he oversees plant operations, prepares DMRs, and manages compliance reporting and recordkeeping. Scott served as a laboratory technician, where he performed chemical and bacteriological analysis, maintained QA/QC procedures, managed lab inventory, and updated SOPs to support accurate, defensible results. His lab, operations, and compliance experience positions him to deliver reliable data that supports both regulatory compliance and day-to-day process decisions.

*"A strong lab program supports every part of plant performance. Reliable data helps operators respond faster, managers make better decisions, and the utility stay ahead of compliance. As a lifelong Oklahoma native, I'm excited about bringing [CLIENT] a lab program built on accountability, accuracy, and local commitment."*

## Tanner Pipher, PE, CIP Construction Manager

**Tanner** will coordinate capital improvements so [CLIENT] can advance projects without disrupting operations or compliance. Based in Oklahoma, Tanner brings **8 years of water and wastewater engineering experience focused on design and construction-phase support.** His Oklahoma project experience includes **major treatment improvements in Tulsa, biosolids planning for Haikey Creek WWTP, and utility and pipeline work in Lawton.** He'll work closely with [CLIENT], designers, contractors, and operations staff to support construction sequencing, shutdowns, startup, and integration of new assets into ongoing operations.

*"I believe good construction coordination starts with understanding operations. Projects succeed when construction, shutdown planning, startup, and day-to-day plant performance all work together. [CLIENT] has major improvements ahead, and there's real value in helping those projects move forward!"*

## Reuse guidance

Universal: staffing an O&M contract with three roles most competitors leave off the org chart — a dedicated asset manager, a laboratory manager, and a CIP construction manager whose job is explicitly to keep capital work from disrupting operations or compliance. That last framing ("coordinate capital improvements so the utility can advance projects without disrupting operations or compliance") is worth lifting verbatim for any client with an active CIP.

Pursuit-specific: names, years, home city, and prior project lists. The NexGen EAM sentence is a live-pursuit claim — confirm the individual is still on that implementation, and confirm external-use approval, before restating it.
