# Retention-First Workforce Continuity Strategy for O&M Transitions

## Principle

Maintaining uninterrupted compliance and operational stability during a contract transition should be framed as a top-level priority: the mobilization plan is structured around a disciplined, phased approach specifically designed to **eliminate compliance risk, maintain operational continuity, and provide immediate stability** for the client, facility staff, and other stakeholders.

Prioritize **continuity of leadership and institutional knowledge** by retaining the existing facility and collection-system workforce to the greatest extent possible. Transition planning should begin immediately upon Notice of Award, with the primary objective of maintaining **stable day-to-day management and minimizing disruption** for client staff and the operating team.

## Retention-First Staffing Strategy

- **Workforce retention:** Offer employment to all qualified, interested incumbent staff to retain facility-specific expertise, relationships, and operating history.
- **License and certification continuity:** Maintain required licenses and certifications by confirming credential status early and supporting renewals/training to ensure uninterrupted compliance coverage.
- **Targeted staffing support:** Supplement staffing where needed (e.g., vacancies, coverage gaps, or specialized needs) with qualified resources to ensure management continuity from Day 1.

## Management Handoff and Knowledge Retention

Implement a structured leadership transition that preserves what is working today and strengthens governance over time:

- **Early leadership alignment:** The incoming [PROJECT MANAGER] meets with client leadership and current facility leadership to confirm near-term priorities, reporting expectations, decision authority, and communication cadence.
- **Role clarity and continuity:** Maintain clear lines of authority and responsibility while integrating the management team into the firm's safety, reporting, and performance systems.
- **Institutional knowledge capture:** Formalize knowledge transfer through structured interviews, job shadowing, review of existing SOPs and logs, and documentation of critical operational "tribal knowledge" (e.g., seasonal conditions, equipment quirks, alarms, vendor nuances, and historical maintenance pain points).
- **Employee-focused communications:** Conduct onboarding discussions and transition check-ins so staff understand expectations, benefits, training pathways, and how the incoming operator will support them.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four-part framework (retention-first hiring, license/certification continuity, structured leadership handoff, formalized institutional-knowledge capture) applies to any O&M transition regardless of facility size or region, and directly counters evaluator concerns about service disruption or loss of local knowledge during a contract changeover.
