# Staffing Coverage, Shift, and On-Call Structure

A staffing plan for a small-to-mid-size WWTF and collection system should ensure adequate staffing in compliance with applicable discharge permit requirements and staff certification requirements. A clear, tiered coverage structure:

- **Base Coverage:** Standard weekday coverage window (e.g., Monday through Friday, 7 a.m.-3:30 p.m., adjustable to the client's preference), with staggered start/stop coverage during construction or high-flow conditions.
- **Weekend/Holiday Coverage:** Two-person rounds daily; sampling and compliance requirements performed per the discharge permit.
- **24/7 On-Call:** One treatment-plant operator and one collections technician on rotation, with a defined phone-response commitment (e.g., ≤20-minute phone response) and site-response commitment (e.g., ≤60-minute site response), calibrated to meet the RFP's stated requirements.
- **Storm Events:** Pre-staged teams at the treatment plant and critical pump stations, with provisions for sleeping arrangements maintained to support extended events.

Framing coverage this way — a named weekday base window, explicit weekend rounds, and quantified on-call response-time commitments — gives evaluators a concrete, auditable answer rather than a general statement that "the facility will be staffed appropriately."

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four-tier structure (Base / Weekend-Holiday / 24/7 On-Call / Storm Events) and the practice of stating explicit phone- and site-response time commitments for on-call coverage.

Pursuit-specific: adjust the base coverage window to the facility's actual operating hours; recalculate response-time commitments to meet or beat whatever the specific RFP specifies; adjust storm/emergency provisions to the region's actual extreme-weather risk profile (e.g., coastal storm surge vs. inland flooding vs. wildfire-driven power outages); confirm on-call rotation depth against actual proposed FTE count.
