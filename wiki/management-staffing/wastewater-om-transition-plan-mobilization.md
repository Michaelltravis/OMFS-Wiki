# Wastewater O&M Transition Plan and Mobilization Approach

## Transition Plan with Approach and Timeline of Activities Necessary to Achieve the Commencement Date

Transitioning operations of [CLIENT]'s WWTF, collection system, pump stations, and flood control infrastructure is a critical responsibility requiring careful planning, experienced leadership, and disciplined execution. Jacobs brings proven experience successfully transitioning complex wastewater systems while **maintaining uninterrupted service** and workforce continuity. [CLIENT] must be confident that service levels, permit compliance, communication, and workforce stability will not be compromised. Our transition approach is designed to eliminate risk through disciplined planning, experienced leadership, and a structured operational handoff that establishes a culture of accountability from Day 1.

Jacobs has successfully transitioned multiple complex water and wastewater systems from previous operators. Recent examples include West Basin Municipal Water District and Jackson Water (JXN Water), where Jacobs assumed operational responsibility while maintaining uninterrupted service and workforce continuity. In both cases, utility leadership has recognized the effectiveness of Jacobs' disciplined transition approach and the positive impact on employee engagement and operational confidence.

> "Given the complexity of West Basin's operations, the transition period required extensive coordination and technical expertise. Jacobs deployed a deep bench of subject matter experts and worked collaboratively with District staff to ensure a smooth transition from the previous contract operator of more than 20 years. Overall, the transition was managed very effectively, with minimal challenges, and resulted in a smooth and successful onboarding for a system of this complexity."
> — Susanna Li, Manager of Engineering, West Basin Municipal Water District

> "In the throes of the water crisis, only Jacobs was willing to answer our calls for assistance. Since that time in December 2022, they have proven to be outstanding partners, delivering on their commitments and totally bought in to our shared mission of ensuring all Jacksonians have access to safe drinking water, all the time. Once again, Jacobs has stepped up and promised operational savings as they begin to operate our wastewater facilities. We look forward to expanding their mission, applying that same commitment to the people of Jackson with a focus on protecting public health and the environment with their wastewater expertise."
> — Ted Henifin, JXN Water Interim Third Party Manager

Transition activities will begin immediately upon award and continue through Commencement and the early months of operation. Organized into three phases — Pre-Start Transition, Initial Transition (Days 1–30), and Extended Transition (Days 31–120) — this structured approach ensures **continuity, leadership alignment, and uninterrupted regulatory compliance**. Key milestones include completion of due diligence within 30 days of award, workforce integration prior to Commencement, SCADA and alarm validation before the Commencement date, and confirmation of regulatory reporting systems prior to assumption of operations. We will coordinate with [STATE ENVIRONMENTAL AGENCY] and EPA Region 1 to confirm operator certification coverage, reporting contacts, and regulatory communication protocols prior to Commencement. Personnel integration, systems alignment, safety programs, compliance protocols, and emergency procedures will be coordinated in advance, **making the transition seamless** for regulators, residents, and the community.

Refined through numerous municipal transitions and successfully applied at comparable wastewater facilities across EPA Region 1 and the Northeast, this framework ensures Day 1 readiness, proactive risk management, and stabilized workforce culture. Our transition plan specifically **mitigates common operational risks** such as staff turnover, incomplete asset records, SCADA access limitations, and emergency response readiness. A high-level Gantt chart outlining transition activities, including the job-shadowing period, is provided in an appendix.

We encourage [CLIENT] to speak directly with Jacobs' municipal clients who have experienced our transition process firsthand. Their feedback consistently **reflects strong communication, organized mobilization, and early operational improvements** that set the foundation for a high-performing, long-term partnership.

### Workforce Continuity and Employee Engagement

The existing staff at [CLIENT]'s facilities represent essential institutional knowledge and operational continuity. Employment will be offered to qualified and interested personnel, and retention will be prioritized to **preserve system stability and protect [CLIENT]'s investment**. Our mobilization model blends local workforce continuity with regional technical depth so WWTF, collection system, pump station, and flood control operations remain fully staffed and supported from Commencement forward.

If unexpected staffing gaps arise, Jacobs' regional bench of licensed operators and maintenance professionals will provide immediate interim coverage. During transition, roles will be aligned to operational needs and individual skill assessments, and responsibilities will be clarified early to strengthen accountability and readiness.

Jacobs' transitions typically result in **strong employee engagement and retention** by providing clear leadership, defined expectations, competitive compensation, and structured training pathways that support **operational continuity and regulatory compliance**. Employee feedback from recent transitions shows a clear improvement in satisfaction after joining Jacobs, reflecting our strong onboarding, support systems, and people-focused culture: staff satisfaction rose from 3.6 to 4.1 at West Basin, California, and from 2.8 to 4.3 at Jackson, Mississippi (Exhibit 5-20).
