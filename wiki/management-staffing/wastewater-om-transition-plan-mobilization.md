# Wastewater O&M Transition Plan and Mobilization Approach

Transitioning operations of a client's WWTF, collection system, pump stations, and flood-control infrastructure is treated as a critical responsibility requiring careful planning, experienced leadership, and disciplined execution — designed to eliminate risk through a structured operational handoff that establishes a culture of accountability from day one.

**Phased structure.** Transition activities begin immediately upon award and continue through commencement and the early months of operation, organized into three phases: **Pre-Start Transition**, **Initial Transition (Days 1–30)**, and **Extended Transition (Days 31–120)**. This structure ensures continuity, leadership alignment, and uninterrupted regulatory compliance. Key milestones typically include completion of due diligence within 30 days of award, workforce integration prior to commencement, SCADA and alarm validation before the operational start date, and confirmation of regulatory reporting systems prior to assumption of operations. Coordination with state/regional environmental regulators confirms operator certification coverage, reporting contacts, and regulatory communication protocols prior to commencement.

**Immediate mobilization and governance.** Upon award, a Transition Leadership Team is activated and a formal governance structure is established with the client: decision authority, communication cadence, issue escalation, and accountability assignments are confirmed at the outset so progress remains transparent and measurable. Structured due diligence is completed immediately, including:
- Review of NPDES permit requirements, monitoring/reporting obligations, and compliance history
- Detailed review of O&M manuals, SOPs, CMOM documentation, and historical performance data
- Verification of certified operator coverage and license/grade requirements
- Asset inventory validation and critical equipment condition assessment (including maintenance backlog)
- SCADA/telemetry review, remote access validation, alarm management testing, and cybersecurity verification
- Flood control readiness review (pumps/gates), standby power verification, and storm response procedures

All readiness milestones are tracked against the transition schedule to ensure facilities remain stable and compliant throughout transition.

**Proven track record and risk mitigation.** A transition framework refined through numerous municipal transitions and successfully applied at comparable wastewater facilities is designed to ensure Day 1 readiness, proactive risk management, and a stabilized workforce culture. The plan specifically mitigates common operational risks such as staff turnover, incomplete asset records, SCADA access limitations, and emergency response readiness gaps. A high-level Gantt chart outlining every transition activity, including the job-shadowing period, is typically provided as a proposal appendix. Prior municipal clients who have experienced the transition process firsthand consistently report strong communication, organized mobilization, and early operational improvements that set the foundation for a high-performing, long-term partnership.

**Proven success transitioning complex systems.** A track record of successfully transitioning multiple complex water and wastewater systems from previous operators — while maintaining uninterrupted service and workforce continuity — is a strong proof point for evaluators concerned about transition risk. For example, one large western water utility recognized the effectiveness of a disciplined transition approach and its deep bench of subject-matter experts in achieving a smooth handoff from a contract operator of more than 20 years, with minimal challenges. In another case, a Southeast municipal water utility credited the incoming O&M partner with stepping up during an active water-supply emergency and delivering on commitments to protect public health while pursuing operational savings. In both cases, utility leadership specifically credited the disciplined transition approach with improving employee engagement and operational confidence.

**Transition leadership team.** A Transition Leadership Team — typically led by a dedicated [TRANSITION MANAGER] — oversees the integration process and serves as the primary interface with the client, maintaining a practical, hands-on presence and clear accountability throughout the transition. Core on-site leadership roles: [PROJECT MANAGER] and [REGIONAL OPERATIONS MANAGER].

**Regional specialist support bench.** Behind the on-site leadership team, a broader regional bench of specialists is typically made available to support transition and ongoing operations, covering roles such as: Regional Technical Support Team Leader; Regional Health & Safety; Regional O&M Technical Specialist; Process Engineering; Capital Projects; Wastewater Solutions Engineer; Regional Maintenance & Lead Electrician; Operations Technology/IT; Asset Management; Biosolids Technology Leader; Biosolids O&M Specialists; Dewatering Specialist; Regulatory Compliance; Energy Management/Sustainability; Odor Control; Grant/Loan Specialist; Financial Analyst; and Regional Admin. This structure supplements the on-site team with specialized expertise for solving operational issues, optimizing processes, and future planning needs without requiring every discipline to be staffed permanently on site.

**Workforce continuity.** Existing staff at the client's facilities represent essential institutional knowledge and operational continuity. Employment is typically offered to qualified and interested existing personnel, with retention prioritized to preserve system stability and protect the client's investment. A mobilization model blending local workforce continuity with regional technical depth keeps the WWTF, collection system, pump station, and flood-control operations fully staffed and supported from commencement forward. If unexpected staffing gaps arise, a regional bench of licensed operators and maintenance professionals provides immediate interim coverage. During transition, roles are aligned to operational needs and individual skill assessments, with responsibilities clarified early to strengthen accountability and readiness. Well-run transitions typically produce measurable improvements in employee engagement and retention, reflected in post-transition satisfaction surveys — attributable to clear leadership, defined expectations, competitive compensation, and structured training pathways.

**Training and operational readiness.** After onboarding, structured skills assessments are conducted and targeted training implemented to reinforce process control, safety procedures, emergency response, and regulatory compliance requirements. Cross-training across plant and collection operations builds redundancy and resilience. Site-specific operational integration should validate, early in transition: activated-sludge (or other applicable) process performance under variable loading/seasonal conditions; high-flow and wet-weather management procedures (including contingency/bypass protocols); pump station reliability, wet-well condition, and telemetry functionality; flood control system readiness and gate/pump operations; cleaning priorities and I/I investigation approach; and odor monitoring, control, and complaint-response procedures. Given a client's exposure to storm events and coastal conditions, emergency preparedness and standby power validation should receive early priority, with alarm notifications, communication protocols, and contingency response procedures confirmed prior to the operational start date.

**Asset inventory and condition assessment.** At commencement, a comprehensive asset inventory and baseline condition assessment is conducted for process equipment, electrical systems, instrumentation, pump stations, and flood control infrastructure. Within the first 90 days, prioritized, data-driven recommendations are provided for asset renewal, reliability enhancements, and capital planning aligned with risk, lifecycle principles, and the RFP's required planning deliverables.

**Communication and knowledge transfer.** A formal Transition Communications Plan is implemented immediately following award, establishing stakeholder contacts, meeting cadence, documentation standards, and readiness verification procedures. Regular coordination meetings with client leadership confirm progress and resolve issues proactively. Knowledge transfer is deliberate and structured: the transition team works alongside existing staff during familiarization to capture process operations, maintenance practices, SCADA/telemetry procedures, reporting workflows, and document baseline conditions.

**Assurance of continuity and compliance.** From day one, full responsibility for regulatory compliance is assumed — including monitoring, sampling, laboratory coordination, reporting, and regulatory communication. Sampling programs, reporting workflows, and alarm systems are validated in advance to prevent any lapse in obligations, with redundant coverage and regional backup resources identified before the operational start date.
