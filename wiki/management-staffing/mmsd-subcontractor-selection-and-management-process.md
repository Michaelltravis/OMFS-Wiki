# Identifying, Selecting, and Managing Subcontractors — Five-Step Framework

Jacobs personnel will perform core O&M functions, including asset management, laboratory, safety, and training services. When we do engage subconsultants, beyond those partners named in **Exhibit II-10**, they'll supplement, rather than replace Jacobs' primary O&M workforce. This approach will provide [CLIENT] with consistent quality, faster response times, and direct accountability, while retaining flexibility for engaging local businesses in meaningful roles.

Our 5-step assessment process, illustrated in **Exhibit II-11**, requires subcontractors to provide documentation of safety performance, financial strength, resources, and capability.

**Exhibit II-11. Framework for subcontractor engagement.**

1. Evaluate if internal resources can self-perform
2. Assess technical or cost efficiency of subcontracted option
3. Review safety, compliance, and small business considerations
4. Submit planned subcontract engagement through Governance Committee if required
5. Track local firm participation and share metrics with [CLIENT]

Under the direction of our procurement lead, Kim Wilson, our procurement team will identify categories of subcontractors that will be needed for this contract to supplement our self-performed services. We'll identify multiple subcontractors in each category and aim to execute Master Service Agreements with them. This will ensure that back-up subcontractors will be available in an emergency or for specialized tasks. As part of this effort, we'll collaborate with [CLIENT]'s Procurement Department to identify service providers who've been working with [CLIENT] and providing quality and responsive service. Our goal is continuity and maximizing engagement of local registered firms, small businesses, and start-up businesses. Typical services we would subcontract include:

- Specialized technical testing (e.g., membrane integrity, coatings inspection, vibration calibration)
- Short-duration work involving significant equipment mobilization (e.g., large-scale painting, tank cleaning, crane services)
- Public outreach and community development (e.g., workforce development, training, community outreach)
- Emergency surge support when simultaneous critical events occur
- Capital improvement plan support

Kim will review our purchase order agreement templates with [CLIENT] to make sure that contract terms align with requirements of our contractual terms with [CLIENT] and adhere to [CLIENT]'s procurement goals and guidelines. Subcontractors will be integrated into the organization structure based on the types of services they provide, for example:

- Our onsite Subcontractor Administrator will manage material suppliers and maintenance services subcontractors, in coordination with our Maintenance and Asset Management team.
- Our regional procurement team will manage material suppliers in coordination with onsite management.
- Our regional procurement and IT teams will manage software providers in coordination with onsite management.

Subcontractor work orders will be subject to the same scheduling, permitting, and safety protocols as our own work. We'll proactively manage risk by requiring subconsultants to carry appropriate insurance, including indemnification clauses in contracts, and monitoring compliance with insurance updates and renewals.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the five-step gate (self-perform test → efficiency test → safety/compliance/small-business review → governance approval → participation tracking), the Master Service Agreement strategy that converts subcontracting into emergency depth, the typical-services list, and the three-way split of who manages which subcontractor type (onsite administrator, regional procurement, regional procurement plus IT).

Pursuit-specific: the procurement lead's name; whether a Governance Committee approval step exists in the contract; the client's procurement guidelines and incumbent service providers the team intends to carry over.
