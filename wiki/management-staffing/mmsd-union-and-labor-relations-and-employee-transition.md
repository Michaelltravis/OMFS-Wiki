# Union and Labor Relations — Recognizing the CBA and Transitioning Represented Employees

Led by our Labor Relations Lead, Jeff Williams, we've met with several union leaders (AFSCME, IAM, IUOE, Steamfitters, Electricians, and Painters) and their members to introduce Jacobs and our culture. Our early engagement with union representatives has offered insights into what's important to their union members, such as benefits and apprenticeship programs. We'll continue this collaborative discourse with unions to foster shared accountability and trust. We'll continue to build on that during the CBA process and beyond.

At [CLIENT], the active CBA will need to be recognized by the successor contractor if all current employees are to be transitioned to the new contractor. As such, the unions will most likely seek to understand what, if any, obligations [CLIENT] will maintain and what obligations will be assumed by Jacobs. **Jacobs intends to honor the CBA and all terms and conditions established therein as far as the law and our agreement with [CLIENT] are concerned.** We'll work with the unions and [CLIENT] to ensure the interests of all stakeholders are adequately accounted for through the transition. Specific elements of our plan to work with the unions follow and are described in **Exhibit II-7**.

## Process for transitioning unionized employees

- Develop a competitive benefits and compensation package that complies with negotiated CBA terms and that the unions will support. Negotiate change in health benefits, retirement, and other aspects of the CBA with the unions to confirm support for changes.
- Continuously communicate with union representatives and [CLIENT] to encourage partnership and early issue resolution.
- Consult with management, HR, and the union to help oversee changes.

## Integrating the union workforce to build a positive culture from Day 1

We'll communicate with potential employees through the union about our hiring plan, open positions, application procedures, and interview process. Early in the process, our benefits specialists will host workshops to explain our program and answer questions from employees or their family members. Our process for hiring represented employees is outlined below:

- Notify union of contract start date and our intent to retain represented employees.
- Request represented employees' contact information, classifications, seniority, and training.
- Request the current union benefits package.
- Hold union-specific workshops to introduce our management team and answer questions.
- Notify the union weekly about the hiring process, including the numbers interviewed and the numbers, names, and classifications selected.
- Following contract award, enter into a successor agreement with the union.

We'll schedule periodic labor/management meetings to increase communication, improve safety and productivity, and create a partnering relationship. **Twice yearly, we'll host a roundtable with [CLIENT] and union representatives to review project performance.** (See details on our robust employee transition approach built on decades of experience in ***Section IV.B. Pre-term Transition Activities***.)

> "Jacobs offers exceptional resources and a strong commitment to developing its people. The company emphasizes implementing plant improvements correctly and prioritizing long-term performance and reliability over lowest initial cost. Jacobs is also open to innovative approaches, such as partnering with machine-learning organizations to develop data-driven algorithms that reduce chemical consumption. This approach is why I am proud to be a part of Jacobs' team."
>
> — **Richard Piovoso, Union Lead Operator, Wilmington, DE Wastewater Treatment Facility**
