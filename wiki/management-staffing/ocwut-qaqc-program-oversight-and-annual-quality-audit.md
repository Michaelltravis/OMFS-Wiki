# Jacobs' QA/QC Program

## Management Oversight

Quality doesn't happen by accident — it's built into every process, verified through independent review, and corrected promptly when gaps are found. Jacobs' QA/QC Program for [CLIENT], a regional water utility trust operating a multi-plant wastewater system, will be led by Project Director **Steve**, who will provide management oversight independent of day-to-day operations, and supported by the full-time QA/QC Manager **Josh Alleman** reporting to Project Manager **David** with a direct line to **Steve**.

This structure, illustrated in **Exhibit 1-5**, directly addresses [CLIENT]'s requirement for a Management Oversight and QA/QC Plan that goes "beyond general policy statements" and "clearly identifies the processes, responsibilities, and tools" used to manage performance, verify compliance, and correct deficiencies. This structure will further support oversight of Jacobs' internal audit program, discussed below, and ensure audit results are acted upon to deliver results. Exhibit 1-5 shows the QA/QC organizational oversight structure: the Project Director provides project management and quality oversight; the Project Manager owns day-to-day delivery; and the QA/QC Manager draws on the audit team, compliance team, regional support team, and corporate audit and quality resources.

## QA/QC Plan Development and Internal Audit Program

Upon contract award, Jacobs will develop a comprehensive QA/QC Plan (draft due November 2, 2026; final due December 31, 2026, per the contract schedule). The plan will define how we monitor daily operations, maintenance activities, regulatory compliance, safety practices, and reporting obligations. It will specify internal review procedures prior to report submittal and methods for tracking every required deliverable.

Our QA/QC program is anchored by an Annual Quality Audit — a structured process we've developed and refined over our O&M group's 40-year history. This audit is quantifiable, repeatable, and leads to actionable results. A dedicated QA/QC team investigates 70 specific topics with a focus on contractual requirements, including training, project documentation, succession planning, communication plans, inventory, CMMS maintenance, work order status, sampling plan status, process control plans, data management, and business and budget management. The audit follows a four-step process, shown in **Exhibit 1-6** (graphic asset ID 142_009385):

- **Step 1 — Preparation.** The audit team sends an information request to the Project Manager; alerts go to the Compliance Team and regional O&M technical support of the upcoming audit; the Project Manager provides preliminary information for review.
- **Step 2 — Onsite Assessment.** Field assessments, staff interviews, and review of project records, with a follow-up scheduled within three weeks to discuss results.
- **Step 3 — Evaluation.** Information provided is reviewed for completeness and accuracy, followed by a review meeting with the Project Manager and Geographic Manager.
- **Step 4 — Action Plan.** The Project Manager begins corrective tasks; proof of task completion is logged to the internal Jacobs Corrective Action Plan database; reminders and progress checks occur at 30, 60, and 90 days; the audit closes out when all tasks are recorded as completed/resolved.

New projects are audited 6 months after startup, and the frequency of follow-up audits is determined by the project score across O&M and management areas. Results are compared to historical performance and benchmarked against all Jacobs' O&M projects nationally, giving [CLIENT] the benefit of lessons learned across our entire portfolio.

## Compliance Verification Before Report Submittal

Every report delivered to [CLIENT] — including monthly operating reports, NPDES Discharge Monitoring Reports, Payment Requests, Maintenance and Repair Fund expenditure reports, and Rehabilitation and Replacement expenditure reports — will undergo internal review before submittal. QA/QC Manager Josh will verify data accuracy, completeness, and consistency against source records. This is especially critical for the Monthly Payment Request, which includes self-reported compliance with Performance Requirements. The RFP makes clear that failure to accurately report compliance will result in consequences up to and including breach. We take that standard seriously and will build verification checkpoints into every reporting workflow.
