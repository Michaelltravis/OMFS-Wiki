# Turlock Zero Liquid Discharge Facility O&M

**Turlock, CA**

Jacobs is providing O&M support for the 0.75-MGD Zero Liquid Discharge Facility for Turlock Irrigation District (TID)'s combined cycle 250 MW power plant.

**Owner:** Turlock Irrigation District
**Contract dates:** 2005 – Ongoing
**Reference:** Mike Tehada, Combustion Turbine Department Manager, TID, PO Box 949, Turlock, CA 95381, 209.883.3455, matehada@TID.org
**Plant size (max monthly average):** 0.75 MGD

## Relevant experience

### Detailed description of the facilities operated

The TID industrial zero liquid discharge system includes micro filtration, sodium zeolite softeners, weak acid cation (WAC) exchange softeners, a de-carbonation unit, high efficiency RO systems, a demineralized RO system, mixed bed units, and crystallizer evaporators.

### Challenge

TID invested in a zero-liquid discharge, RO water treatment system at its power plant in Turlock, CA. The facility treats process water from the cooling tower and supplies demineralized water to the steam generators. The water balance is crucial to proper operation of the power plant. The District needed a skilled O&M partner to operate and maintain the new system.

### Solutions/outcome

The District partnered with Jacobs as its contract O&M provider. Upon assuming O&M responsibility of the new facilities, we discovered operational challenges associated with the facility design and are helping resolve them while making the system run more efficiently and effectively. Programmable logic controllers (PLCs) and a human machine interface (HMI) computer system control the power plant's water balance, and they require constant monitoring.

Our staff keeps tank capacities at a minimum level to facilitate effective and efficient power plant operations. We are working to improve the HMI system by programming and tuning loops, adjusting set point controls, adding data collection, and troubleshooting the system. Jacobs installed new valving, plumbing, instrumentation, feedback control, and trend monitoring to improve O&M. The onsite team maintains PALL micro filtration units to better control turbidity and improve backwash techniques.

Staff optimized process chemical aids using bench jar testing, experimentation, and mechanical piping configuration changes. The team refined the WAC exchanger regeneration process to operate smoothly by implementing a more accurate acid control valve, tuning, and rinse procedure. Staff developed WAC rinse procedures that use one-fifth of the water and take less time than the old procedures required. These procedure improvements led to better techniques in scrubbing and regenerating the NAZ softeners to maintain lower differential pressure and higher throughput values to reduce brine waste.

We increased mixed beds throughput to exhaustion from 480,000 gallons to 500,000 gallons, a four percent increase without loss of quality. We improved crystallizer run time by changing procedures for the belt press, flash tank, FCHX, and brine waste heat exchanger cleaning. Jacobs' changes translate to lower costs and more efficient O&M for the client, helping the District achieve its goals for the power plant.

### Sustainability + savings

Jacobs employees focus on continuous improvement. At the Turlock project, staff researched and developed a new cleaning method for RO equipment using bulk chemicals in place of special-order RO cleaning chemicals. The new method generates $147,000 in chemical savings for the client annually.
