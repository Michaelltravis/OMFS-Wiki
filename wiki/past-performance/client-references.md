# Exhibit 3-5 — Reference Projects (Section 3, p. 11)

Verbatim as it appears in the source (marked *Confidential/Proprietary* in the original document). Full narrative versions of these five projects are in the sibling `project-*.md` files in this folder.

## Waterbury Wastewater System O&M | City of Waterbury | Waterbury, CT
**Size:** 27 MGD
**Years of Service:** 2018 – Present
**Annual Project Fee:** $6M
**Owner Contact:** Mike LeBlanc, Director of Finance | 203.574.6840 x7059 | mleblanc@waterburyct.org
**Tags:** EPA Region 1 · Odor Control Study · Large Collection System · Pump Stations

**Services Provided:** We developed a holistic approach to utility operations, combining operations, asset management, technical services, planning, and regional support. Under a 10-year agreement, Jacobs operates and maintains the 27 MGD WWTP, approximately 310 miles of sanitary sewer, and 20 pump stations. Our approach includes close monitoring of treatment processes and equipment, proactive maintenance, staff training, and long-term facility planning.

**Facilities Managed:** Since November 2018, the City and Jacobs have worked as partners to manage, improve, and optimize the City's water pollution plant, collection system, and related utilities. The City estimated cost savings of up to $12.7 million compared to the previous operating approach.

**Accomplishments:** We are assisting with managing an approximately $25 million phosphorous upgrade, which includes tertiary filtration and permanent chemical addition. We also volunteer staff hours annually to support riverbank cleanups through the City's Naugatuck River Brigade program, which focuses on the health of the Naugatuck River.

## Westerly WWTP O&M and DBO Services | Town of Westerly | Westerly, RI
**Size:** 3.3 MGD
**Years of Service:** 2017 – Present
**Annual Project Fee:** $2.4M
**Owner Contact:** Max Sposato, Utilities Director | 401.348.2561 | msposato@westerlyri.gov
**Tags:** EPA Region 1 · Septage Receiving · Energy Optimization · Biosolids Management

**Services Provided:** We adjusted hauling schedules, monitored energy use, and deployed quality systems to make sludge handling more efficient, achieving more than $500,000 in savings against the original 6-year forecast and about $250,000 in savings against the adjusted forecast over the last 3 years.

**Facilities Managed:** Jacobs operates the 3.3 MGD wastewater treatment facility, including nine pump stations and associated utility equipment. The plant accepts hauled commercial and domestic septage, with Jacobs managing an industrial pretreatment program (IPP).

**Accomplishments:** In the first 18 months of the contract, we improved the facility by implementing "in-house" clarifier rehabilitations, completing corrective action items inherited from the previous contract operator, improving the disinfection system upgrades, removing six 30-yard containers of trash and debris from throughout the facility and pump stations, and improving general facility and pump station appearance through landscaping, painting, and vegetation removal.

## Southbridge WWTP | Town of Southbridge | Southbridge, MA
**Size:** 3.77 MGD
**Years of Service:** 2025 – Present
**Annual Project Fee:** $1.7M
**Owner Contact:** John Jovan, Jr., Town Manager | 508.764.5405 | jjovan@southbridgemass.org
**Tags:** EPA Region 1 · AI CCTV Inspection · Odor Control Study · Composting/Biosolids

**Services Provided:** We are leveraging our unique strengths to make significant investments in the Town's facilities and system upgrades to improve operability and performance. This investment will allow for more efficient and timely distribution of compost and the potential for revenue generation through product sales.

**Facilities Managed:** Jacobs was unanimously selected by the Town to operate and maintain the 3.77-MGD WWTP, 48 miles of collection system, and 11 lift stations. Under a 5-year contract, we transitioned the operations of the WWTP from the previous contractor and assumed full operating responsibility on February 1, 2025.

**Accomplishments:** The Jacobs Design-Build business group was awarded a contract to design and manage an $85 million nitrogen reduction upgrade at the plant. We improved composting operations by optimizing the wood amendment-to-biosolids ratio and adjusting blower operations based on pile temperatures to enhance process performance and product quality. The Town has procured new screening equipment to further improve compost quality, with implementation planned. We recently completed an odor control and dispersion study and are supporting evaluation of improvements to reduce odors and enhance community compatibility. We are also bringing subject matter expertise in biosolids management and odor control to support plant staff, ongoing operations, and continuous improvement planning.

## South Huron WWTP O&M | South Huron Valley Utility Authority (SHVUA) | Rockwood, MI
**Size:** 24 MGD WWTP, collection system, pump stations
**Years of Service:** 2019 – Present
**Annual Project Fee:** $5.3M *(as shown in Exhibit 3-5 — note this differs from the "$xxM" placeholder shown on the Appendix B project-description page for the same project; see `project-south-huron-mi.md` reuse-notes. Confirm the correct figure with the account team.)*
**Owner Contact:** Tim Neighbors, Chairman | 734.675.4919 | tneighbors@woodhavenmi.org
**Tags:** Large Regional System · Biosolids Management · System Optimization · Process Upgrades

**Services Provided:** We are providing O&M and management services for the wastewater system, which services approximately 90,000 residents. Responsibilities include 24/7 operations, maintenance, lab services, regulatory compliance, industrial pretreatment, and administrative support, all supported by computerized systems for operations and asset management. We are also performing O&M of 36 miles of interceptor piping and two lift stations, including monitoring, preventive maintenance, and reliability management.

**Facilities Managed:** The WWTP uses screening and grit removal, primary clarification, and a multi-stage anoxic/oxic activated sludge process with secondary clarification and disinfection. Solids are managed through thickening and stabilization and supported by enhanced biosolids processing systems. The system also includes 36 miles of interceptor piping and two major lift stations, integrated through SCADA for monitoring and control.

**Accomplishments:** Jacobs successfully transitioned operations from the previous provider and implemented targeted training programs that strengthened workforce capability and retention. The team optimized biosolids management, reducing solids volume, chemical usage, and energy demand while improving overall plant performance. Jacobs also supported implementation of advanced biosolids processing, including thermal hydrolysis and Class A biosolids production, providing a more sustainable and cost-effective long-term solution.

## City of Traverse City | Regional WWTP DBO | Traverse City, MI
**Size:** 8.5 MGD
**Years of Service:** 1990 – Present
**Annual Project Fee:** $3.5M
**Owner Contact:** Art Krueger, Director of Municipal Facilities | 231.922.4923 | akrueger@traversecitymi.gov
**Tags:** MBR Treatment · Industrial Pretreatment · Biosolids Management · Energy Optimization

**Services Provided:** Since 1990, Jacobs has partnered with the City to provide operations, maintenance, and design-build services for the wastewater treatment facility. We implemented a comprehensive operational strategy and computerized maintenance management program to restore compliance and support long-term performance. Our approach integrates operations, asset management, capital planning, industrial pretreatment, and ongoing process optimization to deliver reliable and cost-effective service.

**Facilities Managed:** Jacobs operates and maintains the 8.5 MGD wastewater treatment plant, supporting a population of approximately 50,000, along with pump stations and satellite treatment systems in the surrounding region. The facility utilizes advanced membrane bioreactor (MBR) technology and includes solids handling, digestion, and dewatering systems.

**Accomplishments:** We restored regulatory compliance and resolved enforcement actions early in the contract and have maintained consistent compliance since. Jacobs supported major capital improvements, including a $1.5 million biological nutrient removal upgrade and a $31 million MBR upgrade, one of the largest of its kind at the time. Through ongoing optimization efforts, we reduced electrical consumption by more than 30% and achieved additional savings in chemicals and natural gas. Our maintenance program has extended the lifespan of critical assets, including membrane systems exceeding expected service life, while delivering long-term cost savings and operational reliability.

Use Exhibit 3-3 to substantiate broader "more than N facilities of comparable size" qualification claims; it is a capability/checkmark matrix rather than a references table, so pair it with Exhibit 3-5 or Appendix C (`similar-facilities-table.md`) when a narrative description is needed instead of a checkmark.
