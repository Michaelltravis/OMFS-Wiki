# Waterbury Water and Wastewater System O&M Odor Control and Results

> Protection of the Naugatuck River is a key priority for our City. We went through a careful process to select our O&M contractor, and I’m very pleased that we chose Jacobs. I’ve seen a lot of improvements since our partnership began in 2018, and I rest easier knowing that Kevin Dahl and his team are taking excellent care of our wastewater system.
>
> — Mayor Neil O’Leary, City of Waterbury, CT

## Project Challenges and How We Responded

**Odor Control:** Four chemical wet scrubbers serving the headworks, solids handling areas, and incineration building were in disrepair at contract start, and the on-site incinerator was also contributing to odor source. We completed an odor study, **implemented a short-term operational control plan that reduced public complaints by more than 55% between contract years one and two, and helped develop a $2M capital upgrade**. The odor control upgrade included a carbon absorber polishing stage added downstream of the primary scrubbers, replacement of aging air piping and chemical dosing pumps on scrubber system #1, and new dosing pumps on scrubber system #2. The **phased approach of source identification, short-term stabilization, and permanent improvement** is directly applicable to OCWUT's odor control requirements.

## What Made This Partnership Work: Unique Characteristics That Benefited Both Parties

Waterbury retained full ownership and decision-making authority, and we structured operations to support that model through **regular reporting, transparent communication, and collaborative capital planning**. On the regulatory side, we identified an opportunity in Connecticut's Nitrogen Credit Exchange Program; in 2022, the WWTP **earned a $57,151 nitrogen credit for the City**. Staff development and community engagement have remained priorities throughout this partnership. We also developed a training program for **transitioned and new staff,** successfully **integrating 26 City employees into our operations team**.

**Maintenance:** Jacobs manages Waterbury's assets in Maintenance Connection CMMS, **tracking preventive maintenance, work orders, parts inventory, and equipment history across the WWTP, collection system, and pump stations**. At contract start, we developed an operations and asset management plan covering the treatment plant and collection system, and a preventive maintenance program. We then executed capital upgrades, including odor control, HVAC, secondary clarification, maintenance building improvements, and site security improvements, while maintaining continuous operations. The **32-person on-site team carries out day-to-day maintenance alongside Jacobs' regional support staff**, providing technical depth for complex repairs. This integrated model of CMMS, in-house capital delivery, and regional technical support aligns directly with OCWUT’s asset preservation and housekeeping performance requirements.

## Proven Results

- **99.57% NPDES permit compliance** rate over the past 5 years
- Up to **$12.7M in estimated cost savings** compared to prior operating approach
- **Odor complaints reduced by more than 55%** between contract years one and two
- **$2M odor control upgrade** completed
- **1,100 dry tons of WFP lagoon sludge removed** and diverted to beneficial reuse within 90 days of WFP contract start

**Collection System:** We stood up a comprehensive CMOM program covering sewer cleaning, CCTV inspection, FOG management, and CIPP repairs. **Monthly sewer inspection rates increased 100%; sewer problem areas were reduced by nearly 15%**, and the team cleaned and inspected more than 1.5 million linear feet of pipe.

- **$57,151 nitrogen credit** earned for the City through Connecticut's Nitrogen Credit Exchange Program

## Lessons Learned: Integrated Experience Improving Plans and Delivery

**WFP Sludge Backlog:** When we assumed the WFP contract, the previous operator had filled both sludge lagoons and all four GeoTubes to capacity. **Within 90 days, we cleared and graded a 2-acre City-owned parcel, built a new GeoTube laydown area, dredged the lower lagoon, and removed 1,100 dry tons of solids** for beneficial reuse at less than half the cost of conventional disposal, **solving a 35-year problem in the first contract quarter.**

Waterbury shows that inherited deficiencies don’t have to define recovery. A rapid, **well-documented condition assessment, clear prioritization, and early action can turn deferred maintenance and operational backlogs into early wins** that build client confidence. OCWUT will benefit from that same approach.
