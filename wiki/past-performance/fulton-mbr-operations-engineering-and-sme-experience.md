# MBR Operations, Engineering, and SME Experience

In addition to our design/build/operate MBR projects, we operate several MBR plants that were designed by others. This includes Short’s Brewing Company (Traverse City, MI) as well as plants in Crescent City, CA and Dodge City, KS. While we have designed Kubota plants, but do not currently operate one, we’ve taken steps to familiarize ourselves with their operational aspects. We recently met with Kubota technical staff to discuss the Big Creek WRF project and gain insights into its design and operation. We visited the large Kubota plant in Canton, OH, including the adjacent Kubota research facility. On that visit we were able to observe membrane removal from the tanks, including replacement of individual cartridges. We also visited the Kubota plant in Piedmont, SC, a Jacobs design, where we learned the importance of maintaining a complete inventory of spare parts.

MBR expertise is but a subset of Jacobs’ wastewater engineering expertise. Each year, Jacobs has 20-25 papers at WEFTEC, the industry’s technical conference and exposition. Jacobs always scores highly in ENR’s rankings of engineering firms. In 2024, we were the #1 Design Firm, the #2 Professional Services Firm, and the #4 Environmental Firm. Jacobs has many communities of practice (CoPs), whose members engage in monthly calls, discussion threads, and technical queries. As an example, the Water Resource Recovery CoP (a fancy name for wastewater treatment) has 783 active members. It is easy for a Jacobs’ engineer to identify individuals with expertise in any aspect of wastewater engineering.

Jacobs has a full-service design center in Atlanta, with more than 250 water staff in Georgia. In addition to our contractual role to operate and maintain the County’s MBR facilities, we can offer engineering services as needs arise.

SCOTT LEVESQUE is Atlanta-based and will serve as a project resource for the County and the Jacobs’ operations team. Scott has decades of experience with wastewater engineering and serves as Jacobs’ global resource for MBR. He has worked with all aspects of MBR technology: design, troubleshooting, pilot testing, membrane procurement, and membrane replacement/upgrade. He was a technical reviewer for Water Environment Federation’s Membrane Bioreactor Manual of Practice and membrane-related portions of Design of Municipal Wastewater Treatment Plants. He served on the committee that developed ANSI/AWWA Standard B130, Membrane Bioreactor Systems and was the committee chair for its recent revision. He has designed and evaluated municipal and industrial MBR processes on five continents ranging from 0.1 to 55-mgd capacity, including greenfield projects and conversions from conventional activated sludge. His WEFTEC 2021 paper, “A Practitioner’s Perspective on the Design of Large Bioreactor Facilities” described the rapid implementation of MBR technology and presented design considerations relating to energy efficiency, mixed liquor filterability, membrane tank flow split, handling peak flows, and meeting intense aeration requirements. He maintains connections with all major MBR equipment suppliers to stay abreast of current and anticipated product developments. In summary, Scott’s MBR experience and expertise is unrivaled.
