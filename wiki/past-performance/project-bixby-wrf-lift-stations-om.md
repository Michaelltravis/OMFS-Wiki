# Bixby Water Reclamation Facility and Lift Stations O&M

**CITY OF BIXBY | BIXBY, OK**

**Contract dates:** June 2021 – Ongoing
**Size of facilities:** 2.8 MGD SBR water reclamation facility (5.0 MGD ultimate design capacity); 18 lift stations
**Land application of sludge:** No
**Owner information:** Dylan Warner, Public Works Director, 116 West Needles, Bixby, OK 74008; T: 918.366.4430; E: Dwarner@bixbyok.gov
**Relevant personnel:** Jacob Smith, Project Manager; Luke Lenard, Client Account Manager; Steve Carpenter, Geographic Manager; Ryan Harrold, Regional Manager; Kevin Dahl, Regional Manager; Amy Dembinski, Asset Manager; Tanner Pipher, Project Engineer; Robert Coons, Regional Maintenance; Joe Ramos, Regional Support of Operations for Startup; Brian Daniels, Lead Mechanic/Industrial Electrician

## Facility Overview

The Bixby Water Reclamation Facility (BWRF) is a 2.8-MGD sequencing batch reactor (SBR) with an ultimate design capacity of 5.0 MGD, serving approximately 28,609 residents. Jacobs operates the BWRF 7 days a week and manages 18 associated telemetry-equipped lift stations. The scope includes **industrial pretreatment program (IPP) administration; fats, oils, and grease (FOG) management program; and CMMS tracking all maintenance work orders, labor, parts, and inventory costs**.

## Jacobs' Role and Relevance to OCWUT

Jacobs holds a 10-year, $8.3M contract for **full O&M of the BWRF**, beginning before the facility reached final acceptance. The contract has three phases: construction inspection coordination (Phase 1); SBR startup with initial staffing and telemetry installation on seven lift stations (Phase 2); and ongoing full O&M with seven full-time employees (Phase 3). When post-commissioning operations revealed that the facility was under-designed for actual loading conditions, we **mobilized our broader technical resources through our One Jacobs model** to develop alternative compliance strategies with the City.

This engagement is directly relevant to OCWUT in two ways: it's an active Oklahoma contract, so **our team operates daily within the ODEQ regulatory framework**; and the phased startup, including **managing operations through an active punch list and equipment commissioning**, closely parallels OCWUT's South Canadian WWTP.

## Project Challenges and How We Responded

**Equipment Startup and Commissioning:** Starting up a new facility still under construction is one of the toughest challenges in contract operations. At Bixby, we managed equipment startup failures, a centrifuge issue that shut the unit down for over a week, pump damage caused by aging lift stations, and repeated problems from utility power surges. **Our team addressed each issue methodically by reprogramming the bar screen, coordinating manufacturer support for the centrifuge, and using CMMS-tracked maintenance across all 18 lift stations to stay ahead of recurring failures**.

**Maintenance:** Taking over a brand-new facility midconstruction meant there were no historical maintenance records, equipment baselines, or schedules to rely on. **We built the maintenance program from the ground up** by implementing the CMMS, loading a field-verified asset registry based on Phase 1 inspections, and developing preventive maintenance schedules for equipment throughout the facility and all 18 lift stations. Starting with accurate as-built data rather than design drawings gave us a stronger maintenance foundation and reduced risks including wrong spare parts and unplanned downtime.

**Odor Control:** The previous holding pond system at Bixby caused ongoing odor and capacity issues for the City and surrounding community. The new BWRF was designed to resolve those issues, and **we managed the transition with community expectations in mind**. We also established a structured **FOG management program at contract start to reduce odor at the source** by limiting fats, oils, and grease in the collection system. In addition, telemetry across all 18 lift stations helps our team monitor wet well conditions, identify abnormal detention times, and respond before odor issues become compliance or community concerns.

## Proven Results

- Successfully stood up a **new SBR facility from preconstruction inspection through full ongoing O&M**
- **CMMS deployed from day one**, tracking all labor, parts, and inventory costs
- **Telemetry installed on lift stations**, enabling remote monitoring and faster response
- **IPP and FOG management** programs established, building the regulatory compliance foundation
- Under-design issue navigated through **alternative compliance strategies** developed with City support
- **Chronic odor and capacity issues at the north transfer station holding pond resolved**

## Lessons Learned: Integrated Experience Improving Plans and Delivery

With the South Canadian WWTP under construction, we'll bring the same phased, hands-on startup approach used at Bixby: **early engagement during construction, telemetry and CMMS deployment ahead of full commissioning, and an operations team experienced in resolving equipment issues while maintaining compliance**. Our **One Jacobs model** gives OCWUT access to engineering and technical specialists across the firm — the same bench we relied on at Bixby when the facility required an alternative solution. Because our team already operates within ODEQ's regulatory framework, we can focus on execution from day one.

## What Made This Partnership Work: Unique Characteristics That Benefited Both Parties

The City of Bixby needed an operator that could be a true partner, and we were involved from the start. **By having our operations team onsite during construction, we identified issues early, documented as-built conditions, and built a CMMS based on the actual facility** rather than design drawings. That foundation supported reliable, cost-effective long-term operations, and the City trusted us to lead that transition from the old holding pond system to the new BWRF. When the under-design issue emerged, we brought in technical resources from across the company to evaluate the problem, develop engineering solutions and cost estimates, and work with the City on compliance alternatives. Throughout the process, **we maintained transparent reporting and gave the City direct access to OMFS and Engineering leadership for timely coordination and decision making**.
