**CITY OF BIXBY | BIXBY, OK**

## Facility Overview

The Bixby Water Reclamation Facility (BWRF) is a 2.8-MGD sequencing batch reactor (SBR) with an ultimate design capacity of 5.0 MGD, serving approximately 28,609 residents. Jacobs operates the BWRF 7 days a week and manages 18 associated telemetry-equipped lift stations. The scope includes **industrial pretreatment program (IPP) administration; fats, oils, and grease (FOG) management program; and CMMS tracking all maintenance work orders, labor, parts, and inventory costs**.

## Jacobs' Role and Relevance to OCWUT

Jacobs holds a 10-year contract for **full O&M of the BWRF**, beginning before the facility reached final acceptance. The contract has three phases: construction inspection coordination (Phase 1); SBR startup with initial staffing and telemetry installation on seven lift stations (Phase 2); and ongoing full O&M with seven full-time employees (Phase 3). When post-commissioning operations revealed that the facility was under-designed for actual loading conditions, we **mobilized our broader technical resources through our One Jacobs model** to develop alternative compliance strategies with the City.

This engagement is directly relevant to OCWUT in two ways: it’s an active Oklahoma contract, so **our team operates daily within the ODEQ regulatory framework**; and the phased startup, including **managing operations through an active punch list and equipment commissioning**, closely parallels OCWUT’s South Canadian WWTP.

## Project Challenges and How We Responded

**Equipment Startup and Commissioning:** Starting up a new facility still under construction is one of the toughest challenges in contract operations. At Bixby, we managed equipment startup failures, a centrifuge issue that shut the unit down for over a week, pump damage caused by aging lift stations, and repeated problems from utility power surges. **Our team addressed each issue methodically by reprogramming the bar screen, coordinating manufacturer support for the centrifuge, and using CMMS-tracked maintenance across all 18 lift stations to stay ahead of recurring failures.**

**Odor Control:** The previous holding pond system at Bixby caused ongoing odor and capacity issues for the City and surrounding community. The new BWRF was designed to resolve those issues, and **we managed the transition with community expectations in mind.** We also established a structured **FOG management program at contract start to reduce odor at the source** by limiting fats, oils, and grease in the collection system. In addition, telemetry across all 18 lift stations helps our team monitor wet well conditions, identify abnormal detention times, and respond before odor issues become compliance or community concerns.
