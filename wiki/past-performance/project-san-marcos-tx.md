## Facility Overview and O&M Scope

The City of San Marcos operates a 9-MGD WWTP serving a growing Texas community along the ecologically sensitive San Marcos River. The facility includes a full treatment train consisting of **influent screening, grit removal, primary clarification, biological nutrient removal, secondary clarification, tertiary sand filtration, UV disinfection, post-aeration, biosolids dewatering and disposal, three odor biotrickling filters, and a Type I reclaimed water program**. Reclaimed water supports cooling towers, irrigation, dust control operations, and the City's animal shelter, conserving hundreds of millions of gallons of potable water annually. The plant also interfaces with the City's collection system of lift stations, which are critical during storm events.

San Marcos is a flagship example of what long-term, **full-service O&M** looks like when it's done right. For 20 years, Jacobs has **managed plant operations, regulatory compliance, biosolids management, reclaimed water production, lift station monitoring, laboratory services, and capital project support**. The regulatory rigor required for discharge to the San Marcos River mirrors the compliance standard OCWUT holds at North Canadian and Deer Creek. Our track record with consistent Texas Pollutant Discharge Elimination System permit compliance and measurable cost reductions reflects what we'll deliver for OCWUT's permit-sensitive NPDES obligations.

Our scope has expanded with the City's needs, including **technical support for more than $10M in capital improvements** since 2017. Today, we're managing those responsibilities while the City constructs a second WWTP and upgrades the existing facility. Our team works alongside third-party engineering firms throughout capital project delivery using a collaborative model that keeps design decisions grounded in operational reality, regardless of who holds the engineering contract. This scope aligns directly with OCWUT’s needs: **activated sludge operations, tertiary treatment, UV disinfection, odor control, biosolids management, reclaimed water, lift station monitoring, and continuous compliance on a sensitive discharge permit.**

## Odor Control and Optimization

**Odor Control:** San Marcos discharges to the San Marcos River, one of Texas's most ecologically sensitive waterways, meaning odor management isn't just a neighbor relations issue; it carries direct regulatory and reputational weight. We operate and maintain three biological scrubbers that control odors across the headworks, solids handling, and treatment areas. Biotrickling filters require active management: media condition, airflow balance, nutrient dosing, and pH must be continuously monitored and adjusted to maintain removal efficiency. **Our team maintains these systems as part of the daily operations, tracking performance data and proactively scheduling media inspections and replacements to prevent degradation-driven odor events.** This preventive approach eliminates odors without the chemical costs and handling concerns associated with chemical scrubbing systems.

**Process and Cost Optimization:** Over time, chemical usage created both cost and environmental pressure at San Marcos. Through enhanced biological phosphorous removal and process control adjustments, **our team reduced ferric chloride use by 65%**. We also **cut electricity consumption by more than 174,000 kWh annually** through operational changes alone, with no capital investment required. When biosolids hauling costs increased, **we restructured dewatering operations to reduce polymer use and hauling frequency, lowering hauling costs by roughly 50%**.

**Maintenance:** We implemented a structured predictive maintenance program centered on lubrication analysis for critical rotating assets. This approach tracks lubricant condition over time to detect contamination, wear, and degradation before equipment failures occur — reducing unplanned downtime, extending asset life, cutting oil consumption, and deferring capital replacement. Paired with routine condition assessments and equipment rehabilitation scheduling through our CMMS, it provides a disciplined framework for managing preventive and corrective maintenance, parts inventory, and equipment history. **The result is a facility that has sustained strong reliability and compliance performance over 20 years without reactive maintenance driving operational disruptions.**

## Proven Results, Partnership, and Lessons Learned

- **65% reduction** in ferric chloride use.
- **174,000+ kWh in annual electricity savings** — $22,000 per year.
- **40% reduction** in polymer use; ~50% reduction in biosolids hauling costs.
- **323 million gallons** of reclaimed water reused annually.
- **$10M+ in capital project technical support delivered since 2017.**

At OCWUT, we'll bring the same disciplined predictive maintenance program, process optimization mindset, and collaborative coordination model that made San Marcos an award-winning facility. San Marcos demonstrates **sustained, accountable O&M across a full treatment train**. We’ll apply the same process optimization discipline that reduced chemical costs at San Marcos to OCWUT's activated sludge systems, belt presses, and odor control equipment.

We’re currently managing active construction programs alongside daily operations at San Marcos, and that experience will directly **support coordination of OCWUT's CIP pipeline without compromising compliance or service delivery**.

Two decades of service at San Marcos reflect a team that treats the City's system as its own. We embedded ourselves in the City's planning processes, co-authored capital improvement strategies, and delivered technical services that supported over $10M in out-of-scope capital upgrades since 2017. Our team also partnered with Texas State University to support their Direct Potable Reuse Pilot onsite and led community programs, including the annual Great Texas River Cleanup. That depth of community integration, and our commitment to the City's mission, helped keep this partnership strong for two decades. One of the clearest examples of the team's culture is that an intern recruited from a local university progressed into the project manager role, demonstrating our investment in local talent development.
