## Relevant experience

### Detailed description of the facilities operated

The Clovis WWTP/WRF was built in anticipation of the future needs of the City of Clovis community. Prior to its construction, all of the City of Clovis' wastewater was conveyed to the Fresno-Clovis Regional WWTP. Today, the facility is a California Title 22 Quality Treatment Facility with effluent suitable for discharge into the environment.

The Clovis WWTP serves as a scalping plant. It treats up to 2.8-MGD with anything over that amount going to the Fresno-Clovis Regional WWTP. Treatment at the Clovis WWTP/WRF includes membrane bioreactor process (MBR) with centrifuges for biosolids processing and a belt filter press (BFP) as back up. MBR is delivering the highest quality water and provides exceptional effluent quality, creates the least environmental impact by use of a small footprint, significantly reduces costs, and offers simplified expansion for future needs.

### High performance facilities

Since commencing operations in 2009, the Clovis WWTP/WRF has performed well and has been able to reliably and consistently meet all State of California NPDES and water reclamation regulatory requirements. Regulatory, environmental, and odor contract performance guarantees have been met consistently and reliably. Jacobs has also worked very closely with our client on several improvements to ensure treatment plant sustainability well into the future.

### Implementing improvements

Our O&M history at the City of Clovis has resulted in various lessons learned and improvements:
- The MBR, membrane-specific cleaning, and back-pulse process have been improved. The system now utilizes MemPulse technology to extend fiber life through cleaning and reduce the need for maintenance cleans.
- We have improved the SCADA format and system to provide more operability and control of equipment on- and off-site, improved monitoring from process equipment, and improved screen configurations based on operator input.
- We have improved the solids handling process through intensive testing of sludge quality and polymer choices that work best with an MBR/aerobically digested sludge. Through this testing we have learned the best process parameters to maintain the sludge quality and minimize solids production.

> Our MBR process produces great quality water for Title 22 reuse. Good screening at the head of the plant is important to keep material from fouling the membranes. Properly maintaining the blowers and the aeration system is the other big piece of it, because good air scour is critical. Nobody wants to replace membranes early–they are expensive! In general, there are more instruments that need to be calibrated and maintained, and operators will need to learn some new things. But once you learn the process, the system is easy to run.
>
> — Tony Duffy, Jacobs Plant Manager at the Clovis WWTP/WRF
