# Waterbury Water and Wastewater System O&M

**CITY OF WATERBURY | WATERBURY, CT**

**Contract dates:** November 2018 – Ongoing  
**Size of facilities:** WWTP: 27 MGD (wet weather: >54 MGD); WTP: 38 MGD design/25 MGD avg.; 310 miles of sanitary sewer, 20 pump stations  
**Land application of sludge:** No  
**Owner information:** Michael LeBlanc, *Director of Finance/Water Pollution Control*, 210 Municipal Road, Waterbury, CT 06708; T: 203.574.6840 ext. 7059; E: mleblanc@waterburyct.org  
**Awards:** 2022 | WEF NutrientSmart Silver Partner Award; 2021 | Chief Operator: Connecticut Operator of the Year — NEWEA; 2013 | National Association of Clean Water Agencies  
**Relevant personnel:** Kevin Dahl, *Transition/Regional Manager*; Scott Golden, *Startup and Operations Support*; Amy Dembinski, *Asset Manager*

## Facility Overview

Jacobs operates the City of Waterbury's **27-MGD activated sludge WWTP**, approximately **310 miles of sanitary sewer**, and **20 pump stations** under a 10-year agreement that began in November 2018. The treatment process includes **screening and grit removal, primary treatment, secondary aeration and clarification, UV disinfection, and chemical phosphorus removal**, with wet weather capacity above 54 MGD. We are also supporting a **$25M phosphorus upgrade** and, in July 2023, were awarded the City's **38-MGD surface water filtration plant (WFP) O&M** contract. Across both facilities, we use Maintenance Connection CMMS, SEDARU and SCREAM for collections management, and Hach WIMS for operations data. **The facility has maintained a 99.57% permit compliance rate over the past 5 years.**

## Jacobs’ Role and Relevance to OCWUT

Waterbury selected us after combined sewer overflows in late 2017 impacted the Naugatuck River—a high-visibility compliance issue that required a structured operational response. We were chosen based on a comprehensive facility assessment and improvement strategy, and the City has since estimated up to **$12.7M in savings compared with its prior operating approach.** The scope is directly relevant to OCWUT: **multi-facility operations, active capital work, a client that retains ownership and decision-making authority, and a long-term partnership built on transparent, accountable O&M.** Waterbury also demonstrates our ability to expand scope based on performance, with the City’s water O&M contract awarded competitively in 2023.

As part of the **holistic approach**, Jacobs on-site and regional support staff manage the collection system and capacity, management, operations, and maintenance (CMOM) regulations including sewer cleaning; CCTV; callouts; fats, oils, and grease (FOG); and cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) repairs.

## Project Challenges and How We Responded

**Transition:** Jacobs transitioned the O&M of Waterbury’s wastewater facilities from City operations, a process led by **Kevin Dahl** that started **90 days prior to the start of the project.** Our staff transition plan focused on retaining as many qualified existing employees as possible. As a result, **100% of represented staff were offered employment, and they enjoyed equal or better compensation packages.** The process was highly **collaborative and transparent**, and everyone benefited from their continued institutional knowledge.
