## Description of Services Provided

Since November 2018, the City and Jacobs have worked as partners to manage, improve, and optimize the City's water pollution plant, collection system, and related utilities. The City estimated cost savings of up to $12.7 million compared to the previous operating approach.

After sewer overflows in late 2017 affected the Naugatuck River, Waterbury sought an expert operator and chose Jacobs. We developed a holistic approach to utility operations, combining operations, asset management, technical services, planning, and regional support. Under a 10-year agreement, Waterbury retains ownership and decision-making for its 27-MGD WWTP, approximately 310 miles of sanitary sewer, and 20 pump stations. Our approach includes close monitoring of treatment processes and equipment, proactive maintenance, staff training, and long-term facility planning.

## Detailed Description of the Facilities Managed

**Capital Improvements.** Jacobs provides full contract operations services, consisting of grit and screening, primary treatment, activated sludge, and secondary clarification followed by UV disinfection, as well as chemical addition to manage low-level phosphorous limits. Jacobs is also assisting with managing an approximately $25 million phosphorous upgrade, which includes tertiary filtration and permanent chemical addition. As part of the holistic approach, Jacobs on-site and regional support staff manage the collection system and capacity, management, operations, and maintenance (CMOM) regulations including sewer cleaning; CCTV; callouts; fats, oils, and grease (FOG); and cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) repairs. Jacobs also oversees a merchant sludge incinerator operated by Synagro.

**Treatment Systems and Processes.** The system uses preliminary screening and grit removal followed by primary treatment, secondary aeration and clarifiers, seasonal UV, and wet weather sodium hypochlorite disinfection. Tertiary filters are under construction. The plant has a 27-MGD design flow and <54-MGD wet weather flow. There is also chemical addition for total phosphorus (TP) removal.

**Community Involvement.** The Waterbury team decided to sponsor a newer program called the Naugatuck River Brigade focused on the health of the Naugatuck River and protecting this valuable resource. Jacobs plans to volunteer staff hours to help provide riverbank cleanups in the coming year.
