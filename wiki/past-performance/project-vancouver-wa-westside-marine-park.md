# Westside and Marine Park WWTPs O&M

**CITY OF VANCOUVER | VANCOUVER, WA**

**Contract dates:** January 2016 – Ongoing  
**Size:** 28.26-MGD Westside WWTP; 16.1-MGD Marine Park WWTP; 3.2-MGD industrial waste lagoon; eight major lift stations  
**Owner information:** Frank Dick, Wastewater Treatment Engineering Manager, 4500 SE Columbia Way, Vancouver, WA 98661 | T: 360.487.7179 | E: frank.dick@cityofvancouver.us  
**Land application of sludge:** No; **Relevant personnel:** Amy Dembinski, Asset Manager

## Facility Overview and Delivery

Jacobs manages O&M for two activated sludge WWTPs in Vancouver, including the 28.26-MGD Westside WWTP, the 16.1-MGD Marine Park WWTP, a 3.2-MGD industrial waste lagoon, and eight major lift stations. The scope includes an accredited laboratory, industrial pretreatment program (IPP) management, and compliance with EPA NPDES permits and Clean Air Agency Title V permits.

In 2016, **Jacobs replaced a 38-year incumbent operator in one of the most significant Pacific Northwest O&M transitions.** Since contract start, we have delivered **full O&M services** while leading a $25M, multi-year SCADA modernization program and **supporting energy upgrades and odor control improvements.** All capital work was completed under an integrated delivery model with no service interruptions.

This **multi-facility, multi-permit scope** closely reflects the OCWUT contract. Vancouver includes two primary WWTPs, multiple lift stations, an industrial pretreatment program, and an active CIP pipeline. It also demonstrates our **One Jacobs model** at work: when complex technical challenges arose, specialists from across the firm supported the local operations team, giving the client direct access to our broad technical depth.

## Transition, Odor, and Asset Management

**Transition:** With a 38-year incumbent in place, a **structured transition was critical to continuity.** We assembled a locally based management team bringing in experienced Jacobs staff to anchor the culture from day one as Plant Manager, Asset/Maintenance Manager, and Safety Manager—then interviewed existing staff and extended offers to the most qualified. Working closely with the City, we built a joint milestone schedule and executed it transparently: **90% of all transition activities were completed within 90 days,** and all **start-up activities wrapped within 180 days.** A six-month employee survey confirmed the approach worked—**staff reported improved communication and higher overall satisfaction compared to the prior operator.**

**Odor Control:** Vancouver's facilities include an active biosolids incinerator, one of the more odor-sensitive and emissions-regulated processes in wastewater treatment. We manage incinerator operations using Continuous Emissions Monitoring (CEM) systems that track regulated air quality parameters in real time, supporting Title V permit compliance and community confidence. **As part of the integrated capital improvement program, we delivered odor control system upgrades at both plants by** identifying sources, assessing existing control infrastructure, and implementing improvements to reduce emissions at the fence line and within process areas. A $500,000 heat exchanger investment **extended the life of the aging incinerator, deferred an unplanned shutdown, reduced confined-space entry risks for maintenance staff, and avoided an estimated $275,000–$400,000 in near-term capital costs.**

We implemented an asset management framework that integrates Maintenance Connection CMMS with our proprietary asset management platform. Repairs and replacements are prioritized based on risk, condition, and return on investment, aligned with ISO 55001 asset management standards. Predictive maintenance includes Augury vibration analysis for rotating equipment to detect imbalance, misalignment, and bearing wear before failures occur, and Fluke SmartView thermal imaging for electrical systems to identify overloads and insulation issues. **This layered approach, CMMS-tracked schedules, condition-based predictive tools, and capital planning informed by asset risk scores, gives Vancouver visibility into equipment health across the full facility portfolio.**

## Results, Partnership, and Lessons Learned

**SCADA and Capital Delivery:** Vancouver's legacy Wonderware SCADA system and PLCs had reached the end of their operational life, creating reliability and cybersecurity risk across a two-plant system. **We led a $25M, multi-year modernization of the SCADA and control infrastructure,** replacing PLCs, upgrading HMI systems, and improving network architecture without a single service interruption.

Our **transition approach prioritized staff retention and integration, preserving operational continuity** while quickly standing up new performance systems. The transition included open houses for incumbent employees and their families, filling available positions with existing staff where possible, and completing all transition requirements on time and to the City's satisfaction.

As OCWUT transitions from its current operator, we'll apply the same structured approach we used in Vancouver by **prioritizing staff integration, standing up performance systems quickly, and maintaining compliance from day one**. We'll draw on our **One Jacobs model** to bring specialized technical resources to work alongside the local operations team. The CMMS, predictive maintenance tools, and process optimization practices proven in Vancouver can be deployed from the start at OCWUT.

From there, we built a culture of continuous improvement. The team completed 20 energy projects, secured $900,000 in utility rebates, and launched a glove recycling program later adopted at more than 50 Jacobs sites nationally. **Vancouver’s recognition as a WEF "Utility of the Future Today" reflects what's possible when an operator and owner share a vision.**

We also support community and environmental initiatives in Vancouver, including Earth Day stream cleanups, habitat restoration, environmental education in partnership with Clark County Public Utility District, and volunteer efforts with local charities. Through operational reliability, capital support, and community involvement, we are a trusted partner in Vancouver’s wastewater utility success story.
