# Biosolids and Residuals Management Program

All biosolids processing, thickening, storage, hauling, and disposal is managed in full compliance with 40 CFR Part 503 and applicable state regulations, while maintaining the client's current thickened/dewatered sludge management approach and hauling program to its existing disposal outlet. The program is designed to provide resilient, predictable operations that protect public health, minimize odor impacts in sensitive settings, stabilize cost exposure in a volatile regional disposal market, and support long-term operational sustainability. Consideration is given to the facility's location, seasonal population fluctuations, and limited regional disposal outlets that influence hauling logistics and disposal reliability.

The approach emphasizes disciplined solids inventory management, optimized thickening and hauling practices, and proactive monitoring of regional disposal capacity trends. An Annual Innovation Workshop (or equivalent recurring forum) can be used to scope a structured **Biosolids Strategic Review** evaluating thickened versus dewatered solids scenarios, hauling distances and market outlets, odor risk considerations, lifecycle cost impacts, and potential funding opportunities, documented in a formal memorandum with short- and long-term recommendations. Implementation strategies should prioritize minimizing capital costs while maximizing operating-cost savings, with any design kept ready for execution at the appropriate time.

## Stabilization, Thickening, and Inventory Management

Sludge thickening and residuals handling are optimized to increase solids concentration, reduce chemical and energy use, lower hauling frequency, and prevent solids accumulation in process tanks. A gravity (or other) thickener serves as the primary solids-concentration step, producing thickened sludge suitable for transport while maintaining stable plant process performance and minimizing odor potential.

Seasonal variability (e.g., cold-weather freezing risk limiting thickening or storage flexibility) is addressed through adaptive operating strategies, with possible enhancements such as adjusting the thickening schedule or evaluating storage-tank insulation improvements reviewed with the client and incorporated into asset management and capital planning as appropriate.

A formal biosolids inventory management protocol establishes target sludge age, storage durations, and action thresholds, limiting excessive solids retention that can lead to odor generation, septicity, or downstream process instability, while maintaining operational flexibility during wet-weather events or market disruptions. Hauling schedules and solids production trends are reviewed routinely to ensure sufficient flexibility is maintained during high flows, maintenance events, or disposal constraints. Process performance is tracked through solids balance monitoring, key thickening/biosolids production KPIs, and routine equipment inspections, with operational adjustments guided by data and reviewed during weekly process meetings.

Residuals handling practices are coordinated with the site's odor control program; biosolids handling procedures, staging practices, and removal frequencies are aligned to minimize odor impacts, with mitigation measures implemented promptly if temporary staging becomes necessary.

## Hauling, Disposal, and Regulatory Compliance and Reporting

The established biosolids disposal pathway is maintained, confirming that hauling logistics, scheduling, and documentation are handled efficiently and in full regulatory compliance. Given the volatility of regional biosolids disposal markets (production volumes, hauling costs, disposal capacity), market conditions are actively tracked to anticipate changes and reduce cost risk:

- Track dry tons produced and unit hauling/disposal costs.
- Monitor market trends and regulatory developments.
- Provide early notification to the client of significant cost or regulatory changes.
- Support evaluation of alternative disposal or beneficial-reuse strategies, if needed.

Quarterly cost-trend tracking against an established cost-per-dry-ton baseline provides early visibility of market shifts; if pressures increase, operational adjustments or alternative outlets are evaluated in coordination with the client while maintaining reliable biosolids removal. All chain-of-custody documentation, biosolids production records, and disposal reports are maintained in audit-ready electronic format and integrated into the client's compliance reporting systems.

All required biosolids reports are prepared accurately and on time, with documentation of production volumes, quality parameters, and final disposition maintained in secure electronic systems and integrated into the compliance dashboard. Interstate biosolids transport and disposal documentation is managed to meet all applicable regulatory requirements across affected jurisdictions. Biosolids production rates, inventory levels, and hauling efficiency are actively managed to reduce the likelihood of reaching any contractual cost-adjustment threshold, focusing on stabilizing costs through operational optimization rather than reacting to market changes. All biosolids-related assets (thickening equipment, pumping systems, storage infrastructure) are incorporated into the asset management/CMMS system, including preventive maintenance schedules, condition monitoring, and lifecycle planning.

## Strategic Planning and Future Market Conditions

Regional biosolids markets can face tightening disposal capacity, longer hauling distances, and increasing disposal costs. To support long-term resiliency, the team works with the client to explore operational and infrastructure strategies that improve flexibility and reduce risk, which may include on- or off-site dewatering options, partnerships with regional facilities that provide processing capacity, or other approaches that expand disposal alternatives — potentially reducing hauling volumes, lowering transportation-related greenhouse gas emissions, and strengthening overall system resiliency.
