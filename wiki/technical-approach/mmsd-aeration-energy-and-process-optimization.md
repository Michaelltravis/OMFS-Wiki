# Aeration Optimization — Unlocking Energy and Process Gains at a Large WRF

**Aeration — Unlocking Energy and Process Gains.** The primary water reclamation facility has a strong track record of meeting or exceeding permit requirements with the existing configuration. However, the amount of air used for bioreactor aeration and channel mixing **provides an opportunity to further reduce energy-related operating costs**. We'll work with [CLIENT], a regional sewerage district operating two large water reclamation facilities, to continue improving these systems. Key dynamics impacting aeration blower efficiency at the facility include:

- Big bubble channel mixing will bring new anaerobic/anoxic volume to the biological process. This may improve biological phosphorus removal but could also impact the loading on the aeration basins.

- Dissolved oxygen (DO) variability observed across the aeration basins in service (See **Exhibit IV-18**) indicate that individualized basin setpoints, **improved load balance, and refined airflow distribution to the aeration basins would yield substantial energy and process benefits.**

We'll embed these operational insights into Jacobs' Intelligent O&M and hybrid optimizer digital tools to deliver near-term gains while informing [CLIENT]'s long-term aeration system upgrade strategy with defensible, data-driven recommendations. Jacobs will also identify targeted intensification measures or infrastructure improvements for implementation by [CLIENT], aimed at further enhancing aeration basin efficiency, including:

- **Expanded Blower VFDs Capabilities:** Only one blower at the facility is currently equipped with a VFD and is primarily used as the fast-start standby unit. Following the channel air improvement efforts and related optimizations, the aeration system will benefit from a more conventional most-open-valve blower control strategy. Additional VFD capabilities being implemented by [CLIENT] will enable blowers to better match real-time oxygen demand, **significantly improving overall system efficiency**.

- **Settleability Improvements:** Implementing hydrocyclones or MBAS may increase secondary clarifier capacity, allowing fewer aeration basins to operate under normal conditions and reducing air demand. **These improvements also enhance wet weather handling and provide greater operational flexibility across the system**.

**EXHIBIT IV-18. DO VARIABILITY IN AERATION BASINS HIGHLIGHTS OPPORTUNITIES FOR AERATION CONTROL IMPROVEMENTS** (graphic asset `360_007CAM_2`) — DO variability indicates opportunities for improvement in aeration control (e.g. leaks, PLC logic issues, etc.) that would reduce electricity requirements.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the rhetorical move of opening with the client's strong permit record before naming the improvement opportunity — essential in a challenger bid where the incumbent's compliance record is good. Also universal: pairing an observed data signal (DO variability) with the specific control fixes it implies, and separating what the operator will do (embed insights in digital tools, refine setpoints) from what the owner would implement capitally (VFDs, hydrocyclones/MBAS).

Pursuit-specific: every aeration dynamic named here. Confirm blower/VFD counts, whether channel mixing is being converted, and whether the client has an aeration upgrade study underway before citing them.
