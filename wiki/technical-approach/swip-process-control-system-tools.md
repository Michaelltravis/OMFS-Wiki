# Companywide Process Control System (CPCS) and Supporting Operations Tool Set

## Process control

Jacobs will implement its Companywide Process Control System (CPCS) — a data- and results-driven framework that leverages modeling, analytics, and standardized controls to optimize treatment processes. CPCS reports are reviewed by onsite staff and Jacobs' regional technical specialists to verify compliance and identify performance optimization opportunities.

Supporting the CPCS is a suite of proven tools based on decades of operational experience and industry best practices, summarized below.

## Proven operations tools

**Unit Process Control Procedures (UPCPs)** — UPCPs provide Jacobs' "operations road map," establishing key performance indicators (KPIs) and parameters the team tracks, driving process adjustments based on results, directing continuous improvement, and clearly establishing communication venues and related control of process adjustments. UPCPs also provide a level of detail for O&M personnel to make informed decisions. *Benefit:* the client gains transparency through shared UPCPs, promoting confidence in consistent compliance with all permits and effluent requirements. Documented procedures and process targets are shared with client management, operators, maintenance, and laboratory staff. The detailed information provided maximizes efficiency and minimizes the potential for errors and downtime.

**Sampling Plan** — The Sampling Plan includes a well-thought-through and clearly defined list of sampling locations, a sampling map, and parameters to be sampled at each location. It also identifies methods to be used, proper chain-of-custody techniques, and proper preservation procedures. Jacobs ensures all sampling and reporting activities comply with applicable permit requirements, providing timely and accurate submittals to regulatory agencies to demonstrate ongoing compliance. *Benefit:* a properly written and implemented sample plan is critical to compliant and efficient facility operation. Reliable, accurate data starts with representative sampling to achieve effective solids balances and process control.

**Sample Tracking Tool (STT)** — The STT is used by operations staff to monitor and track process and compliance sampling and analysis requirements. This tool is developed and implemented at the start of each year. As samples are taken and analyzed in-house or sent to a third-party laboratory, samples are checked off by the person who pulled them and then checked off the same day by a second independent party to confirm that all process and compliance samples are taken as required. *Benefit:* a key element of compliance is ensuring that samples and analyses are performed at the locations and frequencies outlined in the permit and scope of work. The STT provides the necessary quality assurance to make sure nothing is missed by the onsite team.

**Data Management** — Data collection is driven by the Sampling Plan and is collected through lab analysis or online instrumentation and entered manually or directly from SCADA. Jacobs installs and uses HachWIMS to allow for the use of Jacobs' CPCS. Jacobs utilizes a Laboratory Information Management System (LIMS) in conjunction with HachWIMS to centralize data collection, storage, and analysis. This system offers real-time access, customizable reporting, and trend analysis, which helps the client monitor water quality metrics seamlessly. *Benefit:* direct access to the data management software allows the facilities to "mine" the database for specific data. Regular reports provide the client with a quick summary of how the plant and individual unit processes are performing. Exporting data into Jacobs' CPCS allows regional support staff to analyze data and trends to assist the onsite operations staff in real-time decisions and to drive optimization.

**Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)** — SOPs are developed to ensure safe work practices and consistent operations of equipment and that systems are implemented and followed by O&M staff. SOPs typically contain precautionary safety steps, followed by startup, shutdown, and routine monitoring of the equipment, as well as the detailed information needed to complete the work properly, safely, and efficiently. *Benefit:* SOPs provide consistent and standard procedures and ensure staff safety, operations performance, and equipment reliability. Development of SOPs is an ongoing process; current SOPs in place at the facilities will be reviewed and updated as needed. Critical SOPs will be developed first; new SOPs will be developed as required.

**Operator Round Sheets** — Daily round sheets record readings, observations, and preventive maintenance tasks that typically identify potential upsets or problems with equipment before they arise. Round sheets are generated daily through the CMMS and completed by the operator at least daily. Unusual observations are documented, and the data is uploaded into the CMMS. Unusual observations or readings are reported immediately to the Operations and Compliance Manager so appropriate action can be taken in a timely manner. *Benefit:* one of the most effective ways to maintain control of the treatment process and operational equipment is to perform and document daily rounds on the treatment process. This allows the operator to make any necessary process adjustments or create maintenance work orders to proactively correct a maintenance item. Operator round sheets also identify general housekeeping actions for keeping the facility safe and clean.

**Peak Flow Tests** — A monthly test of peak membrane capacity is performed on each membrane train. Success is demonstrated by operation at design peak flow for one hour without the train switching to transmembrane pressure control. *Benefit:* the client will know that its membranes are ready to handle design peak flows.

## Reuse guidance

The CPCS framing and all seven tool descriptions (UPCPs, Sampling Plan, STT, Data Management, SOPs, Operator Round Sheets, Peak Flow Tests) are fully generic and reusable verbatim for any water/wastewater O&M pursuit — none reference the pursuit's client name or location. Swap "HachWIMS"/"LIMS" references if the target pursuit uses a different data-management platform, and drop or adapt the Peak Flow Tests entry for non-membrane treatment trains.
