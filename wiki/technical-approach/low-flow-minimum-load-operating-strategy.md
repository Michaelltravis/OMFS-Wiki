# Operating Under Low-Flow and Minimum-Load Conditions

Our process control strategy also addresses low-flow and minimum-load conditions — periods that carry their own operational risks. During sustained dry weather, reduced hydraulic loading can lead to excessive solids retention times, over-aeration relative to actual oxygen demand, and elevated detention times that promote septic conditions in underutilized basins.

- **At North Canadian**, where the three-plant configuration provides significant operational flexibility, we'll take basins out of service during low-flow periods to maintain appropriate velocities and loading rates through active treatment trains, reducing energy consumption while preventing stagnation.

- **At Deer Creek**, where organic loading already approaches design capacity during normal conditions, low-flow periods present an opportunity to recover biological stability, rebuild solids inventory, and perform targeted maintenance on blowers, filters, and clarifiers.

- **At Chisholm Creek and South Canadian**, low-flow strategies will focus on adjusting SRT targets, reducing basin service, and throttling back aeration to match actual demand — preventing the over-aerated conditions we observed at both facilities during our site visits.

In all cases, our UPCPs will define specific operating setpoints and basin configurations for low-flow conditions, so operators have clear guidance rather than defaulting to the same operating posture regardless of loading.
