# Phased No-Cost and Low-Cost Energy and Chemical Optimization Opportunities

## Collaboration on opportunities for phased energy optimization

Beyond daily optimization, **we'll collaborate with [CLIENT] to evaluate additional no-cost and low-cost operational improvements**. Based on our experience at comparable facilities, focus areas will include:

- **Pumping system optimization**—confirming pumps operate within preferred efficiency ranges and reducing unnecessary energy use across influent, effluent, and return flows
- **Solids retention time (SRT) and process optimization**—managing biomass inventory to match treatment needs and minimize associated aeration demand (see **Process Control Strategy** section for more information)
- **Aeration system optimization**—focusing on air delivery, distribution, and control strategies to match real-time process needs without over-aeration—particularly relevant at North Canadian and Deer Creek where our site observations identified potential for improved dissolved oxygen (DO) management (see **Process Control Strategy** and **SCADA/OT/Cybersecurity** sections for more information)
- **Primary treatment optimization**—maximizing removal of biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) and total suspended solids (TSS) in the most energy-efficient process step to reduce downstream aeration and solids handling demand
- **Plant water use optimization**—reducing unnecessary internal recycling that increases pumping and treatment loads
- **Chemical optimization**—data-driven polymer, ferrous chloride, and lime dosing to reduce cost while maintaining compliance
- **Solids handling efficiency**—improved cake solids performance to reduce hauling volume, fuel, and land application costs

Following baseline development, we'll **prioritize no-cost and low-cost operational improvements first**, followed by evaluation of utility incentives, grants, and cost-effective investments, where justified, under the Maintenance and Repair (M&R) or Repair and Replacement (R&R) fund framework, as briefly illustrated in **Exhibit 1-26**.
