# Settleability Optimization Strategies Table — Seven Targeted Improvement Areas

**EXHIBIT IV-17. POTENTIAL OPTIMIZATION STRATEGIES TO ADDRESS SETTLEABILITY CHALLENGES**

|**Targeted Improvement Area**|**What Jacobs Will Do**|**Expected Benefit for [CLIENT]**|
|---|---|---|
|**1. Primary Clarifier Operation**|▪ Reduce unwanted fermentation by mechanical thickening instead of thickening in clarifier<br>▪ Continue assessment of CEPT/primary filtration<br>▪ Collaborate in additional efforts aimed at increasing primary sludge contribution at the secondary-treatment facility while mitigating impacts on biosolids product quality at the drying facility through dryer mixing improvements and MHP/volatile solids reduction (VSR) technologies|▪ Lower aeration demand to improve settleability<br>▪ Increase biogas production (20% with CEPT)<br>▪ Maintain digester capacity|
|**2. Selector Zone Optimization**|▪ Strengthen anaerobic conditions using intermittent mixing<br>▪ Optimize/minimize RAS rates to reduce nitrate loading<br>▪ Balance primary fermentation with EBPR carbon needs|▪ Enhanced settleability and reduced filament growth<br>▪ Improved biological phosphorus removal performance and stability|
|**3. Controlled Low-DO Operation**|▪ Implement a structured DO profile that supports simultaneous nitrification-denitrification and limits aerobic filament growth<br>▪ Reduce nitrate returned to the selector via DO and RAS strategies<br>▪ Balance SRT to maintain nitrification while supporting EBPR|▪ Increased carbon availability for EBPR<br>▪ Reduced NO₃ loading and improved selector zone function|
|**4. Final Clarifier Hydraulics**|▪ Evaluate with [CLIENT] the benefit of a computational fluid dynamics (CFD)-based evaluation of clarifier flow patterns<br>▪ Identify targeted baffle modifications or flow-distribution improvements|▪ Increased clarifier efficiency and capacity<br>▪ Reduced risk of washout during wet-weather peaks<br>▪ Improved effluent clarity and operational stability|
|**5. Enhanced Process Monitoring**|▪ Track influent sulfides, selector SRT/DO, and C:P ratios<br>▪ Implement proactive SVI management rather than reactive troubleshooting, leveraging current [CLIENT] monitoring and applying machine learning|▪ Early detection of filament growth conditions<br>▪ More stable sludge settling performance<br>▪ Improved reliability of EBPR and secondary treatment|
|**6. Control Improvements**|▪ Review aeration control strategies, piping, valves, and PLC logic<br>▪ Apply proven digital control enhancements used successfully at similar facilities|▪ Reduced energy consumption and better airflow distribution<br>▪ More responsive biological process control<br>▪ Enhanced reliability of secondary system performance|
|**7. Intelligent O&M & Hybrid Optimizer**|▪ Apply Jacobs' Intelligent O&M and Hybrid Optimizer to refine DO setpoints, SRT targets, and primary clarifier strategies in real time<br>▪ Integrate predictive analytics with operator workflows|▪ Continuous optimization of aeration energy use<br>▪ Improved EBPR reliability and SVI stability<br>▪ Data-driven decision-making for sustained performance improvements|

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the seven-row progression from mechanical and hydraulic fixes through process control to digital optimization — it shows an operator who exhausts low-cost measures before proposing technology. Rows 2, 3, 5, and 7 apply to nearly any activated-sludge settleability problem. Pursuit-specific: the interplant product-quality row, the 20% CEPT biogas figure, and the client-name references in rows 4 and 5. Pairs with [Settleability and aeration optimization opportunities](mmsd-liquid-treatment-optimization-opportunities.md), which introduces this exhibit.
