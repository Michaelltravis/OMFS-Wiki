# Pump Station Predictive Maintenance and Condition Monitoring

Our evaluation of the pump station locations during our site visits identified a number of areas of concern that we will further investigate and identify how to address if selected, including: certain pump stations utilize outdated “can” type designs. Transitioning these to modern submersible stations is advised to improve operational efficiency, reduce maintenance needs, and enhance safety for maintenance personnel. Reliable telemetry systems are vital for real-time monitoring and efficient management of pump stations. Continuous maintenance and periodic upgrades of communication infrastructure will support prompt detection and resolution of operational issues. The emergency generators at the stations, many of which are new, will be a major focus. Ensuring continuous operation during power outages is critical. Regular maintenance and testing of emergency generators are necessary to provide reliable backup power. This includes load-bank testing ensuring generators can supply the power needed. Odor control systems at the pump stations are another focus area. Effective odor control is essential for community relations and environmental compliance. Routine inspections and timely replacement of odor control media are recommended to maintain optimal performance.

JC Solutions will implement a PdM program for [CLIENT]’s pump stations, which will significantly enhance equipment reliability and reduce unplanned downtime. Thermography: Utilize infrared thermography to detect temperature variations in electrical and mechanical components. This non-invasive technique helps identify issues such as loose connections, overloaded circuits, or failing components before they lead to equipment failure. Motor Current Signature Analysis (MCSA): Apply MCSA “Artesis” to monitor the electrical signals of motors. This method can detect electrical and mechanical faults, including rotor bar damage, misalignments, bearing failures, and vibration by analyzing current and voltage signatures without interrupting operations. These units can also be added for real-time continuous monitoring for critical assets. Vibration Analysis: Implement vibration analysis “Augury” to monitor the condition of rotating equipment. By measuring vibration patterns, this technique can identify imbalances, misalignments, and bearing defects, allowing for timely maintenance interventions. This device can also be upgraded to real-time continuous monitoring for critical assets.

Ultrasonic Testing: Incorporate ultrasonic inspection to detect early signs of wear, leaks, or structural weaknesses in components such as bearings, valves, and pipelines. This non-destructive method uses high-frequency sound waves to identify anomalies that might not be evident through other techniques. Ultrasonic testing is versatile and can be applied to various equipment types, enhancing the overall effectiveness of the PdM program. Ultrasound analysis supports leak detection, mechanical inspection, electrical inspection, electric arc flash detection, steam trap maintenance, valve testing, and acoustic precision lubrication of bearings. Precision alignment of rotating equipment effectively corrects misalignment, a leading cause of bearing failure in rotating equipment. Infrared thermography spots anomalies not otherwise visible to the eye, finding potential faults in electrical and mechanical applications before they lead to equipment failure. Lubrication/oil analysis monitors its condition and degradation of the asset, informing decision for continued operation, filtering, or oil volume change. Vibration measurement identifies misalignments or imbalances before issues or failures develop including critical, high-dollar equipment that traditional RMS vibration measurement cannot detect.

Inventory items found around the facilities will be consolidated, tracked, and put into the most effective storage facilities. Climate control for certain items will be used if needed. Facility painting and appearance by dedicated staff members will uphold the high level of standards for [CLIENT]. Integrating these PdM activities into the maintenance strategy for [CLIENT]’s pump stations will facilitate early fault detection, optimize maintenance scheduling, and extend the lifespan of critical infrastructure. Pump stations’ site appearance is very important and includes maintaining the fences and gates for public safety and security, building appearance, and landscaping. Equipment cleaning—pumps, motors, electrical enclosures.
