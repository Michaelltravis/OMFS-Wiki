# Three-Facility MBR Portfolio and Pump-Station Operating Context

Our history working with [CLIENT] facilities provides us with unique insights into these three sophisticated MBR plants. Our JC Solutions team includes individuals with direct operational experience at these facilities, giving us firsthand knowledge of both their design strengths and operational challenges. We have also drawn on our site visits and thorough review of the documentation provided during the procurement process, as well as visits in the past year to other similar Kubota MBR facilities and the Kubota research center to validate our understanding and insights. Based on those efforts, we’ve developed a comprehensive understanding of the current state of these assets, [CLIENT]'s operational philosophy, and future needs. We recognize [CLIENT]'s commitment to exceptional compliance performance and aesthetic considerations, particularly at [FACILITY B], a 15-MGD MBR facility near high-value residential areas. Our O&M approach will balance these priorities while identifying opportunities for process optimization, particularly in the areas of chemical usage, membrane management, and biosolids handling.

[FACILITY A], a 32-MGD (max month average) MBR plant, is completing an upgrade and is designed with future expansion capability to 38 MGD. The facility utilizes a comprehensive treatment process including screening, grit removal, primary clarification, biological nutrient removal, membrane filtration, UV disinfection, and post-aeration before discharge to the receiving river. [FACILITY B] features aesthetically-pleasing design elements to blend with surrounding high-value residential areas. The facility utilizes Kubota membranes and includes innovative design elements such as a self-cleaning trench-style pump station. The plant discharges to the receiving river and is located in close proximity to a water intake facility. [FACILITY C], a smaller facility rated at 2.6 MGD (annual average capacity), currently operates at approximately 0.8 MGD. The plant was converted from a conventional oxidation ditch system to an MBR process, repurposing existing structures including oxidation ditches for bioreactors and former clarifiers for post-anoxic zones. Unlike the other facilities, this plant is physically located in an adjacent county but serves [CLIENT].

Major assets include 28 wastewater pump stations in the [CLIENT] service area, plus associated forcemain air release valves; 5 potable-water pump stations; and land-application-system/reuse pump stations at [FACILITY B] and [FACILITY C]. The wastewater pump stations pump influent to the three treatment facilities. The scope of services requires operating and maintaining these pump stations on a 24-hour basis, including wet-well cleaning (minimum quarterly), inspections, and ensuring continuous operation to prevent sanitary sewer overflows. There are both wastewater pump stations and potable-water pump stations, though work on the latter is limited to maintenance and inspection services only, not operations.
