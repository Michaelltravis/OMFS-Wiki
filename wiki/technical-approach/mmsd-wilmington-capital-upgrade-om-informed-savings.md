# O&M-Informed Capital Upgrade at Wilmington Delivers 20% Cost Savings

**CAPITAL PROJECT UPGRADE INFORMED BY O&M AT WILMINGTON DELIVERS 20% COST SAVINGS**

The secondary treatment upgrades at Wilmington Wastewater Treatment Facility posed challenges similar to [CLIENT]'s $91M aeration upgrade at its larger water reclamation facility. The four-phase Wilmington project was originally planned to begin with a blower replacement to achieve energy savings. However, based on feedback from our operators, we elected to design a diffuser replacement first, which would enable the blowers to provide the desired results. During 90% design review, our operators proposed compressing the schedule to work on two aeration basins at a time, instead of one. Our process engineers verified that we could maintain compliance during MMAD flows and loads. As a contingency plan, we set up a temporary polymer to promote settling during higher flow/loads. Our operators' suggestion reduced the construction schedule by 40% and reduced costs by 20%.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the story beat — operators challenge the planned sequence of a capital project, engineers verify compliance can be held, a contingency is put in place, and the client gets a shorter schedule and a lower cost. It is the strongest short-form evidence in this range for the claim that an integrated operator-plus-engineer team changes capital outcomes. It works as a callout box beside any capital-support or asset-management narrative.

Pursuit-specific: the comparison anchor — here, the client's $91M aeration upgrade at its larger facility. Swap that for a capital project the target client is actively planning so the analogy lands; without a real anchor the story reads as generic past performance.
