# Compliance Recovery at a Multi-Train Activated Sludge Plant — Stabilize First, Optimize Second

At North Canadian, our process control strategy will focus first on re-establishing and sustaining permit compliance through stabilization of biological treatment, secondary clarification, and hydraulic performance. Past compliance challenges have been driven by hydraulic instability, equipment reliability issues, and limited operational flexibility, which together reduced the plant's ability to manage solids inventory and respond effectively to wet weather events. The immediate objective is to stabilize the process, protect clarifiers, and restore predictable performance under both dry- and wet-weather conditions.

**A core element of our compliance recovery strategy will be implementing SRT-based wasting.** This approach links daily wasting volumes directly to a defined biological target rather than relying on fixed schedules or reactive adjustments. SRT-based wasting improves control of biological activity, reduces the risk of sudden biomass loss or over-accumulation, and gives operators a specific, measurable quantity of solids to waste each day. By maintaining a controlled solids inventory, operators will better protect secondary clarifier performance, reduce the likelihood of TSS and BOD excursions, and align sludge production with downstream solids handling and dewatering capacity, as illustrated in **Exhibit 1-13**.

**EXHIBIT 1-13.** *How SRT-based Wasting Supports Better Clarifier Performance, Reduces Excursions, and Stabilizes Solids Production* (graphic 112_009385) — The top graph shows reactive wasting with volatile swings in solids inventory — over-accumulation followed by urgent wasting and washout risk, leading to TSS/BOD excursions and clarifier issues. The bottom graphic demonstrates how SRT-based wasting keeps solids inventory within a tight target range, producing stable clarifier performance and predictable sludge production. Jacobs' operators are given exact daily values to waste each day to ensure solids volumes stay in target.

Secondary clarifier performance will be further strengthened through routine sludge blanket monitoring and trending, combined with ongoing solids flux and state point analysis. These tools will help operators understand how MLSS concentration and settling characteristics affect available clarifier capacity, particularly during wet weather. This information will guide decisions on wasting, flow routing, and step feed operation to prevent hydraulic overload and solids washout.

DO monitoring in the aeration basins will be expanded to address the uneven air distribution we observed — poor DO at the front of the train and excessive DO at the tail end. Increased DO profiling will allow operators to confirm that aeration is being applied where it's needed, prevent localized low oxygen conditions that contribute to process instability or odor generation, and identify whether all basins need to remain in service at current loading levels. In the near term, enhanced monitoring will support day-to-day operational awareness. Over time, the data will establish a reliable aeration performance baseline, positioning the facility for future optimization efforts, including refined aeration strategies and potential DO-based control once process stability and instrumentation reliability are firmly established.

The over-aeration conditions observed across the system represent one of the most direct opportunities to reduce energy consumption. Aeration typically accounts for the largest share of electricity use at activated sludge facilities, and [CLIENT]'s baseline data confirms significant energy intensity — averaging approximately 1,964 kWh/MG at North Canadian/Witcher. Our DO monitoring and optimization efforts will tie directly into the required Energy Efficiency Plan. By right-sizing aeration to actual biological demand, we'll work toward maintaining energy usage within the +5% variance of the established baseline while improving treatment performance.

As compliance stability is achieved, near term enhancements will focus on wet weather resilience and process flexibility. Step feed operation will be evaluated and implemented as appropriate to better distribute organic loading during peak flow events and protect clarifier performance. Existing ferric or ferrous systems will be assessed for chemically enhanced primary treatment during extreme wet weather to reduce solids and organic loading to the activated sludge process and provide additional protection against washout.

Longer term process control improvements will be pursued once consistent compliance is demonstrated. These include evaluating opportunities to add or reintroduce anoxic selector zones to improve settling and reduce aeration demand, assessing underutilized basins for solids reduction or aerobic digestion, and selectively rehabilitating filtration assets to add process stability during upset conditions. All improvements will be prioritized based on measurable benefits to compliance with reliability and operational sustainability.
