# On-Site Hypochlorite Generation (OSHG) Conversion — Operating Experience and Transition Considerations

The planned transition to OSHG at multiple [CLIENT] facilities is one of the most operationally significant changes in this contract. It eliminates gaseous chlorine—and with it, the PSM/RMP compliance requirements at North Canadian and Deer Creek—while introducing a fundamentally different chemical management approach. Jacobs brings direct, relevant experience to this transition. **See Section 3 – Experience and Qualifications of the Firm and Management Team** for **detailed discussion of our OSHG O&M experience.**

Regardless of the specific systems [CLIENT] ultimately selects for Deer Creek and North Canadian, the core operational and maintenance considerations are consistent across OSHG technology. Our experience gives us direct knowledge of:

- **Electrolytic cell maintenance and replacement cycles**, including the proprietary cartridge systems (such as PSI/Microclor® units, where cartridges cost approximately $20,000 each) that require careful lifecycle management and warranty tracking.
- **Brine quality management and softening system upkeep**—critical for protecting cell life and maintaining consistent hypochlorite production.
- **Sodium hypochlorite storage and degradation management**, including strength monitoring, turnover rate optimization, and temperature control to maintain effective chlorine concentration.
- **Hydrogen gas ventilation and safety monitoring**—a key safety consideration for on-site generation systems that require dedicated monitoring and response protocols.
- **Integration with existing disinfection control systems and SCADA**—transitioning from gas feed control to liquid chemical dosing with automated residual feedback.
- **PSM/RMP transition**—a significant benefit of the conversion is the elimination of gaseous chlorine PSM requirements at both North Canadian and Deer Creek. Currently, both facilities maintain PSM/RMP programs for their chlorine gas systems. Once OSHG replaces gas, those requirements and their associated compliance burden, training requirements, and safety risk are eliminated. This is a meaningful improvement in both worker safety and regulatory simplification for [CLIENT].

We will leverage the direct operating experience we'll build at South Canadian's new PSI/Microclor® units (online by contract start) to inform our integration planning for the Deer Creek and North Canadian installations. This gives us facility-specific knowledge of the same equipment family before the next generation systems are commissioned.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the six-item list of OSHG operating considerations (cell/cartridge lifecycle, brine quality and softening, hypochlorite storage and degradation, hydrogen ventilation, SCADA and residual control integration, PSM/RMP elimination) reads as operator credibility in any disinfection-conversion pursuit. The closing move — using experience on an already-commissioned unit of the same equipment family to de-risk later installations — is reusable wherever a client is phasing a technology across facilities.

Pursuit-specific: the facility names, the vendor (PSI/Microclor®), the $20,000 cartridge figure, and the claim that PSM/RMP goes away at two named plants. Verify which facilities actually carry PSM/RMP today before repeating the elimination benefit.
