# Direct Potable Reuse Readiness — Additional Monitoring Value-Add and Named Expert Bench

## Preparing for Direct Potable Reuse

[Firm] understands [CLIENT] plans to incorporate direct potable reuse (DPR) into its water supply portfolio. DPR will likely require additional monitoring beyond the requirements for indirect potable reuse (IPR), especially when integrating two existing facilities. [Firm] proposes a value-added task to evaluate potential additional monitoring and possibly install additional analyzers at [FACILITY] and at [SECOND FACILITY]. The additional data collection taken today could build a data repository to give regulators confidence in a future DPR scenario.

## Named DPR/IPR Expert Bench

[Firm] is supported by a deep bench of process and planning experts in IPR and DPR. Melanie Holmer, PE, served on the WateReuse California Board of Directors and its DPR working group, combing through every aspect and iteration of the draft and final DPR regulations, in addition to development of DPR project or permit planning for Pure Water Los Angeles, Pure Water Southern California, and Valley Water. Larry Schimmoller, PE, is the Process Lead for the DC Tillman Progressive Design-Build and engaged with LADWP on innovative research for alternative DPR treatment trains. Jim Lozier, PE, and Mike Hwang, PE, are recognized experts in membrane filtration and RO, and Scott Levesque, PE, and Colin Fitzgerald, PE, bring industry expertise in MBR and water resource recovery, respectively.

## Reuse guidance

The "preparing for DPR" framing (offering forward monitoring/data-repository work ahead of an anticipated regulatory requirement) is a reusable pattern for any client signaling a future DPR transition — position it as building regulatory confidence today rather than waiting for the requirement to be codified. The named-expert paragraph is a strong differentiator for potable reuse pursuits — keep names, titles, and credentials verbatim (per wiki policy, staff names are never anonymized), but confirm each person's current availability/role and willingness to be cited before reuse, and update the specific named IPR/DPR projects (Pure Water Los Angeles, Pure Water Southern California, Valley Water, DC Tillman) if a given expert's project history has changed.
