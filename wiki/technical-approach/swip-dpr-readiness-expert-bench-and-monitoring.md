# Direct Potable Reuse Readiness — Additional Monitoring Value-Add and Named Expert Bench

## Preparing for Direct Potable Reuse

We understand [CLIENT] plans to incorporate DPR into its water supply portfolio. DPR will likely require additional monitoring beyond the requirements for IPR, especially when integrating two existing facilities. We propose a value-added task to allow for [Firm] to evaluate potential additional monitoring and possibly install additional analyzers at [FACILITY] and at [SECOND FACILITY]. The additional data collection taken today could build a data repository to give regulators confidence in a future DPR scenario.

## Named DPR/IPR expert bench

[Firm] is supported by a deep bench of process and planning experts in IPR and DPR. Melanie Holmer, PE, served on the WateReuse California Board of Directors and its DPR working group, combing through every aspect and iteration of the draft and final DPR regulations, in addition to development of DPR project or permit planning for Pure Water Los Angeles, Pure Water Southern California, and Valley Water. Larry Schimmoller, PE, is the Process Lead for the DC Tillman Progressive Design-Build and engaged with LADWP on innovative research for alternative DPR treatment trains. Jim Lozier, PE, and Mike Hwang, PE, are recognized experts in membrane filtration and RO, and Scott Levesque, PE, and Colin Fitzgerald, PE, bring industry expertise in MBR and water resource recovery, respectively.

## Reuse guidance

Two reusable moves sit in this passage. The first is the "prepare for DPR now" offer: rather than waiting for the client to be told what extra monitoring a future DPR permit will demand, the proposal offers to evaluate the additional monitoring and install analyzers today, so the client arrives at the regulator with a data repository already built. That framing transfers to any client signaling a future regulatory step-change — position it as building regulatory confidence early rather than as scope growth. The second is the named-expert paragraph, which works because each name carries a specific, checkable credential and a named program (WateReuse California Board of Directors and its DPR working group; Pure Water Los Angeles; Pure Water Southern California; Valley Water; DC Tillman Progressive Design-Build; LADWP alternative DPR treatment train research) instead of a generic claim of depth. Keep names, titles, and credentials verbatim per wiki policy, but confirm each person's current role, availability, and willingness to be cited, and refresh the named programs if a given expert's history has changed. Replace [FACILITY] and [SECOND FACILITY] with the actual facility names for the target pursuit; the passage assumes two interconnected plants, so if the target has only one site, drop the integration clause rather than inventing a second facility.
