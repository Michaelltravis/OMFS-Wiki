# Continual Improvement Loops and the Preventive Maintenance Program

## Continual Improvement and Learning Loops

We'll institutionalize learning at every level with monthly KPI reviews with the Maintenance Committee, quarterly Preventive Maintenance Optimization (PMO) checkpoints, annual internal audits by dedicated technical experts, and after-action reviews for major events. All process changes, including task edits, new PdM routes, and priority rule adjustments, follow change control through governance, so improvements stick and institutional knowledge increases rather than erodes. **Our objective is to transition [CLIENT] — a southcentral US municipal water utility trust operating four wastewater treatment plants and a major pump station — from reactive maintenance to a predominantly planned environment, where most work is scheduled in advance, and backlog is actively managed.**

## Preventive Maintenance Program

PM is the backbone of asset protection — but only when it's calibrated to the actual condition and criticality of the equipment. Over-maintaining wastes resources; under-maintaining invites failure. We will implement a PMO strategy that uses asset data, condition monitoring results, and operating analytics to right-size PM frequencies for every asset class at every facility. **All maintenance work is planned, prioritized, and scheduled through a disciplined weekly planning process that aligns available resources to risk-based priorities and KPI commitments.**

PM scheduling and execution will flow through NexGen, with automated work order generation based on manufacturer recommendations adjusted for site-specific conditions. At [CLIENT], those adjustments matter more than most:

- H₂S exposure at North Canadian and Deer Creek accelerates corrosion and component wear.
- Seasonal wet weather events drive peak loading on grit and pumping systems.
- The introduction of new CIP equipment at South Canadian will require manufacturer-prescribed warranty maintenance on a strict schedule.

We'll integrate structured lubrication management, including contamination control and oil analysis, directly into the PM program rather than treating it as a standalone effort. We'll continue performing PM tasks on equipment while out of service, and per the Treatment Process Rotation Plan requirements. [CLIENT]'s right to observe PM task execution is understood and welcomed. **Transparency in maintenance delivery builds the trust this program depends on.**

**Exhibit — Jacobs' Risk Management System** (graphic asset ID 166_009385). The system is structured to address asset performance, reliability, lifecycle risks (through prioritization), condition monitoring, lifecycle analysis, and contingency planning. Its process comprises regular condition assessments, predictive maintenance, and risk profile updates. Its outcomes are enhanced performance, optimized investments, ensured safety compliance, and minimized lifecycle costs while maximizing asset value. It is measured with KPIs and feedback loops that drive continuous improvement.

Universal: the four-part improvement cadence and change control commitment; the PMO right-sizing argument; the weekly planning discipline; lubrication management folded into PM rather than run separately; the welcome extended to client observation of PM execution; and the risk management system exhibit's structure-process-outcomes-measures framing.

Pursuit-specific: the three PM-frequency adjustment drivers and the facility names they cite, the CMMS platform name, the Maintenance Committee's title, and any contractual process rotation plan.
