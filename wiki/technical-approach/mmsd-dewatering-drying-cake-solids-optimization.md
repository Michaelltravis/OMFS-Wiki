# Dewatering and Drying Optimization — Cake Solids, Mixing, and Backup Dewatering

**Maximize the share of digested biosolids sent to the heat-drying process—beyond the current ~30–35% limit—while maintaining nitrogen content and product quality.** To increase digested biosolids feed, we first need to manage the factors that currently limit how much can be blended into the total dryer feed. The key risks are changes in pellet quality, higher chaff and dust production, and a drop in product nitrogen content. To address these issues, we propose a pilot study to evaluate replacing the existing dryer screw mixers with a more effective pug mill mixer equipped with VFD drives. We understand [CLIENT], a regional sewerage district operating two large water reclamation facilities, will soon study adding new dryers at the second facility, and those new dryers may include improved cake mixing. However, it may be more cost-effective to upgrade mixing in the existing drying and dewatering (D&D) facility now, rather than waiting for construction of a new dryer facility.

**Increasing the concentration of dryer cake feed solids to reduce energy needed for drying.** The energy required to produce the heat-dried biosolids product could be more than half of the energy used by [CLIENT]. We estimate that for every 1% increase in cake solids, $350K/year in purchased natural gas can be saved. In addition, increasing the cake solids would potentially increase dryer capacity, resulting in more available dryers and more reliable product production.

[CLIENT], along with Jacobs team member Marty Dierker, is currently running tests on the feasibility of increasing cake dryness. Thickening and dewatering chemicals; thickened and dewatered sludge concentrations; increased chaff and dust production; pellet shape and hardness; and dryer throughput could each adversely affect cake solids and product quality. Adding a more efficient pugmill mixer, as discussed above, could help address some of these issues, but must be carefully evaluated and tested to ensure product quality is not compromised.

Jacobs' experience optimizing belt filter press performance and reducing polymer/ferric dose at several similar facilities can also be applied to [CLIENT] operations. We suggest [CLIENT] explore using an online analyzer on the feed sludge line of the common belt filter press, which would provide **more accurate pacing of polymer to mass, rather than flow and polymer dose, and reduce required operator attention. In addition, we recommend continuing [CLIENT]'s work with vendors to determine the optimum type and dose of polymer.**

**Improving Dewatering Reliability at the Second Facility.** When the D&D facility is shut down for planned maintenance, an alternative method of biosolids removal is required. The plate and frame presses at the second water reclamation facility are typically used for dewatering, and that cake is land applied or landfilled. The plate and frame presses, however, are challenging and costly to operate and maintain. We'll purchase a portable belt filter press to be stationed and ready at the facility to supply dewatering on a temporary basis when necessary. Once the system is operational, the dewatered cake would be transported into storage for temporary staging until disposal or Class B land application. This provides a more reliable, lower cost solids disposal method compared to the plate and frame presses or leasing dewatering equipment from a third party and ensures availability of temporary dewatering when needed, especially as drying assets age.

*Plate and frame are challenging to operate and expensive to maintain. Jacobs brings alternatives to improve reliability.*
