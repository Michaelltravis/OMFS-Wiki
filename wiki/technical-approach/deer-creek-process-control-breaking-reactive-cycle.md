# Breaking the Cycle of Reactive Operations

At Deer Creek, the process control strategy will focus on restoring and maintaining compliance through improved solids handling, filtration reliability, and disinfection stability. Previous permit excursions have largely been tied to inconsistent wasting practices, manual filtration operation, and unstable disinfection control during flow variability. The site visit confirmed these conditions: MLSS was light with excessive DO and early-stage foaming, RAS pumps were running in static mode rather than flow-paced, and the sand filters were being backwashed manually.

Initial actions will include implementing SRT-based wasting to stabilize biological activity and give operators predictable, day-to-day control of solids inventory. This will support stable secondary clarifier performance, more consistent sludge management, and improved coordination with downstream solids handling. Filtration systems will be returned to full automated control with routine turbidity and TSS monitoring to optimize backwash cycles and maintain consistent effluent quality. Disinfection reliability will be improved by tying chlorine and sulfur dioxide pacing directly to process flows and treatment conditions.

We recognize that long-term CIP improvements are planned to address the severe H₂S conditions in the belt press room. In the short term, our focus will be on operational adjustments that we can implement immediately. We'll evaluate existing solids handling strategies for both WAS and primary sludge to reduce excessive detention times and uneven loading to the sludge holding tanks, which currently receive both streams with no mixing or aeration, creating the septic conditions that drive H₂S levels. By improving the timing, blending, and frequency of sludge transfers and pressing operations, we'll limit septic conditions and reduce sulfide generation, providing rapid relief from localized odor impacts while longer-term solutions are evaluated.

Near term enhancements will include re-implementing the existing anoxic selector zones — offline since 2019 but with submersible mixers replaced and ready to return to service — to improve settling performance and reduce aeration demand. Expanded DO monitoring will address the over-aeration observed during our site visit (baseline energy intensity averages 1,763 kWh/MG at Deer Creek) while laying out the groundwork for future aeration control optimization. Longer-term efforts will focus on refining aeration efficiency, improving solids handling reliability, and using operating data to enhance performance while maintaining compliance margins.
