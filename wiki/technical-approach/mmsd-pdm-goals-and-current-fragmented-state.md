# Predictive Maintenance Goals and the Current Fragmented PdM State

## 1. Jacobs understands and agrees with [CLIENT]'s goals for PdM

Today's PdM environment at [CLIENT], a regional sewerage district operating two large water reclamation facilities and a regional conveyance system, is fragmented. Multiple vendors provide vibration, oil, and OEM-specific analytics, but results are not consistently visible, comparable, or actionable. [CLIENT] recognizes that its current data architecture does not yet support asset-level traceability of condition, work history, and failure modes—limiting the value of predictive analytics. [CLIENT] is therefore seeking a PdM program that consolidates disparate data sources, removes black-box vendor dependencies, strengthens data quality, and produces reliable, automated insights tied to specific assets and components.

Jacobs understands that [CLIENT] wants a **coherent, transparent, and scalable strategy** for PdM—one that begins with the assets of highest consequence and expands as value is demonstrated. [CLIENT] expects Level 3 PdM to be priced separately from the base O&M fee, consistent with the RFP, and to leverage its existing infrastructure rather than adding burdensome hardware or software. The program must align with [CLIENT]'s criticality rankings, integrate analytics into Jacobs' workflows, and support [CLIENT]'s formal governance bodies through clear reporting and structured decision-making.

Jacobs' approach reflects [CLIENT]'s goals: PdM architecture that builds on [CLIENT]'s current systems, elevates the value of data they already collect, and adds targeted advanced technologies where they create meaningful gains in reliability, cost efficiency, and operational resilience.

## 3. Current PdM at [CLIENT]

[CLIENT] already performs important PdM activities, but they remain siloed across multiple vendor platforms. Vibration and oil analyses cover more than 200 assets, while OEM systems for equipment such as Solar Turbines, Caterpillar engines, and Bentley aeration provide additional diagnostic insights. Although these datasets contain valuable information, they are not consistently integrated into maintenance decision-making. Reports may not be reviewed in real time, and corrective work is not always tied to specific assets within the CMMS.

Today, many maintenance activities are recorded at higher-level locations rather than at the individual asset or component level. This limits [CLIENT]'s ability to correlate PdM findings with past work, trend condition data over time, or identify systemic degradation patterns. A key benefit of the Level 3 PdM program is the opportunity to strengthen asset-level documentation within the CMMS, improving the visibility, traceability, and long-term value of PdM insights.

Jacobs' Level 3 PdM program addresses these challenges directly by consolidating all available PdM inputs, strengthening data discipline, and making insights visible and actionable through the CMMS.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the three-move structure — name the fragmentation, restate the client's goals in their own terms, then promise architecture that builds on what they already own. Also universal: the "records at location level, not asset level" diagnosis, which is true at most utilities and is the hook for asset-level CMMS discipline. Pursuit-specific: asset counts, OEM platform names, and the separately-priced-scope framing.
