# Membrane Cleaning, Alkalinity Addition, and Mixed-Liquor Filterability

**[FACILITY B] Sodium Hypochlorite.** JC Solutions predicts that the amount of sodium hypochlorite required for membrane cleaning is 14 gal./MG. Additional sodium hypochlorite is needed for odor control at 10 gal./MG. As noted above, the incumbent is not using sodium hydroxide for odor control and may not be using the correct amount of sodium hypochlorite.

**[FACILITY B] Citric Acid.** We predict using 6 gal./MG of citric acid.

The membranes at all three plants are cleaned using sodium hypochlorite (to remove organic foulants) and citric acid (to remove inorganic foulants).

**[FACILITY C] Sodium Hypochlorite.** JC Solutions predicts that sodium hypochlorite for membrane cleaning is 18 gal./MG. Additional sodium hypochlorite (whether liquid or pellets) is needed for reuse water at 36 gal./MG.

**[FACILITY C] Citric Acid.** Our predicted amount of citric acid is 11 gal./MG.

**[FACILITY A] Sodium Hypochlorite.** The membranes are too new for [CLIENT] to have learned how much chemical will be required long term for membrane cleaning. Our predicted usage is based on two cleanings per year using sodium hypochlorite and one clean per year using citric acid. The amount of each chemical per submerged membrane unit (SMU) is based on quantities listed in Kubota’s “Submerged Membrane Unit Instruction Manual (SP Type).” Predicted usage of sodium hypochlorite for membrane cleaning is 30 gal./MG, before adding the additional amount of sodium hypochlorite required for odor control at 8 gal./MG.

**[FACILITY A] Citric Acid.** Our predicted amount of citric acid is 1 gal./MG.

Monthly reports include influent data for BOD and TSS but lack data for total Kjeldahl nitrogen (TKN), ammonia, and alkalinity. While historical measurements are those required by the NPDES permit, there is insufficient information on which to base process simulations using historical wastewater characteristics. Using the original design basis for TKN, ammonia, and alkalinity, process simulation indicates that far more sodium hydroxide would be required for alkalinity addition at 82 gal./MG. Our figure already considers the sodium hydroxide contained in Phos-Zorb. In fact, high historical usage of Phos-Zorb at this plant suggests that the incumbent may be overfeeding Phos-Zorb for alkalinity addition, which is not a cost-effective operating strategy.

While sludge settleability is not important to an MBR process, mixed liquor filterability is because it’s affected by operation of the biological process, especially temperature and solids retention time (SRT). Longer SRT is needed at lower temperature to ensure adequate removal of organic foulants. In addition, the best filterability is achieved when surface wasting is practiced.

[FACILITY B] is designed for effective surface wasting, with mixed liquor from all bioreactors passing the foam wasting pump station before it splits to the membrane tanks. Wasting can also be done from RAS, which is at a higher concentration. While surface wasting is beneficial to plant housekeeping (by avoiding foam) and membrane performance (by improving filterability), sludge wasting from RAS is beneficial to dewatering because higher dewatering feed concentration may increase cake concentration and reduce disposal costs. We plan to begin plant operation in the same manner as the incumbent and gradually shift toward surface wasting, ultimately finding the best balance between the two wasting locations.

[FACILITY A] also has a foam wasting pump station between the bioreactors and membrane tanks, but all the mixed liquor does not pass this point. Surface wasting at [FACILITY A] will be less effective than at [FACILITY B], but it may be possible to compensate by operating at longer SRT or by operating more membrane tanks. [FACILITY A] also has sludge wasting from RAS. As with our approach at [FACILITY B], Jacobs will begin plant operation in the same manner as the incumbent and then gradually shift toward surface wasting to find the best balance between wasting locations.

[FACILITY C] does not appear to be equipped for surface wasting, and JC Solutions will investigate how this useful feature could be added. **How It Benefits [CLIENT]:** Supports long-term membrane health and maintenance cost reductions.
