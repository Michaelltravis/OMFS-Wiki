# Exhibit 1-1 Matrix — Wet Weather and Solids Management

### Wet Weather and Solids Management

| Understanding of [CLIENT]'s Goals | Key Current/Future Challenges | Jacobs' Strategies to Deliver Results |
|---|---|---|
| **Manage peak wet weather flows across the system, including Witcher Pump Station FEBs and wet weather basins at NC, DC, and SC** | **Wet weather events can exceed NC's rated flow capacity, meaning FEBs and wet weather basins are central to the operating strategy;** Witcher FEB use is discouraged due to odor concerns near a neighboring school; coordinated drawdown across geographically dispersed facilities (25–50 minutes apart).<br>**Wet weather basin sludge septicity:** settled sludge accumulates in NC and Witcher wet weather basins during events and becomes septic, driving significant H₂S generation when basins are returned to service.<br>**COM-4** carries 1.25% of Fixed Monthly Fee-at-risk. | **Coordinated wet weather management procedures** covering proactive preparation, real-time flow balancing, and post-event drawdown across all facilities.<br>**Post-event drawdown and basin cleaning** initiated promptly per Schedule 3 (drain and clean within 7 days of the end of wet weather storage need; 21-day limit triggers fees at risk). Specific basin management and drawdown sequencing protocols to be developed.<br>**Cross-facility training and tabletop drills** on coordinated drawdown and wet weather protocols, with engineering support from Jacobs' water resource specialists. |
| **Stabilize and strengthen the biosolids and residuals program, including land application across ~170 permitted sites (~13,500 acres), inter-facility sludge transport, Hefner WTP residuals management, and septage receiving** | **Planned transition from land application to landfill disposal targeted for 2029;** DC biosolids heat drying facility under construction (introducing new odor and energy management considerations to plan for through the Operational Integration Plan under Schedule 18).<br>**Current biosolids handling is affected by equipment availability** — belt press shutdowns at NC due to H₂S, paper/rag contamination from failed grit and screening systems.<br>**CC sludge is transported to DC or NC for processing,** since on-site dewatering is not part of the current facility.<br>**3-day pad storage limit;** Sensitive Sites list required for land application locations with odor risk.<br>**Plastics and foreign material in NC lime-stabilized biosolids** create landowner nuisance and compliance risk for the land application program; current debris removal equipment performance is limited.<br>**COM-5** carries 1.0% of Fixed Monthly Fee-at-risk. | **Pursue landfill disposal as the near-term path** — reduces equipment maintenance burden, simplifies odor management, and eases compliance with the 3-day storage requirement.<br>**Integrated Solids Management Plan addressing** inter-facility sludge logistics and wet weather contingency planning.<br>**Landfill capacity validation and pricing;** transition plan from land application to landfill; heat dryer operation integration and odor mitigation approach to be developed.<br>**Restore debris removal equipment at NC** to eliminate foreign material from biosolids before land application; evaluate additional solutions with [CLIENT] if existing equipment can't meet performance requirements.<br>**CC liquid hauling optimization:** evaluate thickening and mobile dewatering options to reduce load volume, hauling cost, and community/traffic impact.<br>**Operator training** on inter-facility sludge logistics, heat dryer operation, and landfill disposal protocols supported by Jacobs' biosolids and solids management experts. |
