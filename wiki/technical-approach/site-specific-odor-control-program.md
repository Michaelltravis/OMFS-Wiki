# Site-Specific Odor Control Program and Evaluation Framework

For a facility located within a sensitive setting with homes close to the facility boundary, odor conditions influenced by long force mains, saltwater intrusion, and grease accumulation (contributing to hydrogen sulfide generation in pump stations and portions of the collection system) are addressed through a Site-Specific Odor Control Plan that proactively manages odors, minimizes community impacts, and continuously refines control strategies using monitoring data and operational observations.

## Five-Step Odor/Corrosion Evaluation Framework

A structured evaluation and improvement framework quantifies odor and corrosion conditions, establishes acceptable impact thresholds, evaluates operational and treatment alternatives, and supports selection of appropriate control strategies:

1. **Quantify Odor & Corrosion** — sampling and measurements; sewer process modeling.
2. **Establish Goals: Acceptable Odor/Corrosion Impacts** — define acceptable level of odor and desired sewer pipe life.
3. **Determine Impacts** — air dispersion modeling; odor impacts; corrosion rates.
4. **Evaluate Control Alternatives** — vapor-phase, liquid-phase, and control-through-design options; operational control; lifecycle cost estimates.
5. **Select Alternative & Conceptual Design.**

## Proactive Monitoring and Early Detection

A structured odor monitoring program focuses on early detection and prevention. Routine odor patrols are conducted at the treatment facility, pump stations, force main discharge locations, and facility perimeter areas, with increased frequency during warm weather and low-flow conditions when detention times rise and septicity risks are greatest. Operators use portable hydrogen sulfide detection equipment and field observations to assess odor intensity and potential off-site migration; monitoring data, weather conditions, and observations are documented and reviewed for trends. Internal hydrogen sulfide action thresholds trigger proactive operational response before off-site odor impacts occur. Where appropriate, installation of fixed hydrogen sulfide sensors at strategic locations is evaluated to support real-time monitoring and improved process control.

## Treatment Facility Odor Management

Within the treatment facility, odor generation is minimized through optimized process control and solids management. Dissolved oxygen (DO) levels are maintained to prevent septic conditions, sludge age and solids retention time are managed to reduce sulfide formation, and biosolids processing frequency is adjusted as needed to limit storage duration. The performance and capacity of existing ventilation and odor control systems serving headworks, thickening areas, and sludge storage infrastructure are evaluated, with particular attention to confirming adequate air movement in confined areas (e.g., a gravity thickener) that may indicate insufficient ventilation or undersized odor treatment systems. Where warranted, operational improvements are identified and potential capital upgrades recommended through the CIP process.

## Collection System and Pump Station Odor Control

Long force mains and pump stations are major contributors to hydrogen sulfide formation and odor risk. Wet wells are inspected routinely for grease buildup and septicity, and cleaning frequencies adjusted proactively; pump cycling strategies are optimized to reduce detention time and limit hydrogen sulfide generation. Existing chemical treatment practices for hydrogen sulfide and FOG control (dosing practices, feed locations, chemical selection) are reviewed using industry best practices to verify that dosing rates are appropriate and not excessive relative to sulfide loading conditions, with current odor-control chemistries/technologies monitored, controlled, and periodically evaluated and feed locations/dosing rates/cleaning triggers adjusted based on measured conditions and observed results.

Real-time monitoring technology deployed for I/I purposes (e.g., manhole-mounted units) can also be modified/paired with specialized hydrogen sulfide sensors, providing reliable, real-time measurement of hydrogen sulfide levels over extended periods. This supports more accurate dosing that reduces chemical use, minimizes odor, and extends the life of collection system assets, and can serve as a diagnostic tool to help minimize or eliminate the use of certain chemical dosing programs. Hydrogen sulfide trends are reviewed as part of preventive maintenance planning to support corrosion prevention and long-term asset preservation.

## Rapid Complaint Response and Investigation

All odor complaints are responded to within 1 hour of notification. Upon receipt of a complaint, qualified personnel assess the reported location, document odor characteristics and weather conditions, and review plant and collection system operating conditions, including pump station performance, wet well conditions, and force main detention times. Corrective actions are implemented promptly when warranted. All complaints and responses are logged, and summaries of odor monitoring activities, investigations, and corrective actions are provided to the client monthly, with the project manager serving as the primary point of contact to ensure timely communication and transparency.

## Documentation and Reporting

Detailed records of monitoring activities, complaints, corrective actions, and chemical usage are maintained, and odor trends are reviewed periodically to identify recurring conditions and refine control strategies. This proactive, responsive, and data-driven approach protects neighborhood quality of life while maintaining regulatory compliance and operational excellence, and it also supports identification of equipment improvement needs.

## Long-Term, Sustainable Odor Control Improvements

While the odor control plan focuses on effective management of existing infrastructure, additional monitoring, process modeling, or engineering evaluation may be recommended where operational measures alone are insufficient to better quantify odor sources and determine the most effective long-term mitigation strategies. Evaluations may include sewer process modeling, air dispersion analysis, or lifecycle evaluation of alternative odor control technologies. Recommended improvements are coordinated with the client through the CIP process to ensure odor mitigation strategies align with operational needs, infrastructure protection, and fiscal priorities.

## Reuse guidance

Tailor per pursuit: confirm current names/availability of chemical dosing technologies and sensor products before citing them; only include the coastal/saltwater framing for applicable geographies.
