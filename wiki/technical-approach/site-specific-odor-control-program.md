# Site-Specific Odor Control Program and Evaluation Framework

[CLIENT]'s WWTF — a coastal New England municipal facility — is located within a sensitive coastal setting with homes close to the facility boundary. Odor conditions in the community are influenced by long force mains, saltwater intrusion, and grease accumulation, which contribute to hydrogen sulfide generation in pump stations and portions of the collection system. Consistent with Section 1.5 of the RFP, Jacobs will implement a **Site-Specific Odor Control Plan** to proactively manage odors, minimize community impacts, and continuously refine control strategies using monitoring data and operational observations.

Our approach follows a **structured evaluation and improvement framework** that quantifies odor and corrosion conditions, establishes acceptable impact thresholds, evaluates operational and treatment alternatives, and supports selection of appropriate control strategies. The process, illustrated in **Exhibit 5-8** (graphic asset `128_Hull_0091KO_1`), reflects Jacobs' standard methodology for evaluating odor conditions across treatment facilities and collection systems:

1. **Quantify odor and corrosion** — sampling and measurements; sewer process modeling (WATS).
2. **Establish goals: acceptable odor/corrosion impacts** — what level of odor is acceptable, and what sewer pipe life is desired?
3. **Determine impacts** — air dispersion modeling, odor impacts, corrosion rates.
4. **Evaluate control alternatives** — vapor phase, liquid phase, control through design, operational control, and lifecycle cost estimates.
5. **Select alternative and conceptual design.**

## Proactive Monitoring and Early Detection

A structured odor monitoring program will focus on **early detection and prevention**. Routine odor patrols will be conducted at the WWTF, pump stations, force main discharge locations, and facility perimeter areas, with increased frequency during warm weather and low-flow conditions when detention times rise and septicity risks are greatest.

Operators will use portable hydrogen sulfide detection equipment and field observations to assess odor intensity and potential off-site migration. Monitoring data, weather conditions, and observations will be documented and reviewed for trends. Internal hydrogen sulfide action thresholds will trigger **proactive operational response before off-site odor impacts occur**. Where appropriate, we will evaluate installation of fixed hydrogen sulfide sensors at strategic locations to support **real-time monitoring and improved process control**.

## Rapid Complaint Response and Investigation

We will respond to all odor complaints **within 1 hour of notification**. Upon receipt of a complaint, qualified personnel will assess the reported location, document odor characteristics and weather conditions, and review plant and collection system operating conditions, including pump station performance, wet well conditions, and force main detention times.

Corrective actions will be implemented promptly when warranted. All complaints and responses will be logged, and summaries of odor monitoring activities, investigations, and corrective actions will be provided to [CLIENT] monthly. The Project Manager will serve as the primary point of contact to ensure **timely communication and transparency**.

## Wastewater Treatment Facility Odor Management

Within the WWTF, odor generation will be minimized through **optimized process control and solids management**. DO levels will be maintained to prevent septic conditions, sludge age and solids retention time will be managed to reduce sulfide formation, and biosolids processing frequency will be adjusted as needed to limit storage duration.

We will evaluate the performance and capacity of the existing ventilation and odor control systems serving the headworks, thickening areas, and sludge storage infrastructure. Particular attention will be given to confirming adequate air movement to meet safety and odor control requirements. High sulfide concentrations in confined areas, such as the gravity thickener, may indicate insufficient ventilation or undersized odor treatment systems. Where warranted, we will identify operational improvements and recommend potential capital upgrades through [CLIENT]'s CIP process.

## Collection System and Pump Station Odor Control

[CLIENT]'s long force mains and pump stations are major contributors to hydrogen sulfide formation and odor risk. Wet wells will be inspected routinely for grease buildup and septicity, and cleaning frequencies will be adjusted proactively. Pump cycling strategies will be optimized to reduce detention time and limit hydrogen sulfide generation.

[CLIENT] currently uses chemical treatment for hydrogen sulfide and FOG control. We will review existing chemical dosing practices, feed locations, and chemical selection to ensure that dosing remains **effective and cost-efficient**. Industry best practices will be applied to verify that chemical dosing rates are appropriate and not excessive relative to sulfide loading conditions. We will also monitor, control, and periodically evaluate the current use of bioxide, In-Pipe Technology, and Wet Well Wizard-style optimization practices, adjusting feed locations, dosing rates, cleaning triggers, and field routing/priorities based on measured conditions and observed results.

As part of our SmartCover® implementation, the units can also be modified from monitoring I/I and incorporated with H2Scents®, a specialized sensor that provides **reliable, real-time measurement of hydrogen sulfide levels** over extended periods. H2Scents® allows real-time identification of hydrogen sulfide levels, supporting more accurate dosing that reduces chemical use, minimizes odor, and extends the life of the collection system assets. H2Scents® can be used as a diagnostic tool to help minimize or eliminate the use of bioxide and In-Pipe Technology. Hydrogen sulfide trends will be reviewed as part of preventive maintenance planning to support corrosion prevention and long-term asset preservation.

## Documentation and Reporting

We will maintain detailed records of monitoring activities, complaints, corrective actions, and chemical usage. Odor trends will be reviewed periodically to identify recurring conditions and refine control strategies. Through this proactive, responsive, and data-driven approach, we will protect neighborhood quality of life while maintaining regulatory compliance and operational excellence. This approach also supports identification of equipment improvement needs.

## Innovation and Sustainable Odor Control Strategies

While the odor control plan focuses on effective management of existing infrastructure, we will identify **long-term, sustainable odor control improvements** where operational measures alone are insufficient. Consistent with the evaluation framework shown in **Exhibit 5-8**, additional monitoring, process modeling, or engineering evaluation may be recommended to better quantify odor sources and determine the most effective long-term mitigation strategies.

Evaluations may include sewer process modeling, air dispersion analysis, or lifecycle evaluation of alternative odor control technologies, where warranted. We will apply advanced sewer process modeling tools, such as WATS modeling, to **better understand odor generation and evaluate system-wide mitigation strategies**, as further described in Appendix F. Any recommended improvements will be coordinated with [CLIENT] through the CIP process to ensure that odor mitigation strategies align with operational needs, infrastructure protection, and fiscal priorities.
