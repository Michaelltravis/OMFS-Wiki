# 10-Box Asset Management System, Elements 6–10 — People, Risk, Improvement, Performance, Outcomes

**6. Organization and People.** **Staff for outcomes:** Provide a dedicated **Planner/Scheduler**, cross-trained maintenance crews, and **CMMS Champions** on each crew to coach mobile use and data quality. Provide targeted training (CMMS, PMO methods, RCA, electrical/mechanical specialties) and embed **Jacobs' housekeeping standards** as preventive tasks. Align warehouse roles to planning (kitting, long-lead tracking, parts QA), so equipment, parts, and materials are ready for scheduled tasks.

**7. Risk Management.** **Use a common consequence-by-likelihood matrix for WRF and conveyance to drive prioritization.** Apply failure mode libraries and vulnerability scenarios (e.g., wet-weather pumping, tunnel access, critical MCCs, high-risk interceptors) to pre-plan contingencies and spares strategies. Risk reports are reviewed in Governance and feed the Capital Improvement Project (CIP) candidate list. The risk management system is structured to address asset performance, reliability, lifecycle risks through prioritization, condition monitoring, lifecycle analysis, and contingency planning; its process is regular condition assessments, predictive maintenance, and risk profile updates; its outcomes are enhanced performance, optimized investments, assured safety compliance, and minimized lifecycle costs while maximizing asset value — all measured with KPIs and feedback loops that drive continuous improvement (graphic 273_007C).

**8. Review and Continual Improvement.** **Institutionalize learning loops.** Hold monthly KPI reviews with the Maintenance Committee, quarterly PMO checkpoints, annual internal audits by dedicated technical experts, and after-action reviews for major events. All process changes (task edits, new PdM routes, priority rules) follow change control through the committees so improvements stick.

**9. Performance Management.** **[CLIENT] will have live access to dashboards** showing **PM compliance**, **CM backlog and aging**, mean time between failures/mean time to repair (**MTBF/MTTR**), **asset availability**, **labor/parts cost**, and **evidence lag (time to close with proof)**. We'll align thresholds to Standards of Performance and publish exception lists with owners and due dates.

**10. Values and Outcomes.** **The AMS converts field work into measurable results:** higher uptime, fewer repeats, safer plants and tunnels, defensible budgeting, and risk-ranked reinvestment. The same data stream builds **obsolescence roadmaps** and **CIP business cases**, so [CLIENT] invests where **reliability per dollar** is highest.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the six-KPI dashboard list, the four-part improvement cadence, the CMMS Champion role on each crew, and the element 10 closing that converts field work into budgeting and reinvestment. "Evidence lag (time to close with proof)" is the metric that distinguishes this program from a standard PM-compliance promise — keep it.

Pursuit-specific: the vulnerability scenarios in element 7; the performance standards the thresholds are aligned to; whether a dedicated Planner/Scheduler position is priced in the staffing plan — if it is not, this element cannot be claimed.
