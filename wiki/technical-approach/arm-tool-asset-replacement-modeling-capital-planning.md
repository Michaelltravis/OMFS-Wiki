# ARM Tool — Asset Replacement Modeling for Capital Planning and the Annual Facilities Status Report

Capital dollars go further when investment decisions are determined by actual asset conditions, risk, and lifecycle cost — not emergency-driven spending or deferred maintenance backlogs. **The AMS described in our Maintenance Plan generates a continuous stream of data that directly informs capital planning: condition scores, failure trends, cumulative repair costs, and remaining useful life estimates.** That data gives [CLIENT], a Southcentral US regional water utility trust, the objective basis to fund the right projects at the right time.

Jacobs' ARM tool (**Exhibit 1-33**) provides a data-driven framework for capital planning decisions, evaluating each asset against three triggers:

- **Asset Risk:** criticality multiplied by likelihood of failure, based on condition assessment and operating environment
- **Asset Age:** actual age versus typical useful life for the asset class, adjusted for site-specific conditions like H₂S exposure
- **Annualized Maintenance Cost:** trending corrective expenditure, flagging assets where ongoing repair costs approach or exceed replacement value

Assets are continuously evaluated to determine the optimal intervention — maintain, repair, rehabilitate, or replace — based on condition, risk, and lifecycle cost. **The ARM output is a prioritized, risk-ranked reinvestment plan — a multi-year look-ahead that gives [CLIENT] a defensible, transparent basis for capital budgeting.** The tool also identifies when an asset crosses from routine maintenance (M&R Fund) into rehabilitation or replacement territory (R&R Fund or CIP), flagging the transition early enough for [CLIENT] to plan accordingly.

**Exhibit 1-33.** *Jacobs' ARM Tool* — graphic asset ID `169_009385`. The exhibit shows each asset scored against Trigger A – Asset Age (from installation date), Trigger B – Asset Risk, and Trigger C – Annualized Maintenance Cost. Its accompanying text reads: our staff regularly help clients develop both capital plans and actionable R&R plans. When making R&R recommendations for facility assets, Jacobs' approach integrates with annual and multi-year look-ahead capital plan development, including our ARM procedure. This procedure results in a collaborative approach that: can use existing condition assessment information as a starting point; considers total maintenance costs and asset replacement asset value so a preliminary prioritized R&R plan may be developed; highlights troubled assets and potential for costly asset replacements; and identifies candidate assets for further net present value analysis to determine action on high-risk assets. The benefits of the ARM process are a well-organized and prioritized R&R plan.

## Annual Facilities Status Report

By October 1 of each year, we'll submit recommendations to retire, rehabilitate, replace, or upgrade/expand facility components as part of the Annual Management, Operations, and Maintenance Summary as required in Schedule 1 and Schedule 16. Each recommendation will include a cost estimate, the anticipated benefits, and projected cost savings per Schedule 20 — supported by condition assessment data, maintenance cost trends, risk rankings, and lifecycle projections from NexGen and the ARM tool. These recommendations will align with [CLIENT]'s budget development cycle, positioning capital requests to compete effectively for funding. [CLIENT] retains sole discretion on acceptance of any recommendations.
