# Biosolids Resilience, Emerging Contaminants, and Dryer Support

Jacobs manages many dewatering operations across the country every day. At almost any given time, we have an average of five mobile dewatering operations in place and operating at our facilities through either emergency or temporary needs. We will call on our expertise with these options, including a deep understanding of the nationwide inventory of mobile dewatering equipment, and respond to emergency dewatering needs [CLIENT] may experience in a timely manner.

Jacobs is following the nationwide issue of emerging contaminants closely, with special attention to PFAS in biosolids. We are actively following the new industry of manufacturers building the growing technology of pyrolysis and gasification. If and when the time comes to discuss the future of processing of biosolids, we can supply a robust and current knowledge.

We understand the importance of a more effective biosolids solution for [FACILITY A], a WRF. In addition to our base scope of services and plan to provide a comprehensive assessment of the potential for Class B biosolids at the facility, our team is uniquely positioned to assist [CLIENT] with the design-build and financing options for a biosolids dryer at [FACILITY A]. Leveraging our local expertise, including Jacobs' Jay Horton and Rocco Koekemoer's recent experience with a similar dryer facility for Gwinnett County and our established relationships with trusted [CLIENT] contractors such as Archer Western, we could provide a streamlined delivery model.

**We also offer access to innovative financing approaches. Jacobs has an experienced team that can assist in identifying and securing funding sources for biosolids treatment including thermal drying and other thermal treatment technologies.** Jacobs recently secured a $9.8 million grant from the USDA for Tulsa, Oklahoma, to help offset the cost of building a $45 million biosolids fertilizer manufacturing center. We also assisted the City of Hendersonville, NC, to secure a $14.5 million principal forgiveness grant from the North Carolina Clean Water State Revolving Fund to pay the entire cost of building a new drying facility to eliminate the need for landfilling. In both cases, funding is being used to build facilities to produce marketable biosolids products instead of relying on disposal options such as landfilling. In addition, Jacobs has developed partnership opportunities with technology providers to build and operate thermal drying/gasification facilities that can be funded by utilities or by a capital investment group to minimize capital outlay for our clients. We look forward to discussing these innovative funding options with you to secure biosolids management security for years to come.
