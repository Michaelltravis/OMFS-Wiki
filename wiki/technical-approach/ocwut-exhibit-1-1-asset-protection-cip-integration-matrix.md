# Exhibit 1-1 Matrix — Asset Protection, Maintenance, and CIP Integration

### Asset Protection and Maintenance

| Understanding of [CLIENT]'s Goals | Key Current/Future Challenges | Jacobs' Strategies to Deliver Results |
|---|---|---|
| **Protect [CLIENT]'s infrastructure investment through disciplined maintenance aligned with Standards of Maintenance (Schedule 20) and lifecycle planning** | **Current operator in "firefighting" maintenance mode** — critical equipment chronically out of service with no documented repair plan (e.g., return activated sludge (RAS) pumps, blowers, grit systems at NC).<br>**NC:** blower explosion; several motor control centers (MCCs), such as Cutler-Hammer and Siemens-Allis, are approaching the end of their useful life, and parts sourcing is becoming more difficult — both of which we'll factor into the CIP coordination and Asset Management Plan. Solids handling building roof in need of repair.<br>**DC:** Influent pumping building has flooded previously; primary clarifiers and smaller secondary clarifiers are candidates for refurbishment in the CIP window; sand filter blowers need maintenance.<br>**Asset list incomplete** — limited detail on specs/manufacturer, very limited instrumentation and safety equipment listed. Operational Technology (OT) assets not tracked in CMMS.<br>**No dedicated planner/scheduler; 23 maintenance FTEs struggling** across four dispersed plants.<br>**Maintenance performance carries >4% of Fixed Monthly Fee-at-risk** across nine deduction categories; CMMS records determine cost responsibility under Section 3.04(b). | **Thorough Baseline Condition Assessment** during transition, jointly signed with [CLIENT].<br>**Dedicated planner/scheduler** to drive proactive maintenance execution and CMMS discipline.<br>**Regional maintenance team provides heavy maintenance capacity** (crane-certified, specialized mechanical work) beyond base site staff.<br>**Structured preventive maintenance (PM) completion tracking** (95% weekly closure target), vibration analysis, and infrared testing per Schedules 2 and 4.<br>**Complete asset inventory including OT/instrumentation during transition;** reconcile with CMMS.<br>**Culture shift from reactive to proactive maintenance,** grounded in CMMS discipline, structured technician training, and coaching from Jacobs' regional and offsite maintenance experts. |
| **Complete drain-and-clean requirements per Schedule 15 across all facilities; maintain buildings and grounds per Housekeeping Plan** | **Extensive drain-and-clean inventory** spanning clarifiers, basins, digesters, and wet weather structures across four plants and one pump station.<br>**PM-1 deductions for incomplete annual Drain and Clean Plan execution**; housekeeping deficiencies must be corrected within 30 days (MGT-2, 0.40% at risk). | **Annual Drain and Clean Plan developed during transition** with scheduling that maintains process continuity while meeting all required frequencies. |

### Capital Improvement Program Integration

| Understanding of [CLIENT]'s Goals | Key Current/Future Challenges | Jacobs' Strategies to Deliver Results |
|---|---|---|
| **Coordinate with [CLIENT], engineers, and construction contractors to integrate CIP projects without process disruptions or compliance excursions** | **~$200M in planned upgrades over the next decade**, including SC rebuild (8.66 MGD, completion 2026–2027), DC Phase 1 focused on odor control and drying (2029) followed by expansion to 21 MGD (2027) and eventually 27–32 MGD (2032), on-site sodium hypochlorite generation at NC (2028) and DC (2029), and new headworks with grit removal at NC (2027–2028).<br>**Active construction at SC creates concurrent O&M and safety challenges**; no belt press redundancy at the new plant. SC hypo generators use proprietary PSI parts (~$20K/cartridge) — warranty responsibilities unclear.<br>**The CIP is evolving**, with timing and funding commitments firming up over the contract window. | **Dedicated CIP Construction Manager** (per Schedule 10) providing O&M input at 35%, 60%, 100% design, and construction phases per Schedule 16 — and allowing seamless integration with our consulting resources where valuable.<br>**Operational Integration Plan per Schedule 18 addresses staffing, resources, and cost impacts for each CIP project**, including hypochlorite transport from DC to CC.<br>**Leverage CIP coordination to support accelerated investments in critical needs** (odor control equipment, sludge mixing, grit system rehabilitation). |
