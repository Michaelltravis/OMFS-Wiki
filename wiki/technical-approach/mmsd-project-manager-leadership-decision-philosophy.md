# Project Manager's Leadership and Decision-Making Philosophy (First-Person Callout)

**EXHIBIT IV-12. KEVIN'S MANAGEMENT AND DECISION-MAKING APPROACH** (graphic asset `289_007CAM_3`)

My leadership style is grounded in being visible, actively listening, and building meaningful relationships across all levels of the organization. We can only be successful if everyone understands what success looks like for themselves and their teams, and how our individual actions influence each other and our collective outcomes.

As both an operator and an engineer, I've seen how disconnects between the two can result in decisions that aren't fully implemented or goals that become misaligned. At Jacobs, where operations and engineering are fully integrated, our teams bridge this gap every day—decisions are informed by the people who operate the systems and supported by the engineers who design and optimize them. This integration means our operators make our engineers better, and our engineers make our operators better. It's a cycle of shared learning that strengthens both technical performance and team culture.

At [CLIENT]'s complex facilities, where a single operational change—such as when to pump down the tunnel or when to adjust ferric addition—can influence treatment performance, odor control, or biosolids product quality, this culture of respect and collaboration ensures that every decision reflects operational reality, regulatory goals, and long-term sustainability.

A mentor once told me, 'The biology outside the tank is more complex and diverse than the biology inside the tank.' That wisdom reminds me that the people, relationships, and communication among us are just as critical to success as the technical systems we manage.

— Kevin Dahl, Project Manager
