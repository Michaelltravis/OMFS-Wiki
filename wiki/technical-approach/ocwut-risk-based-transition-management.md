# Risk-Based Transition Management Approach

Jacobs will actively manage transition risk from the commencement date through the operational start date using **a disciplined process built on early identification, clear ownership, and proven mitigation strategies**. During the transition period, we’ll track and manage risks across staffing, knowledge transfer, systems access, compliance readiness, asset validation, inventory, and transfer of service so that [CLIENT], a Southcentral water utility trust, enters day one with stable, compliant, and fully prepared operations. Our approach aligns with Schedule 19’s required transition services and milestone-driven readiness framework. All [CLIENT]-specific deliverables will be incorporated into our checklist of standard action items, ensuring that risks are addressed before they affect performance, compliance, or continuity of service.

|Risk area|Potential impact|Jacobs mitigation approach|Outcome for [CLIENT]|
|---|---|---|---|
|Staffing continuity|Loss of institutional knowledge, certification gaps, or insufficient coverage|Offer positions to qualified incumbent personnel, confirm certification coverage early, recruit and onboard during transition, and maintain regional backup and contingency staffing resources.|Fully staffed, qualified team in place on operational start date with no service disruption|
|Knowledge transfer gaps|Incomplete understanding of facility operations leading to errors or inefficiencies|Structured knowledge transfer through document review, shadowing, walkthroughs, SOP review, and multi-shift monitoring; 7-day operational monitoring before startup.|Effective transfer of operational knowledge and consistent performance from day one|
|Regulatory compliance|Missed sampling, reporting errors, or permit noncompliance during transition|Validate sampling programs, laboratory methods, reporting workflows, and compliance obligations during transition; align tools and responsibilities before startup.|Continuous compliance with no transition-related gaps|
|Asset condition uncertainty|Unknown equipment condition or unexpected failures affecting startup reliability|Complete Baseline Physical Condition Assessment; review maintenance history and backlog; jointly sign the Baseline Physical Condition Report with [CLIENT] before startup.|Clear, jointly understood baseline and reduced startup reliability risk|
|Initial inventory gaps|Insufficient spare parts, chemicals, supplies, or equipment at startup|Jointly validate initial inventory during transition, establish minimum inventory levels, identify and address deficiencies before startup, and integrate inventory controls into CMMS.|Required inventory in place to support uninterrupted startup|
|Chemical, polymer, and biosolids readiness|Delays affecting process performance, odor control, dewatering, or solids compliance|Complete polymer and chemical testing before startup, secure preferred vendors, and confirm biosolids equipment and rolling stock within required timeframes.|Confirmed chemical and biosolids readiness supporting stable, compliant startup operations|
|Systems integration|Loss of visibility, alarm failures, data errors, or cybersecurity vulnerabilities|Coordinate early access to systems and data; validate SCADA, CMMS, and IT workflows; develop and submit ITS Implementation and Use Plan; and train staff before startup.|Fully functional systems supporting safe, controlled operations from day one|
|Transition milestone slippage|Missed Schedule 19 deadlines affecting startup readiness|Manage transition through a milestone-driven schedule with clear owners, checkpoints, and escalation protocols; track all deliverables in a centralized transition log.|On-time completion of required deliverables and confidence in operational start date readiness|
|Communication breakdowns|Misalignment between [CLIENT], Jacobs, and incumbent operator|Establish governance at commencement and implement a transition communications plan with defined roles, meeting cadence, and action tracking.|Transparent communication and aligned execution throughout transition|
|Operational disruption at startup|Instability from simultaneous changes in staff, systems, or responsibilities|Sequence activities to support continuity first, complete transfer-of-service planning for the month before and month after startup, and prioritize stabilization before optimization.|Controlled startup that protects performance, compliance, and continuity|
|Safety risks during transition|Increased incident potential from unfamiliarity with site conditions or equipment|Conduct site-specific safety assessments, incorporate safety requirements into onboarding and training, and provide role-specific hazard awareness before independent work.|Safe work environment and strong safety culture from the start of operations|
