# Scrubber Optimization for Offsite Odor Control

Odor control is extremely important to being a good neighbor, and our goal will be to have zero odor complaints. At [FACILITY A] and [FACILITY B], chemical scrubbers are used to treat air from the most odorous parts of the plant. Granular activated carbon (GAC) is used for other areas and as polishing after chemical scrubbers. Given the smaller plant size, [FACILITY C] has only GAC.

At [FACILITY A] and [FACILITY B], the scrubbers were designed to use sodium hypochlorite and sodium hydroxide, and these chemicals work together to remove hydrogen sulfide, the most significant source of odor. Monthly reports show that sodium hydroxide is not being used at [FACILITY B]. As a result, hydrogen sulfide removal by the scrubbers is low, leaving considerable hydrogen sulfide to be removed by GAC. While not definitive, JC Solutions believes sodium hypochlorite also is being underfed. Not using the correct amount of chemicals in the scrubber increases GAC usage and cost. Using the correct amount of chemicals would reduce the combined cost of chemicals and GAC by about $500 per day. In addition, GAC would be available as a backup to the scrubbers, which was the original intent.

Chemical consumption for odor control relates to the concentration of hydrogen sulfide in the wastewater and the amount released to the air within treatment facilities. Scrubber performance is affected by several operational setpoints. Fine tuning will maximize hydrogen sulfide removal (thereby minimizing GAC consumption and replacement) while minimizing chemical cost.

Our odor specialists will use models and work with the scrubber supplier and plant staff to fine-tune operation of the scrubbers at [FACILITY A] and [FACILITY B]. **How It Benefits [CLIENT]:** Lowers GAC replacement frequency and cost; improves operational reliability, and takes a more proactive approach to controlling odor emissions.

JC Solutions’ projected chemical usage at [FACILITY A] and [FACILITY B] is based on using the correct amounts of sodium hypochlorite and sodium hydroxide in the scrubbers. For [FACILITY A], we used airflow and hydrogen sulfide concentration from Brown & Caldwell’s contract documents. For [FACILITY B], we used the same hydrogen sulfide concentration but used [FACILITY B] scrubber design capacities.
