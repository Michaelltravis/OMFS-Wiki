# Odor Complaint Protocol — Intake, Investigation, Verification, and Resident Follow-up

**Clear, Timely, Documented Response to Public Complaints.** While we believe our proactive approach described above will decrease complaints, there still will be complaints until all odorous sources are covered and treated. **Jacobs will implement a proactive, consistent, well-documented odor complaint protocol that exceeds [CLIENT]'s Scope of Services requirements.** Our approach integrates prevention, rapid response, and community engagement—ensuring that odor issues are minimized, investigated promptly, and communicated effectively to both [CLIENT] and the public. Complaints received through [CLIENT] systems, Jacobs' website, hotlines, or social media will be logged into a centralized database with location and observed conditions. Trained staff—validated through odor sensitivity testing—will investigate each complaint using onsite observations, low-level H₂S meters, and data from the odor early warning system. We'll assess root causes and implement corrective action when appropriate. Each complaint will generate a documented summary with findings, mitigation steps, and lessons learned, which we'll leverage to drive continuous improvements. Residents will receive courteous follow-up, and we'll review trends with [CLIENT] to refine procedures and identify systemic improvements. A preliminary outline of the protocol approach is shown in **Exhibit IV-27**.

**Exhibit IV-27. Odor Complaint Protocol** (asset IDs `338_007CAM_2`, `339_007CAM_1`):

1. **Complaint submission** — Residents make a complaint via [CLIENT] and Jacobs websites, odor "hotline," or social media.
2. **Logging details** — Complaint observations (odor characteristics, weather/wind, etc.) entered into database with location.
3. **Forwarding to operations** — Complaints electronically forwarded to operations staff at the appropriate facility.
4. **Investigation** — Trained staff investigate the complaint; root cause is determined.
5. **Odor verification** — Staff use low-level H₂S detectors and their noses to measure odor levels.
6. **Documentation and improvement** — Summary of investigation written into database documenting cause, verification, and mitigation.
7. **Resident notification** — Resident is sent a summary of the investigation.

Jacobs offers more than 20 odor technologists, including 3 in our local office serving the region. Bill Desing, who led [CLIENT]'s collection system odor study, including development of the WATS odor and corrosion model, oversees this group. We'll use this model to take a predictive and proactive approach to mitigating odors. Our goal is zero odor complaints.

All staff that respond to odor complaints will have "their noses tested" to ensure their noses are "average" to avoid them not detecting odor perceived by most people or conversely being overly sensitive to odor not perceived by a typical person. The testing will use industry standard "odor sticks" to qualify staff.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the seven-step protocol, the nose-testing qualification device, and the "exceeds the Scope of Services requirements" framing. Pursuit-specific: the named odor technologist and his connection to the client's prior study — that sentence is the incumbent-displacement lever and only works where Jacobs did the earlier work. Pairs with the WATS model block and the continuous improvement and community outreach block.
