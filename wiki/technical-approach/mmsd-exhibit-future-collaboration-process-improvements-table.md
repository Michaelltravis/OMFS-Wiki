# Exhibit IV-53 (Part 1) — Future Collaboration: Process Improvements

**EXHIBIT IV-53. SUMMARY OF COLLABORATION FOR FUTURE FACILITY AND O&M IMPROVEMENTS AND INNOVATIONS (NOT INCLUDED IN OUR BASE FEE)**

| Future Collaboration Innovation | What we will do for [CLIENT] facilities | Expected Benefits for [CLIENT] | Estimated Future Range of Savings |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PROCESS IMPROVEMENTS** | | | |
| **Addition of Lube Rings for TWAS Pumping** | - Add lube rings to allow pumping of thicker TWAS. | - Cost savings on long-term maintenance and replacement of pump bearings. Confirmed savings on maintenance and replacement at Wilmington and Green Bay. | $0 to $750,000 |
| **Install Pipe to Connect Landfill Gas to New Source** | - Evaluate the feasibility, routing and cost of installing a 600 ft long pipe to connect the Waste Management Landfill to the current landfill gas source. | - Sets [CLIENT] up for reduced dependence on natural gas in the future when it is ready to accept more landfill gas. | $0 to $4,800,000 |
| **Improve Influent Channel Mixing** | - Collaborate with [CLIENT] to evaluate and implement changes to improve influent channel mixing with the goal of reducing aeration requirements. | - Improved influent channel mixing to reduce aeration requirements and ensuing energy consumption. | $1,000,000 to $2,800,000 |
| **Anaerobic Digestion / MHP Pilot** | - Collaborate with [CLIENT] on the digester improvement study and to pilot MHP to improve digester performance. Quantify potential VSR improvements, biogas production. This would also allow feeding more WAS without affecting production of [CLIENT]'s biosolids product. | - Validate and identify actual level of digestion performance improvements, which could be as much as 30–45%. This would increase biogas generation and decrease biosolids production with relatively simple facility additions. | $3,000,000 to $4,500,000 |
| **Sidestream Treatment / Sludge Thickening** | - Consider sidestream treatment and recuperative thickening to increase VSR, increase digester capacity, reduce load to the secondary-treatment WRF's secondary process. Interplant pumping capacity between the two WRFs may be an issue, so model sidestream routing between them. | - Improvement could provide increased VSR, increased digester capacity, and reduction in load to secondary treatment. | $3,000,000 to $4,500,000 |
| **Waste Heat** | - Implement better control and programming to address leaking of turbine waste heat out of the pressure control damper. | - Improved energy efficiency through better controls/programming. | $0 to $480,000 |
| **D&D Dust Minimization** | - Use fabric tube containment in tractor trailer to contain dust during loading of chaff at the facility and hauling and dumping at the landfill. | - Reduced dust at D&D facilities during hauling and provides sustainable practices at the landfill. Dust minimization improves safety at D&D facilities and at the landfill. | Health and Environmental Benefits |
| **Sludge Bulking Control** | - Invest in adding baffles to bioreactors to create selectors to control filaments and help with wet weather capacity. | - The baffles could precede or even replace zoning from conversion to BNR configuration. Anaerobic selectors could control filaments at a much lower cost and operational complexity than RAS chlorination. | Optimization of Process |
| **Belt Filter Presses at the Biosolids-Drying WRF** | - Invest in evaluating means by which dewatered cake can be increased beyond the current 16.5% DS average. Confirm that changing the blend of drier feed stock as well as its dryness does not negatively impact [CLIENT]'s biosolids product. | - Increasing dry solids in dewatered cake reduces energy costs associated with thermal drier operation by reducing external natural gas purchases. | $0-$1,000,000 |
| **HSW Study - Evaluate Additional HSW Sources** | - Invest in labor required to actively market and manage a HSW co-digestion program. Use Jacobs existing database to help [CLIENT] find additional HSW when they have more capacity in place to accept it. | - HSW will increase biogas production without adding digesters, which would contribute to reducing natural gas purchases. | $0 to $2,400,000 |
| **Landfill Gas (LFG) at the Biosolids-Drying WRF** | - Invest in a chiller to remove moisture from pipe so more landfill gas can be used during certain times of the year. [CLIENT] to define location of the chiller. | - Benefit is seasonal. Extends the life of the pipe and provides (roughly) 20% more gas. This would provide an opportunity to have more landfill gas storage. | $500,000 to $2,000,000 |
| **Systems Uptime Extension LFG Supply and Turbine Reliability** | - Build on preliminary discussions with the Emerald Park Landfill to identify LFG system constraints and opportunities to improve reliability and coordination. Evaluate improvement options such as enhanced coordination, operational support strategies, or targeted investments. | - Improved system uptime and confidence in landfill gas as a reliable component of [CLIENT]'s long term energy strategy by addressing turbine downtime drivers beyond the turbines themselves. Reduced operational risk associated with third party landfill gas supply variability. | To be evaluated in collaboration with [CLIENT] |
| **Aeration Control at Both WRFs** | - Implement new control logic and probes to improve aeration control at both WRFs. | - Future power savings of 10% at the biosolids-drying WRF and 20% at the secondary-treatment WRF. | $4,500,000 to $9,000,000 |
| **Mitigate Fugitive Biogas Emissions** | - Invest in installation of a QMAR Lidar camera system at the secondary-treatment WRF to detect fugitive biogas emissions. Camera system can be used at the biosolids-drying WRF to detect and mitigate fugitive emissions from landfill gas utilization. | - Mitigating fugitive methane emissions reduces greenhouse gas impact and supports sustainability goals, while improving health and safety. Capturing lost biogas, estimated at 10% annual losses at the secondary-treatment WRF, lowers reliance on natural gas, saving energy costs. | $500,000 to $2,000,000 |

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four-column layout and the discipline of giving every idea a savings range, a qualitative benefit label, or an explicit "to be evaluated in collaboration." Specificity is the persuasion here — "a 600 ft long pipe," "16.5% DS average," "10% at one plant and 20% at the other" prove the team has looked at the actual plants.

Pursuit-specific: all fourteen ideas.
