# Asset Management System — 10-Box Model Methodology and Predictive Maintenance Technologies

## Asset Management-Centered Maintenance Approach

Jacobs will elevate [CLIENT]'s asset management and maintenance program by leveraging industry-leading tools, strategies, and PdM technologies to enhance asset reliability, reduce failure risk, optimize cost tracking, and proactively plan for obsolescence and capital needs, including capital improvement plan (CIP) development. Given the mix of new and legacy infrastructure across the facilities, a structured, data-driven asset management program is essential to achieving long-term efficiency, cost savings, and system reliability.

Jacobs' Asset Management System (AMS) is aligned with [CLIENT]'s scope of work and ISO 55001 standards. The system is based on Jacobs' 10-Box Model, **Exhibit 2-25**, which supports strategic, tactical, and operational asset management planning. A key deliverable will be a long-term Asset Management Plan that guides daily maintenance, annual calibration of all monitoring equipment, lifecycle cost analysis, and capital planning to support infrastructure resilience and regulatory compliance.

**Above and Beyond — ISO 55001-Aligned Asset Management Program:** Jacobs will implement an ISO 55001-aligned asset management system with integrated predictive and preventive maintenance. Using risk-based prioritization and condition assessments, we will manage the full asset lifecycle—maximizing reliability, minimizing cost, and informing smarter capital planning. ($300,000 embedded value.)

### Jacobs' Asset Management System — the 10-Box Model

The model comprises: Leadership & Governance; Strategy & Planning; Asset Management Decision-Making; Asset Knowledge Enablers (Information Management); a central Life Cycle Delivery wheel (Acquire/Create → Operate → Maintain → Review/Dispose); Risk Management; Values & Outcomes; Performance Management; and an overarching Review & Continual Improvement band.

**1. Leadership and Governance.** A successful asset management program requires clear leadership and accountability. **CPO Mack Mckenzie** and the **Management Team** will work with **Asset Management Support Lead Graham Knowles** and his team to develop the Asset Management Plan (AMP), ensuring alignment with [CLIENT] goals, compliance with industry standards, and strategic resource allocation. At project initiation, Jacobs will conduct a condition assessment and facilitate a risk workshop with [CLIENT] staff. This session will review key deficiencies, perform risk scoring, develop mitigation strategies, and assign responsibilities for corrective actions.

**2. Strategy and Planning.** Jacobs will establish a strategic asset management framework that supports [CLIENT]'s operational objectives and integrates lifecycle cost analysis, risk-based prioritization, and long-term capital investment planning. We will begin by conducting a baseline condition assessment and risk-ranking of critical assets. During the transition phase, Jacobs will update and validate [CLIENT]'s CMMS, Mentor APM, and ensure that the asset registry is accurate, complete, and reflects true field conditions.

**3. Asset Management Decision-Making.** Data-driven decision-making is central to Jacobs' approach. By leveraging CMMS analytics and centralized asset data, Jacobs will optimize work order generation, cost analysis, and prioritization of maintenance efforts. Our system supports proactive obsolescence planning, helping [CLIENT] avoid costly emergency repairs and maintain service continuity.

**4. Lifecycle Delivery and Maintenance Strategy.** Jacobs will manage the complete asset lifecycle, from acquisition to decommissioning, through a proactive strategy that blends PM and PdM, with corrective maintenance tracked in the CMMS against defined service levels. See [Lifecycle Delivery and Maintenance Strategy](swip-lifecycle-delivery-maintenance-strategy.md) for the full passage.

**5. Asset Knowledge Enablers (Information Management).** Accurate and accessible data is essential to asset management success. Jacobs will maintain all asset records in [CLIENT]'s CMMS to support lifecycle tracking, real-time condition visibility, and maintenance scheduling. The system will also support five- and ten-year CIP planning, helping [CLIENT] prioritize infrastructure investments and maintain financial transparency. Asset data will include expected lifespan, spare parts needs, and replacement history, accessible through user-friendly dashboards.

**6. Organization and People.** A high-performing maintenance program depends on the capabilities of its people. Jacobs will align staffing, training, and leadership roles with [CLIENT]'s asset management goals. The onsite team will receive targeted training in asset lifecycle planning, PdM strategies, and CMMS use. **CPO Mack Mckenzie** and **Maintenance Technical Richard Gwinn** will be aided by regional subject matter experts in condition monitoring (e.g., Artesis, Augury AI), lubrication science (tribology), and root cause analysis. These experts will provide continuous guidance and support to ensure system optimization and staff development.

**7. Risk Management.** Following project startup, Jacobs will conduct regular condition assessments and apply a structured risk scoring methodology to prioritize high-risk assets and inform capital investments. Risk profiles will be maintained and used to guide both day-to-day decision-making and long-term financial planning. Our risk-based strategy protects infrastructure value, extends asset life, and supports efficient resource allocation. Condition assessments play a critical role in Jacobs' industry-leading asset management and associated maintenance approach; our field-trained maintenance specialists have completed **condition assessments for 1 million water and wastewater plant assets**. Jacobs' industry award-winning and highly efficient condition assessment process supports asset management excellence.

**8. Review and Continual Improvement.** Jacobs will foster a culture of continuous improvement through annual audits, performance reviews, and system evaluations. These reviews will assess the effectiveness of the asset management program, validate KPI outcomes, and identify opportunities to optimize processes, technologies, or resourcing.

**9. Performance Management.** Jacobs will provide monthly performance reports tracking key indicators including PM effectiveness, mean time between failures (MTBF), mean time to repair (MTTR), and overall equipment effectiveness (OEE). These metrics will support proactive management, drive operational efficiency, and provide [CLIENT] with real-time insights into system performance and reliability.

Jacobs' predictive maintenance technologies support cost-effective, reliability-centered asset maintenance; see [Predictive Maintenance Technologies](swip-predictive-maintenance-technologies.md) for the five-technology toolkit.

**10. Value and Outcomes.** Jacobs' AMS will deliver measurable value to [CLIENT] by maximizing asset life, reducing lifecycle costs, and aligning infrastructure investments with long-term goals. Through a structured, standards-driven approach, we will deliver enhanced resilience, compliance, and financial accountability for [CLIENT]'s facilities.
