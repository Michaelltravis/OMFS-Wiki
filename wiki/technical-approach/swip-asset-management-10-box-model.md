# Asset Management System — 10-Box Model Methodology and Predictive Maintenance Technologies

## Asset Management-Centered Maintenance Approach

Jacobs will elevate the client's asset management and maintenance program by leveraging industry-leading tools, strategies, and PdM technologies to enhance asset reliability, reduce failure risk, optimize cost tracking, and proactively plan for obsolescence and capital needs, including capital improvement plan (CIP) development. Given the mix of new and legacy infrastructure across the facilities, a structured, data-driven asset management program is essential to achieving long-term efficiency, cost savings, and system reliability.

Jacobs' Asset Management System (AMS) is aligned with the client's scope of work and ISO 55001 standards. The system is based on Jacobs' 10-Box Model, which supports strategic, tactical, and operational asset management planning. A key deliverable will be a long-term Asset Management Plan that guides daily maintenance, annual calibration of all monitoring equipment, lifecycle cost analysis, and capital planning to support infrastructure resilience and regulatory compliance.

**Above and Beyond — ISO 55001-Aligned Asset Management Program:** Jacobs will implement an ISO 55001-aligned asset management system with integrated predictive and preventive maintenance. Using risk-based prioritization and condition assessments, we will manage the full asset lifecycle—maximizing reliability, minimizing cost, and informing smarter capital planning. ($300,000 embedded value.)

### Jacobs' Asset Management System — the 10-Box Model

The model comprises: Leadership & Governance; Strategy & Planning; Asset Management Decision-Making; Asset Knowledge Enablers (Information Management); a central Life Cycle Delivery wheel (Acquire/Create → Operate → Maintain → Review/Dispose); Risk Management; Values & Outcomes; Performance Management; and an overarching Review & Continual Improvement band.

**1. Leadership and Governance.** A successful asset management program requires clear leadership and accountability. **CPO Mack Mckenzie** and the **Management Team** will work with **Asset Management Support Lead Graham Knowles** and his team to develop the Asset Management Plan (AMP), ensuring alignment with client goals, compliance with industry standards, and strategic resource allocation. At project initiation, Jacobs will conduct a condition assessment and facilitate a risk workshop with client staff. This session will review key deficiencies, perform risk scoring, develop mitigation strategies, and assign responsibilities for corrective actions.

**2. Strategy and Planning.** Jacobs will establish a strategic asset management framework that supports the client's operational objectives and integrates lifecycle cost analysis, risk-based prioritization, and long-term capital investment planning. We will begin by conducting a baseline condition assessment and risk-ranking of critical assets. During the transition phase, Jacobs will update and validate the client's CMMS, asset performance management tool, and ensure that the asset registry is accurate, complete, and reflects true field conditions.

**3. Asset Management Decision-Making.** Data-driven decision-making is central to Jacobs' approach. By leveraging CMMS analytics and centralized asset data, Jacobs will optimize work order generation, cost analysis, and prioritization of maintenance efforts. Our system supports proactive obsolescence planning, helping the client avoid costly emergency repairs and maintain service continuity.

**4. Lifecycle Delivery and Maintenance Strategy.** Jacobs will manage the complete asset lifecycle, from acquisition to decommissioning, through a proactive strategy that blends PM and PdM. Our preventive maintenance optimization (PMO) and Level 3 PdM programs will replace reactive maintenance with forward-looking diagnostics. Routine field inspections will monitor asset condition, while PdM tools such as ultrasound, vibration analysis, and motor signature assessments will support early detection of issues. Jacobs will complete all PM and PdM activities with in-house teams, supported by regional technical staff as needed. Corrective maintenance tasks will be tracked in the CMMS, issued within 24 hours of detection, and closed within 120 days. Facility appearance and maintenance will be tracked in parallel to ensure all spaces remain safe, clean, and professional.

The CMMS (Computerized Maintenance Management System) workflow integrates a Preventive Maintenance Plan, Corrective Maintenance Plan, and Inventory Activity as inputs, and produces a Preventive Maintenance Schedule, Maintenance Performance Reports/Maintenance Review, Asset Performance/Capital Planning output, and Inventory Status as outputs.

Corrective maintenance will be systematically tracked within the CMMS, ensuring that all failure-related work orders are issued within 24 hours of detection and closed within 120 days. Additionally, Jacobs will uphold high facility appearance standards, ensuring that all buildings, process areas, and fleet equipment remain clean, well-maintained, and professionally managed. Maintenance tasks will be scheduled and monitored through the CMMS, ensuring structured oversight and accountability.

**5. Asset Knowledge Enablers (Information Management).** Accurate and accessible data is essential to asset management success. Jacobs will maintain all asset records in the client's CMMS to support lifecycle tracking, real-time condition visibility, and maintenance scheduling. The system will also support five- and ten-year CIP planning, helping the client prioritize infrastructure investments and maintain financial transparency. Asset data will include expected lifespan, spare parts needs, and replacement history, accessible through user-friendly dashboards.

**6. Organization and People.** A high-performing maintenance program depends on the capabilities of its people. Jacobs will align staffing, training, and leadership roles with the client's asset management goals. The onsite team will receive targeted training in asset lifecycle planning, PdM strategies, and CMMS use. **CPO Mack Mckenzie** and **Maintenance Technical Richard Gwinn** will be aided by regional subject matter experts in condition monitoring, lubrication science (tribology), and root cause analysis. These experts will provide continuous guidance and support to ensure system optimization and staff development.

**7. Risk Management.** Following project startup, Jacobs will conduct regular condition assessments and apply a structured risk scoring methodology to prioritize high-risk assets and inform capital investments. Risk profiles will be maintained and used to guide both day-to-day decision-making and long-term financial planning. Our risk-based strategy protects infrastructure value, extends asset life, and supports efficient resource allocation. Condition assessments play a critical role in Jacobs' industry-leading asset management and associated maintenance approach; our field-trained maintenance specialists have completed condition assessments for over 1 million water and wastewater plant assets firm-wide.

**8. Review and Continual Improvement.** Jacobs will foster a culture of continuous improvement through annual audits, performance reviews, and system evaluations. These reviews will assess the effectiveness of the asset management program, validate KPI outcomes, and identify opportunities to optimize processes, technologies, or resourcing.

**9. Performance Management.** Jacobs will provide monthly performance reports tracking key indicators including PM effectiveness, mean time between failures (MTBF), mean time to repair (MTTR), and overall equipment effectiveness (OEE). These metrics will support proactive management, drive operational efficiency, and provide the client with real-time insights into system performance and reliability.

Jacobs' predictive maintenance technologies supporting cost-effective, reliability-centered asset maintenance include:

- **Ultrasound Analysis** — for leak detection, mechanical inspection, electrical inspection, electric arc flash detection, steam trap maintenance, valve testing, and acoustic precision lubrication of bearings.
- **Precision Alignment** — of rotating equipment effectively corrects misalignment, a leading cause of bearing failure in rotating equipment.
- **Infrared Thermography** — to spot anomalies not otherwise visible to the eye, finding potential faults in electrical and mechanical applications before they lead to equipment failure.
- **Lubrication/Oil Analysis** — to monitor condition and degradation of the asset, informing decision for continued operation, filtering, or oil volume change.
- **Vibration Measurement** — to identify misalignments or imbalances before issues or failures develop, including critical, high-dollar equipment that traditional RMS vibration measurement cannot detect.

**10. Value and Outcomes.** Jacobs' AMS will deliver measurable value to the client by maximizing asset life, reducing lifecycle costs, and aligning infrastructure investments with long-term goals. Through a structured, standards-driven approach, we will deliver enhanced resilience, compliance, and financial accountability for the facilities.
