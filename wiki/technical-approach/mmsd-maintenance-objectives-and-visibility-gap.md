# Maintenance Objectives and the Maintenance Visibility Gap Under the Incumbent

Although facilities appear to be in good physical condition, with some noticeable recent housekeeping, our review suggests that maintenance activities aren't well-documented or visible to [CLIENT], a regional sewerage district operating two large water reclamation facilities and a deep tunnel conveyance system, which limits data and analysis to inform asset management decisions. Given the age and complexity of legacy and newer infrastructure, [CLIENT] needs more than confirmation that assets run. Rather, [CLIENT] needs an integrated and accessible asset registry complete with key attributes, maintenance records, condition data, criticality scores, and risk scores to support long-term, risk-based investment decisions.

**Proven, transparent partner — from Vancouver, WA, to Waterbury, CT.** Jacobs will serve as [CLIENT]'s fully transparent, data-driven asset management/maintenance partner. On projects like Vancouver and Waterbury, we replaced the incumbent operator and implemented robust CMMS- and PdM-based programs that link every finding to a corrective action and measurable result. Our clients see exactly what work we perform, why we do it, and how it protects their assets. We'll bring that same clarity to [CLIENT] — making maintenance fully visible and aligning every decision with reliability, risk reduction, and asset life. (Exhibit IV-41, [CLIENT]'s asset management line of sight: Policy/SAMP → NEXGEN with SCADA and GIS → Plan/Schedule → Execute (Evidence) → KPI Dashboards → Governance → CIP Feedback; graphic 321_007CAM_2.)

It's unclear whether PM and PdM are being performed under the current arrangement, due to the lack of clear reporting that demonstrates:

- Specific findings from PM/PdM inspections
- How failure modes and failure mode and effects analysis are being used to prevent repeat issues
- Trend analysis or asset-health tracking that links work orders to reliability, uptime, and asset life
- Corrective actions that were completed in response

**[CLIENT] has asked for a different model — one that delivers proof of work and verifiable outcomes through clear, auditable documentation.** We recognize the Draft Service Agreement's intent: execute needed work and support authorization and payment with CMMS-based records and supporting documentation. This aligns with how Jacobs runs maintenance programs every day: closed-loop planning, execution, and reporting so [CLIENT] has decision-grade visibility into what was done, why it was needed, and what risk it mitigated. This model advances [CLIENT]'s One Water/One Vision goals and avoids deferred maintenance practices that can introduce operational risk.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four-bullet evidence gap (findings, failure modes, trend analysis, corrective actions completed) is the reusable diagnostic for any incumbent-displacement maintenance section — it is what a client cannot see, stated as a list they can check. The "more than confirmation that assets run" line generalizes to any asset-management-driven pursuit.

Pursuit-specific: the observation that facilities look well kept must come from an actual site visit; the named displacement references; the client's CMMS of record; the service agreement's payment-documentation language, which should be quoted back from the draft contract.
