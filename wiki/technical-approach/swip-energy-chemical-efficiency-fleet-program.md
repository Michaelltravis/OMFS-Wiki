# Energy Optimization, Chemical Efficiency, and Hybrid Fleet Sustainability Program

## Energy optimization

At potable reuse and advanced treatment facilities across California — including Soquel Creek and Pure Water Monterey — Jacobs has implemented SCADA-integrated energy dashboards, variable-frequency-drive (VFD) tuning protocols, and automated control logic to reduce energy intensity across membrane filtration, RO, and UV-AOP systems. **At Soquel Creek, the Jacobs team achieved more than 10% energy reduction through membrane recovery optimization and blower reprogramming.**

For this pursuit, Jacobs will benchmark energy use at each process step (e.g., aeration for MBR, kWh/MG for RO) and apply proven optimization strategies, such as real-time setpoint adjustments and off-peak operations, to drive savings. These changes will be monitored through key performance indicators (KPIs) and reviewed in monthly operations reports.

As part of Jacobs' commitment to energy savings and reduced greenhouse gas emissions, Jacobs will also incorporate fuel-efficient fleet management into project operations. Specifically, Jacobs will include two hybrid vehicles to support daily operations and maintenance of the facilities. These vehicles will be used for site inspections, sampling, and other routine activities, reducing fuel consumption and lowering the project's overall carbon footprint. By integrating hybrid vehicles alongside process-level energy optimization, Jacobs supports the client in realizing benefits not only within facility operations but also across the broader support systems that keep the reuse program running reliably and sustainably.

**Case example (RO CIP frequency):** at the Pure Water Monterey facility, Jacobs reduced RO clean-in-place (CIP) frequency by 35% through membrane monitoring and refined pre-treatment practices — an approach Jacobs will replicate at this facility using performance normalization tools and vendor-integrated service contracts.

## Chemical efficiency

Jacobs will use titration-based dosing, predictive cleaning schedules, and analyzer optimization to ensure minimal chemical use without compromising water quality or regulatory compliance. For the facilities, Jacobs will evaluate log reduction value (LRV) targets in relation to influent variability and optimize chemical feed rates accordingly.

## Reuse guidance

The energy-optimization methodology (SCADA energy dashboards, VFD tuning, per-process-step benchmarking, off-peak operations) and chemical-efficiency methodology (titration-based dosing, predictive cleaning, LRV-informed feed-rate optimization) are fully generic and reusable for any advanced treatment facility. The two named case examples (Soquel Creek's 10%+ energy reduction; Pure Water Monterey's 35% RO CIP frequency reduction) reference real, other Jacobs-operated facilities in California — confirm currency and permission before reuse in a new proposal, and do not attribute these results to a different facility.
