# Low-Pressure Sewer and Grinder Pump System O&M Experience

Building on our experience managing complex collection systems, we bring **extensive experience evaluating, operating, and maintaining low-pressure sewer systems**, including grinder pump networks integrated with broader wastewater infrastructure. Our team understands the **unique operational, maintenance, and asset management considerations** associated with these systems, including pump reliability, pressure system performance, electrical and controls integration, and lifecycle planning.

We have supported utilities with system assessments, capital planning, development of O&M programs, SOPs, and asset management strategies tailored to low-pressure infrastructure. This experience **directly aligns with [CLIENT]'s system of approximately 175 grinder pumps and associated low-pressure piping** — [CLIENT] being a coastal New England municipal wastewater utility — including budgeting for routine pump replacement, SCADA and asset management integration, odor and grease control, and emergency response.

In Westerly, Rhode Island, we are assisting a client in addressing performance challenges within a low-pressure area of its collection system. Our team initiated a **targeted, out-of-scope evaluation to identify root causes and develop practical, long-term solutions** to improve system reliability and performance.

Proposed Project Manager Nathan Callison brings **direct experience managing dual-pressure systems** that incorporate both residential grinder pumps and septic tank effluent pump (STEP) units. Jacobs currently operates the Northport–Leelanau Township Utility Authority collection system, which includes integrated force main and gravity infrastructure, multiple lift stations, and approximately **160 residential grinder pump installations**. This hands-on experience ensures a **strong technical foundation for supporting [CLIENT]'s system**.

## Reuse guidance

This is a qualifications and experience narrative, not a step-by-step O&M methodology — pair it with the pursuit's actual technical approach to low-pressure maintenance (PM schedules, spare-parts stocking strategy, customer-side responsibility boundaries, call-out response times) if the RFP asks for methodology detail. Three things make it work and should survive any rewrite: naming the specific failure modes rather than the technology category; the out-of-scope evaluation sentence, which is a reusable pattern for showing consultative value beyond contract scope; and tying the claim to a named individual with a countable installed base, which is far harder for a competitor to match than a corporate assertion. Tie the dual-pressure experience claim to whichever proposed key-personnel resume is actually included in the staffing plan, and do not carry the claim forward if that person is not on the team.
