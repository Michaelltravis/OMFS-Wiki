# Phased Baseline-to-Optimization O&M Transition Approach

A phased transition approach supports stable and sustainable operations. Initial efforts establish baseline conditions that enable compliance, reliability, and resilience; subsequent optimization focuses on identifying opportunities to enhance efficiency and sustainability. Sustainability initiatives are most effective when built on stable, well-controlled operations — applied prematurely, they may yield short-term gains while increasing long-term risk. The phased framework prioritizes consistent compliance first, then integrates best practices, including KPI target setting and operations strategy development, to ensure disciplined execution and durable outcomes.

## Baseline Operations: Data-Driven Diagnostic Assessment

A coordinated approach integrating operations, maintenance, and process engineering expertise is applied to confirm treatment performance is stable, optimal, and compliant. The process engineering and operations teams evaluate existing operations using data provided through the RFP/due-diligence process, reviewing the full historical operating dataset (typically the most recent 1-2 years), supplemented by on-site tours. This analysis focuses on identifying operational gaps, risks, and improvement opportunities.

A review of plant operational records can reveal data integrity and process-control issues that directly affect day-to-day performance and increase compliance risk, such as:

- **Limited unit-process performance data** (e.g., primary clarifier solids removal and sludge blanket data), which limits optimization and odor management.
- **Chemical usage record anomalies** (e.g., negative values), indicating data entry errors or inconsistent chemical handling/inventory practices that mask true chemical demand and limit optimization efforts.
- **Inconsistent mixed liquor suspended solids (MLSS) and solids inventory control**, where MLSS fluctuates without corresponding changes in flow or wasting practices, suggesting limited aeration-basin mass balance control. Operating at a consistent solids residence time (SRT) is critical to process stability and efficient operations. Sludge wasting occurs intermittently rather than continuously, at normalized wasting rates below typical requirements (e.g., roughly 600–900 lbs/day in this case), which contributes to excess solids inventory, increased clarifier loading, and higher oxygen demand; low sludge volume index (SVI) readings reflect dense, over-aged sludge consistent with insufficient solids turnover.
- **Lack of dissolved oxygen (DO) data**, indicating limited aeration control instrumentation and reduced ability to maintain stable biological conditions — often reinforced by observed poor sludge floc quality during site visits.

Despite such data challenges, a facility may still be maintaining permit compliance for key parameters (e.g., BOD, TSS) — the data gaps are a risk to *future* stability, not necessarily evidence of current noncompliance.

## Whole-Plant Process Simulation Model

A preliminary process simulation model is developed using recent operating data, facility planning information, and available drawings. The model evaluates mass balance across the full complement of unit processes (70+ components in this case) and simulates treatment performance under varying conditions. Results can confirm that a facility is capable of achieving strong performance under its current configuration once core process controls are stabilized, including:

- **Consistent Wasting.** Transitioning to steady, proportional wasting stabilizes MLSS, improves solids retention time (SRT) control, and prevents biomass over-aging.
- **Sludge Storage and Throughput Management.** Improved control of sludge blanket depths and thickening throughput reduces the risk of solids washout and clarifier stress.
- **Predictable Effluent Quality.** With improved SRT management and aeration control, the facility can achieve stable effluent performance with minimal upset risk.

As operations progress, additional data collection refines the process model, which becomes a core component of the operational plan — confirming capabilities, identifying alternative process strategies and enhancements, and supporting operations staff training.

## Targeted Improvement Opportunities

The technical approach combines data-driven diagnostics, process modeling, and targeted operational improvements to achieve stable, efficient, and sustainable performance. Addressing foundational controls — sludge wasting, primary treatment, DO management, and chemical use — supports smoother operations, improved compliance, and reduced operating costs. Evaluations cover primary clarifiers, aeration basins, secondary clarifiers and solids inventory management, and chlorine and caustic chemical feed systems, to ensure balance is maintained across the full treatment process.

| Area | Observed Issue | Improvement Opportunities | Expected Outcome/Benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary Clarifiers | Odor concerns; inconsistent use; mechanical deficiencies; risk of allowing high BOD to secondary | Rehabilitate primary clarifiers (mechanical repairs, skimmers, drives); evaluate odor scrubbers; restore primary clarification as the first BOD-removal step | Reduced odors; lower aeration energy (BOD load reduction); more stable and efficient secondary treatment performance |
| Aeration Basins | Poor DO control; underloaded basins running inefficiently | Install/review/relocate DO probes for representative readings; implement airflow control strategy (e.g., DO-based blower automation); review blower sizing and turndown capability | Reduced energy consumption; improved effluent stability |
| Secondary Clarifiers/Solids Inventory | Intermittent wasting; low SVIs indicate old sludge; overloaded solids inventory | Convert wasting to a continuous or time-paced mode; establish SRT targets with daily MLSS monitoring; maintain consistent sludge withdrawal from clarifiers | Better control of SRT and sludge age; improved clarifier performance; reduced risk of washout or pin floc; smoother biosolids operations |
| Chemical Feed Systems (Chlorine and Caustic) | Negative usage values; poor inventory tracking; risk of over- or under-dosing | Evaluate online pH and chlorine residual analyzers; add chemical tank level sensors or load cells; implement/revise automated or semi-automated feed pacing | Lower chemical costs; reduced compliance risk; accurate inventory control and improved safety |
