# Phased Baseline-to-Optimization O&M Transition Approach

A phased transition approach supports **stable and sustainable operations**. Initial efforts establish baseline conditions that enable compliance, reliability, and resilience. Subsequent optimization focuses on identifying opportunities to enhance efficiency and sustainability. Sustainability initiatives are most effective when built on stable, well-controlled operations; applied prematurely, they may yield short-term gains while increasing long-term risk. Our phased framework prioritizes **consistent compliance**, then integrates best practices, including key performance indicator (KPI) target setting and operations strategy development, to ensure **disciplined execution and durable outcomes**.

## Baseline Operations

A coordinated approach integrating operations, maintenance, and process engineering expertise will be applied to confirm treatment performance is **stable, optimal, and compliant**. Our process engineering and operations teams evaluated existing operations using data and information provided through the RFP. To establish a baseline understanding of current operating conditions at [CLIENT]'s WWTF — a coastal New England municipal treatment facility — our team reviewed the full historical dataset available for 2024 and 2025, supplemented by two on-site tours. This analysis focused on data quality and treatment performance to identify **operational gaps, risks, and improvement opportunities**.

Our review of the plant's operational records revealed several **data integrity and process-control issues** that directly affect day-to-day performance and increase compliance risk, including:

- **Primary Clarifier Data Gaps.** Limited performance data were available for the primary clarifiers, even during periods of operation. The absence of solids removal and sludge blanket data limits optimization and odor management.
- **Chemical Usage Anomalies.** Historical chemical consumption records contain numerous negative values, indicating data entry errors or inconsistent chemical handling and inventory practices. These discrepancies mask true chemical demand and limit optimization efforts.
- **Inconsistent Mixed Liquor Suspended Solids (MLSS) and Solids Inventory Control.** MLSS values fluctuate without corresponding changes in flow or wasting practices, suggesting inconsistent solids inventory management and limited aeration basin mass balance control. Operating at a consistent solids residence time (SRT) is critical to maintaining process stability and efficient operations. Sludge wasting occurs intermittently rather than continuously, with normalized wasting rates (600-900 pounds/day) below typical requirements, which contributes to excess solids inventory, increased clarifier loading, and higher oxygen demand. Low sludge volume index (SVI) readings reflect dense, over-aged sludge consistent with insufficient solids turnover.
- **Lack of Dissolved Oxygen (DO) Data.** No DO data were available, indicating limited aeration control instrumentation and reduced ability to maintain stable biological conditions. This concern was reinforced by observed poor sludge floc quality during site visits.

Despite the data challenges, the facility has maintained NPDES compliance for effluent biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) and total suspended solids (TSS).

Our team developed a preliminary process simulation model using 2024-2025 operating data, facility planning information, and available drawings. This whole-plant model evaluates mass balance across more than 70 wastewater components and simulates treatment performance under varying conditions. Results confirm the facility can achieve strong performance under its current configuration once core process controls are stabilized, including:

- **Consistent Wasting.** Transitioning to steady, proportional wasting stabilizes MLSS, improves SRT control, and prevents biomass over-aging.
- **Sludge Storage and Throughput Management.** Improved control of sludge blanket depths and thickening throughput reduces the risk of solids washout and clarifier stress.
- **Predictable Effluent Quality.** With improved SRT management and aeration control, the facility can achieve stable effluent performance with minimal upset risk.

## Opportunities for Stable and Sustainable Operations

As operations progress, additional data collection will support refinement of the process model. Our wastewater process engineering team will refine the process model using sampling and process data collected at the plant. The model will serve as a core component of the operational plan, confirming capabilities, identifying alternative process strategies and enhancements, and supporting operations staff training.

Our technical approach combines **data-driven diagnostics, process modeling, and targeted operational improvements** to achieve **stable, efficient, and sustainable performance**. Addressing foundational controls, such as sludge wasting, primary treatment, DO management, and chemical use, supports smoother operations, **improved compliance, and reduced operating costs**. Evaluations will include primary clarifiers, aeration basins, secondary clarifiers, and solids inventory management, and chlorine and caustic chemical feed systems to ensure balance across the treatment process. Exhibit 5-5 summarizes representative improvement opportunities.

## Exhibit 5-5. Potential areas for targeted operational improvements to enhance reliability, reduce costs, and strengthen environmental performance

| Area | Observed Issue | Identified Improvement Opportunities | Expected Outcome/Benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary Clarifiers** | Odor concerns; inconsistent use; mechanical deficiencies; risk of allowing high BOD to secondary | Rehabilitate primary clarifiers (e.g., mechanical repairs, skimmers, drives); evaluate odor scrubbers for primary clarifiers; restore primary clarification as the first BOD-removal step | Reduced odors; lower aeration energy (BOD load reduction); more stable and efficient secondary treatment performance |
| **Aeration Basins** | Poor DO control; underloaded basins running inefficiently | Install, review, and/or relocate DO probes for representative readings; implement airflow control strategy (e.g., DO-based blower automation); review blower sizing and turndown capability | Reduced energy consumption; improved effluent stability |
| **Secondary Clarifiers/Solids Inventory** | Intermittent wasting; low SVIs indicate old sludge; overloaded solids inventory | Convert wasting to a continuous or time-paced mode; establish SRT targets with daily MLSS monitoring; maintain consistent sludge withdrawal from clarifiers | Better control of SRT and sludge age; improved clarifier performance; reduced risk of washout or pin floc; smoother biosolids operations |
| **Chemical Feed Systems (Chlorine and Caustic)** | Negative usage values; poor inventory tracking; risk of over- or under-dosing | Evaluate online pH and chlorine residual analyzers; add chemical tank level sensors or load cells; implement/revise automated or semi-automated feed pacing | Lower chemical costs; reduced compliance risk; accurate inventory control and improved safety |
