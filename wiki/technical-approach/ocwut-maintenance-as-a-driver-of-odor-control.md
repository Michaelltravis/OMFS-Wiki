# Maintenance as a Driver of Odor Control

Odor conditions are not only a process issue. They are directly tied to maintenance practices. Current conditions indicate a reactive maintenance approach, where critical equipment remains out of service for extended periods and repairs are deferred or bypassed. This "firefighting" model contributes directly to odor generation, particularly where grit systems, headworks equipment, and solids handling processes are not consistently available. When these systems are not functioning as intended, conditions deteriorate quickly, leading to bypass conditions, solids accumulation, and increased hydrogen sulfide generation.

Our site observations confirmed recurring issues with grit systems, equipment outages, and limited access due to high hydrogen sulfide levels. These conditions compound odor problems and limit the ability to perform necessary maintenance.

We'll address this by prioritizing odor-critical assets within the maintenance program. Preventive and predictive maintenance will focus on headworks, grit systems, ventilation systems, and solids handling equipment. By improving equipment availability and reliability, we'll eliminate one of the primary drivers of odor conditions and enable operators to manage proactively rather than working around equipment limitations.
