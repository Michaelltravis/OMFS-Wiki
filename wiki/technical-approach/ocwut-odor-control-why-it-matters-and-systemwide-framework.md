# Why Odor Management Matters and the Prevent-Detect-Respond-Improve Framework

## Why odor management and control matters to [CLIENT]

Odor is one of the strongest indicators of system performance. It's what your customers experience first, and it directly shapes how your facilities are perceived by the community.

[CLIENT]'s system — a multi-plant regional wastewater system served by long force mains and major pump stations — inherently carries elevated odor risk. Long force mains create conditions for septicity and hydrogen sulfide formation before flow reaches the plant. Pump stations and wet wells act as localized hotspots. Facilities like Deer Creek operate near residential areas where even minor issues quickly become highly visible. Based on our site observations, these risks are driven by a combination of upstream conditions, process variability, equipment availability, and solids handling practices. Elevated hydrogen sulfide levels in dewatering buildings and belt press operations at North Canadian and Deer Creek have already restricted access for routine O&M, delaying essential work and increasing safety risk for staff.

When odor isn't controlled at the source, the consequences compound. Complaints increase, regulatory attention follows, and operations become reactive, relying on excessive chemical use to compensate for instability. Costs rise, reliability falls, and public confidence erodes. Odor is not just an environmental issue. It is a system performance issue, and we will manage it as one. We'll replace the current reactive approach with structured, data-driven control, managing odor through stable processes, reliable equipment, and clear operational accountability. **Given that odor has been a persistent challenge at these facilities, with multiple studies conducted over the years, we've developed a more comprehensive approach to getting ahead of it. Section 8 details the innovative tools and strategies we'll bring to bear, going beyond conventional chemical treatment to address odor at its source.**

## A systemwide approach to odor management

**We'll implement a structured, systemwide approach built around four actions: prevent, detect, respond, and improve.**

We'll **prevent** odor by stabilizing process conditions and controlling hydrogen sulfide generation at the source, maintaining appropriate dissolved oxygen, managing solids retention time, and minimizing detention time in wet wells, force mains, and sludge storage.

We'll **detect** odor through routine field presence and targeted monitoring. Operators will patrol both facilities and the collection system using portable hydrogen sulfide meters, with off-site patrols conducted during known high-odor periods. All findings will be logged with time, weather conditions, and wind direction, creating a traceable record of field conditions.

We'll **respond** quickly and consistently, with written guidelines in place so operator response is immediate and consistent rather than improvised (**Exhibit 1-18**). We'll **improve** continuously by tracking odor data, complaint history, and operational metrics to refine chemical dosing, adjust process control, and target improvements where they're needed most. This approach replaces reactive responses with consistent, repeatable performance.

*Exhibit 1-18. Jacobs' step-by-step process will identify sources of odor, including the expected dispersion. Using this information, improvements can be prioritized to create the most significant positive impact on the neighboring community.*
