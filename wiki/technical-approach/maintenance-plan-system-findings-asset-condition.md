# Maintenance Plan Opener and System-Wide Due Diligence Findings

Jacobs approaches maintenance through an asset management lens — understanding what you have, knowing its condition, and making every dollar count toward long-term reliability, compliance, and safety. Our closed-loop operating model links asset risk, disciplined planning and scheduling, execution quality, and continuous performance feedback, so every maintenance activity is deliberate, traceable, and tied to cost and risk. **The goal isn't just to fix things; it's to prevent failures, extend asset life, and build a program that gets smarter over time.**

We've implemented thousands of asset management programs across North America and around the world, and we didn't just adopt industry best practices; we helped write them. That depth of knowledge is what we'll bring to [CLIENT] — a southcentral US municipal water utility trust operating four wastewater treatment plants and a major pump station — every day.

## What We Found Across the System

You've set the expectation clearly: every maintenance dollar should protect asset value and extend useful life. Meeting that standard starts with truly understanding what you have. During our March 2026 site visits, technical due diligence, and review of available operating data, we developed a detailed picture of where these facilities stand today, and what it will take to move them from reactive maintenance to sustained reliability. That understanding shapes every element of our plan that follows. Four themes emerged consistently:

- **H₂S-driven equipment degradation is the most pervasive maintenance challenge across the system.** At North Canadian, hydrogen sulfide concentrations in the headworks, grit building, and solids handling areas are severe enough to prevent personnel access for routine preventive and corrective maintenance. Personal monitors triggered high-level alarms repeatedly during our walkthrough. Electrical systems, HVAC components, structural concrete, and instrumentation throughout the headworks show visible deterioration from prolonged exposure. At Deer Creek, domed primary clarifiers trap H₂S, and the dewatering building cannot be safely accessed during belt filter press operations. At both facilities, odor control systems are largely non-operational — many are obsolete, with original manufacturers no longer providing support. The Witcher Pump Station's sealed junction box, which receives the force main discharge with no odor control or exhaust, is a suspected major contributor to the concentrated H₂S conditions at North Canadian's headworks. This isn't just an odor problem; it's an access, safety, and asset protection problem that directly limits the maintenance team's ability to do their job.

- **The current maintenance program is operating in reactive mode.** Our due diligence confirmed that maintenance activities have been focused on emergency repairs rather than planned prevention. The pattern is visible in cascading failures we documented: grit system downtime at North Canadian leads to primary clarifier bypass, which sends grit into aeration basins and accelerates wear on downstream equipment. Reporting data shows this cycle repeating nearly every month. At Chisholm Creek, both headworks bar screens broke down, a failure with an estimated $200,000 cost for repairs and bypass pumping that due diligence suggests may have been preventable with proactive maintenance. Across the system, the gap between where these facilities are and where they need to be is the gap between firefighting and a disciplined, planned maintenance culture. Jacobs will close that gap.

- **The existing parts storage and inventory foundation is stronger than we typically see.** The spare parts inventory is already loaded into NexGen with part numbers, descriptions, locations, and minimum/maximum quantities assigned to individual assets. Parts cages at North Canadian are organized and accessible. This is a foundation we'll build on rather than replace — preserving institutional knowledge while adding the planning discipline needed to prevent stockouts of critical spares and manage inventory costs.

- **Significant CIP activity will reshape the maintenance landscape.** South Canadian's new 8.5-MGD oxidation ditch, OSHG, and disc thickener are expected online in summer 2026. Deer Creek is scheduled for a 2027 upgrade, including a new blower building, primary clarifier rehabilitation, and additional secondary clarifiers that will increase capacity to 21 MGD, with a further expansion to 27–32 MGD planned for 2032. North Canadian has a new headworks with grit removal in the 2027–2028 CIP plan. These projects will introduce new equipment with active warranties, new process technologies, and new O&M requirements that the maintenance program must absorb seamlessly.

**Callout — Jacobs asset management leadership.** Jacobs literally wrote the book on water asset management. Our subject matter experts have authored several industry publications at the forefront of asset management, including McGraw Hill's *Water Infrastructure Asset Management: Adopting Best Practices to Enable Better Investments* and WEF's *Implementing Asset Management: A Practical Guide*. ## Reuse guidance

Universal: the asset-management-lens opening, the "we helped write the industry best practices" credential and its two-publication proof, and the four-theme findings architecture that converts a site visit into a diagnosis. The closing line of each theme — naming the consequence for the client, not just the observation — is the device that makes this read as understanding rather than inventory.

Pursuit-specific: every facility name, the H₂S observations and monitor alarms, the $200,000 bar screen failure, the parts-cage finding, and all CIP capacities and dates. Replace with findings from the target pursuit's own due diligence; do not carry another client's conditions forward.
