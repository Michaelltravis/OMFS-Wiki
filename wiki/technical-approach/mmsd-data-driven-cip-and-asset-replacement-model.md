# Data-Driven Capital Improvement Planning and the Asset Replacement Model

Jacobs' data driven CIP approach leverages asset data collected during maintenance activities to optimize performance, reduce risk and ensure asset reliability. [CLIENT] manages hundreds of asset types across its diverse facilities with various operating environments. By developing asset-type specific likelihood of failure factors for the various operating conditions, Jacobs can quantify risk reduction. We'll apply proven methods, including cost-benefit analysis, multicriteria evaluation and risk-based planning, to balance performance, risk and cost effectively within a CIP.

A deep understanding of asset level data and its system-wide impacts lead to development of projects that deliver measurable value. With [CLIENT]'s CIP exceeding $2B, as shown in **Exhibit IV-47**, there are many competing priorities and ongoing projects. It's critical to maintain visibility into current investments and future planned projects to ensure strategic alignment and efficient resource allocation.

**Integrated Approach to CIP Planning.** Jacobs' CIP process is an integral part of our asset management program, focusing on maximizing asset life through strategic maintenance, rehabilitation, and renewal. The goal of our CIP process is to drive effective, defensible decision-making. We use methodologies such as benefit–cost analysis, multi-criteria analysis, and risk-based decision-making to balance performance, risk, and cost, delivering optimal outcomes in terms of community satisfaction, asset reliability, and financial sustainability.

Our CIP process focuses on three improvement and investment streams:

- **Asset Expansion:** Upgrading, creating, or purchasing new assets based on growth or elevated service levels.
- **Asset Renewal:** Upgrading existing assets to maintain required service levels and manage risk.
- **Strategic Investment:** Providing financial returns, resiliency, or future opportunities.

New-build CIP initiatives focus on bridging service level gaps and accommodating growth, while ensuring investments consider full lifecycle costs and [CLIENT]'s funding capabilities for operations, maintenance, and asset renewal. Our asset renewal forecasting approach incorporates historic data analysis, lifecycle estimates, predictive modeling, and direct asset observation to prioritize funding effectively. Maintenance data will flow into risk ranking and obsolescence tracking to identify assets approaching end-of-life or due for upgrade. The output is a living reinvestment list ranked by risk, consequence, and lifecycle costs so [CLIENT] can allocate funds where reliability per dollar is maximized.

**CIP Integration with Jacobs' Asset Management Approach.** Key components of our asset management program that support and inform our CIP process include:

- **Condition Assessment:** Regular evaluations to inform operational status, risk, and maintenance effectiveness.
- **Asset Repair and Replacement (R&R):** Applying our Asset Replacement Model (ARM) for objective, risk-based planning and prioritization, as shown in **Exhibit IV-48**.
- **Asset Condition Evaluation System:** A Jacobs-developed program for risk assessment, condition monitoring, and maintenance strategy development that feeds directly into CIP planning.

Our staff regularly help clients develop both capital plans and R&R by using lifecycle modeling, as well as our ARM procedure. This procedure results in capital plan development, including multi-year look-ahead that integrates with annual and actionable R&R plans. When making R&R recommendations for facility assets, Jacobs' approach considers total maintenance costs, asset condition, and estimated remaining life. The benefits of the ARM process are a well-organized and collaborative approach that:

- Includes replacement asset value so a preliminary prioritized R&R plan may be developed
- Can use existing condition assessment information as a starting point
- Highlights troubled assets and potential for proactive maintenance optimization opportunities through the condition assessment process
- Identifies candidate assets for further net present value analysis to determine action on high-risk or costly asset replacements
- Results in R&R triggers based on actual asset condition, annual maintenance costs, and remaining useful life

**Exhibit IV-48 (graphic `276_007CAM_2`)** shows the ARM tool optimizing return on investment by triggering the right R&R at the right time: the model filters asset history, risk, replacement value, installation date, condition score, and typical life through **Trigger A – Asset Risk**, **Trigger B – Asset Age**, and **Trigger C – Annualized Maintenance Cost** to produce a scheduled replacement plan.
