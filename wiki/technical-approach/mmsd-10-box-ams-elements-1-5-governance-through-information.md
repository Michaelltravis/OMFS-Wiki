# 10-Box Asset Management System, Elements 1–5 — Governance Through Asset Information

We'll tailor **Jacobs' proven 10-Box AMS framework (Exhibit IV-43, graphic 272_007CAM_4)** — based on the IAM 10-Box Excellence Model and consistent with Global Forum on Maintenance and Asset Management guidance — to [CLIENT]'s scope and priorities. [CLIENT] is a regional sewerage district operating two large water reclamation facilities and a deep tunnel conveyance system. Each element is mapped to concrete deliverables, owners, and committee touchpoints so the program is auditable and durable. The collaboration with [CLIENT] and the governance committees will drive outcomes from the AMS.

**1. Leadership and Governance.** **Establish an Asset Management Policy and Maintenance Governance Charter** that define objectives, decision rights, and change control. The Maintenance Committee (with CMMS and SCADA subcommittees) will be the forum for approving PM list changes, data standards, integrations, and exceptions. Outcomes include clear accountability, faster decisions, and one source of truth.

**2. Strategy and Planning.** **Publish the SAMP, which will include measurable objectives** for reliability, risk, safety, and cost. **Complete a baseline condition and criticality assessment** across WRF and conveyance assets; produce a risk-ranked register that drives the Long-Term Maintenance Plan and informs capital improvement planning (see element 7). Tie staffing plans and materials strategy to asset risk and workload.

**3. Asset Management Decision-Making.** **Use risk, criticality, and lifecycle cost to pick the right action at the right time** — PM, PdM, CM, refurbishment, or replacement. Decision standards will be embedded into NEXGEN (job plans, priority rules, approval thresholds) so choices are consistent and traceable. Where trade-offs exist (cost vs. risk vs. performance), we'll document the rationale and the trigger to revisit.

**4. Lifecycle Delivery and Maintenance Strategy.** Deliver a **Long-Term Maintenance Plan** that combines class-based PM with **PdM** options and standard corrective practices. We'll formalize **PM Optimization (PMO)** to review frequencies, failure modes, and job plans at least annually; escalate chronic issues to root cause analysis (RCA) and, where justified, to PdM investments. Operator inspections provide early warning; job plans specify acceptance criteria and proof-of-work evidence.

**5. Asset Information (Knowledge Enablers).** During mobilization, **complete asset registry validation and QR/barcode tagging using a NEXGEN-aligned data dictionary**. Integrate **GIS for linear/conveyance assets** and link **SCADA/Historian tags to equipment records**. Field staff will use mobile devices to scan, execute, and close work with photos, meter reads, parts, and test results — creating a durable audit trail. A CMMS Governance standard will standardize naming conventions, hierarchies, and required fields.

*The 10-Box Model is a globally recognized framework developed by the Institute of Asset Management (IAM) that integrates strategic planning, operational management, and performance optimization. It emphasizes the interplay between technical, financial, and organizational elements, ensuring all aspects of asset management are interconnected and aligned with organizational goals.*

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the framework, the IAM attribution footnote, and the discipline of attaching a deliverable and an owner to every box. Elements 3 and 4 — decision standards embedded in the CMMS, and PMO reviewed at least annually with escalation to RCA — are the two that reviewers score.

Pursuit-specific: the mobilization-window commitments in element 5 (registry validation, QR/barcode tagging) must match the transition plan's schedule; the asset classes named in element 2; the CMMS platform throughout.
