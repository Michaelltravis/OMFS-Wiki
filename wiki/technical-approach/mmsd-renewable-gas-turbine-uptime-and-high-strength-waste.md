# Maximizing Renewable Gas — Turbine Uptime and High-Strength Waste Digestion

**Maximize the amount of landfill gas used in the turbines and dryers while maximizing the power generation in the turbines.** Historical data shows that the generator auxiliary equipment has contributed to most of the down time for the Solar Turbines. Jacobs will work with [CLIENT], a regional sewerage district operating two large water reclamation facilities, during the transition and CMMS programming to ensure the proper PM/PdM is assigned to all the generator system assets. This will include identifying spare parts and ensuring they are onsite to minimize any power generation down time. Maximizing turbine availability will reduce natural gas use and purchased power while increasing the amount of waste heat available for drying and building heat. In the future, when systems such as auxiliary equipment fail, Jacobs will deploy our SMEs to analyze failures, implement corrective actions, and prevent repeat issues.

**Increasing digester gas production via increased high strength waste.** [CLIENT] currently digests high strength waste from Miller-Coors Brewery, airport de-icing fluid, and food waste from a partnership with Insinkerator. One way to increase digester gas production is to increase the amount of high strength waste used. We understand [CLIENT] plans to increase the amount of storage for high strength waste and seeks additional high strength waste sources. **As an added value, Jacobs could partner with [CLIENT] and Marquette University (Marquette) to identify and test potential sources of high strength waste.** We performed a similar evaluation with Marquette and the Green Bay Metropolitan Sewerage District and identified several high strength waste sources that boosted digester gas production. The survey for Green Bay identified industries closer to [CLIENT]'s service area than Green Bay that Green Bay did not use and could be available to [CLIENT].

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the argument that turbine or engine availability is a maintenance outcome, and that transition-period CMMS programming is where that outcome is decided — this is a strong incumbent-displacement point when the failure history is visible in the client's own records. Also universal: offering a university research partnership as a no-cost value add for high-strength waste sourcing, and citing a completed survey at a peer utility in the same state.

Pursuit-specific: the Solar Turbine reference, the named waste generators, and the Green Bay survey result — the Green Bay Metropolitan Sewerage District and Marquette University are real third-party references and stay verbatim.
