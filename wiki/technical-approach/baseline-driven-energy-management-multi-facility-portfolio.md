# Baseline-Driven Energy Management Across a Multi-Facility Portfolio

## Understanding [CLIENT]'s energy landscape

[CLIENT]'s four wastewater treatment facilities and its major influent pump station represent a combined treatment capacity of more than 110 MGD. (Facility-specific energy profiles, baseline energy intensities, and the operational drivers we observed during site visits are documented in the **Process Control Strategy** section earlier in this Technical Approach.)

[CLIENT] has made clear that energy management is not optional; it's a contractual obligation. This includes developing and implementing an Energy Efficiency Plan, maintaining electricity usage within +5% of an annually established baseline, meeting with the General Manager annually to audit usage variances and discuss consumption practices, and reporting monthly and annually on energy performance.

[CLIENT] has confirmed that the Energy Efficiency Plan baseline will be established using the annual average kWh/MG of effluent flow at each facility, a flow-adjusted metric that measures genuine operational efficiency rather than penalizing operators for flow variability outside their control. CIP projects will be considered in baseline development, and [CLIENT] will remain responsible for providing electricity.

We understand these requirements and the operational realities behind them. Energy management at [CLIENT] isn't just about tracking kilowatt hours; it's about managing aging aeration systems, integrating new CIP assets, and making daily process decisions that **keep costs in line without compromising treatment performance or permit compliance**.

## Expertise for energy optimization and more

Senior energy and chemical efficiency strategist Dawn Lesley, PE, will work alongside the on-site team to turn [CLIENT]'s baseline-driven energy management into day-to-day results—advising operators, evaluating energy and chemical optimization, and prioritizing the highest-return improvement opportunities.

**DAWN LESLEY, PE — O&M ENERGY AND CHEMICAL OPTIMIZATION SME.** Dawn leads energy and chemical optimization across Jacobs' O&M portfolio, with documented savings in the millions of kWh at comparable plants. She'll establish defensible facility baselines, train operators on changes that move the kWh/MG needle, and surface low- and no-cost improvements before capital enters the conversation.

## Our approach: baseline-driven energy management

Jacobs will **manage energy as a core operating discipline—embedded in daily decision-making, continuously monitored, and fully transparent to [CLIENT]**. Using historical data, SCADA, and operational trends, we'll establish a clear, defensible baseline for each facility that reflects actual conditions, seasonal variability, and process loading. From that foundation, we'll track performance in real time, convert data into actionable insights, and identify opportunities to reduce energy intensity while maintaining process stability, compliance, and system resiliency.

### Tracking performance where it counts

**Our approach focuses on the largest controllable energy drivers, aeration and pumping, where operational discipline has the greatest day-to-day impact.** The facility-by-facility aeration optimization strategy is described in **Process Control Strategy**, and the flow- and demand-responsive aeration control we'll deploy through SCADA is described in the **Phase 3 Automation Integration** subsection of the **SCADA/OT/Cybersecurity** section earlier in this Technical Approach. Proactive maintenance that keeps motors and blowers operating at peak efficiency is detailed in the Maintenance Plan.

**Our energy management approach provides the performance tracking framework that ties it together.** Energy performance will be tracked using kWh per million gallons treated, or kWh per thousand pounds of pollutant removed. Chemical disinfection dosing will be tracked using pounds of chemical per million gallons treated. This gives [CLIENT] transparent reporting, measurable results, and confidence in our commitment to energy and chemical optimization.
