# Biosolids Transfer, Beneficial Reuse, and Dewatering Optimization

[FACILITY C], a smaller WRF, has a relatively small amount of weekly biosolids that are transported in liquid form to [FACILITY A], a WRF. This general operation appears to be adequate given the proximity of the transport distance and the quantity that must be moved daily. The major issue observed was the loading area at [FACILITY C], which does not provide ease of transfer or spill containment. Temporary hoses were being used for the regular operation of transferring liquid sludge into mobile transport vehicles. While not incorrect by industry standards, when considering best management practices for recurring operations, a more robust transfer system could be easily implemented.

[FACILITY A] aerobic digesters may be able to achieve Class B Biosolids at some time in the future. If the processing of solids proves adequate to meet the federal code for this level of processing, then land application of the sludge/residuals may be a good path forward. In the first year, JC Solutions will conduct an evaluation of opportunities for an alternative beneficial reuse program for the biosolids.

Temporary hose connections are susceptible to leakage, and loading hoses also must be managed correctly after every load to prevent hose spillage. Even small amounts of liquid spillage can accumulate to an unwanted situation. A hardpiped solution could be constructed from the tank wall to the loading area, which would shorten the length of the temporary hose connection. Then a specific spill containment area could be constructed in this loading area to ensure no liquid sludge escapes the confines of the planned loading station.

A complete cost analysis to examine potential savings for beneficial reuse and the costs to achieve Class B as compared to the high cost of landfill disposal will help guide [CLIENT] in evaluating this potentially highly valuable opportunity.

For future biosolids operations, JC Solutions will continue with the existing program of dewatered cake going to the landfill and liquid being transported to [FACILITY A] with attention to some details and considerations for others. [FACILITY A] and [FACILITY B] will continue dewatering as is and send dewatered cake to the landfill. A service provider will be used for this Transport and Disposal service. Several service providers are available at this time, and the decision to choose between them will be based upon best pricing and reliability for [CLIENT].

We will undertake a complete review of the dewatering program and polymer usage for both [FACILITY A] and [FACILITY B] for efficiencies, including polymer bench testing, considering aging of mixed polymer as a method for reduction in dosage quantities and better efficiency for sludge flocculation. Our [FACILITY C] biosolids approach is to continue with the same liquid haul to [FACILITY A] with a review of loading operations to provide best management practices and mitigate risk in relation to the cost for some added improvements in loading equipment.
