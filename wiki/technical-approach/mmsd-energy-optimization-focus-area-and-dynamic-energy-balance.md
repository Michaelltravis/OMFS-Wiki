# Energy Optimization Focus Area — Running Energy Assets as One Coordinated System

**Energy Optimization — Focus Area.** [CLIENT]'s energy costs are significant but driven by different factors than other utilities. Production of [CLIENT]'s heat-dried biosolids product is energy-intensive, and inline storage system pumping can trigger high electrical demand charges. Our energy approach helps [CLIENT], a regional sewerage district operating two large water reclamation facilities, progress toward its 2035 Vision of meeting 100% of its energy needs with renewable sources, without putting compliance at risk. [CLIENT] already uses multiple energy assets, including landfill gas–fueled generation at the primary water reclamation facility, digester gas combined heat and power at the second facility, turbine waste-heat recovery for biosolids product drying, and solar panels. Our role is to run these assets as one coordinated system. We'll align liquids treatment, solids processing, and power generation to lower costs, increase renewable use, and improve reliability. Focus Areas described previously outline how we'll reduce energy use in liquids and solids processes; this subsection focuses on operating [CLIENT]'s power generation systems and capturing usable heat whenever possible.

We'll also work with [CLIENT] to evaluate and integrate capital projects that support long-term energy goals. To guide day-to-day decisions, we propose developing a system-wide, dynamic energy balance that builds on the "Sankey" model Jacobs developed for [CLIENT]'s Energy Plan (see **Exhibit IV-19** above). We'll expand the model to include solids loading, interplant transfers, turbine heat recovery, and gas distribution. The result will function as an operational dashboard across both plants. It will help operators compare options in real time—such as when to transfer sludge between plants, how to run the GE turbines to manage peak demand, and how best to route landfill gas to the Solar Turbines and dryers.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the opening that distinguishes this client's energy cost drivers from the sector norm before proposing anything; the "our role is to run these assets as one coordinated system" positioning, which is how an operator claims value on assets the owner already bought; and the commitment to tie operating decisions to the owner's published sustainability target while stating plainly that compliance is not traded for energy.

Pursuit-specific: the 2035 Vision target, the specific generation assets, the inline storage pumping demand driver, and the prior Energy Plan Sankey model — only claim the model extension where Jacobs actually authored the original.
