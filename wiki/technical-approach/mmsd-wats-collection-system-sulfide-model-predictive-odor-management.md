# WATS Collection System Sulfide Model — Predictive, Systemwide Odor and Corrosion Insight

**Proactive Odor Prevention and Systemwide Insight.** Jacobs' odor management strategy begins with understanding and anticipating odor-forming conditions before emissions occur. Because many odor issues begin in the collection system through H₂S formation, understanding the fundamentals of that process is essential for preventing odors and mitigating them if they occur. The processes are complex, and to better understand them Jacobs has collaborated for the past 10 years with the top global research team on collection system sulfide modeling at Aalborg University (Denmark). The University developed the WATS Model, which predicts sulfide formation and corrosion in wastewater collection systems and can be used to evaluate odor and corrosion mitigation options. Under [CLIENT]'s prior odor study, Jacobs built a systemwide WATS collection system model that identified odor- and corrosion-prone segments by integrating historical H₂S measurements, corrosion data, and complaint patterns, shown in **Exhibit IV-24** (asset ID `335_007CAM_2`).

This model allows operators to evaluate how operational changes such as adjusting pumping rates, altering flow routing, or receiving higher-strength industrial loads may increase odor or corrosion risks before they occur. When elevated risk is predicted, Jacobs prepares targeted mitigation strategies, including chemical addition, temporary vapor-phase treatment, or public notifications. We have successfully implemented these approaches for other utilities such as Oakland County (Detroit area), where Jacobs' Milwaukee staff recommended and deployed a temporary ferric chloride dosing system to control odor-generating sulfides during critical operating periods — a system installed in 2 weeks to mitigate collection system complaints.

There may be discharges to the conveyance system that need to be pretreated or eliminated, which could be contributing to odor issues. Also, the long collection system leading to the second water reclamation facility can lead to septic conditions. Another example of the cause of [CLIENT] odors is deep tunnel storage system pumping through the facility force main to a downstream metering point.

Exhibit IV-26 (asset ID `340_007CAM_3`) shows how we calibrated the model: green, orange, and red dots show measured H₂S concentrations on the map used to calibrate the WATS model. Jacobs will leverage the WATS odor model Jacobs prepared for [CLIENT] to assess operational impacts to odor.
