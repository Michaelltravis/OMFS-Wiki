# Optimizing Solids Processing and Heat-Dried Biosolids Production — Focus Area Opening

**Optimizing Solids Processing and Biosolids Product Production — Focus Area.** [CLIENT], a regional sewerage district operating two large water reclamation facilities, has operated its solids processing and heat-dried biosolids product production systems very effectively for almost a century, consistently meeting production and product-quality objectives. Even with this strong performance, the system remains uniquely complex: sludge streams from the two water reclamation facilities are highly interdependent, and product quality and quantity depend directly on the relative sources and proportions of these solids. Balancing solids transfers through the interplant pumping system adds additional operational challenges, as these decisions influence treatment performance and energy demands across both plants. At the same time, the integrated energy system—biogas, heat recovered from engine generators, renewable landfill gas, and supplemental natural gas—presents both constraints and opportunities within the overall production process. Building on [CLIENT]'s already strong operations, our approach offers targeted ideas to further optimize solids handling, energy use, and biosolids product production in a coordinated, systemwide manner.

Our approach focuses on identifying process and energy optimization while maintaining clean, well-managed facilities that support sustainable solids processing and ensure reliable biosolids product production that consistently meets product specifications and Class A compliance through strategic risk management.

**Efficient Solids Processing to Minimize Costs and Maximize Renewable Energy.** Our goal in operating the biosolids processes is to minimize cost and maximize the use of renewable energy. Through the energy plan we developed for [CLIENT] on a previous project, we understand the nexus between biosolids processing and energy management, as illustrated in **Exhibit IV-19**. To optimize biosolids processing operations, we'll build on past efforts and develop an integrated process and energy model that can be used to run "what-if" scenarios, so we can evaluate with [CLIENT] opportunities such as those described below.

Our firm led the design, construction, and startup of the drying and dewatering (D&D) facility in the early to mid-1980s and the evaluation and design of the replacement belt filter press in 2015. Staff involved with the D&D project, including Bill Desing, Dave Oerke, Dave Porter, and Kile Snider, still work for Jacobs. We'll leverage their knowledge when collaborating with [CLIENT] on potential operational improvements. Additionally, our team can rely on input from our partner Mead and Hunt's staff that have been involved with numerous D&D and rehabilitation projects. This experience provides us with insights into the operations that can be used to help optimize processes.

**EXHIBIT IV-19. INTEGRATED PROCESS AND ENERGY MODELS** (graphic asset `295_007CAM_5`) — Jacobs will refine integrated process and energy models that can be used to run "what-if" solids processing scenarios: waste heat and natural gas to the boiler; continuing [CLIENT]'s efforts to increase cake solids to reduce energy; evaluating waste heat pressure control to minimize waste heat blowoff; primary sludge/WAS and dewatered sludge feed to drying; prioritizing landfill gas to the turbines to reduce cost; and landfill gas turbines producing renewable electricity to plant or grid against total plant electrical consumption.
