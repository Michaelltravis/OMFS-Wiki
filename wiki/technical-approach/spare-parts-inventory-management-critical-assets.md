# Spare Parts and Inventory Management for Critical Assets

We will manage [CLIENT]'s spare parts inventory through NexGen, building on the existing well-organized system already loaded into the platform with part numbers, descriptions, locations, and minimum/maximum quantities assigned to individual assets. [CLIENT] is a Southcentral US regional water utility trust operating four wastewater treatment plants and a major pump station. A dedicated Stores/Parts Manager will maintain inventory integrity by receiving, cataloging, issuing, cycle counting, and reordering.

Our critical spare parts strategy starts with the Schedule 20 definition of Critical Assets and maps their failure-prone components to a stocking plan that supports the 2-day return-to-service requirement (CM-3). Inventory levels will be aligned with Minimum Inventory Levels compliance requirements and verified through regular cycle counts. Automated reorder triggers within NexGen based on minimum/maximum levels will prevent stockouts while controlling carrying costs. Bills of material linked to each asset will enable efficient parts identification during corrective events.

Parts and supplies to be paid for from the M&R Fund will be inventoried with detailed justifications and General Manager approval per Schedule 20. All inventory is the property of [CLIENT].

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the chain from critical-asset definition to failure-prone components to a stocking plan sized against a contractual return-to-service clock; min/max automated reorder triggers; asset-linked bills of material; cycle-count verification; and a named, dedicated stores role. Pursuit-specific: the schedule and requirement citations, the platform name, and the statement that the client owns the inventory. Pairs with the on-call and emergency maintenance response block (on-site critical spares are the first of the three response layers) and the M&R/R&R fund block, which governs how parts are reimbursed.
