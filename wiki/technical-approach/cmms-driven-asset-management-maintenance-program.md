# CMMS-Driven Asset Management and Maintenance Program

**[CLIENT]'s asset environment.** Jacobs understands that [CLIENT]'s wastewater system — a coastal New England municipal system — must perform in a demanding coastal setting. [CLIENT] operates a 3.07-MGD activated-sludge WWTF, seven wastewater pump stations, a downtown stormwater station, approximately 42 miles of gravity and force mains, and a low-pressure grinder system, all within a flood-prone peninsula environment where storms, access constraints, and community sensitivity heighten the importance of **reliable assets and disciplined maintenance**.

While observations suggest the facilities are generally maintained, [CLIENT] is clearly seeking more than routine maintenance support. [CLIENT] wants a partner that will protect its assets through cleaner facilities, more visible accountability, stronger self-performance, better use of Cartegraph and CMMS data, and closer linkage between daily maintenance and future capital decisions. Our **proactive asset management program** improves day-to-day execution while giving [CLIENT] **clearer visibility into condition, risk, and renewal priorities**.

As shown in **Exhibit 5-13**, we bring **visible change** to how [CLIENT]'s assets are managed, maintained, and improved, shifting **from reactive, fragmented practices to a planned, accountable, and self-performed program** (the four shifts and their benefits are set out in [Asset Management Improvement Shifts](asset-management-improvement-shifts-table.md)).

**Proven asset management and maintenance success.** We successfully implement the level of asset management and maintenance [CLIENT] is seeking as a **standard, proven approach across our wastewater O&M projects**. Significant maintenance projects are also supported through regional O&M maintenance resources that supplement on-site teams. Examples of maintenance successes on other O&M projects are included in Exhibit 5-14.

**CMMS-driven asset visibility.** A **preventive-first asset management program** will be implemented, anchored by a CMMS strategy developed to align with [CLIENT]'s adopted systems, workflows, and reporting needs. [CLIENT] has been moving toward Cartegraph, which, through its GIS-enabled environment, offers strong value for linear assets such as the collection system, pump stations, low-pressure sewer infrastructure, and flood-control assets.

We bring experience across a broad range of CMMS and EAM platforms, including Cartegraph and Maintenance Connection. Maintenance Connection has proven especially effective for vertical assets such as WWTF equipment because it supports disciplined work order management, preventive maintenance planning, asset history, and KPI reporting. Rather than forcing a one-size-fits-all solution before transition, we will confirm the most effective CMMS approach for all assets — whether that means using Cartegraph as the primary platform, supporting a complementary plant-focused workflow, or integrating value-add systems, so that [CLIENT] receives one **clear, reliable view of asset condition, maintenance status, and performance**.

## Maintenance Success Built on Proven Systems and Expert Resources

Jacobs' Regional Maintenance Teams provide direct, in-house support to our local O&M staff nationwide — including the Northeast. These teams bring deep technical expertise across HVAC, electrical, SCADA, mechanical systems, pump repair, equipment installation, and structural and facility construction. From routine maintenance to complex facility upgrades, these teams deliver responsive, high-quality service that extends asset life, improves reliability, and minimizes downtime. At the Traverse City WWTF, Jacobs managed the layout and construction of a new equipment room, working with a subcontractor to complete the structure; following construction, we installed a mini-split HVAC system with heat pump to ensure proper climate control in the new space. At the Waterbury WWTF, Jacobs removed and replaced deteriorated instrumentation panels, restoring reliability and integrity to the plant's monitoring and control systems, highlighted in before and after images (**Exhibit 5-14**).

## Commencement Asset Registry Validation

At commencement, we will validate the asset registry across the WWTF, pump stations, flood-control infrastructure, grinder pump system, and collection system; confirm asset hierarchy, GIS linkages, PM libraries, inspections, work history, spare-parts records, and reporting workflows; and implement the agreed-upon CMMS configuration within the required startup period. We will ensure the system supports systematic evaluation of plant performance, well-documented O&M records, [CLIENT] ownership of records, and real-time or on-demand [CLIENT] access. Within the first 90 days, a baseline condition assessment will be completed with **prioritized, data-driven recommendations for renewal, reliability improvement, and capital planning**.

## Planned Maintenance on Critical Assets

Focusing first on critical assets, we'll use reliability-centered maintenance principles to shift [CLIENT]'s wastewater facilities **from reactive work to planned PM and targeted PdM**. Regardless of platform, job plans, manufacturer requirements, inspections, and failure history will be built into the CMMS so it functions as an **operating tool for decision-making**, not just a work order log, as illustrated in **Exhibit 5-15**. Operator rounds will be used to identify issues early, update PM tasks and intervals based on asset performance, and track practical maintenance KPIs such as PM completion, backlog aging, repeat failures, and response time to **support continuous improvement** and monthly review with [CLIENT].
