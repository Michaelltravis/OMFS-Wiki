# CMMS-Driven Asset Management and Maintenance Program

**The client's asset environment.** A wastewater system operating in a demanding coastal setting may include an activated-sludge WWTF, multiple wastewater pump stations, a stormwater pump station, tens of miles of gravity and force main, and specialty assets such as a low-pressure grinder system — all within a flood-prone environment where storms, access constraints, and community sensitivity heighten the importance of reliable assets and disciplined maintenance. Even where existing facilities are generally well maintained, clients are often seeking more than routine maintenance support: a partner that protects assets through cleaner facilities, more visible accountability, stronger self-performance, better use of CMMS data, and closer linkage between daily maintenance and future capital decisions.

**CMMS-driven asset visibility.** A preventive-first asset management program is anchored by a CMMS strategy aligned with the client's adopted systems, workflows, and reporting needs. GIS-enabled platforms (e.g., Cartegraph) offer strong value for linear assets such as collection systems, pump stations, low-pressure sewer infrastructure, and flood-control assets. Plant-focused platforms (e.g., Maintenance Connection) are often more effective for vertical/WWTF equipment because they support disciplined work-order management, preventive maintenance planning, asset history, and KPI reporting. Rather than forcing a one-size-fits-all solution before transition, the most effective CMMS approach is confirmed for all asset classes — whether a single primary platform, a complementary plant-focused workflow, or integrated value-add systems — so the client receives one clear, reliable view of asset condition, maintenance status, and performance.

**Maintenance workflow.** Maintenance is managed through a simple, repeatable four-stage workflow: **Plan** (schedule and bundle work) → **Prepare** (stage parts, tools, and safety requirements) → **Execute** (self-perform routine and most corrective work) → **Close Out** (update asset history and keep backlog current).

**Self-performance with specialist depth.** Routine maintenance and most corrective work is self-performed, supported by regional specialists. Subcontractors are reserved for specialty electrical work, OEM/warranty work, heavy rigging, certified repairs, or other discrete services requiring specialized equipment or credentials. This approach improves responsiveness, reduces handoffs, and reduces reliance on subcontractors for routine work. Specialty collection-system CCTV and sewer-cleaning services may still be delivered through qualified vendors where that model is more economical, while the O&M provider retains planning, oversight, QA/QC, and single-point accountability.

**Housekeeping as a maintenance discipline.** Housekeeping is treated as part of the maintenance program, not work completed only when time allows. Cleanliness, organization, labeling, and minor site upkeep are embedded into rounds, check sheets, and CMMS tasks — resulting in safer facilities, better work preparation, and stronger pride of ownership.

**Whole-system asset planning.** One integrated asset strategy is applied across the WWTF, pump stations, standby power systems, flood-control assets, and any specialty asset classes (e.g., low-pressure grinder systems). CMMS history, observed condition, repeat failures, and obsolescence risk are used to develop practical, decision-ready capital recommendations and to standardize future upgrades. Distinct asset classes (such as grinder pumps) are managed using work-order history and failure data to improve repair planning, replacement forecasting, and lifecycle decision-making.

**Planned maintenance on critical assets.** Focusing first on critical assets, reliability-centered maintenance principles shift facilities from reactive work to planned PM and targeted predictive maintenance (PdM). Job plans, manufacturer requirements, inspections, and failure history are built into the CMMS so it functions as an operating tool for decision-making, not just a work-order log. Operator rounds identify issues early, update PM tasks/intervals based on asset performance, and track practical maintenance KPIs (PM completion, backlog aging, repeat failures, response time) to support continuous improvement and monthly client review.

**Regional Maintenance Team support.** Behind the on-site crew, a nationwide network of regional maintenance teams provides direct, in-house support across HVAC, electrical, SCADA, mechanical systems, equipment installation, and structural/facility construction — from routine maintenance through complex facility upgrades — extending asset life, improving reliability, and minimizing downtime. This capability supplements the on-site team for significant maintenance projects rather than requiring every specialty trade to be staffed permanently on site. For example, at a WWTF in the Upper Midwest, a regional team managed the layout and construction of a new equipment room (working with a subcontractor on the structure) and installed a mini-split HVAC system with heat pump to provide proper climate control in the new space; at a Northeast-region WWTF, a regional team removed and replaced deteriorated instrumentation panels to restore reliability and integrity to the plant's monitoring and control systems.

**Integrating SOPs, training, and storm readiness into maintenance practices.** SOPs and O&M procedures are kept current, reinforced through supervisory review and shift turnover, and supported by structured skills assessments and cross-training to strengthen long-term workforce capability. For facilities in storm-exposed or coastal environments, early priority is also given to standby power validation, flood-control readiness, alarm verification, and contingency procedures so that maintenance planning directly supports system resilience and equipment reliability rather than treating storm readiness as a separate workstream.

**M&R fund governance.** Proactive maintenance planning is paired with transparent cost control. A tiered approval structure typically governs the maintenance & repair fund: minor corrective work orders below an agreed low-dollar threshold may be charged to the fund without client pre-approval; larger corrective work orders at or above that threshold require client pre-approval; on-site labor and consumables are generally not charged to the M&R fund; and specialty subcontracted labor requires client pre-approval where applicable. This gives the client clear visibility into expenditures, balances, and decision points.

**Commencement activities.** At commencement, the asset registry is validated across the WWTF, pump stations, flood-control infrastructure, grinder pump system, and collection system; asset hierarchy, GIS linkages, PM libraries, inspections, work history, spare-parts records, and reporting workflows are confirmed; and the agreed-upon CMMS configuration is implemented within the required startup period. Within the first 90 days, a baseline condition assessment is completed with prioritized, data-driven recommendations for renewal, reliability improvement, and capital planning.

**Technical expertise for capital planning.** Asset condition, criticality, lifecycle cost, and operational risk data (from the initial assessment and ongoing maintenance) are turned into a practical, risk-ranked capital plan, supported by integrated O&M, engineering, process, asset management, and digital resources — helping prioritize renewal, resiliency, and strategic investments with defensible recommendations on scope, timing, sequencing, and funding needs.

**Support for construction projects and facility upgrades.** Structured O&M input is provided from project development through design, construction, startup, commissioning, and warranty for any client-desired construction projects or facility upgrades, including constructability reviews, outage/bypass planning, field support, testing, turnover, and asset-record updates — ensuring new or modified assets are fully integrated into the asset management program and ready for reliable operation on day one.
