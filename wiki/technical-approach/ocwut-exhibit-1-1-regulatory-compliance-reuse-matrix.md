### Regulatory Compliance and Permit Protection

| Understanding of [CLIENT]'s Goals | Key Current/Future Challenges | Jacobs' Strategies to Deliver Results |
|---|---|---|
| **100% NPDES compliance across four WWTPs (combined capacity >110 MGD)** | **North Canadian (NC):** Fecal coliform excursions during storms; aeration performance and solids accumulation contribute to ammonia/total suspended solids (TSS) challenges.<br>**Deer Creek (DC):** Aeration capacity (only two of the four blowers can run simultaneously) constrains ammonia/TSS performance, with the plant approaching its rated design capacity as reflected in the planned CIP expansion.<br>**NC and DC:** Both failed routine and repeat WET tests in Q4 2025 — risk of state-initiated toxicity reduction evaluation (TRE).<br>**System-wide:** Poor process control is a recurring theme; satellite labs (DC, Chisholm Creek (CC), South Canadian (SC)) are currently scoped for field compliance parameters, and many process analyzers are offline.<br>**Disinfection transitions underway:** Chlorine gas to on-site sodium hypochlorite at NC (2028), DC (2029), and SC (new plant). | **Real-time compliance trend monitoring** with structured escalation protocols before excursions become violations.<br>**Proactive process control adjustments tied to influent variability** at each facility.<br>**Strengthen satellite lab capabilities and restore/add online analyzers** (dissolved oxygen [DO], TSS, ammonia) to support data-driven process decisions.<br>**Leverage proven sodium hypochlorite O&M experience** during CIP disinfection transitions.<br>**Coordinate with [CLIENT] on WET test status; implement toxicity source-tracking** if TRE is initiated.<br>**Operator and lab technician training** on compliance protocols, process control, and data quality — with coaching from Jacobs' offsite process and laboratory specialists. |
