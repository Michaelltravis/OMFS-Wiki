# Collection System O&M Program

## [CLIENT]'s Collection System and Its Challenges

[CLIENT]'s collection system — a 42-mile coastal New England municipal system serving roughly 10,000 residents — presents distinct coastal operating challenges, including saltwater intrusion, remaining I/I, long force mains, hydrogen sulfide generation, odor concerns, FOG and solids accumulation, and recurring wet well maintenance. These conditions place added stress on pump stations, force mains, and gravity sewers. The system also includes a low-pressure network with [CLIENT]-maintained grinder pumps that requires responsive service and structured asset tracking.

Our **proactive, data-driven collections program** addresses these conditions through preventive maintenance, targeted cleaning and CCTV, disciplined pump station and wet well management, and structured use of alarm, complaint, inspection, and work-order data. This approach supports earlier identification of issues, improved hotspot definition, strengthened I/I tracking and investigation, optimized odor and septicity control, and clearer prioritization for rehabilitation and capital improvement planning (CIP). Our approach also improves the differentiation between saltwater intrusion and rainfall-driven I/I, allowing corrective actions to **target root causes**. Collection system odor-control considerations are addressed in the Odor Control subsection later in this approach.

## Collection System Operations

Our collection system approach incorporates data, field execution, and clear accountability to stay ahead of recurring issues while improving **system visibility and decision-making**. We will continuously adjust priorities to gain better visibility into system condition, clearer identification of problem areas, and stronger support for rehabilitation and CIP decision-making. Key elements include:

- Responding to sewer overflows within 1 hour or sooner.
- Annual cleaning and CCTV of 20% of the system annually for the first 5 years, then 10% annually thereafter, supplemented by the sewer line rapid assessment tool (SL-RAT) for hotspot identification. Additional attention will be given to recurring problem areas, wet-weather hotspots, and locations associated with odor, grease, or hydraulic restriction concerns.
- Monitoring, controlling, and evaluating [CLIENT]'s use of bioxide, In-Pipe Technology, and Wet Well Wizard-style optimization tools as part of our collections routing and pump station operating strategy, using wet well conditions, odor observations, run-time trends, levels, alarms, and cleaning history to adjust treatment, cleaning frequency, and field priorities based on actual conditions.
- Updating the annual cleaning and inspection plan based on work order history, alarms, CCTV findings, wet-weather performance, and known system priorities.
- Systematically tracking and investigating I/I by correlating field observations, flow patterns, inspection results, and recurring maintenance history.
- Monitoring lift stations and associated force mains for conditions that contribute to septicity, odor generation, corrosion, and premature asset deterioration.
- Maintaining complete, defensible records and reporting in accordance with state regulatory requirements.
- Developing, maintaining, and training on pump station bypass SOPs and a High Flow Management Plan tailored to the facility's hydraulic and wet-weather conditions.

## Support for [CLIENT]'s I/I Reduction Efforts

We will support I/I reduction through a **focused, data-driven program** that protects wet weather performance, reduces peak flows, and supports **long-term collection system reliability**. An I/I baseline will be established from existing system information (including prior studies and historical operating trends), using wet weather indicators, pump station performance, and maintenance history to target the highest risk sewer sheds first.

Using SmartCover® manhole mounted units in the collection system will help identify and reduce I/I in the system. Traditional I/I isolation methodologies require 18 to 24 months of field assessment and analysis, whereas SmartCover® can produce results **up to 12 months sooner**. Selected manholes using SmartCover® units during dry and wet weather periods will be installed, and will be relocated to different parts of the collection system when needed. Additional information on SmartCover® technology is provided in **Appendix E** for [CLIENT]'s review.

Features of the system include: satellite communication provides constant visibility; no confined space entry reduces resources and safety hazards; real-time alarms and notifications provide advanced notice; and a mobile app delivers system insights wherever and whenever.

Our approach **aligns directly with the required collection system work** by integrating I/I investigations into the annual cleaning and CCTV program and the required I/I tracking investigations deliverable. CCTV findings and field observations will be documented, defects will be ranked by severity and I/I potential, and results will be managed through the CMMS/data tools, so progress is **measurable and transparent**. Where inflow sources are identified, Jacobs will also support practical control measures consistent with collection system permit expectations (e.g., identifying and addressing illegal storm connections where applicable).

We will identify areas potentially influenced by saltwater intrusion and distinguish those impacts from groundwater inflow and direct inflow. We will use tools such as SmartCover®, WATS modeling, CCTV findings, field observations, maintenance history, and available wet-weather and tide-influenced flow patterns to **prioritize targeted inspections and corrective actions** including manhole sealing, joint repair, lining, or other rehabilitation where warranted.

## Sewer Pump Station Operations

Our operating approach uses alarm history, pump run-time and level trends, field observations, and cleaning records to identify **early warning signs** such as uneven runtimes, excessive cycling, grit or grease buildup, abnormal wet well conditions, odor generation, and the development of force main or septicity issues. Because [CLIENT]'s pump stations and force mains are central to system performance, we will, where conditions warrant, increase wet well cleaning frequency, adjust maintenance intervals, and **target corrective actions before they become service disruptions**. Available remote monitoring and SCADA information will be used to strengthen response, documentation, and operational decision-making across the pump station network. This same data evaluates current wet well cleaning practices and applies Wet Well Wizard-style optimization, confirming that cleaning frequency, odor and solids control, bypass planning, and maintenance costs align with actual field conditions rather than fixed intervals alone.

| Frequency | Activities |
|---|---|
| **Daily** | Respond to all pump station alarms. |
| **Weekly** (Monday and Friday, or more often as conditions dictate) | Inspect each station and wet well for grease, grit, floatables, corrosion, odor, high-water conditions, and other abnormal operating conditions; confirm security, inspect lights and alarms, record pump hours, review run-time balance/cycling, and create work orders for deficiencies; keep station areas clean, rotate pumps, and exercise generators. |
| **Monthly** | Review alarm history, run times, and station trends to identify emerging maintenance or reliability concerns; operate pumps manually to verify performance, inspect floats and valves, test backup generators, record electrical data where available, and document deficiencies. |
| **Quarterly/Annual** | Remove excess grit, grease, and trash from wet wells and related structures; inspect pumps, floats, level sensors, check valves, and electrical components; load-test generators; and review station condition and maintenance history to identify repairs, rehabilitation needs, or operating changes that will improve reliability and reduce recurring maintenance demand. |

Logbooks document conditions at each station, and predictive, preventive, and emergency maintenance activities will be scheduled accordingly. Wet wells will be periodically pumped down and cleaned with a vacuum truck to remove bottom debris and floatables, and walls will be washed down.

## CCTV and Sewer Line Cleaning

Cleaning and CCTV of 20% of the collection system will occur annually during the first 5 years. An annual summary will also be provided, as well as a 5-year work plan. After 5 years, we will continue cleaning/CCTV on a 10-year cycle, or more frequently, as conditions warrant. These requirements will be met through a **planned work-order-driven program** that uses blockage history, customer complaints, wet-weather observations, alarm trends, pump station findings, and CCTV results to continuously refine priorities. This program will focus on lines and structures associated with recurring maintenance demand, force main discharge impacts, odor and FOG concerns, and suspected I/I contribution areas. We will use NASSCO-based inspection results, cleaning records, and field observations to document recurring defects, **identify developing problems early, and support rehabilitation and CIP planning**.

We reviewed [CLIENT]'s existing third-party resiliency report (a Woodard & Curran study), including the evaluation of acquiring a jetter and CCTV capable vehicle, and agree that a subcontracting approach is the most economical, including cleaning 45,000 feet of collection system. To supplement this approach, Jacobs intends to lease a jetter to provide a **dedicated on-site resource for urgent or hot spot needs**, improving responsiveness and helping offset subcontracting costs.

Specialty services will be delivered through a **competitively priced vendor model with direct Jacobs oversight**, a delivery approach we commonly use on other collection system O&M projects. Based on pricing received from qualified CCTV and cleaning providers, this approach ensures **cost-effective service delivery while maintaining quality and control**. Jacobs retains responsibility for scope definition, schedule management, QA/QC, NASSCO deliverables, and integration of findings into [CLIENT]'s CMMS, annual work planning, and rehabilitation/CIP prioritization.

## Practical, Low-Complexity Innovations

We will also evaluate practical innovations that improve **collection system visibility, response, and cost control** without adding unnecessary complexity. Innovations may include enhanced use of SCADA/alarm trend data, stronger CMMS integration, mobile field documentation, and more flexible cleaning and wet well maintenance tools that allow crews to respond more quickly to emerging issues and recurring hotspots. InfoSense's SL-RAT will be used to assist with these efforts. This tool requires only two operators, minimal setup time, and no confined-space entry. SL-RAT helps the WWTF team **prioritize cleaning**, reduce unnecessary Vactor truck deployments, and **reduce overall maintenance costs**.

## Low-Pressure System/Grinder Pumps

The low-pressure system will be managed through a **structured grinder pump program** focused on responsive service, asset tracking, stocked spares, and proactive replacement planning. With about 175 grinder pumps in service, we will use work-order and failure data to identify repeat issues and support forecasting of renewal needs, **reducing repeat callouts and improving customer service**. Our approach ensures the low-pressure system is managed as a distinct and important part of [CLIENT]'s overall collection system.
