# Collection System O&M Program

A proactive, data-driven collections program addresses coastal operating challenges — saltwater intrusion, remaining inflow/infiltration (I/I), long force mains, hydrogen sulfide generation, odor concerns, fats/oils/grease (FOG) and solids accumulation, and recurring wet well maintenance — through preventive maintenance, targeted cleaning and CCTV, disciplined pump station and wet well management, and structured use of alarm, complaint, inspection, and work-order data.

This approach supports earlier identification of issues, improved hotspot definition, strengthened I/I tracking and investigation, optimized odor and septicity control, and clearer prioritization for rehabilitation and capital improvement planning (CIP). A key differentiator is the ability to distinguish **saltwater intrusion from rainfall-driven I/I**, allowing corrective actions to target root causes rather than applying generic I/I remediation.

## Core Collection System Operations Commitments

- Respond to sewer overflows within 1 hour or sooner.
- Annual cleaning and CCTV inspection of 20% of the collection system for the first 5 years, then 10% annually thereafter, supplemented by a rapid assessment tool (e.g., SL-RAT) for acoustic-based hotspot identification. Additional attention is given to recurring problem areas and wet-weather hotspots or locations associated with odor, grease, or hydraulic restriction concerns.
- Monitor, control, and evaluate the use of odor/corrosion-control tools (e.g., bioxide dosing, in-pipe biological treatment, wet-well cleaning-optimization practices) as part of the collections routing and pump station operating strategy, adjusting treatment, cleaning frequency, and field priorities based on actual conditions.
- Update the annual cleaning and inspection plan based on work order history, alarms, CCTV findings, wet-weather performance, and known system priorities.
- Systematically track and investigate I/I by correlating field observations, flow patterns, inspection results, and recurring maintenance history.
- Maintain complete, defensible records and reporting in accordance with state regulatory requirements.
- Develop, maintain, and train staff on pump-station-bypass SOPs and a High Flow Management Plan tailored to the system's hydraulic and wet-weather conditions.

## I/I Reduction Support

I/I reduction is supported through a focused, data-driven program that protects wet-weather performance, reduces peak flows, and supports long-term collection system reliability. An I/I baseline is established from existing system information (prior studies, historical operating trends), wet-weather indicators, pump station performance, and maintenance history, targeting the highest-risk sewer sheds first.

Manhole-mounted real-time monitoring units (e.g., SmartCover-type technology) help identify and reduce I/I. Traditional I/I isolation methodologies require 18-24 months of field assessment and analysis, whereas continuous manhole-level/flow monitoring can produce results up to 12 months sooner. Selected manholes are monitored during dry and wet weather periods and relocated to different parts of the system as needed. Features of this class of technology typically include:

- Satellite/cellular communication providing constant visibility without confined-space entry.
- Real-time alarms and notifications providing advance notice of surcharge or abnormal conditions.
- Mobile app access to system insights from the field.

I/I investigations are integrated into the annual cleaning/CCTV program and required tracking deliverables; findings are documented, defects ranked by severity and I/I potential, and results managed through CMMS/data tools so progress is measurable and transparent. Where inflow sources are identified, practical control measures are applied consistent with collection system permit expectations (e.g., identifying and addressing illegal storm connections).

## Sewer Pump Station Operations

The operating approach uses alarm history, pump run-time and level trends, field observations, and cleaning records to identify early warning signs — uneven runtimes, excessive cycling, grit or grease buildup, abnormal wet well conditions, odor generation, and developing force main or septicity issues. Because pump stations and force mains are central to system performance, wet well cleaning frequency and maintenance intervals are increased where conditions warrant, targeting corrective action before service disruptions occur. Remote monitoring and SCADA data are used to strengthen response, documentation, and operational decision-making across the pump station network, and to periodically re-evaluate wet well cleaning practices against actual field conditions rather than fixed intervals alone.

### Pump Station Inspection Cadence

| Frequency | Tasks |
|---|---|
| **Daily** | Respond to all pump station alarms. |
| **Weekly** (e.g., twice weekly, more often as conditions dictate) | Inspect each station and wet well for grease, grit, floatables, corrosion, odor, high-water conditions, and other abnormal operating conditions; confirm security, inspect lights and alarms, record pump hours, review run-time balance/cycling, and create work orders for deficiencies; keep station areas clean, rotate pumps, and exercise generators. |
| **Monthly** | Review alarm history, run times, and station trends to identify emerging maintenance or reliability concerns; operate pumps manually to verify performance, inspect floats and valves, test backup generators, record electrical data, and document deficiencies. |
| **Quarterly/Annual** | Remove excess grit, grease, and trash from wet wells and related structures; inspect pumps, floats, level sensors, check valves, and electrical components; load-test generators; review station condition and maintenance history to identify repairs, rehabilitation needs, or operating changes that will improve reliability and reduce recurring maintenance demand. |

Logbooks document conditions at each station, and predictive, preventive, and emergency maintenance activities are scheduled accordingly. Wet wells are periodically pumped down and cleaned with a vacuum truck to remove bottom debris and floatables, and walls are washed down.

## Low-Pressure System/Grinder Pump Program

A low-pressure collection system with customer-owned or utility-maintained grinder pumps is managed through a structured grinder pump program focused on responsive service, asset tracking, and proactive replacement planning. Work-order and failure data are used to identify repeat issues and support forecasting of renewal needs, reducing repeat callouts and improving customer service. This ensures the low-pressure system is managed as a distinct and important part of the overall collection system, sized and resourced according to the actual number of units in service.

## CCTV and Sewer Line Cleaning

Cleaning and CCTV inspection at the committed annual percentage occurs through a planned, work-order-driven program that uses blockage history, customer complaints, wet-weather observations, alarm trends, pump station findings, and CCTV results to continuously refine priorities. The program focuses on lines and structures associated with recurring maintenance demand, force main discharge impacts, and odor/FOG concerns or suspected I/I contribution areas. NASSCO-based inspection results, cleaning records, and field observations are used to document recurring defects, identify developing problems early, and support rehabilitation and CIP planning.

Where a jetter/CCTV vehicle purchase is not economical for the contract scope, a subcontracting or hybrid model can be used — e.g., leasing a jetter for a dedicated on-site resource to handle urgent or hotspot needs while a competitively priced, qualified vendor (under direct contractor oversight for scope, schedule, QA/QC, NASSCO deliverables, and CMMS/CIP integration) delivers the bulk of cleaning/CCTV services cost-effectively. This determination draws on the client's own prior resiliency/capital planning studies (where available) that evaluated jetter and CCTV-vehicle acquisition against the actual footage of the system requiring cleaning (e.g., roughly 45,000 feet in this case) — confirming that a lease/subcontract hybrid is the more economical path than owning a dedicated vehicle for a system of that size. Specialty services are delivered through a competitively priced vendor model with direct oversight, an approach commonly used on other collection-system O&M contracts; pricing is obtained from qualified CCTV/cleaning providers to ensure cost-effective delivery while maintaining quality and control.

## Practical, Low-Complexity Innovations

Practical innovations that improve collection system visibility, response, and cost control are evaluated on an ongoing basis without adding unnecessary complexity. These can include enhanced use of SCADA/alarm trend data, stronger CMMS integration, mobile field documentation, and more flexible cleaning and wet well maintenance tools that let crews respond more quickly to emerging issues and recurring hotspots. A rapid assessment tool such as InfoSense's SL-RAT is well suited to this role: it requires only two operators, minimal setup time, and no confined-space entry, and it helps the O&M team prioritize cleaning, reduce unnecessary vactor-truck deployments, and lower overall maintenance costs.
