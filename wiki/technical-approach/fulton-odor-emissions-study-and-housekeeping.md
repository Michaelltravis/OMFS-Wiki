# Odor Emissions Study and Routine Housekeeping

The JC Solutions team will complete an Odor Emissions Study, at contract start. Jacobs’ odor control experts will perform source sampling and odor dispersion modeling to assess likely sources of odor and performance of existing odor control facilities, and these studies are included in our offer. Samples will be collected from odorous treatment processes and analyzed for odor concentrations and reduced sulfur compounds. The sample results will be used in the odor dispersion model along with site specific hourly atmospheric conditions from the past five years to assess the offsite impact from the odor sources. This is particularly relevant for [FACILITY B], an environmental campus, where the last odor study was performed in 2009 and conditions may have changed, from meteorological conditions to the odor characteristics and loadings to the existing facilities. Modeling will allow us to quickly identify the odor sources with the risk of causing off-site odor complaints and take a proactive approach on odor control.

Our step-by-step process will identify sources of odor including the expected dispersion. Using this information, improvements can be prioritized to have the most significant positive impact on the neighboring community. An example of these improvements include buffer zones where vegetation can further support odor mitigation measures.

Routine housekeeping is an important yet simple way to ensure that odors do not escape from various locations on site. Operators will check the solids processing and the headworks to ensure that doors are kept closed, exhaust systems are operating properly, and that no abnormal amounts of dust or abnormal odors are present. They will check areas within buildings to ensure that H2S levels do not exceed safe levels for operational personnel. Safe levels within buildings for personnel should be low enough to preclude generation of objectionable odors that could extend beyond the facility fence line into the community.

## Reuse guidance

Use the source-sampling, dispersion-modeling, and housekeeping sequence only after confirming site-specific sources, historic meteorology, building conditions, and community receptors.
