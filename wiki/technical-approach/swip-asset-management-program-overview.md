# Asset Management Program Overview — Understanding Client Goals and Value-Added Support Structure

## Understanding the Client's Asset Management Goals and Facility Needs

Jacobs recognizes that [CLIENT] is at the beginning of its asset management (AM) journey and we are committed to supporting the client in building a program that preserves its capital investments while ensuring long-term performance. During our site visit, Jacobs observed that while some elements of asset management are already in place, a full implementation has not yet been completed. We will coordinate closely with the client to ensure full support for the client's Asset Performance Management (APM) program and will implement a robust Asset Management System (AMS) during the transition phase.

The advanced treatment facility is in good condition; however, as a complex underground facility, it requires robust preventive maintenance (PM), predictive maintenance (PdM), and careful calibration programs to sustain current performance and reliability. By contrast, a secondary water-recycling facility that is not currently in active use contains many aging assets. We assume these will not require inclusion in the AMS unless directed by the client, beyond essential pickling and periodic pH monitoring of the membranes to maintain baseline condition.

Jacobs will also integrate the stormwater diversion structures, a pump station, and groundwater injection wells into the AMS. For the pump station—which requires routine vac-truck cleaning services—the AMS will be key to optimizing cleaning frequency in conjunction with utilizing **Jacobs' AquaDNA Deragger** to adjust cleaning frequencies. At the groundwater injection site, Jacobs will apply the same asset management principles to ensure injection well reliability, system integrity, and regulatory compliance.

Our AMS will include a long-term Asset Management Plan developed through a collaborative, data-driven process. This plan will maximize the value of client assets by applying best practices in preventive and predictive maintenance, leveraging condition assessment data, and implementing optimization strategies that extend equipment life and reduce lifecycle costs.

**Above and Beyond — AquaDNA Deragger Value-Add:** Jacobs will work with [CLIENT] during the transition to improve the performance of a lift/pump station, by installing Jacobs' **AquaDNA Deragger** at no additional cost (a $75,000 value). This technology will help ensure continuous operation and help reduce vac-truck cleaning frequency. ($75,000 embedded value.)

## Integrated Asset Management and Maintenance Drives Delivery Excellence

Jacobs' projects are fully embedded into our interconnected internal network of subject matter experts, field professionals, and support teams. No project exists in isolation. This structure enables shared learning, resource efficiency, and exceptional client outcomes. The facilities will benefit from Jacobs' integrated approach to maintenance and asset lifecycle optimization.

**Above and Beyond — Dedicated Regional Maintenance Technician:** In addition to the onsite Maintenance Technician on the project team, Jacobs will assign a separate, dedicated Maintenance Technician based in the region to support both this project and a nearby sister project—ensuring rapid, expert response across all facilities. This shared-resource model provides local familiarity, operational continuity, and cost efficiency without compromising coverage. ($170,000 embedded value.)

## Dedicated Technical Support Structure

Jacobs' operations team will be supported by a national O&M Asset Management and Maintenance Technical Support Team. This team will provide four programs, guided by Jacobs' **Director of Asset Management for O&M, Graham Knowles**, that form the backbone of Jacobs' O&M Asset Management team. Together, they deliver a structured, integrated approach that will not only meet the client's O&M needs but also ensure the organization's asset management maturity continues to evolve toward world-class standards.
