# Proven Energy Optimization Results at Comparable Facilities

This approach has delivered measurable outcomes at other Jacobs-operated facilities:

- At a comparable facility in Pima County, Arizona, demand-responsive aeration control **delivered approximately $300,000 in annual energy savings while maintaining full compliance**.
- At a 3-MGD facility in Carol Stream, Illinois, **36 energy efficiency projects over 5 years reduced electricity costs by $52,000 annually** and **earned $33,000 in utility incentives**, with a secondary benefit of 466 metric tons CO₂-equivalent in avoided greenhouse gas emissions.

At [CLIENT], we'll establish current kWh/MG baselines at each facility, identify the highest-return opportunities, implement improvements systematically, and track results in monthly reporting.

## Additional opportunities beyond the O&M scope

Through our due diligence, process modeling, and site observations, we've identified additional opportunities for energy and chemical optimization that go beyond the day-to-day O&M scope described above. These include data-driven tools and advanced analytics that have delivered measurable results at comparable facilities. Because these opportunities involve approaches, technologies, and investment outside the base O&M scope, they are presented in **Section 8 — Innovative and/or Alternative Recommendations**, where [CLIENT] can evaluate them on their individual merits and potential return on investment.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the two-proof-point structure (one large-plant aeration control result stated in dollars, one small-plant sustained-program result with project count, savings, incentives, and avoided emissions), followed by the four-step commitment sentence — baseline, prioritize, implement, report. The "beyond the O&M scope" paragraph is a reusable device for keeping the technical approach clean while signalling upside; it works in any pursuit that has a separate innovations or alternatives section.

Pursuit-specific: the reference facilities themselves — swap for proof points that match the pursuit's plant size and process — and the cross-reference to Section 8.
