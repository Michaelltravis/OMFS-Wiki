# Settleability and Aeration Optimization Opportunities — Hydrocyclones, Step Feed BNR, and MBAS

*Specific Optimization Opportunities at the Two WRFs.* We'll address two high-priority operational challenges at the two water reclamation facilities that highlight the complexity and importance of optimizing liquid treatment processes in collaboration with [CLIENT] to **ensure reliable performance, regulatory compliance, and cost efficiency**. At the secondary-treatment facility, persistent settleability issues that have resulted in effluent violations call for solutions such as primary clarifier stabilization, selector zone optimization, and enhanced process control. At the biosolids-producing facility, there's opportunity for significant gains by improving aeration efficiency, refining channel mixing, and applying intelligent process control. Together, these approaches underscore our commitment to leveraging advanced technologies and operational expertise to deliver robust, sustainable solutions for [CLIENT].

**Addressing Settleability Issues.** Settleability has been an ongoing challenge for [CLIENT]. Jacobs commends [CLIENT] on upgrading the diffuser system and including an optional unaerated zone to select out filamentous organisms. Jacobs will partner with [CLIENT] to maximize the benefit of these upgrades while pursuing additional targeted improvements, including those presented in **Exhibit IV-17**.

As stated in the diffuser upgrade design, the new selector zone alone cannot guarantee a sufficient reduction in sludge volume index (SVI) to maintain treatment and prevent clarifier overloading. Beyond optimizing existing and planned components, there are opportunities for low-cost improvements and process intensification with short payback periods that we'll discuss with [CLIENT]. **Jacobs brings proven technologies and experience to help unlock these gains, including the following examples:**

- **Hydrocyclones.** Hydrocyclones improve sludge settleability and SVI by selectively wasting lighter flocculant sludge while retaining denser granules and functional organisms (phosphorus accumulating organisms, ammonia-oxidizing bacteria, nitrite-oxidizing bacteria) that stabilize biological processes. This technology is widely adopted, compatible with ongoing aeration upgrades, and requires minimal infrastructure (e.g., upgrade waste activated sludge [WAS] pumping facilities). Jacobs has implemented hydrocyclones globally with strong results.

- **Step Feed Optimization.** Upgrading the existing step feed process to step feed biological nutrient removal (BNR) introduces flow into a downstream anoxic zone, enabling denitrification and reducing settleability impacts. This approach increases selector zone hydraulic retention time, lowers NO₃ loading in RAS, and improves phosphorus removal. **A two-pass step feed configuration could boost capacity by 10–15%.**

- **Mobile Biofilm Activated Sludge (MBAS).** MBAS, developed by Jacobs and Nuvoda, uses mobile plant-based media integrated with MLSS to enhance treatment without major footprint expansion. Unlike fixed-film systems, MBAS media circulates with RAS and is retained and returned to the aeration basins via screening of the WAS stream. At the secondary-treatment facility, MBAS could increase capacity to improve wet-weather handling, increase nitrification, improve settleability and enable biological phosphorus removal. While blower upgrades and aeration control remain necessary, **MBAS offers a cost-effective retrofit with significant performance gains. It could increase treatment capacity by up to 40%.**

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the challenger move — name the unresolved problem, credit the client's existing investment, cite the client's own design documentation for why more is needed, then offer named technologies with quantified upside and honest caveats (blower upgrades still required). The three technologies travel to any conventional activated-sludge pursuit with settleability or capacity pressure. Pursuit-specific: every percentage, the diffuser upgrade reference, and which facility carries which problem.
