# JV Community Concerns, Complaint Response, and Regulatory Engagement

JC Solutions' initial focus will be enhanced housekeeping: cleanliness, routine maintenance, and aesthetic improvements such as groundskeeping. We will prioritize safety and environmental compliance. Recognizing the history of odor and noise complaints, we will act immediately to mitigate these issues, with particular attention to sludge and biosolids handling, roadway impacts, and nuisance odors.

The closest neighborhoods include Inverness, Roswell Pointe Condominiums, and Willeo Creek at Roswell Apartments near [FACILITY A], a water reclamation facility; Foxhall and Willow Tree neighborhoods near [FACILITY C], a water reclamation facility; and Ellard near [FACILITY B], an environmental campus. During the site visit, overall housekeeping at all three wastewater-resource facilities was satisfactory; however, there is an opportunity to improve overall appearance through a more consistent and comprehensive aesthetic approach, which may also help with odor and noise complaints.

Because many neighborhoods have a history with a nearby facility, a standard process for handling complaints about operations, especially odor, is critical to being a good neighbor. Working with operations staff, JC Solutions will develop a complaint-reporting system that gives residents and citizens a direct way to communicate concerns. The system will provide 24/7 odor-complaint notification and response and maintain a log of each call: the contractor employee receiving it; the nature of the complaint; contact information; the summary of communication; complaint date, time, and location; wind speed and direction, temperature, and relative humidity for odor complaints; root cause; and action taken, including root-cause correction. It will include a dedicated phone line, and we will explore an online form, mobile app, and other accessible reporting options.

JC Solutions will receive and acknowledge complaints as required, follow up with a full investigation and action plan, and create a rapid-response team to investigate and address odor complaints as soon as reported. The protocol includes a feedback mechanism so residents feel heard, with follow-up on actions taken, expected duration, and other relevant details. Monthly summaries of public complaints, response actions, and resolutions will go to [CLIENT], and follow-up with each complainant will confirm satisfaction with the resolution. Complaint data and locations may be analyzed to identify patterns and sources, supporting targeted solutions and stronger odor management for ongoing or longer-term issues.

For sludge or biosolids spills affecting roadways or public spaces, emergency-containment procedures will be activated and local authorities notified; spill updates will be communicated through pre-approved public channels. We will minimize noise through sound-dampening technology and designated hours for noisy work. If complaints occur, we will reassess equipment and processes and communicate mitigation steps to affected parties.

JC Solutions will maintain strong relationships with [CLIENT] personnel, Georgia EPD, EPA Region 4, and relevant authorities. Regular coordination meetings will review operational status, compliance, and community concerns. We will participate in public meetings as required, providing updates on facility performance, environmental stewardship, and capital improvements, with professional visual aids, educational materials, and subject-matter experts. As an active Corporate Member of the Georgia Association of Water Professionals, JC Solutions will remain engaged in industry practices and peer collaboration across the state; participation also provides a forum to share community-outreach results. We will use real-time monitoring equipment and regular odor inspections, and a rapid-response team will investigate complaints, apply immediate corrective measures, and maintain communication with the reporting party throughout resolution.

In Jackson, Mississippi, Jacobs operates public drinking-water facilities in a complex stakeholder landscape that includes the EPA, Mississippi Department of Health, third-party engineers, subcontractors, and JXN Water under federal oversight. Jacobs also engages local schools, community groups, and other stakeholders under the direction of a federal judge. Through a collaborative and transparent approach, Jacobs navigates this environment while supporting seamless treatment-system O&M and safe drinking water for the community.
