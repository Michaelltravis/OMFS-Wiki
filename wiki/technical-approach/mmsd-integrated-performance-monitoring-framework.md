# Integrated Performance Management Framework

Jacobs will deliver a comprehensive, data-driven framework for monitoring, tracking, and reporting operational parameters across [CLIENT]'s wastewater system — a regional sewerage district's conveyance, treatment, and biosolids operations. Our approach focuses on transparency, actionable insights, and continuous improvement through validated, real-time data and analytics.

Jacobs' performance monitoring program unifies **process, maintenance, and cost data within a single analytics framework**. The **Intelligent O&M** platform, successfully deployed at systems such as in Jackson, MS, and Wilmington, DE, provides continuous data validation, analytics, and performance visualization through automated workflows and machine learning. This framework supports compliance tracking, performance measurement, and fulfillment of the Annual Settlement requirements in Article 61 of the Service Agreement.

In partnership with [CLIENT], Jacobs will align monitoring with the **Standards of Performance** in Exhibit C and jointly establish KPIs for shared-cost items such as energy, chemicals, and biosolids. Using data from **SCADA, CMMS, Hach WIMS, NEXGEN, and financial systems, Jacobs will generate unified dashboards accessible to both [CLIENT] and Jacobs**. These dashboards provide a single, defensible view of our performance, linking process control, cost management, and compliance.

Jacobs will configure its Intelligent O&M platform to monitor all Exhibit C parameters, including effluent quality, solids handling, and conveyance reliability. Real-time dashboards will display validated data from all source systems, while alarms and KPI exceptions will automatically generate corrective-action logs. Monthly joint reviews will evaluate efficiency, reliability, and compliance trends, and all sampling and laboratory data will maintain full traceability for permit documentation.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the sequence — state the framework, name the platform and where it already runs, commit to jointly set KPIs, then describe what the dashboard actually shows and how exceptions are handled. The load-bearing move is "unified dashboards accessible to both [CLIENT] and Jacobs": one shared view, not a contractor report the client receives after the fact.

Pursuit-specific: the source systems, the named performance-standards exhibit and contract article (here Exhibit C and Article 61 — replace with the pursuit's own citations), and the reference deployments. Where an RFP publishes a parameter list, mirror its language so the evaluator can tick the requirement.
