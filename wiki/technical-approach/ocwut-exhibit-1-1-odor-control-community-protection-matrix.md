# Exhibit 1-1 Matrix — Odor Control and Community Protection

### Odor Control and Community Protection

| Understanding of [CLIENT]'s Goals | Key Current/Future Challenges | Jacobs' Strategies to Deliver Results |
|---|---|---|
| **Minimize odors and meet Odor Control Plan performance metrics: <0.5 mg/L dissolved sulfide at NC influent, <4 ppb H₂S at all fence lines, <10 ppm H₂S at bar screens/belt presses; 12-hour monitoring intervals; >5% exceedance triggers fee-at-risk deductions (COM-3, 2.0% of Fixed Monthly Fee, 90% compliance threshold)** | **NC:** Headworks access is currently limited by H₂S concentrations (readings up to 200 ppm at belt press). Of 30 odor scrubbers, only three operational. BioWay tower offline and unmaintained. Carbon scrubbers reach their effective loading range where influent H₂S exceeds approximately 10 ppm. Grit system performance has been compromised by the access limitation; monthly spills have been tied to cleaning and repair work that can't proceed safely until headworks H₂S levels are addressed. Long-term H₂S exposure has affected concrete condition on the primary clarifiers, which we'll document in the Baseline Condition Assessment for CIP prioritization. The underutilized 2.5-acre compost pad provides uncovered biosolids storage currently, allowing direct odor emissions and limiting drying capacity.<br>**DC:** Seven odor scrubbers, three operational. Belt press room inaccessible during operations. The current waste activated sludge (WAS) and primary sludge co-storage configuration (unmixed/unaerated holding tank) drives septicity before dewatering — a strong candidate for the sludge mixing/aeration improvements in the CIP. The domed primary clarifier configuration concentrates H₂S within the process enclosure. Peroxide dosing (300 mg/L per RFP) is not achieving targets. Facility borders a high-income residential neighborhood. Temporary lime trailers on-site 1–2 years while the silo is rehabbed.<br>**Witcher:** Flow equalization basin (FEB) odor concerns limit basin use; school and residences nearby. No chemical dosing currently in place despite being a significant collection system odor source. H₂S exposure has caused significant corrosion at the influent. Currently uses Super D masking agent only.<br>**CC:** Three carbon scrubbers with media not renewed or replaced.<br>**System-wide:** Chemical dosing for odor control inconsistently applied and poorly automated. Gas monitoring systems unprogrammed or non-functional across facilities. | **Four-layer odor control strategy:**<br>**1) Source control:** Evaluate dissolved sulfide levels arriving at each plant; chemical dosing at Witcher (ferrous chloride or peroxide in wet well) to reduce H₂S before it reaches NC.<br>**2) Equipment restoration:** Assess and rehabilitate the BioWay tower at NC (engage Bio Air or ECS); replace exhausted carbon media with properly sized biological or chemical scrubbers where H₂S exceeds 10 ppm.<br>**3) In-plant process control:** Maintain aerobic conditions in critical process areas, actively manage sludge age and solids inventory to prevent H₂S formation at the source, and minimize detention time in sludge handling across all facilities. At DC specifically, separate WAS/primary sludge storage and optimize peroxide dosing ahead of the belt press. Eliminate masking agents in favor of targeted source treatment.<br>**4) Early-contract odor assessment:** Identify highest-risk sources, develop a prioritized mitigation plan, support [CLIENT]'s CIP planning for long-term odor control infrastructure — including evaluation of covered storage at North Canadian's underutilized 2.5-acre compost pad to reduce biosolids emissions and expand drying capacity.<br>**H₂S safety and odor-source identification training for operators**, with ongoing support from Jacobs' odor control, biosolids, and solids management specialists. |
| **At DC specifically: supply hydrogen peroxide at 300 mg/L in sludge transfer line prior to belt press, targeting 86% H₂S and 93% mercaptan reduction, alongside reduced ferrous chloride feed (per RFP Section 1.4e)** | **Current dosing not achieving targets.**<br>**Aeration capacity at DC** also constrains process stability during peak loading, which is a known driver of the planned CIP improvements.<br>**Sludge mixing and aeration improvements** included in the CIP. | **Optimize peroxide dosing location and rate to maximize contact time** before the belt press, coordinated with ferrous chloride feed adjustments to stabilize belt press room conditions.<br>**Advocate through CIP coordination** for expedited sludge mixing/aeration improvements at DC and share O&M insights that may inform project sequencing.<br>**Detailed DC Odor Control Plan** with dosing protocol, monitoring, and corrective action triggers to be developed. |
