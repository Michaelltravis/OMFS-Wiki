# Landfill Gas Power Generation and Waste Heat Strategy

**Power Generation at the Primary Facility.** The facility's complex energy system relies heavily on landfill gas and natural gas to fuel the Solar and GE turbines that supply both electricity and critical waste heat for drying [CLIENT]'s biosolids product—[CLIENT]'s single largest energy demand. Jacobs' operating strategy for [CLIENT], a regional sewerage district operating two large water reclamation facilities, focuses on maximizing renewable fuel use, heat recovery, and turbine uptime while minimizing demand charges as such:

- **Enhance demand management**, including coordinated load shedding and exploring short-duration aeration adjustments where appropriate and compliant.

- **Maximize the use of available landfill gas**, directing it to the turbines whenever possible, and utilizing any excess in the four dual-fuel dryer burners.

- **Maximize Solar Turbine availability** through targeted PM/PdM, compressor-system reliability improvements, and optimized landfill gas moisture control (including evaluating potential gas conditioning enhancements such as upstream chilling).

- **Use waste heat efficiently**, prioritizing waste heat use first in the dryers, then the waste heat boiler, while minimizing waste heat blown off for pressure control.

- **Operate the GE turbine strategically** to mitigate peak electrical demand, while coordinating with [CLIENT] on future Solar Turbine expansion based on current feasibility studies.

Together, these strategies will **increase renewable heat utilization, reduce purchased power, and improve resilience of the drying process**.

*Solar Turbines: Targeted PdM will increase turbine uptime and renewable energy generation.*
