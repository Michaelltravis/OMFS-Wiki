# Swing Zones and Internal Mixed Liquor Recycle Optimization

[FACILITY A], a wastewater reclamation facility, and [FACILITY B], a wastewater reclamation facility, include swing zones in their bioreactors, meaning these zones can be aerated or mixed. Several considerations affect the decision of whether to aerate a swing zone, and this is best evaluated using process simulation.

When a swing zone is unaerated, it operates as an anoxic zone. Anoxic zones degrade organic matter using nitrate rather than oxygen, and this decreases aeration requirements and energy consumption of the overall process. It also recovers alkalinity, which decreases chemical use. In general, a swing zone should be unaerated when not needed to meet ammonia limits.

However, allowing nitrate to reach zero in an anoxic zone works against EBPR, which is used at all three plants. At [FACILITY B], this can be prevented by providing sufficient internal mixed liquor recycle (IMLR) pumping.

We will conduct an evaluation of the feasibility of moving the existing nitrate zone to whichever location is the end of the anoxic zone. If it is infeasible to move the probe, we will discuss with [CLIENT] the possibility of adding a dedicated probe to the swing zone.

Drawings of [FACILITY C], a wastewater reclamation facility, show a fermentation zone with two compartments followed by an anoxic/swing zone. IMLR is piped to the first fermentation zone, which is not normal design practice for biological nutrient removal. When IMLR is used at [FACILITY C], it delivers nitrate to the first compartment, making all three compartments operate as an anoxic zone. When IMLR is not used, all three compartments operate as a fermentation zone. In addition, there is no aeration capability in any of these compartments, so the term “swing” does not apply.
