# Annual Innovation Workshop Program

An Annual Innovation Workshop, provided at no cost to the client, brings together client staff, stakeholders, and O&M staff, as well as industry-recognized leaders in areas such as process engineering, biosolids, hydraulics, wet-weather flow management, pump station design and operations, digital optimization, and energy management. The workshop reviews the prior year's accomplishments and establishes new goals and innovations to address the client's evolving needs and long-term vision, co-developed with the client into a full-day agenda that reflects the client's priorities and desired outcomes.

This annual meeting is designed to ensure the team continuously looks forward to future needs and trends, helps the client address emerging challenges and technology, and works to eliminate complacency over time.

Representative topics for a first workshop can include:

- **SSES and I/I Assessment** — using CCTV findings, field observations, flow patterns, maintenance history, and manhole-mounted real-time monitoring data to distinguish groundwater inflow, direct inflow, and saltwater intrusion influences and prioritize corrective actions.
- **Standby Power/Energy and Resiliency Roadmap** — evaluating generator upgrade strategies, connection improvements, fuel strategy, energy management, and solar potential.
- **Biosolids Strategy Review** — evaluating thickened versus dewatered solids and disposal pathways to define a resilient, phased strategy that reduces hauling, odor, emissions, and long-term market risk.
- **Regionalization Opportunities** — exploring partnerships to improve system efficiency, capacity, and long-term sustainability.
- **Underused-Asset Reuse Evaluation** — assessing feasible removal, repurposing, or resiliency uses for underused facility space (e.g., a decommissioned incinerator) to accommodate future solids, storage, or backup power needs.
- **AI in Wastewater** — identifying near-term applications and future opportunities to enhance operations and decision-making.
- **PFAS Strategy and Regulatory Outlook** — evaluating emerging requirements and treatment approaches to support compliance planning.

## Executive-summary framing and client testimonial

An executive summary can introduce this program with a partnership-first framing: "We believe successful wastewater operations are built on strong partnerships. Our team will operate as **an extension of [the client]'s staff**, maintaining open communication, transparency, and alignment with [the client]'s priorities while proactively identifying opportunities to strengthen long-term system performance and resilience." The workshop is then introduced as reinforcing that approach — "a structured, collaborative forum that brings together [the client]'s staff and Jacobs SMEs to review system performance, evaluate prior-year accomplishments, and establish targeted goals for the coming year focused on continuous improvement while avoiding complacency over time."

Close with a data-to-planning linkage sentence: "By connecting day-to-day operations with technical expertise and forward-looking planning, we help [the client] move beyond reactive operations toward continuous, measurable improvement. This process ensures alignment with evolving regulatory requirements, improves decision-making, and provides a **clear path from operational challenges to implementable solutions**."

**Client testimonial (proof-of-concept quote — sanitize the attribution before reuse):**

A real, current, and authorized client testimonial like this is one of the strongest credibility devices available for this program — it validates the "not generic training" claim from a client's own voice.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the concept of a no-cost, co-developed annual workshop that feeds the CIP and keeps the contract forward-looking rather than purely reactive. Tailor per pursuit: select 4–6 topics genuinely relevant to the target facility's known issues (pulled from the RFP, site visit, or planning documents) rather than reusing the full list verbatim — evaluators can tell when a topic list wasn't customized. If available and appropriate, reference (without confidential detail) that similar workshops have been conducted for other contract-operations clients as a proof point.
