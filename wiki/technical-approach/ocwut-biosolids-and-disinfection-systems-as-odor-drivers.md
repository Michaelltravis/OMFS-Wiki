# Biosolids and Disinfection Systems as Odor Drivers

[CLIENT] — a multi-plant regional wastewater utility — operates a large and complex biosolids land application program, with approximately 170 permitted sites and more than 13,000 acres. Site observations identified several challenges that directly affect odor, including high moisture content in cake, debris in biosolids, storage limitations, and hydrogen sulfide generation in dewatering areas. [CLIENT]'s requirements explicitly link biosolids management to odor control performance, making consistent solids quality and handling practices critical to systemwide odor control.

We will manage biosolids with a focus on consistency, stability, and odor reduction. This includes improving dewatering performance, reducing storage time, coordinating hauling and land application activities, and evaluating alternatives such as landfill use where conditions or storage constraints require it. We'll manage sensitive sites and coordinate application practices to minimize community impact, especially in areas with higher visibility.

[CLIENT] is transitioning from chlorine gas to OSHG at key facilities. Our team will support this transition by integrating hypochlorite generation systems into daily operations, optimizing dosing strategies, and supporting startup and operator training. By aligning disinfection practices with process control, we'll reduce variability that can contribute to downstream odor conditions and improve the reliability and acceptance of land application sites.
