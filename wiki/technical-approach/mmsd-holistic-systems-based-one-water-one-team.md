# Holistic, Systems-Based Approach

[CLIENT]'s wastewater system functions as a single, highly integrated network in which conveyance, the inline storage system (ISS), and the two water reclamation facilities operate together to achieve permit compliance. [CLIENT] is a regional sewerage district serving a large Midwest metropolitan service area. Jacobs' **One Water, One Team** approach (**Exhibit IV-40**, asset `211_007CAM_2`) reflects this reality by promoting unified operational control, coordinated decision-making, and clear communication across the entire system. By managing the system as one interconnected process, staff can anticipate system interactions, respond proactively to changing conditions, and maintain consistent environmental performance. The exhibit shows the conveyance system and treatment system joined under One Water, One Team through holistic analysis, multidisciplinary decision-making, shared knowledge, collaborative planning, and integrated operations.

To support this approach, Jacobs will integrate operations, maintenance, laboratory functions, and real-time data from SCADA, CMMS, and Hach WIMS into a shared, data-informed management framework. This strategy aligns with [CLIENT]'s Digital Transformation Framework and its long-term objective of improving data accessibility and decision support across the organization. Jacobs recognizes that advancing toward a more unified data environment is best achieved through phased implementation, validation, and collaboration with [CLIENT] staff.

Process, hydraulic, and performance data will be evaluated collectively to provide visibility into system-wide impacts and emerging trends, enabling timely operational adjustments before conditions affect treatment stability or permit limits. Through incremental integration and practical use cases, Jacobs will help [CLIENT] strengthen system understanding, improve coordination across functions, and advance [CLIENT]'s digital objectives while maintaining reliable, compliant operations.

## Cross-facility process optimization

Jacobs will align operating strategies between the two water reclamation facilities to improve process stability, chemical efficiency, and energy use. This includes coordinating treatment strategies, sharing process knowledge, and applying predictive tools that identify developing process issues before they affect effluent quality.

## Knowledge sharing and operational consistency

Operations and maintenance practices will be documented within a shared knowledge framework that captures SOP updates, lessons learned, and systemwide best practices. This promotes consistent operations between facilities and supports staff development and cross-training.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the systems argument — because the assets are hydraulically and operationally coupled, they must be managed under one decision-making structure — plus the three supports that follow it (integrated data, cross-facility alignment, shared knowledge base). The phased-implementation caveat is worth keeping; it disarms the evaluator who has seen digital promises fail.

Pursuit-specific: the asset list in the opening sentence, the theme phrase, and the client's digital strategy document. On a single-facility pursuit, recast "cross-facility" as cross-shift and cross-discipline consistency; the knowledge-framework paragraph still applies unchanged.
