# Asset Management System — ISO 55001 Framework, SAMP, and Governance

A maintenance program without an asset management framework is just a task list. We'll work closely with [CLIENT] — a southcentral US municipal water utility trust operating four wastewater treatment plants and a major pump station — to develop a structured Asset Management System (AMS) aligned to ISO 55001 and documented in a Strategic Asset Management Plan (SAMP) that defines policy, roles, decision rights, resources, and implementation timeframes. **We'll tailor our proven AMS framework, built on the globally recognized model developed by the Institute of Asset Management, to [CLIENT]'s scope and priorities.** Every element will map concrete deliverables, owners, and governance touchpoints, so the program is auditable, durable, and drives measurable results (**Exhibit 1-29**).

Maintenance Manager **Brian Daniels** will lead day-to-day maintenance delivery across all facilities, and Asset Manager **Amy Dembinski, CRL**, will lead development of the AMS and its strategic implementation. **Amy** brings 18 years of CMMS expertise, having **administered more than 100 CMMS databases across Jacobs' portfolio**, and will be supported by our O&M Asset Management Team.

## Governance, Strategy, and Decision Making

We'll establish a maintenance governance charter that guides [CLIENT]'s asset management strategy and drives development of the Asset Management Policy — defining objectives, decision rights, and change control. The SAMP will set measurable targets for reliability, risk, safety, and cost. During mobilization, we'll complete a baseline condition and criticality assessment across all [CLIENT]'s assets and produce a risk-ranked register that drives our Maintenance Plan, informs capital improvement planning, and ties staffing and materials strategy to asset risk and workload. Maintenance decisions are driven by a standardized risk model — likelihood of failure and consequence of failure — so resources are consistently prioritized to the assets that matter most.

For decision making, we'll use risk, criticality, and lifecycle cost to select the right action at the right time: PM, PdM, corrective, refurbishment, or replacement. Decision standards will be embedded into the enterprise asset management platform through job plans, priority rules, and approval thresholds, so choices are consistent and traceable. Where trade-offs exist between cost, risk, and performance, we'll document the rationale and the trigger to revisit. Monthly and quarterly governance forums will review performance, risk, and maintenance outcomes, keeping accountability visible, and the program aligned to [CLIENT]'s objectives.

**Exhibit 1-29.** *Jacobs Will Tailor Its Asset Management Program to [CLIENT]* — "Line of sight" ensures a comprehensive asset management approach from planning to delivery (graphic asset ID 163_009385). The exhibit steps down through four tiers:

- **Organizational Strategic Plan** — ensures clear understanding of the organization's objectives and mission to customers; incorporates any existing strategic plans that relate to the assets (i.e., service goals, sustainability).
- **Asset Management Policy** — articulates senior management commitment to asset management and continual improvement; provides "top down" direction regarding expectations and mandatory requirements for asset management and defines the key principles that underpin asset management at the organization.
- **Strategic Asset Management Plan (SAMP)** — defines what the organization intends to achieve from asset management activities and by when, through SMART asset management objectives; identifies and prioritizes key initiatives that support delivery of the AM Policy; provides a high-level overview of resources and timescales for implementation.
- **Asset Management Plans (AMPs)** — provide the approach to managing the assets over the short, medium, and long term; outline the long-term plan for the assets including service expectations, timelines and funding, and resource requirements.
