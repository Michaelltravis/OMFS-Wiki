# Energy Management Program for Wastewater O&M

Energy is managed as a **core operating discipline** embedded in daily decision-making, continuously optimized, and fully transparent — not a periodic initiative. The approach establishes a **baseline-driven energy management program** aligned with the client's stated performance standard (e.g., a target kWh/MG figure). Using historical data, SCADA, and operational trends, a clear, defensible baseline is developed that reflects actual facility conditions. From that foundation, performance is tracked in real time, data is converted into actionable insights, and opportunities are identified to reduce energy intensity while maintaining process stability, compliance, and resiliency.

**Optimizing high-impact energy uses.** Focus is placed on the largest controllable energy drivers — aeration, pumping, and solids handling — where operational discipline has the greatest impact. Proven practices include optimized setpoints, equipment run-time strategies, proactive maintenance, and operator-friendly performance tracking, with transparent reporting and measurable results (particularly important where a shared-savings/gainshare structure applies to both savings and excess use).

**Collaboration on opportunities for phased energy optimization.** Beyond daily optimization, focus areas for improvement typically include:
- **Pumping system optimization** — ensuring pumps operate within preferred efficiency ranges and reducing unnecessary energy use across influent, effluent, and return flows
- **Solids retention time (SRT) and process optimization** — minimizing excess biomass and associated aeration demand
- **Aeration system optimization** — including air delivery, distribution, and control strategies to match process needs without over-aeration
- **Primary treatment optimization** — maximizing removal of BOD and TSS at the most energy-efficient process step to reduce downstream energy demand
- **Plant water use optimization** — reducing unnecessary internal recycling that increases pumping and treatment loads

Following baseline development, no-cost and low-cost operational improvements are prioritized first, followed by evaluation of utility incentives, grants, and cost-effective capital investments where justified.

**Proven success with energy (and chemical) optimization.** Intelligent O&M tools and data-driven optimization capabilities can identify and sustain savings. For example, at a wastewater facility in the Southwest US, aeration blower performance was optimized using real-time data and dissolved-oxygen control strategies, achieving a 20% reduction in energy use and approximately $300,000 in annual savings while maintaining full compliance. Similarly, at a large WWTP in the Mid-Atlantic region, a predictive modeling approach reduced chemical usage by more than 20%, demonstrating how data-driven optimization can deliver measurable savings without compromising performance.
