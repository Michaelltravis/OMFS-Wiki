# Energy Management Program for Wastewater O&M

Jacobs manages energy as a **core operating discipline** embedded in daily decision-making, continuously optimized, and fully transparent. [CLIENT], a coastal New England municipality, expects its partner to actively manage power utilization, provide clear monthly reporting, and continuously identify opportunities to **improve efficiency and reduce costs**.

We will establish a **baseline-driven energy management program** aligned with [CLIENT]'s performance standard of 2,500 kWh/MG. Using historical data, SCADA, and operational trends, we will develop a clear, defensible baseline that reflects actual facility conditions. From that foundation, performance will be tracked in real time, data will be converted into actionable insights, and opportunities will be identified to **reduce energy intensity while maintaining process stability, compliance, and resiliency**.

## Optimizing High-Impact Energy Uses

Our approach focuses on the largest controllable energy drivers, including aeration, pumping, and solids handling, where operational discipline has the greatest impact. Proven practices include optimized setpoints, equipment run-time strategies, proactive maintenance, and operator-friendly performance tracking. Our approach provides [CLIENT] with **transparent reporting, measurable results, and confidence in shared savings** under the 50/50 gainshare structure for both energy savings and excess use.

## Collaboration on Opportunities for Phased Energy Optimization

Beyond daily optimization, we will collaborate with [CLIENT] to evaluate additional opportunities for improved efficiency. Based on our experience at similar facilities, focus areas will include:

- **Pumping system optimization,** ensuring pumps operate within preferred efficiency ranges and reducing unnecessary energy use across influent, effluent, and return flows
- **Solids retention time (SRT) and process optimization,** minimizing excess biomass and associated aeration demand
- **Aeration system optimization,** including air delivery, distribution, and control strategies to match process needs without over-aeration
- **Primary treatment optimization,** maximizing removal of BOD and TSS in the most energy-efficient process step to reduce downstream energy demand
- **Plant water use optimization,** reducing unnecessary internal recycling that increases pumping and treatment loads

These strategies represent proven best practices Jacobs has successfully implemented at comparable facilities. Following baseline development, we will prioritize **no-cost and low-cost operational improvements first**, followed by evaluation of utility incentives, grants, and cost-effective capital investments where justified.

## Proven Success with Significant Energy (and Chemical) Optimization

We will explore the value of leveraging our Intelligent O&M tools and data-driven optimization capabilities to identify and sustain savings. These tools have proven highly successful at a range of facilities. For example, at a wastewater facility in Arizona, Jacobs optimized aeration blower performance using real-time data and DO control strategies, achieving a **20% reduction in energy use and approximately $300,000 in annual savings** while maintaining full compliance. Similarly, at a large WWTP in the Mid-Atlantic region, our predictive modeling approach reduced chemical usage by more than 20%, demonstrating how data-driven optimization can deliver measurable cost savings without compromising performance.

By combining disciplined operations, advanced analytics, and a collaborative improvement process, we provide [CLIENT] with a **transparent, accountable, and continuously improving energy management program** that reduces lifecycle costs, supports sustainability goals, and delivers shared financial benefits.
