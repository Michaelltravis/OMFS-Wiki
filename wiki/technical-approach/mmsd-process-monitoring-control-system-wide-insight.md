# Process Monitoring and Control for System-Wide Insight

[CLIENT], a regional sewerage district, has one of the nation's most complex wastewater systems, including a 4,800-mile collection network, numerous pump stations, interplant sludge pumping, an inline storage system (ISS) for wet weather flow, and two water reclamation facilities, one with significant biosolids fertilizer production. These assets function as a single, interconnected system, and operational decisions in one area directly influence performance, reliability, and compliance elsewhere. Achieving operational excellence requires system-wide insight into how each element interacts with another. To translate this into actionable strategies and to enable operators to make informed and timely decisions, our approach focuses on the following:

- **Integrated Cross-Facility Monitoring and Best-Practice Control:** We'll combine SCADA data, online sensors, laboratory results, process models, and operator observations into a single unified view that enables early detection of upsets, rapid troubleshooting, and precise control of aeration, clarifiers, chemical dosing, sludge handling, and solids processing. Because the two WRFs are operationally linked through the ISS, solids transfers, and system hydraulics, we'll emphasize coordinated decision-making across facilities. Operators at both WRFs will work from shared dashboards, common data sets, and aligned control strategies to protect compliance while optimizing energy use, hydraulics management, and solids management.

- **Predictive Operating Insight:** To anticipate operating conditions before they develop, we pair operator knowledge with advanced digital tools—such as Intelligent O&M analytics, process models, and a digital twin—allowing staff to prepare for changes in loading, tunnel discharges, equipment stress, or solids characteristics and maintaining stability across the system. Details on these digital tools are included in *Section 2.3*.

- **Optimization of Energy, Chemicals, Solids, and Process Stability:** Adjustments to aeration strategies, chemical addition, wasting rates, solids loading, and dryer feed management have direct impacts on energy consumption, biosolids quality, wastewater treatment, odor, and overall system resilience. Jacobs' O&M and technical teams will continuously evaluate and optimize these areas while preserving stable, compliant operations.

We've identified **key operational focus areas in Exhibit IV-14** that offer the biggest opportunities to improve performance across [CLIENT]'s system. Each requires specialized expertise and proven digital tools for success. We introduce each key focus area in this exhibit, then describe our approach for each in more detail.

Backed by decades of wastewater O&M experience, our approach will deliver practical operating strategies grounded in data and reinforced by disciplined monitoring and control across all facilities. During the pre-term period and throughout the operational term, we'll combine our team's expertise with the knowledge of the local staff and [CLIENT] personnel to review, refine, and strengthen these strategies. **Exhibit IV-15** summarizes our core practices and provides representative control strategies for [CLIENT]'s key operational focus areas. Our operators will also use Jacobs' digital tools to make day-to-day decisions easier in a system that is interconnected and constantly changing.

> When teams and people have been in the same roles for a long time, practices naturally evolve to meet changing conditions, but that evolution isn't always intentional. Periodically leveling up by revisiting the basics and the original design intent helps determine whether a reset is needed. This approach embodies our inclusive OneWater culture and our willingness to challenge the accepted.
> — Kevin Dahl, Project Manager
