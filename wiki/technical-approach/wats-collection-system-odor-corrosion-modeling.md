# WATS Collection-System Odor and Corrosion Modeling Approach

## Wastewater Aerobic/Anaerobic Transformations in Sewers (WATS) Modeling

Jacobs will apply the Wastewater Aerobic/Anaerobic Transformations in Sewers (WATS) model to support a data-driven understanding of odor and corrosion conditions across a select portion of [CLIENT]'s collection system. [CLIENT] is a coastal New England municipality operating a 3.07-MGD wastewater treatment facility and a 42-mile collection system. WATS is the industry's leading sewer process model, developed through collaboration between Jacobs and global experts at Aalborg University and continuously refined through application on complex collection systems worldwide.

Unlike traditional approaches that rely heavily on limited field sampling and extended pilot testing, WATS simulates the physical, chemical, and biological processes that drive sulfide generation, odor release, and corrosion within the collection system. The model evaluates wastewater characteristics, detention time, turbulence, air-liquid mass transfer, and ventilation dynamics to predict hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) concentrations and corrosion rates throughout the system.

Jacobs will develop and calibrate a WATS model tailored to a portion of [CLIENT]'s collection system using targeted field data. Once calibrated, the model will provide a representation of odor generation and transport, enabling rapid evaluation of mitigation strategies under varying operating conditions. If the results from the WATS modeling are beneficial, we can discuss implementing it throughout the entire collection system.

Through this approach, Jacobs will:

- Identify odor and corrosion hotspots, including areas not readily apparent through field observations
- Optimize and refine sampling locations, potentially reducing the number of required sites
- Evaluate liquid- and vapor-phase treatment alternatives across the system
- Determine optimal chemical dosing locations and rates
- Support sizing and placement of odor control infrastructure

WATS enables Jacobs to simulate multiple "what-if" scenarios in hours rather than the weeks or months required for traditional pilot programs, significantly improving decision-making speed and confidence.

Jacobs has applied this approach on large, complex systems to support planning and design decisions, including work for utilities, where WATS modeling informed identification of priority odor and corrosion zones and evaluation of system-wide mitigation strategies.

By integrating WATS modeling into [CLIENT]'s program, Jacobs will provide a defensible, science-based foundation for decision-making — reducing uncertainty, minimizing unnecessary pilot testing, and delivering cost-effective solutions that protect assets and improve community outcomes.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the description of what WATS models (sulfide generation, odor release, corrosion via wastewater characteristics, detention time, turbulence, air-liquid mass transfer, ventilation dynamics), the Aalborg University collaboration credential, the five outcome bullets, and the "hours rather than weeks or months" speed differentiator are standard Jacobs capability statements that apply to any collection-system O&M or planning/design pursuit with odor or corrosion concerns.

Pursuit-specific: replace `[CLIENT]` and the facility descriptor, and calibrate the described scope ("a select portion of the collection system") to the actual pilot area under discussion. The closing "if the results are beneficial, we can discuss implementing it throughout the entire collection system" line is a low-risk scaling offer — keep it when the pilot area is small. Verify current Jacobs case studies and utility references before citing "Jacobs has applied this approach on large, complex systems" — swap in a named, approved reference project where one is available.
