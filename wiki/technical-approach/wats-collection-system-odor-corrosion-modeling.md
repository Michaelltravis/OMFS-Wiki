# WATS Collection-System Odor and Corrosion Modeling Approach

Jacobs applies the Wastewater Aerobic/Anaerobic Transformations in Sewers (WATS) model to support a data-driven understanding of odor and corrosion conditions across a select portion of [CLIENT]'s collection system. WATS is an industry-leading sewer process model, developed through collaboration between Jacobs and global experts at Aalborg University and continuously refined through application on complex collection systems worldwide.

Unlike traditional approaches that rely heavily on limited field sampling and extended pilot testing, WATS simulates the physical, chemical, and biological processes that drive sulfide generation, odor release, and corrosion within the collection system. The model evaluates wastewater characteristics, detention time, turbulence, air-liquid mass transfer, and ventilation dynamics to predict hydrogen sulfide (H2S) concentrations and corrosion rates throughout the system.

Jacobs develops and calibrates a WATS model tailored to a defined portion of the client's collection system using targeted field data (e.g., continuous H2S monitoring at representative manholes/structures). Once calibrated, the model provides a representation of odor generation and transport, enabling rapid evaluation of mitigation strategies under varying operating conditions. Where results are beneficial, the approach can be scaled to the full collection system.

Through this approach, Jacobs will:

- Identify odor and corrosion hotspots, including areas not readily apparent through field observations
- Optimize and refine sampling locations, potentially reducing the number of required monitoring sites
- Evaluate liquid- and vapor-phase treatment alternatives across the system
- Determine optimal chemical dosing locations and rates
- Support sizing and placement of odor control infrastructure

WATS enables the simulation of multiple "what-if" scenarios in hours rather than the weeks or months required for traditional pilot programs, significantly improving decision-making speed and confidence. Jacobs has applied this approach on large, complex systems to support planning and design decisions, including work for utilities where WATS modeling informed identification of priority odor and corrosion zones and evaluation of system-wide mitigation strategies.

By integrating WATS modeling into the O&M program, Jacobs provides a defensible, science-based foundation for decision-making — reducing uncertainty, minimizing unnecessary pilot testing, and delivering cost-effective solutions that protect assets and improve community outcomes.
