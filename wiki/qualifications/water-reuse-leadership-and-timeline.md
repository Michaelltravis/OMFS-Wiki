# Water Reuse Industry Leadership — 60+ Year Innovation Timeline

## Unrivaled industry expertise in advanced treatment and reuse

Jacobs has a long history of innovation and advancement in water reuse (see Exhibit 2-4). Since the 1960s, we've partnered with clients to develop cutting-edge recycled water solutions. We're passionate about recycled water and making the most of this vital resource. We combine global best practices and knowledge of client objectives to develop tailored recycled water solutions.

Standing at the forefront of advanced water treatment innovation, we bring [CLIENT], a coastal Southern California municipal water utility, unparalleled access to not only our O&M professionals, but the industry's most comprehensive team of water technology experts. Our distinguished bench of specialists includes pioneering minds in:

- Next-generation water reuse solutions
- Cutting-edge advanced oxidation processes
- RO/MF/UF membrane applications and optimization
- Emerging water treatment technologies and processes

As leaders in the recycled water space, not only can we successfully conduct O&M services for recycled water facilities, but we also bring deep technical insight from our global SMEs. Our staff of experts in a wide variety of fields enables us to bring our clients the capacity to permit, design, research, install, manage, and monitor municipal reuse projects.

## Jacobs is recognized on the global stage for our leadership in potable water reuse

Our company is particularly proud of receiving the 2015 Stockholm Industry Water Award. CH2M, now Jacobs, was selected to receive this prestigious and internationally acclaimed award in recognition of our more than 50 years of leadership in advancing state-of-the-art methodologies and technologies to clean water for potable reuse, as well as increasing public acceptance of recycled water. This recognition is a huge accomplishment for our company, as we are the first and only consulting engineering firm to win this award. (Callout graphic `131_008A26`.)

**Exhibit 2-4. Jacobs' Long History of Leadership in Water Reuse** (graphic `144_008A26`)

| Decade | Project | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1960s | South Lake Tahoe Advanced Water Treatment | Becoming better stewards of fresh water bodies by designing and operating the first full-scale advanced wastewater treatment facility. |
| 1970s | Upper Occoquan Regional Water Reclamation | Cost-effectively reclaiming drinking water by creating new solutions that lead to the world's first and largest indirect potable reuse plant. |
| 1980s | Denver Water Potable Reuse Demonstration | Addressing concerns about public health, safety and reliability by designing projects that were milestones in the advancement of water purification technology. |
| 1990s | Reedy Creek Water Reuse and McAllen Refuge Study | Making potable reuse affordable for wide-spread adoption with technology that was first in the world to use microfiltration coupled with reverse osmosis for advanced water reuse. |
| 2000s | Singapore NEWater factories and Education Center | Becoming better stewards of fresh water bodies by designing and operating the first full-scale advanced wastewater treatment facility (international). |
| 2000s | Denver Water Recycling Plant | Using biological aerated filtration (BAF) for ammonia removal on reclaimed water and capturing interest from technologists around the world. |
| 2000s | South Lake Tahoe Advanced Water Treatment (Australia) | Advancing potable reuse on both the East and West coasts of Australia to reinforce water supplies against drought. |
| 2000s | Oxnard Advanced Water Purification Facility | Increasing reliability, reducing the cost, improving dependability, and enhancing stewardship of local water supply through recycling and reusing wastewater. |
| 2010s | HRSD's Sustainable Water Initiative for Tomorrow (SWIFT) | Recharging the Potomac Aquifer System in Southeast Virginia replenishes this major aquifer while simultaneously reducing nutrient discharge to the sensitive Chesapeake Bay. |
| 2020s | Las Virgenes Municipal District Pure Water Program | Building an advanced water purification facility to treat tertiary recycled water for indirect potable reuse through reservoir water augmentation. |
| 2020s | Inland Empire Utilities Agency Chino Basin Program | Enhancing local water sustainability and drought resilience through indirect potable reuse at one of Southern California's largest groundwater storage basins. |
| 2020s | Soquel Creek Water District Advanced Water Purification Facility | Replenishing the Santa Cruz Mid-County Groundwater Basin with transmission lines, injection wells, upgrades to the Santa Cruz WWTP, and the new advanced MF/RO/UVAOP water treatment plant with a 1.7 MGD capacity. |
| 2020s | Donald C. Tillman Advanced Water Purification Facility (AWPF) | Producing purified water suitable for groundwater replenishment for one of the largest potable reuse projects in the U.S. |
