# Contract Exceptions - Change in Law, Uncontrollable Circumstances and Indemnification

Jacobs' proposal pricing is made with the assumption that the following changes to the Agreement have been accepted. We look forward to discussing these terms further with [CLIENT], a coastal New England town.

| Section | Issue | Proposed Revision |
|---|---|---|
| 2.02, Change in Law | While Jacobs agrees that changes in tax law as applicable to Jacobs revenue and overall tax burden should not qualify as a Change in Law, changes in tax law which are assessed on goods and consumables purchased by Jacobs for the Project should qualify as a Change in Law. | In no event shall a change in any federal, state or local tax law, except for changes assessed on Contractor's purchase of goods or consumables required for the services set forth in this Agreement, be considered a Change in Law. |
| 2.02, Uncontrollable Circumstances, Exclusions | Jacobs agrees that the Contractor is the party who bears the risk of complying with prevailing wage requirements. However, if prevailing wage law were to change during the term of the Agreement, this change should qualify as a Change in Law. | Any impact of prevailing wage laws, except for Changes in Law for prevailing wage laws, on the Company's costs; |
| 7.01 | Jacobs will be accountable for any consequence resulting from its errors. However, Jacobs should not be responsible for liabilities incurred for circumstances beyond its control and requests the indemnification to be modified accordingly. | The Company shall protect, defend, indemnify and hold harmless [CLIENT] and its agents, officials, employees, servants, and consultants, including contractors or subcontractors with whom [CLIENT] may have contracted, from and against, (i) all claims, demands, fines, assessments, fees, penalties, injunctive relief and/or orders imposed by a court of competent jurisdiction, any agency of the United States or the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, or a third party in an action pursuant to 33 U.S.C. 1365 as a result of the Company's negligent acts, omissions or willful misconduct resulting in the failure to meet the requirements of any permit, law, order, or regulation applicable to the performance or non-performance of Company's obligations or operations under this Agreement and (ii) any and all claims, demands, fines, liabilities, damages, judgment losses, costs, expenses, suits, actions, and causes of action of every kind and character, which may arise in favor of any third party on account of illness, disease, environmental pollution, loss or destruction of property, services, wages, death or personal injuries, to the extent caused by the negligent acts or omissions or willful misconduct of the Company or its officials, directors, employees or agents in the performance or non-performance of its obligations or operations under this Agreement. Further, the Company agrees that its aforementioned duty to protect, defend, and indemnify [CLIENT] shall include the obligation to indemnify [CLIENT] for all reasonable expenses, court costs and attorney's fees including those incidents to appeals incurred by or imposed upon by [CLIENT] in connection with the above indemnity for any loss, damage, injury, or other casualty. Upon [CLIENT] successfully establishes the right to indemnity hereunder, the Company further agrees to pay all reasonable expenses and attorney's fees incurred by [CLIENT] in establishing the right to indemnity. This Article shall survive the termination of this Agreement and the Company's obligations under this Section shall not be limited in any way by the Company's insurance coverage as required by this Agreement. |

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the three-column Section / Issue / Proposed Revision layout, the opening sentence that ties pricing to the assumed exceptions while inviting discussion, and the substantive positions - tax-law changes on purchased goods and consumables qualify as a Change in Law, a change in prevailing wage law qualifies as a Change in Law while day-to-day prevailing wage compliance stays with the contractor, and indemnification is keyed to negligent acts, omissions or willful misconduct rather than strict liability.

Pursuit-specific: every article number, the state named in the indemnity, and the citizen-suit citation.
