# Financial Transparency/Disclosure Practices and Litigation History Disclosure

## Financial Transparency and Disclosure

[CLIENT], a Southern California municipal water utility, can review Jacobs' comprehensive financial performance and stability through our publicly available SEC filings and disclosures. Jacobs publishes all SEC filings and disclosures on its investor relations page: https://invest.jacobs.com/financials/default.aspx. This transparency demonstrates our commitment to financial accountability and provides [CLIENT] with independent verification of our financial strength and performance history.

As a **Fortune 500 company** with unmatched financial stability, Jacobs will provide [CLIENT] with the financial security and operational expertise necessary for successful advanced water treatment operations. Our **proven track record on comparable advanced water treatment projects, combined with our single-entity guarantor structure** and **40+ years of O&M experience**, positions Jacobs as the ideal long-term financial partner for [CLIENT]'s critical infrastructure needs.

Jacobs' ownership structure promotes a growth strategy based on long-term relationships with clients, quality service, and innovation in areas that meet clients' evolving needs, unlike private-equity ownership models, which typically prioritize short-term financial return over long-term value.

## Minimal Litigation History

Jacobs has over 300 global operating entities, with approximately 100 operating entities in the U.S. and Canada, and a combined global talent force of more than 45,000 and approximately $15 billion in revenue. Jacobs provides a full spectrum of services, including O&M, scientific, technical, professional, construction, and program management for business, industrial, commercial, government, and infrastructure sectors. Given Jacobs' size, disputes, claims, arbitrations, or lawsuits may arise from time to time and in the ordinary course of its business. However, the number of such instances is minimal, especially considering the nature of work and the size of the company. Jacobs seeks to avoid litigation and aims to resolve any such disputes in an amicable manner, whenever possible.

The company considers its claims to be confidential to the company and its clients. While the outcome of pending disputes is often difficult to predict, senior management and General Counsel believe that no pending dispute, claim, arbitration, or lawsuit will result in a materially adverse impact on the company's consolidated financial statements or its ability to perform its obligations under any contract. Any litigation that is considered to have the potential for a material impact on the company's operations is disclosed in Jacobs SEC filings, which can be found online at https://invest.jacobs.com/overview/. While we strive to have every agreement run its duration, we understand that in isolated cases, circumstances beyond our control can drive our clients to change their approach to operations and maintenance. In such instances, we make every effort to ensure a successful transition whether to a new operating firm, or back to our client. While Jacobs does not formally track contract terminations internally, to the best of Jacobs knowledge, **Operations Management International, Inc. (OMI, a Jacobs subsidiary)** has not been terminated for cause or default or replaced as a result of having been terminated for cause under any operations contract in the last five years. Any failure to complete work awarded would have been based upon mutual agreement to change the scope of the work originally awarded and would not have been due to a failure to perform under a contract.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the framing that litigation volume is proportionate to firm size, the SEC-filing transparency reference with live investor-relations URLs, the publicly-traded-versus-private-equity ownership contrast (useful against private-equity-backed incumbents), and the "not terminated for cause in the last five years" disclosure pattern are reusable for any public-agency O&M pursuit requiring legal and litigation disclosure. Pursuit-specific: legal and contracts staff must reconfirm the termination-history claim and any pending-litigation disclosures are current before each submission — this is a compliance-sensitive statement, not marketing copy.
