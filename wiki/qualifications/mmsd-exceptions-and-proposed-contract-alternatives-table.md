# Exceptions and Proposed Contract Alternatives Table

Jacobs proposes the following alternatives from the terms and conditions for [CLIENT]'s consideration, each of which we believe offers [CLIENT] a more advantageous and balanced contractual arrangement. In alignment with [CLIENT]'s response to question 37, Jacobs anticipates future discussions with [CLIENT] to address potential areas requiring clarification as we refine the final agreement. In addition, Jacobs would like to underscore two items found at the bottom of the following table that are of particular importance to our team. They are key considerations that we believe are essential to establishing a successful, long-term partnership and that we have intentionally highlighted within our proposal.

| **Topic, Impacted Sections** | **Proposed Alternative Approach** | **Benefit to [CLIENT]** | **Annual Price Reduction** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exceptions with cost component** | | | |
| Insurance, Section 7.03, Exhibit J | Section 7.03: Strike "required insurance" from the exclusions to the Limitation of Liability. Exhibit J — please see attached document. | The insurance requested by [CLIENT] is outside of the coverage available under Jacobs corporate program and would involve procuring project specific insurance at a substantial cost. With a few minor revisions to the insurance requirements and the limitation of liability, the project can be insured under Jacobs' corporate program at a significant cost savings to [CLIENT]. | $1,143,000/year |

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four-column structure (topic and impacted sections / proposed alternative approach / benefit to the client / annual price reduction), the split between "exceptions with cost component" and "proposed clarifications without price impact", and the framing paragraph that positions exceptions as a path to a balanced long-term partnership rather than as resistance. The move of quantifying what an exception saves the client — rather than only what it protects the bidder — is the reusable persuasion device and travels to any pursuit where insurance or escalation-index requirements exceed the corporate program.

Pursuit-specific: every section and exhibit number, the escalation index named in the draft agreement, the indemnity clarification (tied to a client-branded biosolids product manufactured by the operator), and both dollar figures. The $1,143,000/year insurance figure is the price of self-standing project-specific insurance avoided by aligning Exhibit J with the corporate program; the $682,000/year figure is the contingency avoided by substituting CPI-Water and Sewerage Maintenance for CPI-All Items. Recompute both with risk, insurance, and estimating before reuse.
