# Confidentiality Designation and Trade-Secret Legal Basis

## E. Confidentiality Statement

**Confidentiality Designations and Legal Basis under Wisconsin Open Records Laws** — Consistent with RFP Section 3.4, we've labeled only specific pages of this proposal as confidential where we believe the information involves proprietary "trade secrets" exempt from public disclosure under Wisconsin public records law § 19.36(5); § 134.90(1)(c). Identified pages include one or more of the following types of proprietary trade secrets:

- Proprietary value/innovation packages derived from Jacobs-funded investments
- Client-identifying, client-confidential, or non-public client-specific information
- Proprietary technologies, tools, methods, processes, and "how we do it" internal frameworks
- Proprietary reporting tools and processes including custom computer programs, designs, and interfaces
- Internal pricing, cost approaches, and pricing assumptions
- Proprietary transition mobilization sequencing, risk controls, and internal tools and processes

Jacobs derives economic value from these proprietary trade secrets not being generally known, and Jacobs takes reasonable measures to maintain the confidentiality of the information on the identified pages. Public disclosure would likely (1) allow competitors to replicate, reverse-engineer, or underbid Jacobs, causing competitive harm, or (2) reveal details about our other clients that may be regarded as confidential or non-public information.

Jacobs acknowledges that [CLIENT], the regional sewerage district, will make the final determination concerning any exemption that applies under the applicable public records laws.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the whole architecture of the statement — narrow, page-specific designation; the six trade-secret categories; the two-part harm test (competitive replication or underbidding, and exposure of other clients' non-public information); and the closing concession that the agency decides. Evaluators read blanket confidentiality claims as a compliance risk, so the "only specific pages" opening carries real persuasive weight and should lead every version of this block.

Pursuit-specific: the RFP section number that governs confidentiality, the state statutes cited, and the actual list of designated pages. Where the RFP requires a schedule of designated pages, attach it and keep this narrative as the justification.
