# Odor Control Capability and Case Studies

**Odor Control – Turning Complaints into Compliance at Waterbury.** When Jacobs assumed operations at Waterbury's 27-MGD WWTP, the City had been receiving significant odor complaints driven by four chemical wet scrubbers in disrepair and a secondary source from the on-site merchant incinerator. Jacobs conducted a formal odor study to identify and quantify each source, then executed a two-track response: an immediate operational control plan that reduced public complaints by more than 55% within the first year, followed by a $2M capital upgrade that included a carbon absorber polishing stage, new chemical dosing pumps, and replacement of aging air piping across both scrubber systems. The result was a permanent, measurable fix to a problem that had eroded community confidence in the facility. (Case study callout graphic ID `126_009385`.)

Odor control is one of the most visible performance measures in wastewater operations. Jacobs brings experience across the full odor control life cycle, including formal odor studies, dispersion modeling, operational improvements, capital upgrades, and ongoing fence line monitoring.

We follow a consistent framework of field sampling and source identification first, dispersion modeling to quantify off-site impact, operational improvement where possible, and capital recommendations sized to deliver the most community benefit per dollar invested. Operators conduct routine H2S monitoring using portable meters, make scheduled fence line patrols during high-odor seasons, and follow documented corrective action protocols when readings exceed thresholds.

**At Waterbury, Connecticut, Jacobs reduced public odor complaints by more than 55% within the first contract year** through a formal source characterization study and a structured two-track response: immediate operational controls followed by a **$2M capital upgrade to chemical scrubber systems**.

**Jacobs manages odor and air quality compliance across two WWTPs with an active biosolids incinerator in Vancouver, Washington**, using Continuous Emissions Monitoring (CEM) systems to track regulated parameters in real time under a Title V air permit. We delivered capital improvements to odor control systems at both plants as part of our integrated capital management program alongside a $500,000 heat exchanger investment that extended the incinerator's operational life, deferred an unplanned shutdown, and eliminated associated confined space and odor risks.

Jacobs designed and operates an advanced odor control system in **Agua Nueva, Arizona, that removes virtually all facility odors without additional chemicals and with minimal maintenance requirements**. We achieved this deliberate design outcome by embedding operations expertise into the facility's engineering from the start.

In **Courtright, Ontario**, Jacobs led odor control upgrades at a WWTP that had received persistent community complaints, including **introducing a biofilter, activated sludge recycle process, and final effluent reuse pumping station supported by air dispersion modeling and environmental compliance approval applications.**
