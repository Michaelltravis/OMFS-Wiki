# Resume — Dave Haverly (Safety and Security, Jones Island Water Reclamation Facility)

**Safety and Security, JIWRF – Dave Haverly**

**Education and Licenses**

- BS, Environmental Studies
- OSHA 40-hour Hazardous Waste Site Personnel
- OSHA 30-hour Industry Outreach Training, USACE Construction Quality Management
- USACE EM385-1-1 Training
- OSHA 3085 – Principles of Scaffolding

**Experience Summary**

- 21 years of experience leading safety programs, including 10 years at WWTFs, pump and collection stations, and other high-risk energy facilities, with recent experience at Waterbury, North Hudson Sewerage Authority, and City of Woonsocket municipal WWTFs.
- Manages site-wide training, inspections, incident investigations, and OSHA compliance.
- Builds proactive, safety-first culture through visible leadership and education.

**Select Projects**

- Safety Professional, City of Waterbury, CT, WWTF O&M
- Safety Professional, NHSA Hoboken, NJ, and West New York WWTFs
- Safety Professional, City of Woonsocket, RI, Regional WWTF
- Safety Professional, City of Allentown, PA, Lehigh Wastewater Pretreatment Facility

**Benefit to MMSD:**

Dave has worked with our Project Manager, Kevin Dahl, during the transition of several WWTF O&M contracts to Jacobs. They'll tailor successful strategies from these contracts for MMSD, aiming to implement a safety culture centered on visible leadership, prevention through education, and accountability at every level. Dave has consistently protected people, assets, and operations in critical facilities such as JIWRF.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the strongest reusable element here is the opening of the benefit paragraph — naming the project manager this person has already transitioned contracts alongside. It answers the evaluator's unspoken question about whether a proposed team has ever worked together, and it costs one sentence. The three-part statement of the safety culture being implemented (visible leadership, prevention through education, accountability at every level) is house language and should be kept in that order and in those words so it recurs identically wherever the safety culture is described elsewhere in the proposal. The experience summary's structure — years, then the subset of those years in wastewater and high-risk facilities, then the named recent municipal WWTFs — is the pattern to follow for any resident site safety lead.

Pursuit-specific: the OSHA and USACE training list, the project list, and the facility named in the final sentence. Waterbury CT, North Hudson Sewerage Authority (Hoboken and West New York NJ), Woonsocket RI and Allentown PA are real assignments at other utilities used as evidence and are kept verbatim. Where the pursuit has one facility rather than two, drop the facility qualifier from the role title.
