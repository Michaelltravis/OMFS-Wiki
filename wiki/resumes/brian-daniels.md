# Brian Daniels
## MAINTENANCE MANAGER

### Why Brian?
- **Proven Wastewater Maintenance Leader.** Brian brings 30 years of hands-on maintenance leadership across wastewater treatment plants, a biosolids management site, and extensive lift station assets, giving him direct experience with the same types of equipment and facilities OCWUT must maintain. He's held long-term responsibility for directing maintenance work and keeping critical assets available across a multi-site wastewater system.
- **Structured CMMS and Reliability Focus.** Brian has managed CMMS work order execution, preventive and corrective maintenance scheduling, and predictive maintenance tools including thermography, vibration analysis, and ultrasound testing. His approach supports stronger planning, improved maintenance discipline, reduced downtime, and longer asset life.
- **Strong Electrical and Mechanical Depth.** As a licensed industrial electrician and experienced maintenance leader, Brian brings practical field expertise across pumps, motors, panels, generators, controls, and utility electrical systems, supporting safe and reliable operation of critical wastewater assets. That depth also supports effective troubleshooting, emergency response, and maintenance readiness for essential equipment.
- **Aligned with OCWUT's Maintenance Priorities.** Brian's background in staff direction, maintenance scheduling, emergency response, startup support, and multi-site wastewater asset management positions him to build the structured, accountable maintenance program OCWUT is seeking. He brings practical leadership that supports reliability, operational continuity, and long-term asset performance.

### Summary
As Maintenance Manager, Brian will lead a disciplined maintenance program focused on asset reliability, work planning, and accountability across OCWUT's treatment plants, solids facilities, and lift station assets. His 30 years of wastewater maintenance leadership includes CMMS management, preventive and predictive maintenance, staff supervision, hands-on electrical and mechanical support for critical utility equipment, daily work order execution, PM scheduling, emergency response, troubleshooting, and repair-versus-replace decision making. Brian will strengthen maintenance planning, support startup and capital integration, and help maintain critical assets safely and consistently, focusing on long-term reliability and operational readiness.

### Relevant Experience
**Maintenance Manager | City of Fayetteville Utilities | Fayetteville, AR.** Brian led maintenance operations for Fayetteville's wastewater system, including two wastewater treatment plants, one biosolids management site, and 56 lift stations across three communities. He managed the CMMS work order system, directed and scheduled staff, and oversaw preventive, predictive, and corrective maintenance across the system. He developed PM schedules, issued corrective work orders, tracked repair costs, and supported repair-versus-replace decisions to strengthen reliability and asset performance. He also applied thermography, vibration analysis, and ultrasound testing to reduce downtime and improve maintenance planning.

**Lead Mechanic/Industrial Electrician | City of Bixby | Bixby Water Reclamation Facility and Lift Stations O&M | Bixby, OK.** Brian worked on site during startup and early operations of the 2.8-MGD Bixby Water Reclamation Facility and 18 lift stations. He trained staff, maintained treatment and pumping assets, and supported operations during heavy rain events while helping stabilize a facility still moving through commissioning and punch-list work. He also provided NFPA 70E arc flash training and supported maintenance readiness in an active Oklahoma DEQ operating environment.

**Maintenance Support/Emergency Power and NFPA 70E Training | City of Duncan | Wastewater Treatment and Reuse O&M | Duncan, OK.** Brian provided NFPA 70E training for water and wastewater mechanics and responding during heavy rain events and power outages. His role included connecting 400-kW generators to maintain backup power for the plant, helping preserve operational continuity during critical disruptions in an Oklahoma wastewater environment.

**Maintenance Support/NFPA 70E Training | JXN Water | Public Drinking Water and Wastewater Facilities O&M | Jackson, MS.** Brian provided NFPA 70E arc flash training for water and wastewater mechanics during rapid mobilization and system stabilization. In a utility environment challenged by deferred maintenance, staffing gaps, and urgent reliability needs, his role helped strengthen safe work practices and maintenance readiness across a complex multi-facility system.

**Maintenance Support / Electrical Projects and NFPA 70E Training | City of San Marcos | WWTP O&M | San Marcos, TX.** Brian provided NFPA 70E arc flash training and completing electrical projects tied to wastewater operations. His work included lift station controller upgrades and other field electrical improvements that strengthened reliability and maintenance responsiveness across wastewater assets.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four-bullet argument for a Maintenance Manager — asset-type match, a structured CMMS and predictive-maintenance method, hands-on electrical/mechanical depth, and alignment to the client's stated maintenance priorities. The predictive-maintenance triad (thermography, vibration analysis, ultrasound) named with its purpose — reduce downtime, improve planning — is the concrete detail that separates this from a generic maintenance bio and should survive any rewrite. So should the repeated NFPA 70E arc flash training across four projects, which lets one resume carry both a maintenance and a safety-culture claim; on pursuits that score safety, cite this profile in the safety section as well.

Pursuit-specific: the OCWUT framing, the solids-facility and lift-station references, and the Oklahoma DEQ environment. The licenses are all Arkansas — on an out-of-state pursuit, state the reciprocity plan rather than implying in-state licensure. The Fayetteville entry supplies the portfolio-scale numbers (two WWTPs, one biosolids site, 56 lift stations, three communities) and should be re-scaled honestly rather than compared loosely to a larger bid portfolio.
