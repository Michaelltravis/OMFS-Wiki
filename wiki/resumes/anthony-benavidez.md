### Responsibilities
As the Project Manager for Jacobs at the Spokane County Regional Water Reclamation Facility (SCRWRF), Anthony’s role aligns with the County’s expectations. His daily activities involve providing guidance for the operations and maintenance of MBR plants, ensuring all contractual and regulatory requirements are met, managing and leading facility improvements, encouraging community involvement, and maintaining process control and regulatory compliance. Anthony also monitors budgets and oversees the financial plan, leads the quality process, develops the annual Project Business Plan, and manages all personnel functions and objectives through effective communication.

Anthony maintains his technical operational skills at the highest level and stays informed of the latest developments in the field. He establishes relationships with subordinates, vendors, contractors, and others he interacts with during work assignments, while maintaining a solid working relationship with the client. He implements training programs to advance professional skills and mentors new project managers and contract operating teams with a written plan. Additionally, he reviews monthly contract service reports for each installation, advises management on project status, regulatory compliance, and technical/plant management attitudes, and fosters interest and support in the development of cost-saving programs.

“The MBR treatment process is highly data-driven and requires continuous monitoring. Operators must be proactive, careful and diligent—MBRs aren’t as forgiving as a conventional activated sludge process. It’s a chemical process more than a biological process. Cleaning the membranes properly is key, as is managing the aeration system. Once the membranes get fouled, they can’t be recovered quickly.

The team’s ability to work with instrumentation and small mechanical components is important. Proper training is essential. Jacobs’ membrane technologists are really good—we pull them in all the time for advice or send them data to review. The membrane manufacturer will always point fingers elsewhere, so it’s great to have someone who can read the data and spot trends to help solve a problem.”

— Anthony Benavidez
