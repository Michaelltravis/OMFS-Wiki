# Resume — Monty Sedlak, PMP, PgMP, CSP (Maintenance & Asset Manager, Conveyance)

**Maintenance & Asset Manager (Conveyance) – Monty Sedlak, PMP, PgMP, CSP**

**Education and Licenses**

- MS, Organizational Leadership
- BS, Environmental Engineering
- Project Management Professional (PMP), Program Management Professional (PgMP), Certified Safety Professional (CSP)
- Mobius Institute – Vibrations Analyst Category II
- Water Professional International Wastewater Treatment Certification Class 4 Operator and Collection Certifications
- Class A Wastewater Operator License, PA

**Experience Summary**

- Supported 250+ O&M contracts, led asset and resource optimization, and developed reliability-centered and condition-based maintenance programs.
- Implemented predictive practices, KPIs, and training to boost retention and performance and built positive relationships with union teams.

**Select Projects**

- Project Director, City of Pembroke Pines, FL, Wastewater O&M
- Project Manager, City of North Miami Beach, FL, Water Treatment Facility O&M
- Sr. Executive, City of Vancouver, WA Westside and Marine Park WWTFs O&M

**Benefit to MMSD:**

Monty brings expertise in preventive and predictive maintenance strategies to support MMSD's 85% PM goal and foster a high-performing environment, while building and maintaining positive relationships with union employees.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the credential stack is the argument. PMP and PgMP say the person can run maintenance as a managed program; CSP says maintenance will be executed safely; the Mobius Category II vibration analyst certification says predictive maintenance is a technique this person personally practices rather than a word in the proposal; and the operator and collection certifications say they can hold a licence on the client's system. Where a pursuit's maintenance evaluation criteria reward predictive and condition-based maintenance, list the analyst-level certification explicitly — a claim to do predictive maintenance without a named certified analyst is weak.

The second experience bullet connects predictive practices and KPIs to retention, which is the link most maintenance resumes miss: better-planned work is a retention argument, not only a reliability argument. Carry that sentence structure.

Pursuit-specific: the 250+ contracts figure is corporate-portfolio exposure rather than personal delivery and should be stated as such; the Florida and Washington project list should be swapped for comparable conveyance or collection system work when the pursuit is collection-heavy. The 85% PM goal is the client's own target on this pursuit — confirm the target client's figure before restating it.
