# Resume — Bill Desing, PE (Chief Engineer and Offsite Support Lead)

**Chief Engineer and Offsite Support Lead – Bill Desing, PE**

**Education and Licenses**

- MS, Environmental Engineering
- BS, Chemical Engineering
- Professional Engineer: WI

**Experience Summary**

- 35+ years of experience began career as a co-op at JIWRF and has since supported more than $100M in upgrades to the JIWRF.
- Understands MMSD's nutrient reduction, liquids and solids processing, biogas use, biosolids management/disposal, odor control, energy evaluation, and greenhouse gas reduction/decarbonization efforts.
- Played a key role in MMSD's energy plan and greenhouse gas inventory by quantifying emissions, identifying energy-saving opportunities, and aligning infrastructure planning with climate goals.
- Global lead for odor control and author of publications and presentations on odor issues, including revised WEF Odor Control Manual of Practice and served as senior reviewer or project manager for 50+ wastewater odor projects.

**Select Projects**

- Project Manager, MMSD Landfill Gas Turbine Design, Greenhouse Gas Inventory, System-wide Energy Study, Power Monitoring, Collection System Odor Study, Engineering Improvements (water filtration, solids handling, material convenience), Pump Station Design, Milorganite Storage Facility
- Project Manager, Metropolitan Sewer District of Greater Cincinnati Odor Control Master Plan, Cincinnati, OH
- Odor Control Consultant, London Thames Tunnel, London, UK

**Benefit to MMSD:**

Bill understands MMSD's systems, complexities, and interconnections. With this background, he'll identify SMEs from our Milwaukee or global offices to help MMSD explore solutions to odor control, asset management, maintenance, compliance, biosolids, wet weather, and other challenges. We'll avoid the need for outside engineering consultants, and address concerns proactively and more collaboratively than your current operator.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: three transferable devices. First, the offsite support lead as a named role — one engineer who is accountable for pulling the right specialist out of the firm, which turns a vague corporate bench into a single point of contact. Second, the "we'll avoid the need for outside engineering consultants" claim, which is the operator-plus-engineering-firm differentiator stated as a cost consequence for the client rather than as a capability. Third, the explicit comparison at the end — "more collaboratively than your current operator" — the sharpest incumbent-displacement sentence on the pursuit, usable wherever the client's dissatisfaction with the incumbent is known and the tone of the proposal supports a direct comparison.

Pursuit-specific and non-transferable: the co-op-to-chief-engineer history at the client's own facility, and the list of the client's own programs the engineer has personally worked on. When this pattern is available on another pursuit it should be mined the same way — name the client's projects the person delivered, not the categories of work they know. The odor control credentials (WEF Manual of Practice authorship, 50+ odor projects) travel to any odor-sensitive pursuit on their own.
