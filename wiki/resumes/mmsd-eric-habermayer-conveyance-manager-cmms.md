# Resume — Eric Habermayer, IAM (Conveyance Manager and CMMS Implementation Project Manager)

**Conveyance Manager and CMMS Implementation Project Manager – Eric Habermayer, IAM**

**Education and Licenses**

- BS, Civil and Environmental Engineering
- Institute of Asset Management (IAM) Certification

**Experience Summary**

- 17 years of experience in wastewater collection planning, design, construction, commissioning, and operation.
- Combined sewer facility real-time control optimization, wastewater collection flow monitoring, and NPDES program management.
- Wastewater, water, and stormwater infrastructure asset management, CMMS data management and analysis, and asset information systems and management.
- Implements centralized CMMS and risk-based condition assessments to guide investments for municipal WWTFs.

**Select Projects**

- Flow Monitoring Task Lead, Seattle Public Utilities CSO Reduction Program, Seattle, WA
- City of Chicago, IL Department of Water Management NPDES Permit Support
- Asset Management Consultant, Jurupa Community Services District Sewer Condition Assessment and Asset Management Plan, Jurupa Valley, CA
- Asset Management Consultant, Clayton County Water Authority, Clayton County, GA
- Seattle Public Utilities System Operations Planning Analysis Management, Seattle, WA

**Benefit to MMSD:**

A native of Madison, Eric's wet weather CSO experience for Seattle Public Utilities and strong expertise in asset management for collections systems will allow him to address MMSD's conveyance challenges and needs.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the pairing of a conveyance operations role with ownership of the CMMS implementation is the idea worth carrying. On most pursuits those are two people and two sections, and the seam between them is where asset data goes stale; naming one person accountable for both closes it. The Institute of Asset Management certification is the evidence that "asset management" here means a discipline with a standard behind it, and should be listed among the credentials rather than described in narrative.

The benefit paragraph opens with a local-roots clause before any technical claim. That order is deliberate on a pursuit where the client cares about regional commitment, and it is a cheap, high-value move wherever a genuine in-state connection exists — but it must be true and specific, not "familiar with the region."

Pursuit-specific: the CSO and real-time control content suits combined-sewer clients; on a separate-sewer system, lead instead with the condition assessment and CMMS bullets. The named asset management consulting clients (Jurupa Community Services District, Clayton County Water Authority) are other utilities cited as evidence and stay verbatim, but should be swapped for the most size-comparable systems available.
