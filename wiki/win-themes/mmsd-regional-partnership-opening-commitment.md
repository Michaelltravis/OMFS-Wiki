# Regional Partnership Opening — More Than an O&M Contract

> **Jacobs looks forward to collaborating with [CLIENT] to develop creative regional partnership strategies that will foster community trust and provide economic growth opportunities for local and regional residents and businesses.**
>
> — Greg Fischer, President of OMI (Jacobs' O&M Group)

This O&M contract represents far more than the operation and maintenance of [CLIENT]'s sewerage infrastructure. [CLIENT] is a Midwest regional sewerage district serving a multi-county service area. It is a long-term commitment by Jacobs and our entire proposed team to serve as a fully integrated partner—one aligned with [CLIENT]'s culture, priorities, and mission. Together, we are dedicated to protecting public health and safety, prioritizing the communities we serve, and preserving and enhancing the environment for future generations.

Jacobs' regional partnership strategies will contribute to equity, sustainability, and economic growth for [CLIENT]'s service area and regional communities. We'll support and complement the outreach and regional initiatives that [CLIENT] is currently spearheading (community engagement, workforce development, small business and supplier development, and research and development). While we view these activities as critical, we've also proposed above and beyond ideas that will catalyze partnerships and produce measurable regional benefits.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the two-move opening — reframe the contract as a partnership rather than a service purchase, then split the offer into "we will support what you already do" and "we have also proposed ideas that go beyond it." That split is what keeps a community-benefits section from reading either as flattery or as a promise to replace the client's own programs.

Pursuit-specific: the named client initiatives in the second paragraph, the regional descriptor on first use of [CLIENT], and the executive named on the pull quote.
