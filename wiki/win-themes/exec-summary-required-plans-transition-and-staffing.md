# Executive Summary Required Plans — Draft Transition Plan and Staffing & Training Plan

## Required plans: built for [CLIENT], ready on day one

**Draft Transition Plan:** Transition Manager Kelly Irving to lead a phased mobilization—from governance and due diligence through full operational control—with risk-based management addressing the 10 highest-probability risks. Positions offered to all qualified incumbent staff. A jointly signed baseline condition assessment completed before the Commencement Date.

**Staffing & Training Plan (Schedule 10):** Jacobs workforce of 96 FTEs (plus another 13 biosolids staff from our subcontractor) with 24/7 coverage at every facility. A high-performance culture built on competitive compensation, BeyondZero® safety, certification incentives for operators and mechanics, and cross-training across facilities—shifting the team from reactive response to proactive ownership of plant performance. Jacobs' dedicated Resource Planning Group to fill vacancies fast and prevent turnover that has plagued current O&M.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the compression pattern — each required plan gets a bold label, three or four commitments, and nothing else, so the executive summary previews the plan without duplicating it. Three devices inside travel especially well in challenger bids: naming the transition manager as a person, promising a jointly signed baseline condition assessment before the commencement date (which protects both parties and signals confidence), and naming the corporate recruiting function as the answer to incumbent turnover rather than simply asserting that turnover will improve.

Pursuit-specific: the transition manager's name, the FTE counts and the prime/subcontractor split, the schedule number, and the count of highest-probability risks. The phrase "turnover that has plagued current O&M" is only defensible where the incumbent's turnover is documented in the RFP, in public board minutes, or in site-visit findings.
