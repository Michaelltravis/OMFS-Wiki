# Embedding a Proven-Turnaround Case Study Callout Inside a Technical Methodology Section

A short, shaded callout box describing a prior successful engagement can be placed directly inside a technical-approach subsection — immediately following the methodology it validates — to show the evaluator that the proposed process (here, the five-step odor/corrosion evaluation framework) is not theoretical but has already produced a measurable result for another client.

**Illustrative sanitized example** (structure and genuine content preserved; reference client generalized):

> **Proven Odor Control Improvement — [REFERENCE CLIENT]**
>
> When Jacobs assumed operations of [REFERENCE CLIENT]'s WWTP, the community had experienced persistent odor concerns under the previous operator. Early in the contract, Jacobs convened a focused odor control workshop with client leadership and Jacobs' process specialists to identify odor sources and prioritize practical, operations-based solutions.
>
> This effort led to targeted operational improvements, including enhanced solids management, optimized aeration and process control, and refined residuals handling practices. These changes addressed the root causes of odors without requiring immediate capital investment.
>
> As a result, the facility experienced a measurable reduction in odor complaints and improved process stability — demonstrating the ability to quickly diagnose complex odor issues and implement cost-effective solutions in partnership with the client. The same structured, collaborative approach is proposed for proactively identifying odor sources, optimizing operations, and implementing practical solutions that protect the surrounding community.

## What makes this callout effective

- **Placed at point of need**: it sits directly beside the odor-control methodology it supports, not in a separate past-performance section the evaluator may skim past.
- **Names the mechanism, not just the result**: a specific early-contract workshop convening client leadership and process specialists — mirroring the very framework (structured evaluation, stakeholder engagement) being proposed for the new client.
- **Emphasizes capital-light solutions**: "without requiring immediate capital investment" directly counters a common evaluator concern that odor fixes always mean expensive capital projects.
- **Closes with an explicit bridge**: the final sentence ties the case study's success factors back to what's being proposed for the new client, rather than leaving the connection implicit.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the callout-box placement technique (drop a compact, 3-paragraph proven-result narrative immediately after the methodology section it substantiates, rather than only in an appendix), the workshop-driven diagnostic approach as a reusable technique, and the closing bridge sentence that explicitly connects the case study to the current proposal's commitment. This pattern works for any technical capability claim (not just odor control) that has a genuine, citable prior success.

Pursuit-specific: replace the reference client and every outcome detail with a real, currently-authorized case study relevant to the capability being proposed — do not reuse this specific narrative's substance for a different client's proposal. Confirm the case study is not subject to a confidentiality restriction before including it.
