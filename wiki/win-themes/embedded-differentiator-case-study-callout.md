# Proven Odor Control Improvement Case Study Callout (Town of Southbridge, MA)

The following callout runs as a shaded sidebar directly alongside the Site-Specific Odor Control Plan methodology, so the evaluator meets the proof at the moment the process is being claimed.

> **PROVEN ODOR CONTROL IMPROVEMENT — TOWN OF SOUTHBRIDGE, MA**
>
> When Jacobs assumed operations of the Town of Southbridge's WWTF, the Town had experienced persistent odor concerns under the previous operator. Early in the contract, Jacobs convened a focused odor control workshop with Town leadership and Jacobs' process specialists to identify odor sources and prioritize practical, operations-based solutions.
>
> This effort led to targeted operational improvements, including enhanced solids management, optimized aeration and process control, and refined residuals handling practices. These changes addressed the root causes of odors without requiring immediate capital investment.
>
> As a result, the facility experienced a measurable reduction in odor complaints and improved process stability, demonstrating Jacobs' ability to quickly diagnose complex odor issues and implement cost-effective solutions in partnership with the client. Jacobs brings this same structured, collaborative approach to [CLIENT]. We proactively identify odor sources, optimize operations, and implement practical solutions that protect the surrounding community.

## Why this callout works where it sits

- **Placed at point of need.** It sits directly beside the odor-control methodology it supports, not in a separate past-performance section the evaluator may skim past.
- **Names the mechanism, not just the result.** A specific early-contract workshop convening client leadership and process specialists mirrors the very framework — structured evaluation, stakeholder engagement — being proposed for the new client.
- **Emphasizes capital-light solutions.** "Without requiring immediate capital investment" directly counters a common evaluator concern that odor fixes always mean expensive capital projects.
- **Carries the incumbent-displacement message without attacking anyone.** "Persistent odor concerns under the previous operator" states the before-condition as fact and lets the outcome do the arguing.
- **Closes with an explicit bridge.** The final sentences tie the case study's success factors back to what is being committed for the new client, rather than leaving the connection implicit.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the placement technique (a compact three-paragraph proven-result narrative set immediately beside the methodology it substantiates, rather than only in an appendix), the workshop-driven diagnostic opening, the capital-light framing, and the closing bridge sentence. The pattern transfers to any technical capability claim — energy, compliance turnaround, maintenance backlog recovery — that has a genuine, citable prior success. Pursuit-specific: the Southbridge, MA reference itself. Because it is a reference client rather than the pursuit client, the name and details are kept verbatim, but confirm the reference is currently permissioned, that the facts still hold, and that Southbridge is not doing duty elsewhere in the same proposal; substitute a region-appropriate alternative if it is.
