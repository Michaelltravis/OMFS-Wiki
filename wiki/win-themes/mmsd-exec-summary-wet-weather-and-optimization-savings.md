# Wet Weather Flow Management and Optimization That Delivers Budget Value

## Wet Weather Flow Management to Support the Goal of Zero Overflows

We'll apply predictive forecasting, coordinated tunnel and WRF operations, and disciplined decision-making to prevent overflows and basement backups. Our approach emphasizes proactive readiness, resilient wet weather response, and close coordination with [CLIENT] management — [CLIENT] is a Midwest regional sewerage district operating two large water reclamation facilities and a deep tunnel conveyance system. **Susan Moisio, PE as Wet Weather and Conveyance Director,** will direct our conveyance experts to help [CLIENT] innovate and address current and future challenges. We've also proposed deploying digital tools which provide a real-time view of the network for improved performance.

## Optimization That Delivers Value for [CLIENT]'s Budget

**Leveraging our intelligent O&M tools, we have already identified reductions of 20-30% in power and chemical use compared to prior years—efficiencies that are directly reflected in our price.** We'll continue to target additional optimization opportunities in [CLIENT]'s highest shared cost areas, including power, chemicals, fuel, and biosolids handling, using data analytics, SCADA insights, process modeling, and targeted pilots. Our digital tools that use data science and machine learning have helped achieve 20–30% chemical and power savings for existing O&M clients, such as Wilmington, DE, and West Basin, CA. For example, in taking over the West Basin operations (the largest reuse facility in North America) from the incumbent operator, we reduced chemical consumption by 15-40% and at Wilmington by 23%. Ultimately, our focus is to continuously optimize cost and risk, while sustaining compliance and performance.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the optimization paragraph's argument sequence — savings already identified, savings already priced in, named client proof at two utilities, then the commitment to keep going in the client's highest shared-cost areas. This is the strongest way the library has to make an efficiency claim credible, because it names the utilities and the percentages rather than saying "we typically achieve."

Pursuit-specific: the 20–30% figure (re-derive it from the pursuit's own baseline data), the tunnel/conveyance references, and the named Wet Weather and Conveyance Director. The wet weather paragraph presumes a deep tunnel system and a stated zero-overflow goal; for a pursuit without one, keep the predictive-forecasting and coordinated-operations mechanics and drop the tunnel language.

Keep verbatim: West Basin Municipal Water District and City of Wilmington, DE are cited as past-performance evidence at other utilities, not as the pursuit client — their names, percentages, and the "largest reuse facility in North America" descriptor stay as written.
