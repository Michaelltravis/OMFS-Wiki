# Cover Letter Opening — Coastal Operating Context and Named Leadership With a Bench Behind It

[CLIENT]'s — a coastal New England municipality's — residents, businesses, and coastal environment depend on wastewater operations that protect public health, maintain regulatory compliance, and support the community's quality of life. The community's coastal setting, proximity of residences to the treatment system, and the importance of storm preparedness, odor control, and effective biosolids management require a disciplined and responsive operating partner. Addressing challenges such as inflow and infiltration (I/I) and wet weather peak flows, and coordinating capital improvements with daily operations also demands an experienced and collaborative team. Jacobs understands these priorities and has tailored our approach to support [CLIENT]'s operational goals, protect [CLIENT] assets, and deliver resilient, high-quality service.

Project Manager Nathan Callison and Regional Operations Manager Scott Mangold bring hands-on experience managing our municipal wastewater operations across New England. Their leadership will strengthen operational accountability, safety culture, and process reliability while promoting strong housekeeping practices, regulatory compliance, and clear communication with [CLIENT]. Jacobs also brings regional and national wastewater specialists and industry-leading tools to reinforce our local team and expand available resources. During our site visits, we engaged subject matter experts to complement our team's evaluation of the facility's current condition, regulatory drivers, and long-term opportunities for improvement. This combined perspective allows us to tailor our services to [CLIENT]'s needs and focus on delivering operational stability while identifying opportunities to optimize energy use, odor control, biosolids management, and chemical efficiency.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the two-move opening — recognize the client's specific operating context in the client's own terms before making any claim about Jacobs, then introduce named on-site leadership immediately paired with the regional/national bench standing behind them, so the evaluator sees both a face and depth. The closing sentence of each paragraph does the persuasive work: paragraph one ends on the outcome ("resilient, high-quality service"), paragraph two ends on the optimization agenda (energy, odor, biosolids, chemicals) that the technical approach then has to deliver.

Pursuit-specific: every operating condition named in paragraph one must be lifted from the target RFP, the site visit, and the client's own permit and complaint history — a coastal, residence-adjacent, wet-weather-driven profile does not transfer to an inland or industrial pursuit. Replace Nathan Callison and Scott Mangold with the actual proposed Project Manager and Regional Operations Manager and adjust the region named for their municipal wastewater experience. Keep the site-visit sentence only where subject matter experts actually attended.
