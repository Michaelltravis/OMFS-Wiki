# Executive Summary Innovation Table — Three Named Innovations With Quantified Client Benefit

| INNOVATION | BENEFIT TO [CLIENT] |
|---|---|
| **Alternative Escalation Index Methodology** | An estimated $620,000 in Base Fee savings over the first contract term (~$155K/year) by tying escalation to actual water/sewer cost drivers rather than CPI-U South. |
| **Discounted Engineering Services (2.6 multiplier)** | Lower-cost access to Jacobs engineers who already know your facilities—ready to support an updated Odor Study, Replica Hydraulic Model covering the plants and Witcher Pump Station, NexGen Phase 2 asset data buildout, CIP design, biosolids thermal drying, PFAS readiness, and grant funding. |
| **Potential Chemical & Energy Savings with Intelligent O&M** | AI-enabled tools that turn operating data into proven savings—delivering 20% chlorine reduction ($200K/year) at Wilmington, DE and 20% polymer reduction ($600K/year) at Vancouver, WA. |

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the table itself — two columns, the innovation named in bold on the left, and on the right a benefit expressed in the client's money, the client's studies, or a reference site's measured result. No row describes a capability; every row describes what the client gets. Also universal is the sequencing: a commercial alternative first (which reads as the bidder giving something up), then discounted access to the firm's own engineers, then a technology play backed by two reference outcomes.

Pursuit-specific: every figure, the escalation basis, the multiplier, the named pump station, the EAM platform, and the list of studies. The study list works because each item is something the client had already identified as a need; rebuild it from the target RFP and site-visit notes rather than offering a generic menu.

Where the pursuit forbids commercial content in the technical volume, keep rows one and two for the price proposal and carry only the third row here.
