# Regional Partnerships and Public Outreach for Community Benefits

We're inspired by your vision—and your ongoing partnership and outreach initiatives. [CLIENT] is a Midwest regional sewerage district with an established community-benefits program. We'll build on your foundation and our partnerships in the region. We'll explore above and beyond ideas with you within four strategies—community health and environmental enhancement, workforce development and economic growth, sustainability and resilience, and research and development. Our goal is to identify those ideas within each strategy that have the greatest impact on equity, sustainability, and economic growth and bring the most value to [CLIENT] and ratepayers. Examples of these ideas are captured on the next page, and are elaborated upon in ***Section V***.

## Regional Partnership Ideas to Enhance the Community

- Planning, designing, and supporting the construction of permanent lasting infrastructure, such as a public fishing pier
- Providing green infrastructure training to local small businesses and partnering with MATC, Wisconsin's state-certified wastewater operator program, as well as internship and apprenticeship programs
- Helping [CLIENT] acquire and protect river corridor lands
- Forming research collaboratives to provide treatment innovations that will ultimately protect ratepayers
- Providing SMEs to support [CLIENT]'s research facility with research and business planning to ensure it stays financially stable

**To achieve these goals, we're assigning regional and public outreach to our DPM, Rick Warner, PE,** a recognized authority on public infrastructure partnerships. Rick will lead our team alongside Toyin Ogunfolaju, Megan Myszka, and small local businesses PRISM Technical Management & Marketing Services, P3 Development Group, and Mike Tate to align goals and strategies with your vision.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four strategies (community health and environmental enhancement; workforce development and economic growth; sustainability and resilience; research and development), the explicit selection test applied to candidate ideas, and the move of putting community benefits under a named deputy-level leader instead of a coordinator — which is what makes the commitment legible to an evaluator.

Pursuit-specific: every example idea, the state operator-training partner, the river corridor and research-facility references, and the named local partner firms. Note that the ideas deliberately range from permanent physical infrastructure to workforce pathways to research funding — keep that range when substituting.
