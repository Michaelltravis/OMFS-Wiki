# Training, Cross-Training, and Knowledge Transfer

Training for our staff will consist of in-house training, and training through water and wastewater associations such as the Water Environment Federation (WEF), the American Water Works Association (AWWA), and the WateReuse Association. We will also provide training for our associates through private sources such as the American Water College, which got its start in California in 2006 and provides water and wastewater training for all 50 states. Its many course offerings include California-approved specialized training courses such as advanced water treatment.

We will cross-train across the advanced water treatment facility and the urban runoff recycling facility, pair [CLIENT] interns with mentors, and implement a formal training plan (EPA/Title 22/ELAP sampling, safety, QA/QC, SOPs) with documented completion and incentives.

**Benefits to [CLIENT] callout:** *"Staff stability and improved morale with a resilient, multi-skilled workforce; reduced reliance on overtime/contractors; and continuous capability growth for [CLIENT] staff and interns."*

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the structure of naming the training sources by organization — in-house, industry associations, and a specific private provider — instead of describing training generically; listing the formal training plan's actual content domains (regulatory sampling, safety, QA/QC, SOPs) so the plan is legible as a scope of work rather than an intention; and closing with "documented completion and incentives," which tells the evaluator how compliance with the plan will be proven. The benefits callout is the most transferable element: it translates a training program into the three outcomes a public-agency owner cares about — staff stability and morale, less overtime and contractor spend, and capability growth for the client's own people, not just the contractor's.

Pursuit-specific: swap the private training provider and the state-approved course catalog for what applies in the target state; rebuild the cross-training pairing around the actual facilities in scope; and keep the intern-mentor commitment only where the client has, or wants, an internship pipeline — where it applies, it is a low-cost, high-credit community and workforce-development commitment.
