# Project Narrative — Newly Mobilized Contract Framed Around Investment and Innovation (Small New England WWTP)

Jacobs was unanimously selected by [CLIENT] to operate and maintain a 3.77-MGD wastewater treatment plant, 48 miles of collection system, and 11 lift stations in the Northeast under a 5-year contract, transitioning full operating responsibility from the previous contractor on February 1, 2025.

**Origin.** The town ran a competitive procurement after determining it needed a fresh start on its wastewater operations; Jacobs was selected as the operator judged best suited to the town's needs.

**Investment framing.** Because the write-up is published soon after mobilization, it leads with forward-looking investment commitments rather than a multi-year track record. As part of the partnership, Jacobs is leveraging its capabilities to make significant investments in the town's facilities and system upgrades to improve operability and performance; this investment is expected to enable more efficient, timely distribution of compost and to create potential for future revenue generation through product sales. Planned upgrades include:
- Improving composting operations with new screening equipment to reduce bulking-material requirements and produce a higher-quality, more marketable end product
- Conducting an odor control and dispersion study with value-engineering improvements to reduce odor impact, as a responsible neighbor of the surrounding community
- Implementing innovative technologies and enhancements — such as AI-enhanced CCTV monitoring and analysis (the proposal names the Dragonfly AI platform as the specific tool) — so inspection staff time is maximized efficiently
- Providing technical expertise to plant staff throughout operations and through annual innovation workshops focused on continuous improvement and future planning

**Client validation.** The client publicly expressed confidence in the selection process and in Jacobs specifically as best suited to reinforce successful operation of the system going forward.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: when a reference project is too new to show outcomes, reframe the write-up around (1) how the award was won (unanimous vote / competitive process — a credibility signal in itself), and (2) specific, dated investment and technology commitments rather than vague promises. Naming a concrete technology commitment (e.g., AI-enhanced CCTV analytics, an odor dispersion study) is more credible than "we will bring innovative solutions." Pursuit-specific: swap in whatever specific technology or process investments are actually planned or already funded for the reference contract — don't invent commitments that weren't made.
