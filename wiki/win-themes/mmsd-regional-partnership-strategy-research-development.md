# Strategy 04 — Research and Development Initiatives

*Research and development initiatives create a collaborative ecosystem where plant operators, [CLIENT] leadership, city planners, and regional partners can work together to test, refine, and scale cutting-edge water treatment technologies—which will deliver cleaner water, stronger resilience, and measurable social and environmental benefits.*

**Building on [CLIENT]'s Water Reclamation Facility Research Center**

- Jacobs will invest in joint research with [CLIENT] to complement our annual Innovation Workshop. Ideas from our Innovation Workshop could become research topics that [CLIENT] and Jacobs' SMEs explore at the Research Center (Center).
- Jacobs will fund common staff or research lab equipment to support joint research we conduct together.
- Leverage Jacobs' vast grant/proposal writing and research expertise to develop future research and innovation collaborations and funding opportunities with The Water Research Foundation (WRF), other water research organizations, and funders.
- Jacobs' financial consultants will pursue grant applications (WRF, NSF, EPA, others) on behalf of [CLIENT] to support the Center's operations.
- Provide access to Jacobs PFAS SMEs to support PFAS research at the Center.
- Provide technical support to advance data analytics, attract national research partnerships, and integrate global best practices.
- Provide insights on cost-effective collection system infrastructure maintenance from our AI-powered Dragonfly tool or from our other research around optimizing tunnel storage and pumping.

**Support Business Case Development**

- Develop a business plan framework in partnership with [CLIENT] focused on assuring the Center remains financially sustainable. Rick Warner helped develop a similar framework for the Nevada Water Innovation Institute.
- Use Jacobs' expertise and tools (e.g., Simetrica-Jacobs) for wellbeing valuation, econometrics, and equity analysis to measure and demonstrate how [CLIENT]'s investment in water treatment innovations (e.g., Proteus Upflow Media Filter, GI Performance Testing, Landfill Gas Upgrading Pilot, and Watercourse Corridor Study) improve community health, resilience, and environmental outcomes.
- Examine insights to guide scaling and investment decisions and create frameworks to integrate social value into technology development.
- Translate advances in research findings into social value statements that resonate with stakeholders and funding sources.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the structure of a research offer that costs the client nothing to accept — our people, our grant writers, our equipment money, our workshop pipeline feeding your research agenda — and the business-plan deliverable that addresses the unspoken worry about who pays for a research center after the ribbon cutting. Naming the individual who built a comparable framework elsewhere is what turns that offer from a claim into a credential.

Pursuit-specific: the center's name, the client's named pilot technologies, the research funders relevant to the region, and the individual cited for the precedent framework. Simetrica-Jacobs, the Innovation Workshop, and the Dragonfly tool are corporate assets and travel unchanged.

Pairs with: [Jacobs' recent WRF research portfolio](mmsd-regional-partnership-wrf-research-portfolio.md), which supplies the evidence behind this offer, and [Strategy 03, sustainability and resilience](mmsd-regional-partnership-strategy-sustainability-resilience.md).
