# Project Narrative — Incumbent Turnaround Plus Progressive Design-Build (Small Coastal WWTP)

Jacobs operates a 3.3-MGD wastewater treatment facility, nine pump stations, and associated utility equipment for [CLIENT] in the Northeast, including an industrial pretreatment program (IPP) and management of hauled commercial and domestic septage.

**Origin.** The town wanted to reduce cost and risk after years under a large incumbent operator whose original contract did not include biosolids management and disposal. Jacobs identified the biosolids gap as an opportunity to help the client realize additional savings.

**Early wins (first 18 months).** Jacobs adjusted hauling schedules, monitored energy use, and deployed quality systems to make sludge handling more efficient, achieving more than $500,000 in savings against the original 6-year forecast and about $250,000 against the adjusted forecast over the following 3 years. Concurrently, the team:
- Implemented in-house clarifier rehabilitations
- Completed corrective action items inherited from the previous operator (state-agency-mandated from a prior facility inspection)
- Improved the disinfection system, eliminating the callouts the previous contract operator estimated at approximately 32 per year for sodium-hypochlorite-dosing problems
- Removed six large (30-yard) containers of accumulated trash and debris from throughout the facility and pump stations, recycling scrap metal and returning the proceeds to the client
- Improved general facility and pump station appearance through landscaping, painting, and vegetation removal

**Progressive design-build.** In 2021, under a consent agreement with the state environmental agency, Jacobs helped the town lower its nitrogen discharge limits and update its facilities plan — running hydraulic models, assessing existing equipment, and making capital recommendations. Jacobs was selected as the designer specifically because of its operational knowledge of the plant, positioning the O&M contract as a pipeline into a design-build capital project rather than a separate procurement.

**Recognition.** The facility received consecutive-year permit compliance recognition from the state clean water association — a multi-year "consistent permit compliance" award plus a separate award for three-or-more years of complete permit compliance — as well as a regional EPA operations and maintenance excellence award from an earlier year, together spanning most of the life of the contract.

**Outcome.** The client publicly credited Jacobs with an innovative proposal that increased the annual O&M budget by $100,000 while saving the town $100,000 annually in contract cost, and noted the team was ready to take over operations ahead of the contract's official start.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the "gap identification" hook (biosolids wasn't in the incumbent's scope — find the equivalent gap in the target's current contract) and using a punchy, dated list of first-18-months wins to make an abstract "we'll improve operations" promise concrete. Universal also: positioning O&M as the on-ramp to a design-build capital project when a consent order or facilities-plan update is in play. Pursuit-specific: the "eliminated ~X callouts per year" and "removed N containers of debris" bullets are exactly the kind of specific, visual, low-cost win worth replicating — replace with real, verifiable numbers from the actual reference project, not fabricated ones.
