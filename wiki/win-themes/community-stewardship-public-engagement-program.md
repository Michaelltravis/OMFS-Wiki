# Community Stewardship and Public Engagement Program

Community stewardship is approached as an **operating responsibility, not an occasional outreach effort** — a partner that aligns with the client's values, communicates well, and works collaboratively with client staff and the broader community. Facilities sited within or near residential areas, where severe weather or nuisance conditions can quickly affect neighbors, call for practical actions that matter locally: clear communication, visible follow-through, support for appropriate community sponsorship opportunities, joint facility familiarization, and emergency training with police, fire, and other first responders. The underlying goal is to help the community understand how reliable wastewater operations protect public health, reduce neighborhood impacts, and support quality of life.

**Representative community stewardship activities:**
- **Coordinating facility familiarization sessions and emergency-response training** with local police, fire, and other first responders so they understand the plant and collection system before an incident occurs.
- **Hosting periodic plant tours or small open-house events** for residents, schools, and community groups to show how wastewater operations protect public health, nearby waters, and neighborhood quality of life.
- **Supporting classroom visits, STEM outreach, and educational partnerships** that help local students understand treatment processes, water stewardship, and water-sector careers.
- **Participating in community events with simple, practical public education materials** that explain how proactive operations reduce odors, minimize service disruptions, and support reliable service.
- **Joining local environmental stewardship and cleanup efforts** that reinforce the connection between reliable utility operations and protection of the community's natural environment.
- **Creating internships, mentoring, and career-awareness opportunities** with local or regional schools to build awareness of water-sector careers.
- **Partnering with local schools for murals and exploring native planting options** to support plant beautification.
- **Supporting appropriate community initiatives and organizations** in ways that are visible, practical, and consistent with the community's character.

**Proof-point examples (generalize/replace per pursuit).** At one Northeast wastewater treatment plant O&M contract, the on-site team supported a local river-cleanup brigade through organized river cleanups, provided plant tours, delivered STEM education, and contributed community donations and holiday food drives. At another Northeast wastewater treatment plant O&M contract, the team partnered with a local high school art department to create a facility mural and supported plant tours, STEM education, and a student scholarship program.

## Reuse guidance

The framing device — community stewardship as an "operating responsibility, not an occasional outreach effort," anchored by a short activity menu — is universal and works for any municipal O&M pursuit regardless of community size. Select 3-5 activities that best match the target community's character (e.g., lead with first-responder familiarization and STEM outreach for a dense residential/urban community; lead with environmental cleanup partnerships for a coastal or waterfront community).
