# Community Stewardship and Public Engagement Program

## Community Stewardship

We approach community stewardship in [CLIENT], a coastal New England municipality, as an **operating responsibility, not an occasional outreach effort**. [CLIENT] wants a partner that aligns with its values, communicates well, and works collaboratively with [CLIENT] staff and the broader community, as demonstrated through our **proven community stewardship practices** shown in **Exhibit 5-21**. Our goal is to serve [CLIENT] as both a high-performing operator and a trusted community partner. We will build on proven community stewardship practices implemented across our Northeast O&M projects.

The WWTF sits in a residential area in a dense coastal town where severe weather can quickly affect operations. We will focus on **practical actions that matter locally**: clear communication, visible follow-through, support for appropriate [CLIENT] sponsorship opportunities, joint facility familiarization, and emergency training with police, fire, and other first responders. Our goal is to help the community understand how **reliable wastewater operations protect public health**, reduce neighborhood impacts, and support quality of life. Activities may include:

- **Coordinating facility familiarization sessions and emergency-response training with [CLIENT]'s police, fire, and other personnel** so first responders understand the plant and collection system before an incident occurs.
- **Hosting periodic plant tours or small open-house events** for residents, schools, and community groups to show how wastewater operations protect public health, nearby waters, and neighborhood quality of life.
- **Supporting classroom visits, STEM outreach, and educational partnerships** that help local students understand treatment processes, water stewardship, and water-sector careers.
- **Participating in community events with simple, practical public education materials** that explain how proactive operations reduce odors, minimize service disruptions, and support reliable service.
- **Joining local environmental stewardship and cleanup efforts** that reinforce the connection between reliable utility operations and protection of [CLIENT]'s coastal environment.
- **Creating internships, mentoring, and career-awareness opportunities** with local or regional schools to build awareness of water-sector careers.
- **Partnering with local schools for murals and exploring native planting options** to support plant beautification.
- **Supporting appropriate [CLIENT] initiatives and community organizations** in ways that are visible, practical, and consistent with [CLIENT]'s close-knit character.

**EXHIBIT 5-21.** *Proven community stewardship, tailored for [CLIENT]*

At the Waterbury WTP & WWTP O&M project in Connecticut, Jacobs supports the Naugatuck River Brigade through river cleanups, plant tours, STEM education, community donations, and holiday food drives. At the Westerly WWTP in Rhode Island, Jacobs partnered with the local high school art department to create a facility mural and supports plant tours, STEM education, and a student scholarship program.

## Reuse guidance

The framing device — community stewardship as an "operating responsibility, not an occasional outreach effort," anchored by a short activity menu and two live examples — is universal and works for any municipal O&M pursuit regardless of community size. It is also a quiet incumbent-displacement argument: the activities are things a current operator visibly is not doing. Select three to five activities that best match the target community's character (lead with first-responder familiarization and STEM outreach for a dense residential community; lead with environmental cleanup partnerships and native planting for a coastal or waterfront one). Pursuit-specific: the residential-siting and severe-weather sentence, the "close-knit character" descriptor, and the two proof points, which name the Waterbury WTP & WWTP O&M project in Connecticut and the Westerly WWTP in Rhode Island — swap them for other permissioned reference projects if these already appear elsewhere in the same proposal, and confirm the sponsorship and scholarship commitments are ones the pursuit can actually fund.
