# Executive Summary Opening — Site-Verified Understanding and the Case for Change

*[CLIENT]'s wastewater system — [CLIENT] being a southcentral U.S. municipal water utility trust — is growing fast and preparing for nearly $200 million in capital improvements. It needs more than a contract operator—it needs a partner ready to step in day one, improve permit compliance, tackle H₂S head-on, and hold operations steady through every project.*

## We know [CLIENT] — and what's at stake

Our proposal isn't built on assumptions. Before writing a single page, our team completed multi-day site visits at all five facilities, reviewed available data, and held focused strategy sessions with process, odor, maintenance, lab, automation, and safety subject matter experts (SMEs).

**What we found confirmed the urgency.** North Canadian's headworks are inaccessible for routine maintenance due to H₂S readings up to 200 ppm. Only 3 of 30 odor scrubbers system-wide are operational. Both North Canadian and Deer Creek failed WET tests in Q4 2025. The current maintenance program is reactive—critical equipment chronically out of service, no documented repair plan, and cascading failure patterns repeating nearly every month.

## History serving [CLIENT]

Jacobs' deep understanding of [CLIENT]'s goals and concerns also comes from decades providing consulting and engineering services on 300+ water and wastewater projects.

## Why Jacobs?

**We also see the opportunity.** These aren't design-limited facilities. With the right process discipline, maintenance rigor, odor strategy, and a culture shift from reactive to proactive, they can perform at an award-winning level— and we've built this proposal around facility-by-facility plans and the staff development needed to get there.

BENEFIT TO [CLIENT]: *A partner that did its homework and built this proposal around verified, facility-specific conditions—not assumptions or generic playbooks. A plan grounded in what your facilities need right now.*

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the three-move structure — prove the homework, state the verified failures, then reframe them as latent capability the client already owns — plus the "isn't built on assumptions / not generic playbooks" framing and the italic scene-setter that names the client's own capital program before naming any Jacobs credential. The "BENEFIT TO [CLIENT]" callout that closes each beat runs through the whole executive summary and should be carried across as a device.

Pursuit-specific: every number and every facility-specific finding. Do not carry the H₂S ppm reading, the 3-of-30 scrubber count, the WET test failures, or the 300+ prior projects into another pursuit — they are this client's facts. The count of facilities visited must match the actual site-visit record.
