# Executive Summary Technical Approach — Five Pillars from Compliance to Performance Transparency

## Technical approach: facility-specific, results-driven

Every element of our Technical Approach is grounded in what we observed during site visits and what the RFP requires. From day one, every team member will have clear line of sight to [CLIENT]'s performance goals and how their role delivers them—making accountability part of how we work, not an afterthought. Here's how we'll deliver results across the areas that matter most:

**Compliance & Process Control:** Real-time compliance trend monitoring with structured escalation protocols. SRT-based wasting, DO optimization, and aeration right-sizing at every plant. Equipment upgrades at satellite labs plus operator training and online analyzer restoration to support data-driven process decisions.

**Odor & H₂S Control:** A four-layer strategy—upstream source control at Witcher, equipment restoration, in-plant process adjustments, and targeted monitoring. Backed by proven results at Waterbury, CT (55%+ complaint reduction from source identification and odor control upgrades). Understanding of priority repairs and CIP recommendations needed for odor control success.

**Maintenance & Asset Management:** Highly experienced Maintenance Manager and Asset Manager and on-site team supported by regional resources. Robust asset management program and strong focus on preventive management execution. Predictive maintenance (vibration analysis, thermography, oil analysis) deployed from day one. NexGen EAM advantage—our consulting team is already [CLIENT]'s implementation consultant and brings deep NexGen expertise from other successful projects.

**CIP Construction Integration:** Dedicated CIP Construction Manager backed by Jacobs corporate engineering. Experience providing O&M input at 35%, 60%, and 100% design phases. Maintenance of Plant Operations (MOPO) planning that starts early and maintains full compliance throughout construction—no treatment disruptions.

**Performance Transparency:** Real-time dashboards connected to data systems, giving [CLIENT] on-demand visibility into compliance status, odor data, and maintenance KPIs. Independent QA/QC oversight above day-to-day operations. Monthly Payment Requests that accurately self-report against all Schedule 5 requirements.

BENEFIT TO [CLIENT]: *Your O&M team draws on process, maintenance, odor, SCADA, asset, and other experts across Jacobs — so root causes get solved once and optimization happens seamlessly. Every strategy ties to a measurable RFP outcome, and we inspire staff through a high-performance culture that explains the "why and how" each role affects the whole.*

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the five-pillar frame (compliance and process control, odor, maintenance and asset management, CIP integration, performance transparency) and the rule that each bullet names a mechanism, a tool, or an analogue result — never an adjective. The self-reporting commitment on monthly payment requests and the "independent QA/QC oversight above day-to-day operations" line both convert transparency from a claim into a structure, and travel well.

Pursuit-specific: the named pump station in the odor strategy, the satellite-lab equipment scope, the EAM platform, the design-phase percentages, and the schedule number. Where odor is not a driver, replace that pillar with the client's actual top risk rather than keeping it for symmetry.
