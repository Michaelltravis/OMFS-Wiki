# Project Narrative — Holistic Utility Partnership Drives Multi-Million-Dollar Cost Savings (Large Coastal WWTP)

[CLIENT], a municipal utility operating a 27-MGD wastewater treatment plant in the Northeast, retains ownership and decision-making authority for its plant, approximately 310 miles of sanitary sewer, and 20 pump stations under a 10-year operations agreement with Jacobs. Since the partnership began, [CLIENT] has estimated cost savings of up to $12.7 million compared to its previous operating approach.

**Origin.** [CLIENT] sought an experienced contract operator after sewer overflows affected a nearby river and threatened water quality. The utility ran a competitive selection process and chose Jacobs based on a combined operations, asset management, technical services, planning, and regional-support model.

**Scope and approach.** Jacobs provides full contract operations services covering grit and screening, primary treatment, activated sludge, and secondary clarification, followed by UV disinfection with wet-weather chemical (sodium hypochlorite) backup and chemical addition for low-level phosphorus limits. Jacobs is also assisting with managing an approximately $25 million phosphorus-removal upgrade (tertiary filtration and permanent chemical addition). On-site and regional support staff manage the collection system under a CMOM program — sewer cleaning, CCTV inspection, callout response, fats/oils/grease (FOG) management, and cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) repairs — and Jacobs oversees a third-party-operated merchant sludge incinerator serving the facility. The overall approach includes close monitoring of treatment processes and equipment, proactive maintenance, staff training, and long-term facility planning.

**Treatment train.** Preliminary screening and grit removal, primary treatment, secondary aeration and clarification, seasonal UV disinfection with wet-weather sodium hypochlorite backup, and chemical addition for total phosphorus removal. Design flow is 27 MGD with wet-weather flow capacity below 54 MGD; tertiary filters were under construction at the time of writing.

**Community involvement.** The project team sponsors a river-health volunteer program and plans to contribute staff volunteer hours to riverbank cleanups.

**Outcome.** The client publicly credited the partnership with cost savings estimated at up to $12.7 million relative to the prior operating approach, while also citing improved confidence in day-to-day plant management and a careful, deliberate operator-selection process driven by protecting a sensitive local waterway.
