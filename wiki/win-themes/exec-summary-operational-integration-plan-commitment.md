# Executive Summary Required Plan — Operational Integration Plan for Bringing CIP Projects Into Operations

**Operational Integration Plan (Schedules 16 & 18):** Structured, repeatable process for bringing each major CIP project—onsite hypochlorite generation at North Canadian and Deer Creek, the Deer Creek heat drying facility, and the systemwide biosolids disposal transition—from construction into daily operations. Detailed staffing impacts, maintenance adjustments, training sequences, and risk mitigation for each project. Backed by direct PSI MicroClor® operating experience at similar projects.

BENEFIT TO [CLIENT]: *Plans built from verified, facility-specific intelligence—not boilerplate. Delivered on schedule, coordinated with the [CLIENT] General Manager, and structured to drive accountability from day one.*

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the concept of an operational integration plan as a standing, repeatable process applied project by project, and the four-part per-project deliverable — staffing impacts, maintenance adjustments, training sequences, risk mitigation. In any pursuit with an active capital improvement program, this is the highest-value paragraph in the required-plans sequence, because it addresses the handover gap that clients have usually experienced first-hand.

Also universal: closing the plans sequence with a benefit callout that contrasts verified, facility-specific intelligence with boilerplate, names the client executive the deliverables are coordinated with, and states the delivery commitment.

Pursuit-specific: the three named capital projects, the plant names, the schedule numbers, the product name, and the client's executive title (General Manager here). Replace the project list with the target client's actual near-term CIP projects — an accurate list of three beats a generic promise to integrate "all projects."
