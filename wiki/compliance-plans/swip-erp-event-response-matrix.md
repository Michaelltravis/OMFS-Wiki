# ERP Event-Response Matrix — Key Elements in the Emergency Response Plan

Exhibit 2-28. Key Elements in the ERP.

| Event | Jacobs' Approach |
|---|---|
| Chemical Spill Response | Individual safety data sheets (SDS) will guide proper clean-up and control measures. |
| Asset/Equipment Failure Response | Identification of asset conditions, critical assets, and acquiring equipment spares that may be placed in inventory so emergency replacements can be made. |
| Fire Response | Facility staff will be trained on fire prevention and fire suppression. Local and regional maintenance staff are available for equipment/facility recovery operations. |
| Flood Response | Proper maintenance of facilities so flood control equipment and measures at the facilities are in top condition. Regional staff, properly certified and qualified, are available to fill in for staff shortages that may result from regional flooding. Jacobs' regional technical support staff are available immediately to optimize plant chemical dosing and operations should flooding affect incoming raw water quality. |
| Severe Weather Response | Maintenance of generators, mock drills, debris protection, and extended power contingency. |
| Pandemic/Health Emergency Response | A comprehensive employee health program, including mental/emotional health. Regional staff are available for assisting with operations and maintenance during extreme events, and an emergency staffing plan will help maintain safe operation under reduced staffing if required. |
| Process Failure Response | Continuous monitoring and evaluation of trends to proactively identify potential upsets. Response will include immediate additional testing to identify atypical contaminants, jar testing, and process optimization, which may be required based on changed water quality conditions. Conduct comprehensive source water quality testing to detect possible contamination. Procurement resources are available to remedy chemical shortages. Leverage Jacobs technical resources to identify the issue and assist in the development of the recovery plan. |
| Power Failure Response | Generator startup procedures; sustained plant operations until utility power is restored. |
| Emergency Telephone Numbers | An emergency contact list with site numbers will be posted at each worksite and include project contacts, including [CLIENT], plant manager, safety manager, the Public Safety Department, police, fire, and ambulance for surrounding communities—emergency (911) and non-emergency numbers, area hospitals, and serious incident reporting and crisis management. |
| Emergency Equipment Inventory | If emergency equipment is a rental item or provided as a specialty service by a vendor, agreements for 24/7 service will be put in place in advance. |
| Continuity of Operations (COO) Plan | An effective COO makes sure of uninterrupted operations during immediate needs, such as a pandemic, and plans for longer range needs such as retirement, promotions, etc. The COO focuses on roles and responsibilities, notification requirements, subcontractor guidelines, quarantine guidelines, exposure reporting, and travel requirements, and identifies resources, both internal and external, to respond to staffing augmentation needs. |
| Pipeline Failure/Blockages | Train field crews on a comprehensive response plan for the various types of pipeline failure to guide them in appropriate response, whether it's bypassing, isolating, or venting/flushing and/or rodding sections. CPO will communicate condition to [CLIENT] and provide updates as appropriate. |
| Advance Contracting | Establish in advance agreements for 24/7 service for any emergency rental equipment or services provided by service vendors. |
| Earthquakes | Public and employee safety is the top priority; staff follow posted instructions to seek shelter and avoid hazards, then calmly evacuate to designated areas, with the Safety Supervisor coordinating communication and re-entry only after structural assessments. |

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the full 14-row matrix is the reusable asset — it answers the "how will you respond to X" compliance question for essentially every emergency category an O&M RFP asks about, and it reads as a single exhibit rather than a wall of narrative. Pursuit-specific: the earthquake row reflects a seismic region and the flood/severe-weather rows reflect a coastal Southern California setting — reweight rows to the target region's hazard profile (add hurricane/coastal-storm, wildfire, or extreme-cold rows as appropriate).
