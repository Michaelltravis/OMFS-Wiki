# Safety Plan and Management with BeyondZero Culture and Multi-Year Performance Metrics

## Health and safety

Jacobs has a long history of keeping its employees and partners safe, consistently outperforming industry safety averages because of its focus on training, accountability, and a culture of caring. Employees look out for one another and provide or accept feedback when observing unsafe behaviors or conditions.

Jacobs' health and safety (H&S) program is based on the principle that all incidents are preventable. Jacobs' behavior-based safety approach emphasizes identifying and correcting unsafe behaviors in real time. A cornerstone of this program is BeyondZero Observations (BZOs), which are required of all supervisors and managers and encouraged across all employees. These observations, paired with coaching and positive reinforcement, provide immediate opportunities to improve safety. Jacobs also facilitates knowledge sharing across projects and regions, ensuring lessons learned are widely applied and the H&S program continues to evolve.

The project team will prepare a customized safety plan for the facilities that reflects Jacobs' proven BeyondZero(R) approach and incorporates site-specific needs, including a Cal/OSHA-required Injury and Illness Prevention Program (IIPP). This plan will deliver a culture of safety that engages all employees, reinforces accountability, and protects both staff and the public.

## Safety tracking and performance

Jacobs uses a Safety Scorecard to measure both proactive (leading) and reactive (lagging) indicators. Five leading indicators — such as BZOs, pre-task planning compliance, and training completion — help identify and address trends before incidents occur. Two lagging indicators, TRIR and DART, provide accountability for corrective action. The Safety Scorecard is shared quarterly with project and regional leadership, ensuring transparency and timely response to any deficiencies.

## Reuse guidance

The BeyondZero narrative, Safety Scorecard leading/lagging-indicator framework, and the practice of pairing corporate-wide multi-year metrics with a specific industry-benchmark comparison graphic are fully generic and reusable for any O&M pursuit's safety-plan section. All numeric figures (ERM by policy year, TRIR by year, RIR/DART benchmark percentages) must be refreshed from current corporate safety data before every reuse — never present a stale reporting cycle's numbers as current, and confirm the RFP's required EMR/TRIR thresholds (if any) are actually cleared before citing "better than industry"/"better than requirement" language.
