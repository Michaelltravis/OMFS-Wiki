# Planned Capital Improvements and Key Integration Considerations (Exhibit 5-10)

Our understanding of the planned capital improvements identified in the client's capital schedule, and of what each one changes for the operator, is summarized below. Each row states the facility affected, the improvement, the estimated completion, and the integration consideration our plan must resolve before operational handover.

**EXHIBIT 5-10.** *Jacobs' Understanding of Planned Capital Improvements Identified in Schedule 18*

| FACILITY | PLANNED IMPROVEMENT | EST. COMPLETION | KEY INTEGRATION CONSIDERATIONS |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deer Creek WWTP** | On-site Sodium Hypochlorite Generation (OSHG) | 2029 | Replaces gaseous chlorine, eliminates Process Safety Management (PSM)/Risk Management Plan (RMP) requirements; new chemical handling protocols |
| **North Canadian WWTP** | OSHG | 2028 | Replaces gaseous chlorine, eliminates PSM/RMP; largest facility – highest chemical demand |
| **Deer Creek WWTP** | Biosolids Heat Drying Facility | 2029 | New 24/7 process unit; significant staffing, utility, and odor management impacts |
| **Systemwide Biosolids** | Transition from Land Application to Landfill Disposal | Targeted 2029 | Affects all facilities; changes hauling, lab requirements, and subcontractor relationships |

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four-column shape and the discipline of writing the integration consideration as an operational consequence — what the operator must staff, train, permit, or re-contract — not as a restatement of the project. The pattern of pairing a chemical-conversion project with the compliance burden it removes (PSM/RMP) is a reusable persuasive move. Pursuit-specific: every facility name, improvement, and date. Place this exhibit directly beneath the integration approach narrative, then let the staffing, cost, training, maintenance, and risk blocks work through the same project list in the same order so the reader can track each project across the section.
