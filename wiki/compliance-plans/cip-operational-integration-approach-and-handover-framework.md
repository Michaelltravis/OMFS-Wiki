# Operational Integration Plan — Approach to Client Capital Improvements and Operational Handover

## Integration Approach and the Client's Planned Capital Improvements

[CLIENT]'s planned capital improvements represent a significant investment in system capacity, reliability, safety, and modernization — [CLIENT] being a Southcentral US municipal water utility trust operating four wastewater treatment plants and a systemwide biosolids program. These projects will reshape how your facilities handle disinfection, biosolids processing, and solids disposal — and each one needs to transition smoothly from construction to daily operations without disrupting treatment performance or permit compliance. That's what this Operational Integration Plan is designed to deliver.

Jacobs will provide a structured, repeatable process for bringing each new facility or upgrade into full operational status with the staffing, training, maintenance systems, and risk management in place before day one of operations. **We've built this framework around the specific CIP projects, and it will adapt as [CLIENT]'s capital program evolves (Exhibit 5-10).**

[CLIENT] retains responsibility for planning, design, construction, and commissioning of these improvements. We'll coordinate closely with [CLIENT] staff, engineers, construction contractors, and regulatory agencies to maintain continuity of operations, permit compliance, and safe integration of new infrastructure throughout every project phase. Our CIP Construction Manager (detailed in the Capital Planning section) will lead coordination during the construction phase per Schedule 16. This Operational Integration Plan picks up where construction ends — at the point of commissioning and operational handover.

[CLIENT] also retains the right to modify, defer, or add CIP projects during the contract term. This plan establishes the integration framework; we will develop project-specific integration plans as each CIP project approaches commissioning, detailing the staffing, training, maintenance, and risk management steps tailored to that project's scope and timeline.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the opening that credits the client's capital investment before describing the operator's role; the sentence that names what a new asset needs in place "before day one of operations"; the explicit statement that the client retains planning, design, construction, and commissioning; and the closing move that the framework is generic and project-specific plans follow as each project nears commissioning. Pursuit-specific: the named process areas being reshaped (disinfection, biosolids processing, solids disposal), the exhibit number, the schedule citation for construction coordination, and the named construction-management role. Use this block as the opener of an Operational Integration Plan; follow it immediately with the planned capital improvements table.
