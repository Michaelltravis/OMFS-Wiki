# Storage Management and Backlog Prevention

Storage management is where Schedule 14 and Schedule 21 requirements converge into direct financial and operational risk. Schedule 14 limits storage to 3 days of production without authorization, and Schedule 21 triggers action and potential fees when storage exceeds defined thresholds. Jacobs will manage well below these limits, not to them. Our structured Storage Management Protocol **(Exhibit 5-9)** ties five daily inputs together into a tiered action system that keeps pad utilization controlled before pressure builds.

## Pad Utilization: Tiered Action Protocol

RFP limit: 3 days of production without authorization.

**Inputs monitored daily:** weather/NOAA · dewatering output · hauling schedule · site availability · pad utilization.

**Under 35%**
- Normal operations
- Daily monitoring continues
- No adjustment needed

**35% – 50%**
- Ops Manager reviews
- Adjust hauling schedule
- Check site availability

**Above threshold**
- Notify [CLIENT] (Schedule 21)
- Document conditions
- Implement corrective action

Daily coordination across all five inputs means no single variable becomes a bottleneck. When weather, equipment performance, or site conditions change, we adapt immediately, rather than allowing pressure to build. This approach protects [CLIENT] — a Southcentral US municipal water utility trust — from fee exposure, reduces emergency hauling costs, and keeps the program operating predictably under changing conditions.
