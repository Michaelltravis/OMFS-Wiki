# Stormwater and Emerging Biosolids Legislation

Among these proposals, House Bill 3403 has emerged as the most substantive and actively advancing measure. House Bill 3403 establishes a 3-year biosolids research pilot program administered by the ODEQ and Oklahoma State University, with defined post-pilot outcomes that could result in continued land application or a phased reduction and potential statewide prohibition beginning in 2029. Notably, the bill includes a potential requirement for incremental reductions in land-applied biosolids, effectively mandating reductions of up to 25 percent per year, culminating in a full cessation plan by December 2029 if adverse findings are reached. As a result, House Bill 3403 currently represents the most likely near-term policy change that could materially affect [CLIENT]'s solids management strategy.

## Emerging Regulatory Risk: Oklahoma's Biosolids Legislation

**One of the most significant regulatory developments affecting [CLIENT]’s program is the continued legislative effort in Oklahoma to regulate, restrict, or potentially prohibit the land application of biosolids**, building on the momentum that began with Senate Bill 3 during the 2025 session. In the 2026 legislative session, multiple measures have been introduced that reflect this ongoing push, including House Bill 3411, Senate Bills 1799, 1818, and 2141. Collectively, these bills signal sustained legislative scrutiny of biosolids management through expanded PFAS testing and disclosure requirements, increased transparency and public notification, tighter application restrictions, and, in some cases, outright prohibition.

The primary drivers behind this legislation are odor concerns, PFAS contamination risk, and potential impacts on farmland and drinking water supplies, all of which are directly relevant to [CLIENT]'s 170-site, 13,500-acre land application program.

**We’re tracking this legislation closely and will help [CLIENT] navigate whatever regulatory landscape emerges.** If land application capacity is constrained or eliminated, we have experience developing contingency disposal strategies, evaluating alternative beneficial reuse pathways, and working with regulators on compliant transition approaches. Rather than waiting for the regulatory outcome, we’ll plan for multiple scenarios so [CLIENT] is never caught flat-footed.
