# CIP Integration — Expected Operating and Maintenance Cost Impacts and the Adjustment Process

## Expected Impacts on Operating and Maintenance Costs

Each CIP project will affect operating and maintenance costs across multiple categories, including labor, utilities, chemicals, maintenance materials, and other recurring expenses. Because these projects are still in design or pre-design stages, specific cost impacts can't be quantified today. However, we recognize the directional nature of these changes:

- **OSHG** will shift chemical costs from purchased product to raw materials (salt/brine) and electrical demand, while eliminating PSM/RMP compliance costs at both Deer Creek and North Canadian, a meaningful reduction in both compliance burden and safety risk.

- **The heat drying facility** will introduce new utility costs (significant energy demand for thermal processing), emissions monitoring expenses, and consumable costs, along with the dedicated staffing discussed above.

- **The landfill transition** will replace land application labor and equipment costs with hauling and tipping fees. Our analysis suggests these costs may be comparable, with landfill potentially offering cost advantages in the near term, given the investment otherwise needed to bring odor control and biosolids quality to compliant levels for continued land application.

As each project progresses through the Schedule 16 design phases, we'll work with [CLIENT] — a Southcentral US municipal water utility trust — to identify, quantify, and formalize cost adjustments through the Service Approval Form and Amendment process. After we have operated a complete CIP project for 2 years, [CLIENT] will conduct an audit using GAAP principles to determine fair and equitable cost adjustments. We'll maintain detailed cost tracking in NexGen from the first day of operations for each new facility, providing [CLIENT] with transparent, auditable data to support that process.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the honest refusal to quantify pre-design projects, the cost-category list (labor, utilities, chemicals, maintenance materials, other recurring expenses), the directional bullet per project, and the closing commitment to day-one cost tracking that feeds the client's own audit. Pursuit-specific: the project set, the contract's cost-adjustment instrument and audit interval, and the EAM platform name. Pair with the CIP staffing block (which the second bullet cross-references) and the maintenance program integration block, which describes the tracking configuration in the EAM.
