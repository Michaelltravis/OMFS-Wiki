# Six-Point Compliance Program

Operations are conducted in full compliance with all applicable federal and state environmental requirements. The objective is predictable, uninterrupted compliance that protects public health, receiving waters, and long-term client interests. A Six-Point Compliance Program integrates ethics, disciplined process control, laboratory excellence, data integrity, auditing, and transparent reporting to deliver defensible NPDES compliance. This structured framework embeds compliance into daily operations rather than verifying it after the fact.

The six pillars:

1. **SOPs and Process Control Expertise** — consistency and precision at every stage of operations; state-agency- and EPA-region-compliant sampling supported by proven QA/QC protocols; cross-functional teams for lab, reuse, stormwater, and biosolids.
2. **Transparency and Reporting** — insight into system performance the client can count on; immediate e-reporting submission and permit-violation alerts; real-time data feeding a web-based client dashboard.
3. **Audits and Project Monitoring** — constant improvement through independent validation; internal baseline and ongoing audits drive improvement; audit results included in monthly client briefings.
4. **Oversight and Support by Global Experts** — compliance strength driven by ongoing oversight and support; a bench of dedicated O&M compliance and reporting subject matter experts supporting permit interpretation, training, rapid response, and future preparedness.
5. **Culture of Ethics** — doing the right thing even when no one is watching; transparency in all regulatory communications; a reporting culture guided by honesty and defensibility.
6. **Compliance Training and Tools** — empowering staff to protect the environment; standardized tools, tracking systems, and evaluation matrices; substantial recurring investment in staff training hours.

## Regulatory Oversight and Change Management

Emerging regulatory developments — including nutrient limits, biosolids requirements, PFAS standards, and cybersecurity guidance — are actively monitored, with impacts evaluated and communicated to the client. This forward-looking approach supports proactive planning rather than reactive compliance.

## Incident Response and Continuous Improvement

In the event of a permit deviation due to extreme weather, equipment failure, or abnormal influent conditions, response actions are immediate and transparent. Corrective actions are implemented promptly, required notifications are completed, and each event undergoes documented root cause analysis. Findings are incorporated into updated procedures to strengthen resilience and prevent recurrence.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the six-pillar structure and the "compliance is a designed system, not a statistic" framing — this works as a standalone compliance-plan centerpiece for any regulated O&M pursuit. Tailor per pursuit: replace all quantitative claims (compliance percentage, training hours, number of dedicated SMEs) with accurate figures relevant to the proposing team and verifiable if challenged; adjust the regulatory change-management list (nutrient limits, PFAS, etc.) to match what's actually emerging in the target facility's jurisdiction. Pair with the Laboratory Management and Data Integrity Program block (expands pillar 1) and the Regulatory Compliance Reporting Dashboard block (expands pillar 2).
