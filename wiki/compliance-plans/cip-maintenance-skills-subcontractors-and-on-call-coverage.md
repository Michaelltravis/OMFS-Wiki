# CIP Integration — Specialized Maintenance Skills, Subcontracted Services, and Emergency Coverage

## Maintenance Staffing and Specialized Skill Sets

Each CIP project introduces equipment and systems that require new maintenance competencies. **We'll identify these skill gaps early and address them through a combination of on-site training, manufacturer certification, and support from our national technical resources.**

- **For OSHG systems**, maintenance staff will need proficiency in electrolytic cell maintenance and replacement cycles, brine system management and softening system upkeep, sodium hypochlorite storage tank inspection, and chemical feed system calibration. At South Canadian, where PSI/Microclor® onsite generation equipment is already being installed as part of the current construction, we will gain direct operating experience with this equipment beginning at contract start, building institutional knowledge that transfers directly to the Deer Creek and North Canadian installations.

- **For the heat drying facility**, maintenance requirements will include thermal equipment and dryer system maintenance, emissions control systems, conveyance and material handling equipment, and high-temperature safety system inspection. Our Regional Maintenance Team and national technical resources, including biosolids SMEs with direct thermal drying experience, will supplement on-site staff for specialized maintenance needs during the initial integration period.

## Use of Subcontractors or Third-Party Services

Certain specialized services associated with the new facilities will require subcontractor support. For hypochlorite generation, this may include electrolytic cell manufacturer service agreements for major overhauls and warranty-covered repairs, particularly important given that PSI repair parts are proprietary and cartridge replacements cost approximately $20,000 each. For the heat drying facility, specialized services will likely include emissions testing, thermal system inspections, and potentially dryer manufacturer service support during the initial operating period. **All subcontractors will be integrated into the work management system (NexGen EAM) with scheduled activities, documentation requirements, and performance tracking.**

## Emergency Response and On-Call Coverage

**We'll update emergency response and on-call coverage as new facilities come online.** For hypochlorite generation, this includes chemical release response protocols, system failure contingencies (with reversion to delivered chemical supply), and hydrogen gas safety monitoring. For heat drying, coverage addresses thermal emergency response, emissions exceedance protocols, and fire/explosion prevention. On-call rotations will expand to cover new process areas, with all emergency response procedures developed during the Schedule 16 Startup Services phase.
