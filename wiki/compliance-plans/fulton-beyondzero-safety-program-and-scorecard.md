# BeyondZero Safety Culture, Work Controls, and Safety Scorecard

We are dedicated to preventing complacency through Jacobs’ BeyondZero® program, which fosters a “culture of caring” that empowers all employees to take responsibility for safety. This program encourages employees to actively look out for one another and to provide or accept feedback when observing unsafe behaviors or conditions. Our safety philosophy is built on the belief that all incidents are preventable, with the root cause typically being unsafe behaviors rather than unsafe conditions. This behavior-based approach is key to avoiding complacency, as it focuses on continually monitoring and correcting unsafe behaviors through real-time observations of work activities.

These BeyondZero Observations (BZO) are not just a management tool, they are a team-wide responsibility. Supervisors, managers, and every employee play a role in ensuring safety by engaging in ongoing observations and feedback. This prevents the development of routine habits that can lead to complacency, ensuring continuous improvement in safety practices. Furthermore, we promote knowledge sharing across projects and regions, which allows us to fully leverage lessons learned and build on the strengths of our broad safety capabilities.

Our comprehensive safety program uses a Health & Safety Work Control Plan to direct, conduct, and document work relative to H&S requirements; activity hazard analyses for all high-hazard tasks; pre-task plans to identify hazards and apply controls; inspections/BZOs; task-appropriate PPE; systematic subcontractor management; and H&S improvement planning using a safety-audit scoring checklist and customized safety scorecard. Assessment of safety training needs begins at transition and then annually. Real-time data track and verify that each employee has received required training using our corporate employee learning and tracking program. More than 100 online safety courses and classroom or practical hands-on safety-training programs are available, including hazard communication, permit-required confined-space entry, bloodborne pathogens, lead, benzene, lockout/tagout, working from heights, PPE, and electrical safety.

During our site visits, we observed several areas requiring improvement and will prepare a customized safety program to address these and other safety-related issues: guarding on rotating equipment; storage of material in front of electrical cabinets; access for elevated work; identification for changes in elevation and trip hazards; and barricading for excavations and other fall hazards.

To keep complacency at bay, Jacobs constantly evolves its safety program by integrating real-time data, continuous training, and performance tracking. Our safety scorecard measures both leading indicators, such as proactive safety behaviors, and lagging indicators, such as incident rates, providing our leadership team with insights into emerging trends and opportunities for improvement. This allows us to address potential safety issues before they result in incidents, promoting ongoing vigilance and improvement across the team.
