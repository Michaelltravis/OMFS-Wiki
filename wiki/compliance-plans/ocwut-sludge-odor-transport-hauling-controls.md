# Odor Management in Sludge Operations and Transport Controls

## Odor Management in Sludge Operations

Sludge handling is one of the highest contributors to odor risk across the [CLIENT] system — a Southcentral US municipal water utility trust operating four wastewater treatment facilities — and Schedule 14 requires sludge and biosolids management to minimize offensive odors at facilities, along transport routes, and at land application sites. We describe detailed odor control strategies in the Odor Control Plan. Here, we focus on how sludge management decisions directly influence odor outcomes, as sludge management decisions and odor outcomes are inseparable. **We manage them as one system.**

## Transport and Hauling Controls

Jacobs will transport sludge and biosolids safely, cleanly, and in full compliance with Schedule 14 requirements. All loads will be properly contained, hauling equipment will be maintained to prevent leaks or spills, routes will be selected and managed to minimize community impact, and drivers will follow defined procedures for safe transport and documentation. Where subcontract haulers are used, Jacobs will maintain full oversight and accountability for performance.

**We'll conduct transport in a manner that eliminates spill potential with immediate cleanup and required notifications in the event of any release, consistent with Schedule 14 requirements.**
