# Understanding of [CLIENT]'s Solids Management Requirements

Managing solids across a four-facility system of this scale requires systemwide coordination and a clear understanding of how sludge moves through the system from generation to final disposal. We have reviewed the Schedule 14 Sludge Management Plans, the Schedule 21 Solids Management requirements, and the broader Service Agreement scope. We understand the operational constraints and performance expectations that will drive success at [CLIENT] — a Southcentral US municipal water utility trust operating four wastewater treatment facilities.

**North Canadian** serves as the primary solids processing and storage hub, with thickening, dewatering, lime stabilization, and stacking pad storage. Site observations confirmed elevated hydrogen sulfide levels in solids handling areas affecting safety, access, and reliability. We'll address these conditions immediately through coordinated process control, odor management, and maintenance planning.

**Deer Creek** presents elevated hydrogen sulfide levels and inconsistent cake quality that directly affect odor and hauling performance. We'll continue hydrogen peroxide dosing, evaluate dosing location, and assess additional solutions to stabilize belt press room conditions.

**Chisholm Creek** relies on coordinated transfer of liquid sludge to North Canadian for downstream processing. This dependency requires proactive planning to optimize solids concentration being transferred, reduce hauling volume, and avoid unnecessary cost and storage pressure at the receiving facility.

**South Canadian** is undergoing significant process changes. Maintaining solids process continuity during construction and startup will be a near-term priority requiring close coordination with construction sequencing and operational planning.

We'll continually evaluate opportunities to improve solids processing, focusing on practical improvements that increase reliability, reduce cost, and strengthen compliance based on actual operating conditions at [CLIENT].

Schedule 14 and Schedule 21 make solids management a real-time operational responsibility. Biosolids storage is limited to 3 days of production without authorization, and performance thresholds are tied directly to financial outcomes.
