# Facility-by-Facility Solids Profiles

*Each facility presents its own operational profile.*

## Primary Solids Processing Hub — North Canadian

**North Canadian** serves as the primary solids processing hub, handling thickening, dewatering, lime stabilization, and stacking pad storage for the system. Site observations confirmed elevated hydrogen sulfide conditions in solids handling areas and a significant presence of plastics in the lime-stabilized biosolids, a nuisance for landowners and a compliance risk for the land application program. Jacobs will begin by restoring debris removal equipment to eliminate foreign material from biosolids before land application. If existing equipment can't achieve the required performance, we'll evaluate additional solutions and present recommendations to [CLIENT] before implementation. We'll evaluate covered storage options at the underutilized 2.5-acre compost pad that would further reduce biosolids volume, expand drying capacity, and control odors. This would produce a better-suited product for land application.

## Odor-Sensitive Neighbor — Deer Creek

**Deer Creek** sits adjacent to a residential development, making odor during sludge handling a high-visibility community concern. The RFP requires hydrogen peroxide dosed at 300 mg/L in the sludge transfer line prior to the belt press, a protocol that should deliver an 86% reduction in hydrogen sulfide and a 93% reduction in mercaptans. Site observations indicate this dosing hasn't been consistently implemented. Jacobs will have this protocol in place from day one, coordinated with ferrous chloride feed adjustments to stabilize belt press room conditions and reduce community impact. We'll also fully support the solids processing upgrades coming online at Deer Creek, proactively optimizing the system as improvements are commissioned.

## Liquid Sludge Transfer — Chisholm Creek

**Chisholm Creek** transfers liquid sludge to North Canadian for downstream processing, a dependency that requires active coordination to avoid hauling inefficiencies and storage pressure at the receiving facility. Jacobs will manage liquid hauling to minimize truck traffic and the safety risks that come with frequent tractor-trailer operations at the facility. We'll also conduct a detailed review of the transfer process to identify opportunities to reduce load volume through thickening improvements or mobile dewatering, directly reducing both transportation costs and traffic exposure.

## Newly Commissioned Facility — South Canadian

**South Canadian** is undergoing a complete facility transformation, with a new 8.66 MGD activated sludge plant anticipated to come online in July 2026, concurrent with Jacobs' operational start date. Rather than inheriting an aging system mid-construction, we'll take over a newly commissioned facility. Our transition planning specifically accounts for a seamless handoff of new dewatering, aerobic digestion, and disk thickening equipment, with operator training, startup support, and process documentation in place before we assume operations. From day one, our biosolids experts, including Todd Williams and Drew Mearns, will establish baseline performance, optimize polymer dosing and dewatering protocols on the new equipment, and build a process control foundation that maximizes cake solids quality and land application efficiency. We will walk in prepared.

## Water Treatment Plant Lime Residuals

**Hefner Water Treatment Plant** lime residuals represent a distinct program within scope. Jacobs will manage collection and transport of lime residuals to approved recipients or [CLIENT]-designated locations in accordance with the Hefner Water Treatment Plant Lime Residuals Plan and applicable regulations, applying the same operational discipline used across the wastewater biosolids program.
