# CIP Integration — Staffing Levels, Classifications, and Resource Impacts by Project

## Staffing, Resource, and Cost Impacts

Each planned CIP project will change how [CLIENT]'s facilities are staffed, maintained, and operated — [CLIENT] being a Southcentral US municipal water utility trust operating four wastewater treatment plants. We'll identify and plan for these changes well before each project reaches commissioning, working through the Schedule 16 design phases to build a complete picture of staffing, resource, and cost impacts. The following analysis addresses each project's anticipated impacts. For detailed staffing levels and organizational structure, refer to our **Staffing and Training Plan earlier in this section.**

### Staffing Levels and Classifications

- **OSHG (Deer Creek and North Canadian):** The transition from gaseous chlorine to OSHG will fundamentally change chemical handling at both facilities. Current operations involve 150-lb cylinder handling at Deer Creek and one-ton cylinders at North Canadian — each with its own safety protocols, PSM/RMP compliance requirements, and operator skill sets. On-site generation shifts the operation to bulk liquid chemical management with electrolytic cell systems, brine handling, and hydrogen gas ventilation. We anticipate that existing operations staff can absorb hypochlorite generation responsibilities with targeted training, rather than requiring additional staff. However, the maintenance skill set will need to expand to include electrolytic cell systems, brine quality management, and chemical feed calibration. We'll finalize staffing projections as system designs are completed.

- **Biosolids Heat Drying Facility (Deer Creek):** This is an entirely new process unit requiring dedicated operator coverage. Heat drying demands 24/7 operation with specialized skills in thermal system management, emissions monitoring, material handling, and high-temperature safety. We'll work with [CLIENT]'s design engineers through the Schedule 16 phases to determine the specific staffing model, including shift structure, operator classifications, and whether dedicated maintenance technicians are needed on-site or can be supported by our Regional Maintenance Team during the initial operating period. Our operations and design subject matter experts (SMEs) will support [CLIENT] to ensure safety, sizing, and equipment reliability is captured during the design phases of the Deer Creek expansion project.

- **Land Application to Landfill Transition (Systemwide):** The shift from land application to landfill disposal will reduce certain staffing requirements while creating new ones. Current land application operations require significant lab resources such as daily pH and fecal coliform testing on each day's biosolids production and the field staff coordinating application logistics. Moving to landfill disposal will reduce lab testing requirements and associated staffing, potentially freeing approximately one FTE on the solids side currently dedicated to lime stabilization activities. In exchange, hauling coordination will increase, and we'll work with subcontractors to manage transport logistics to area landfills. We will work with [CLIENT] to confirm this approach remains the best once landfill disposal is finalized.
