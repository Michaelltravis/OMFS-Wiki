# Laboratory Program, Certification Gap, and QA/QC Integration

## Laboratory and QA/QC integration

Reliable compliance depends on reliable data. Jacobs will build and operate a laboratory program at [CLIENT] that is accurate and fully integrated with operations. [CLIENT] is a Southcentral US municipal water utility trust operating four wastewater treatment plants under an ODEQ regulatory regime.

Our due diligence confirmed that the current laboratory is operating without ODEQ facility-level certification, though Oklahoma regulations allow compliance reporting from a laboratory whose director holds the proper credentials. We will operate the laboratory in full compliance with all applicable permit requirements and approved analytical methods from day one. Leading Jacobs' laboratory program at [CLIENT] is Laboratory Manager **Scott Golden**, who brings 21 years of water and wastewater laboratory and operations experience, a Class A Oklahoma Wastewater Laboratory Operators License, and direct ODEQ compliance experience managing laboratory services, DMR preparation, and IPP coordination at an Oklahoma wastewater utility — meeting the Schedule 10 staffing requirement from day one. Scott will oversee all sampling programs, analytical testing, data validation, recordkeeping, and regulatory reporting across all four facilities. Supporting him is Laboratory O&M SME **Tommy Niknezhad**, who personally conducted the [CLIENT] laboratory due diligence during our March 2026 site visit and brings 10 years of expertise in compliance monitoring, sampling programs, and QA/QC implementation. Together, they bring the credentialed oversight, rigorous internal QA/QC protocols, and site-specific knowledge to build a defensible, reliable laboratory program that supports accurate, timely DMR submissions.

The Sampling and Testing QA/QC Plan, required by Schedule 3 with a draft due November 2, 2026, will govern all sampling and analysis activities, covering procedures for sampling and testing untreated and treated wastewater, operating the laboratory in conformance with all applicable laws, and tying laboratory data directly to process control plan targets, odor control plan requirements, and NPDES permit conditions. All analytical methods will follow EPA-approved standards, with chain-of-custody procedures, calibration verification, and internal data validation maintained before any data is submitted.

## Satellite laboratory limitations and instrumentation gaps

Beyond the North Canadian central laboratory, site visit observations identified significant limitations at satellite facilities. Deer Creek and Chisholm Creek can run field compliance parameters only, with no oven or balance for solids analysis. South Canadian is similarly limited. This creates a dependency on North Canadian for all process control data. Jacobs will account for this constraint in staffing and sampling schedules to avoid gaps in daily operational data.

In-line instrumentation reliability is also a known gap. Ammonia analyzers at North Canadian were observed to be offline and had not been serviced since 2022. Jacobs will assess, service, and restore critical process analyzers during transition and incorporate them into the preventive maintenance program going forward.

We also observed improper disposal of mercury-containing CHEMets at Deer Creek, with used kits in broken glass containers. Jacobs will immediately correct this practice upon assuming operations, implement proper hazardous waste handling procedures, and train staff on EPA-compliant disposal requirements.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the "reliable compliance depends on reliable data" opener; the pattern of naming a credentialed laboratory manager and a due-diligence SME as a pair; the QA/QC plan scope sentence tying laboratory data to process control, odor control, and permit conditions; and the finding-plus-fix paragraphs for satellite lab capacity, offline analyzers, and hazardous waste practice. Pursuit-specific: every named facility, license class, staff name, date, and schedule reference.
