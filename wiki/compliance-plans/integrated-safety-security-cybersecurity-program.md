# Integrated Safety, Security, and Cybersecurity Program for Wastewater Facilities

A fully integrated safety, security, and cybersecurity program is designed to protect personnel, safeguard client assets, and maintain uninterrupted wastewater operations — particularly where facilities operate in a flood-prone environment, serve a dense residential community, and must meet strict NPDES permit requirements. The stated objective: **zero injuries, zero security breaches, and zero service interruptions**, with safety, physical security, and cybersecurity integrated into daily operations, maintenance planning, and emergency preparedness.

**Safety leadership and culture.** Facilities are operated under a proactive safety philosophy (e.g., a "BeyondZero"-style program) emphasizing proactive risk management and shared accountability. Safety leadership begins with the Project Manager and extends to all employees and subcontractors. Daily work activities include pre-task planning, activity hazard analyses (AHAs), and clearly defined safe-work procedures. All personnel have and are expected to exercise stop-work authority whenever unsafe conditions are identified. Safety metrics are reviewed with the client during routine meetings to support transparency and continuous improvement. A corporate safety benchmark example: 5-year average Recordable Incident Rate (RIR) of 0.19 vs. an industry average of 0.62 (60% better), and DART/Lost Time Injury Rate (LTIR) of 0.056 vs. an industry average of 0.20 (72% better), based on BLS data — alongside maintaining an Experience Modification Rate (EMR) below 1.0.

**Site-specific safety and security planning.** Within a defined period after Notice to Proceed (commonly 30 days), a Site-Specific Safety Plan and Site Security Plan are submitted for client review. The Safety Plan addresses confined space entry, lockout/tagout, electrical safety, chemical handling, fall protection, gas monitoring, contractor coordination, and emergency response procedures. For coastal or flood-vulnerable sites, the plan also defines storm preparation protocols, staffing contingencies, and flood response procedures to maintain operational continuity. Security measures address both administrative and physical controls: controlled facility access, secured gates and doors, visitor verification procedures, and annual security assessments, with coordination with local emergency responders to ensure familiarity with facility operations and rapid response during emergencies.

**Physical security evaluation.** Physical security is evaluated across the treatment facility, pump stations, and collection system. Existing deficiencies (e.g., perimeter fencing gaps that allow potential unauthorized access) are addressed through immediate repairs and implementation of routine perimeter inspections. Additional enhancements considered to strengthen long-term site security include improved gate locking, signage, lighting, and surveillance.

**Cybersecurity and SCADA protection.** A cybersecurity plan aligned with industry best practices for OT and industrial control systems uses a defense-in-depth approach to protect system integrity and maintain operational continuity. The plan typically includes: system risk assessment; role-based access controls; secure network segmentation between business IT and SCADA environments; secure remote access protocols and password governance; continuous monitoring to identify abnormal activity; and incident response procedures defining notification, containment, and recovery actions. System backups and recovery capabilities support rapid restoration of operations following a cyber or system event, and annual cybersecurity awareness training is provided to on-site personnel to reduce risks from phishing, credential compromise, and other cyber threats.
