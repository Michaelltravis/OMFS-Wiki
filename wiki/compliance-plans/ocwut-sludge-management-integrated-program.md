# Sludge Management as One Integrated Program

## Why Sludge Management Matters

Sludge management is one of the daily responsibilities in this contract with the highest stakes. When it runs well, it's invisible: storage stays controlled, hauling stays on schedule, land application stays compliant, and the community doesn't think twice about it. When it doesn't, problems compound quickly. Backlog builds on the pad, odor complaints follow, and compliance exposure grows.

**[CLIENT]'s program — a Southcentral US municipal water utility trust operating four wastewater treatment facilities — does not need reactive management. It needs a partner who treats solids as a planned, coordinated operational function from day one.**

Jacobs will manage sludge from generation through final use or disposal as one fully integrated program, connecting process control, dewatering, storage, odor management, hauling, land application, and compliance reporting into a coordinated system that stays ahead of problems rather than chasing them. This Sludge Management Plan addresses Schedule 14 requirements directly and aligns with our Solids Management Plan, Odor Control Plan, and Process Control Plan. Solids, odor, and process performance do not operate independently. We manage them together.

## Understanding the Existing Program

[CLIENT] operates an established and regulated sludge and biosolids program that requires daily coordination and disciplined execution across four facilities, approximately 170 permitted land application sites, and roughly 13,500 acres.

ODEQ-approved Sludge Management Plans are in place for North Canadian, Deer Creek, Chisholm Creek, and South Canadian. Jacobs will manage operations in full compliance with each plan and support renewals, updates, and new site permitting as needed with [CLIENT] approval.

## Sludge Generation, Processing, and Class B Treatment

[CLIENT]'s sludge program operates under four ODEQ-approved Sludge Management Plans, 40 CFR Part 503 Class B requirements, and Schedule 14 and 21 obligations that carry direct financial consequences when storage thresholds are exceeded or reporting lapses **(Exhibit 5-8)**. With four facilities, around 170 permitted land application sites, and roughly 13,500 acres under management, the compliance exposure is continuous, and the only way to stay ahead of it is to manage solids as one fully integrated system. Jacobs will connect every stage from generation through final disposal under a single coordinated program, where dewatering performance, storage levels, hauling schedules, and land application decisions all inform each other. That coordination protects [CLIENT]'s compliance record and drives direct cost savings through optimized polymer use, reduced hauling volume, and the elimination of emergency response premiums.
