# CIP Integration Risk and Continuity of Operations Controls

Transitioning to upgraded facilities introduces specific risks that require proactive planning. We have identified the key risk categories associated with [CLIENT], a Southcentral municipal water utility trust, and its planned CIP projects (**Exhibit 5-11**) and will manage each through targeted mitigation strategies.

**Exhibit 5-11.** *Summary of Key CIP Integration Risks and Mitigation Strategies*

| RISK CATEGORY | SPECIFIC RISK | AFFECTED CIP PROJECT(S) | MITIGATION STRATEGY | SCHEDULE/CONTRACT REFERENCE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operational | Process disruption during tie-ins and system cutover | All CIP projects | MOPO planning developed jointly with [CLIENT] engineers through design phases; contingency process configurations ready before cutover | Schedule 16 (all phases) |
| Operational | Unfamiliar equipment behavior during initial operations | Hypo generation, heat drying | Staff participation in commissioning/proofing, manufacturer support during startup, conservative initial operating parameters | Schedule 16 Startup Services, Schedule 18 §2(b) |
| Operational | Chlorine-to-hypochlorite changeover disrupts disinfection | Deer Creek, North Canadian | Maintain backup delivered chemical supply until on-site generation proven reliable; parallel disinfection capability during transition | Schedule 5 COM-1 (100% NPDES compliance) |
| Staffing | Skill gaps for new technology (electrolytic generation, thermal drying) | Hypo generation, heat drying | Manufacturer training and certification, Jacobs technical resource support, phased assumption of operations (no independent operation until documented competency) | Schedule 18 §2(b), Staffing & Training Plan |
| Staffing | Competing demands from staff learning new systems while maintaining existing operations | All CIP projects | Schedule integration during lower-demand periods; supplemental staffing from Jacobs’ regional resources during transition windows | Staffing & Training Plan |
| Compliance | Permit exceedances during process transitions | All CIP projects | Parallel treatment capability through MOPO, enhanced monitoring during integration, pre-defined trigger points for reverting to prior configurations | Schedule 5 COM-1 (0.75% fee at risk per facility), Schedule 16 |
| Compliance | Emissions non-compliance at heat drying facility | Deer Creek heat drying | Monitoring protocols and operator training established before operational handover, emissions response procedures in place pre-startup | Schedule 12 (Permits), Schedule 18 §2(d) |
| Compliance | Biosolids regulatory transition (land application to landfill) | Systemwide | Early ODEQ coordination, phased transition, contingency for continued land application if heat drying timeline shifts | Schedule 5 COM-5 (0.25% fee at risk per facility), Schedule 18 §2(a) |
| Continuity | Treatment performance degradation during any integration period | All CIP projects | Go/no-go criteria for each operational handover, fallback procedures if new systems don’t perform during proofing, clear [CLIENT] communication protocols throughout transition | Schedule 5 (full Performance Requirements); Service Agreement §3.07 |

Throughout every CIP integration, we will maintain uninterrupted treatment performance and full compliance with Schedule 5 Performance Requirements. Each project-specific integration plan will include defined go/no-go criteria, fallback procedures, and clear communication protocols with [CLIENT].

## Reuse guidance

Use as a project-specific integration risk register. Tailor risk owners, operating constraints, compliance commitments, and project assets; retain defined go/no-go and fallback controls.
