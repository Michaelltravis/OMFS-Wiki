# QA/QC Program with Annual Audit Process (Regional Team Model, 4-Step Audit Lifecycle)

## In-house procedures to provide accuracy, integrity, and quality control

To ensure operational accuracy, data integrity, and ongoing quality control at all facilities, Jacobs will implement a robust Quality Assurance and Quality Control (QA/QC) Program tailored to the client's operational priorities and permit obligations.

Jacobs will develop a comprehensive Quality Assurance Plan outlining the processes, metrics, and verification protocols used to monitor performance across the treatment facility, ancillary facilities, and associated diversion and lift stations. Results are shared with the client through formal channels as detailed in the Communications and Reporting subsection of the proposal.

Jacobs' QA/QC procedures have been refined over four decades of O&M delivery across hundreds of facilities. Each project undergoes a standardized internal audit beginning six months after start-up. The frequency and depth of future audits are driven by performance scores in core operational and managerial areas.

At the facilities, Jacobs will deploy a dedicated QA/QC team drawn from its extensive regional and national technical support resources to conduct comprehensive audits, covering 70+ specific criteria. These include:

- Compliance with WDRs and Title 22 GRRP requirements
- Compliance with South Coast Air Quality Management District (SCAQMD/AQMD) requirements
- Training documentation and certification tracking
- SOP implementation and consistency
- Sampling plans and analytical accuracy
- Maintenance backlog and CMMS accuracy
- Communication protocols and emergency readiness, including a CalEPA-required Emergency Response Plan (ERP) and Injury & Illness Prevention Plan (IIPP)
- Financial stewardship and budget tracking

Once audit results are provided to the local project team's leadership, Jacobs' robust bench of technical resources steps up to help the local team address any gaps that may have been found.

## Annual quality audit — governance model

Jacobs' audit process is organized as a continuous loop centered on the **Local Team**, supported by four surrounding functions: the **Compliance Team**, **Regional Manager**, **Regional Process Specialist**, and **Regional Maintenance Specialist**, with an **Audit Team** completing the cycle.

## 4-step audit process

1. **Preparation** — The audit team sends an information request to the Chief Plant Operator (CPO). Alerts go to the Compliance Team and regional O&M technical support of the upcoming audit. The Director/Manager of Operations and CPO provide preliminary information for review.
2. **Onsite Assessment** — Field assessments, staff interviews, and review of project records are conducted. A follow-up is scheduled within three weeks to discuss results.
3. **Evaluation** — Information provided is reviewed for completeness and accuracy; field observations are compiled; a review meeting is held with the Director/Manager of Operations, CPO, and Geographic Manager; a draft action plan is created to correct deficiencies if needed.
4. **Action Plan** — The Director/Manager of Operations and CPO begin corrective tasks; proof of task completion is logged to Jacobs' internal Corrective Action Plan database; reminders and progress checks occur at 30, 60, and 90 days; the audit closes out when all tasks are recorded as completed/resolved.

Jacobs will also integrate feedback loops between QA/QC audit findings and CMMS updates to ensure lessons learned translate to long-term reliability improvements. By applying this rigor to the facilities' assets, Jacobs will deliver performance consistency, operational integrity, and continuous improvement throughout the contract term.
