# Maintenance Workflow, Self-Performance, and M&R Fund Governance

Maintenance will be managed through a **simple, repeatable workflow** that keeps work visible, prepared, efficiently executed, and fully documented. **Exhibit 5-16** summarizes how maintenance work will be planned, prepared, executed, and closed out at the wastewater facilities of [CLIENT], a coastal New England municipality.

**EXHIBIT 5-16.** *Jacobs' maintenance workflow approach*

| **Plan** | **Prepare** | **Execute** | **Close Out** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schedule and bundle work | Stage parts, tools, and safety requirements | Self-perform routine and most corrective work | Update asset history and keep backlog current |

## Self-Performance with Specialist Depth

Routine maintenance and most corrective work will be **self-performed with support from regional specialists**. Subcontractors will be used only for specialty electrical work, OEM or warranty work, heavy rigging, certified repairs, or other discrete services requiring specialized equipment or credentials. This approach improves responsiveness, reduces handoffs, and aligns with [CLIENT]'s desire for less reliance on subcontractors for routine work. As noted in the Collections subsection above, specialty collection system CCTV and sewer cleaning services will be delivered through qualified vendors when that model is more economical, while Jacobs retains planning, oversight, QA/QC, and single-point accountability to [CLIENT].

## Housekeeping Focused on Clean, Orderly Facilities

Housekeeping will be treated as part of the maintenance program, not as work completed only when time allows. Cleanliness, organization, labeling, and minor site upkeep will be embedded into rounds, check sheets, and CMMS tasks, resulting in **safer facilities, better work preparation, and stronger pride of ownership** across the WWTF and pump stations.

## Whole-System Asset Planning

We will apply **one integrated asset strategy** across [CLIENT]'s WWTF, pump stations, standby power systems, flood-control assets, and low-pressure grinder system. CMMS history, observed condition, repeat failures, and obsolescence risk will be used to develop **practical, decision-ready capital recommendations** and help standardize future upgrades where appropriate. Grinder pumps will also be managed as a distinct asset class, using work-order history and failure data to improve repair planning, replacement forecasting, and lifecycle decision-making.

Proactive maintenance planning will be paired with **transparent cost control**. As summarized in **Exhibit 5-17**, Jacobs will manage [CLIENT]'s M&R fund in accordance with approval thresholds and reporting requirements, giving [CLIENT] **clear visibility into expenditures, balances, and decision points**.

**EXHIBIT 5-17.** *M&R governance*

| **< $3,000 Corrective Work Order** | **$3,000 and above** | **On-Site Labor and Consumables** | **Specialty Subcontracted Labor** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charge to M&R without [CLIENT] pre-approval | [CLIENT] pre-approval required | Not charged to M&R | [CLIENT] pre-approval where required |

## Integrating SOPs, Training, and Storm Readiness into Maintenance Practices

SOPs and O&M procedures will be kept current, reinforced through supervisory review and shift turnover, and supported by structured skills assessment and cross-training to strengthen long-term workforce capability. Because [CLIENT]'s facilities operate in a storm-exposed coastal environment, early priority will also be given to **standby power validation, flood-control readiness, alarm verification, and contingency procedures** so maintenance planning supports system resilience and equipment reliability.

## Reuse guidance

Universal: the four-stage Plan / Prepare / Execute / Close Out workflow exhibit, the self-performance model with its explicit list of what stays subcontracted (specialty electrical, OEM/warranty, heavy rigging, certified repairs), housekeeping-as-discipline, and above all the tiered M&R governance exhibit — a small table that answers the cost-transparency question most municipal evaluators actually care about. The single-point-accountability sentence ("Jacobs retains planning, oversight, QA/QC, and single-point accountability") is the standard answer to an RFP that asks how subcontracted work is controlled. Pursuit-specific: the $3,000 approval threshold and the cost categories excluded from the fund, which come from this contract and must be reset from the target contract; the grinder-pump and flood-control asset classes; and the storm-readiness paragraph, which drops cleanly for inland facilities.
