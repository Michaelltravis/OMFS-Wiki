# Landfill Transition Operational Integration Framework

The systemwide transition from land application to landfill disposal is one of the most significant operational shifts in this contract. It affects all facilities, changes subcontractor relationships, alters lab and monitoring requirements, and sets the stage for the eventual integration of the Deer Creek heat drying facility. We’ll develop a detailed Biosolids Transition Plan in coordination with [CLIENT], a Southcentral municipal water utility trust, as the timeline takes shape.

A key advantage of initiating the landfill transition early in the contract, rather than waiting for the heat drying facility, is that it simplifies the eventual thermal drying integration. Instead of changing multiple elements simultaneously (landfill contracts, hauling logistics, subcontractor scope, regulatory approvals), the early transition establishes those relationships and logistics so that when the thermal dryer comes online, it’s a single internal change rather than a multi-party coordination challenge. The planning framework will address phasing: how and when each facility transitions, coordinated with [CLIENT]’s priorities and the Deer Creek heat drying facility timeline. We may recommend a selective early transition, for example, diverting biosolids with quality concerns (such as paper debris) to landfill first while maintaining land application for compliant material during the transition period.

The framework will address hauling logistics: transport distances, vehicle and equipment requirements, landfill agreements, and scheduling. Area landfills have indicated capacity for [CLIENT]’s biosolids volume, though we will verify capacity commitments and understand restrictions (moisture content limits, percentage of total landfill capacity for wet material) before finalizing agreements. It will also address cost of transport and final disposal: framework for estimating per-wet-ton and per-dry-ton costs, tipping fees, and fuel. The transition will replace current subcontractor costs (currently managed through Synagro) with direct hauling and disposal costs. Staffing and lab adjustments will cover reduced land application crew requirements, re-deployment of lab personnel currently performing daily biosolids testing, and changes to monitoring protocols once land application ceases. Regulatory transition will cover ODEQ notification requirements, permit modifications, and closure of land application sites. Contingency will retain land application capability if the heat drying facility timeline shifts or landfill capacity issues arise.

For our biosolids management strategy, including current operations and long-term solids handling, refer to our Solids Management Plan in this section.

## Reuse guidance

Use this as operational sequencing language, not as the solids-management narrative. Tailor disposal vendors, regulator, transition phases, capacity restrictions, and future treatment asset.
