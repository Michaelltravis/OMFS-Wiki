# Operational Integration of Capital Improvements

[CLIENT], a Southcentral municipal water utility trust, has planned capital improvements that represent a significant investment in system capacity, reliability, safety, and modernization. These projects will reshape how your facilities handle disinfection, biosolids processing, and solids disposal—and each one needs to transition smoothly from construction to daily operations without disrupting treatment performance or permit compliance. That’s what this Operational Integration Plan is designed to deliver.

Jacobs will provide a structured, repeatable process for bringing each new facility or upgrade into full operational status with the staffing, training, maintenance systems, and risk management in place before day one of operations. **We’ve built this framework around the specific CIP projects, and it will adapt as [CLIENT]’s capital program evolves (Exhibit 5-10)** .

[CLIENT] retains responsibility for planning, design, construction, and commissioning of these improvements. We’ll coordinate closely with [CLIENT] staff, engineers, construction contractors, and regulatory agencies to maintain continuity of operations, permit compliance, and safe integration of new infrastructure throughout every project phase. Our CIP Construction Manager (detailed in the Capital Planning section) will lead coordination during the construction phase per Schedule 16. This Operational Integration Plan picks up where construction ends—at the point of commissioning and operational handover.

[CLIENT] also retains the right to modify, defer, or add CIP projects during the contract term. This plan establishes the integration framework; we will develop project-specific integration plans as each CIP project approaches commissioning, detailing the staffing, training, maintenance, and risk management steps tailored to that project’s scope and timeline.

Each planned CIP project will change how [CLIENT]’s facilities are staffed, maintained, and operated. We’ll identify and plan for these changes well before each project reaches commissioning, working through the Schedule 16 design phases to build a complete picture of staffing, resource, and cost impacts. The following analysis addresses each project’s anticipated impacts. For detailed staffing levels and organizational structure, refer to our **Staffing and Training Plan earlier in this section.**

Each CIP project introduces equipment and systems that require new maintenance competencies. **We’ll identify these skill gaps early and address them through a combination of on-site training, manufacturer certification, and support from our national technical resources.**
