##### **POSITIONING HULL’S WASTEWATER PROGRAM FOR ITS NEXT PHASE** 

###### **_The Town of Hull is ready for a change._** 

The Town has invested heavily in resiliency, mechanical upgrades, electrical improvements, and collection system rehabilitation, clear evidence of its commitment to protecting essential infrastructure. But the Town is seeking more than regulatory compliance. It is looking for **stronger leadership, clearer communication, and a proactive asset‑management approach** that anticipates issues before they become problems. Above all, the Town wants a **trusted partner** , one willing to take justified risks, stand behind its decisions, and safeguard the community’s long-term interests. 

Jacobs will deliver that change, **using the approaches listed in Exhibit 2‑1** . The Hull wastewater system supports not only the Town’s residents, but also a sensitive coastal environment and a community that has been valued across the greater Boston region for generations. Maintaining and improving the infrastructure that supports this environment **requires disciplined operations, thoughtful planning, and a long‑term perspective** . 

Serving approximately 10,000 year-round residents and an additional 5,000 seasonal residents, Hull’s wastewater system includes a 3.07-MGD wastewater treatment facility, seven pump stations, a stormwater pumping station, 42 miles of gravity sewer, a lowpressure system, and an ocean outfall. These assets operate under conditions shaped by tidal influence, wet-weather variability, and seasonal demand, requiring **proactive management and operational discipline** .

###### **PROACTIVE ODOR PREVENTION AND SYSTEMWIDE INSIGHT:** 

We will implement a **science‑based odor management strategy** using Wastewater Aerobic/Anaerobic Transformations in Sewers (WATS) modeling to evaluate hydrogen sulfide formation, optimize chemical dosing, and **reduce odor and corrosion risks before they occur** . This approach minimizes trial-and-error field efforts and provides **predictive tools to improve decision‑ making and system performance** . 

**ACCELERATED INFLOW/INFILTRATION (I/I) DETECTION USING SMARTCOVER TECHNOLOGY:** We will deploy **SmartCover®** monitoring units to identify I/I more efficiently than traditional methods. This approach **reduces investigation timelines by up to 12 months** , provides real-time system visibility, and **targets resources where they deliver the greatest impact** .

###### **_Baseline Operations_** 

A coordinated approach integrating operations, maintenance, and process engineering expertise will be applied to confirm treatment performance is **stable, optimal, and compliant** . Our process engineering and operations teams evaluated existing operations using data and information provided through the RFP. To establish a baseline understanding of current operating conditions at the Hull WWTF, our team reviewed the full historical dataset available for 2024 and 2025, supplemented by two on‑site tours. This analysis focused on data quality and treatment performance to identify **operational gaps, risks, and improvement opportunities** . 

Our review of the plant’s operational records revealed several **data integrity and process-control issues** that directly affect day‑to‑day performance and increase compliance risk, including: 

- µ<sup>**Primary Clarifier Data Gaps.**Limited performance data</sup> were available for the primary clarifiers, even during periods of operation. The absence of solids removal and sludge blanket data limits optimization and odor management. 

- µ<sup>**Chemical Usage Anomalies.**Historical chemical</sup> consumption records contain numerous negative values, indicating data entry errors or inconsistent chemical handling and inventory practices. These discrepancies true chemical demand and limit optimization efforts. 

- µ<sup>**Inconsistent Mixed Liquor Suspended Solids (MLSS) and**</sup> **Solids Inventory Control.** MLSS values fluctuate without corresponding changes in flow or wasting practices, suggesting inconsistent solids inventory management and limited aeration basin mass balance control. Operating at a consistent solids residence time (SRT) is critical to maintaining process stability and efficient operations. Sludge wasting occurs intermittently rather than continuously, with normalized wasting rates (600‑900 pounds/day) below typical requirements, which contributes to excess solids inventory, increased clarifier loading, 

and higher oxygen demand. Low sludge volume index (SVI) readings reflect dense, over‑aged sludge consistent with insufficient solids turnover. 

- µ<sup>**Lack of Dissolved Oxygen (DO) Data.**No DO data</sup> were available, indicating limited aeration control instrumentation and reduced ability to maintain stable biological conditions. This concern was reinforced by observed poor sludge floc quality during site visits. 

Despite the data challenges, the facility has maintained NPDES compliance for effluent biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) and total suspended solids (TSS). 

Our team developed a preliminary process simulation model using 2024–2025 operating data, facility planning information, and available drawings. This whole‑plant model evaluates mass balance across more than 70 wastewater components and simulates treatment performance under varying conditions. Results confirm the facility can achieve strong performance under its current configuration once core process controls are stabilized, including: 

- µ<sup>**Consistent Wasting.**Transitioning to steady, proportional</sup> wasting stabilizes MLSS, improves SRT control, and prevents biomass over‑aging. 

- µ<sup>**Sludge Storage and Throughput Management.**Improved</sup> control of sludge blanket depths and thickening throughput reduces the risk of solids washout and clarifier stress. 

- µ<sup>**Predictable Effluent Quality.**With improved SRT</sup> management and aeration control, the facility can achieve stable effluent performance with minimal upset risk.

New Jersey American Water (NJAW) is the largest water service provider in the state of New Jersey, serving approximately 2.8 million people in 190 communities. Following the acquisition of the Bound Brook sewer collection system in August 2022, NJAW wanted to identify and reduce inflow and infiltration (I&I) in the system. Traditional I&I isolation methodologies require 18 to 24 months of field assessment and analysis.

Traditional I&I evaluation methods, such as smoke and dye testing, require 18 to 24 months of field assessment and analysis.

SmartCover’s sewer monitoring technology, combined with data evaluation methods developed by the Hazen and Sawyer (Hazen) environmental engineering team, was selected to accelerate the traditional identification of I&I in the collection system. I&I was identified and analyzed by collecting sewer level data from selected manholes using SmartCover units during dry and wet weather periods. 

In an effort to expedite the characterization of Bound Brook sewer assets, NJAW built out the entire system with 40 satellite-based SmartCover Deployment monitoring units to provide real-time data. I&I identification efficiencies were gained by collecting water level data generated at selected manholes with equipment placed strategically throughout the collection system network. The remote sewer monitoring technology integrated National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) weather (doppler radar) as well as United States Geological Survey (USGS) river/stream and ocean gauges. Typical dry weather level patterns were compared to manhole level responses during rainfall/wet weather events.

#### **APPENDIX F – WATS MODELING** 

###### **WASTEWATER AEROBIC/ANAEROBIC TRANSFORMATIONS IN SEWERS (WATS) MODELING** 

Jacobs will apply the Wastewater Aerobic/Anaerobic Transformations in Sewers (WATS) model to support a data-driven understanding of odor and corrosion conditions across a select portion of Hull’s collection system. WATS is the industry’s leading sewer process model, developed through collaboration between Jacobs and global experts at Aalborg University and continuously refined through application on complex collection systems worldwide. 

Unlike traditional approaches that rely heavily on limited field sampling and extended pilot testing, WATS simulates the physical, chemical, and biological processes that drive sulfide generation, odor release, and corrosion within the collection system. The model evaluates wastewater characteristics, detention time, turbulence, air-liquid mass transfer, and ventilation dynamics to predict hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) concentrations and corrosion rates throughout the system.
